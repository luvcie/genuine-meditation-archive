# Year 2018 - The Direct Path of Awareness

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 12 points · 2018-01-01

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/7nfwid/year_2018_the_direct_path_of_awareness/>

Dear sangha friends,

While many spent their Christmas, I made a pilgrimage trip with some monastic and lay friends to the various sites where the Buddha preached his first sermon, his sutras, as well as his places of birth, enlightenment and parinibbana. Along the same path went several Tibetan Rinpoches and eminent monks. The current Dalai Lama is currently in Varanasi and will proceed on to do the Kalachakra Tantra teaching in Bodhgaya, where the famous Bodhi tree and the Dharma-Throne of Gautama Siddharta are. As such, these holy places are filled with huge emanation power, buddha-fields. It is wonderful to see many practitioners, in thousands to tens of thousands, practicing in accordance to the Buddha.

Now it is the start of the New Year, and it is an essential time to consider one's own practice. Are you seeing the fruits of the Path? Or are you stuck on the Path? If one is stuck, then why is one not asking any questions? If one is not practicing, then why is one not contemplating the suffering of the six realms?

Firstly, one must develop one's merits. By engaging in continuous meritorious work, generosity, charity, giving both internally and externally, to oneself and others, for the well-being of humanity and beyond - one lays down ample karma-seeds for a more conducive environment for genuine Buddhist practice.

I do not mean to give money, to give clothes, to give all these things to people! It is not just the act - this is all training, this is all part of the Path. When you speak rightly, in accordance to the Noble Eightfold Path, abstaining from false-speech, slander, gossip, flowery-speech, and so on, this is a form of giving. Yes, you can certainly give to charities - but without the pure heart, it is incomplete. One must constantly develop warm-heartedness, compassion and kindness, otherwise this path will become unstable.

Another way one gives, is to deeply understand the right method of internal giving. The right method of internal giving, is to directly recognize the primordial nature of awareness and to enter into One Mind with the entire universe.

What is One Mind? Body (external) and Mind (internal) are completely emptied out, completely unified with empty space. This brings us to the second point - how does one go onto awakening?

The second point is hence Non-Discriminative Direct Perception. By maintaining awareness without duality of subject and object, at that one moment, one is one with all the Buddhas.

But for many people, their minds cannot be completely like that - in a few moments, sensations, emotions and afflictions arise, continuously, incessantly, and this pure awareness scatters and is lost completely. One regains it temporarily in sitting meditation, and upon getting up from the seat, this awareness disappears and one is lost again in chasing after sense-desires. When one falls asleep, one's awareness continues scattered.

What is the danger? The danger is exactly this - when the karma-seeds blossom and manifest from the Ground of the Alaya-consciousness (8th level of consciousness), they blossom and turn into phenomena of the six realms.

For example, when anger blossoms, caused by certain conditions that have come together at that one moment, this anger explodes, and having no awareness at that point, this anger is then reinforced and returns seeds into the Alaya-consciousness, where they await to sprout yet again.

Many habits are like this. When one's karma-seeds in anger are dominant, one has a higher chance of going to the Hell Realms; With greed/desire/lust, to the Hungry-Ghost Realms; With Ignorance, the Animal Realms; With jealousy, the Human Realms; With pride, the Asura Realms; With hedonism and pleasure-seeking, the Deva Realms. One is constantly stuck in samsara exactly because of this - the karma-seeds are not prevented from being seeded in the Alaya-consciousness, and upon reaching conditions ripe for their blossoming, they manifest.

In fact, one does not need to be reborn to enter such a realm. As a human being, one experiences the realms - when angry, one is in hell; when prideful, one is an asura! Therefore, when there is pure enlightened thought without afflictions, this is Buddhahood.

Hence from this, one can already see the benefits of constantly engaging in virtuous thoughts as in the Hinayana vehicle. It is a form of countering, a form of proactive karma-seeding, sowing meritorious seeds for Dharma practice.

Only a person with such "good roots" will be able to develop enough merit to have a less afflicted mind. With a less afflicted mind, there is more wisdom to grasp more subtle teachings.

Now in the Mahayana/Vajrayana vehicle, one moves directly into Pure Awareness without any discrimination. Now as I have often explained, there are eight levels of consciousness. The first five are sense-consciousnesses, that of nose, tongue, ear, eye and body consciousnesses. These five senses are like gates with no control or alteration whatsoever - what comes in, comes in - what goes out, goes out. When these five senses are able to be directly perceived, without alteration, this is excellent - however, most of the time, it is not the case.

The sixth is that of the mental sense-consciousness. This sixth consciousness is the absolute key and is often likened to Awareness. When this Awareness is pure and lucid, it is able to know the coming and going of phenomena without being involved or dragged away. This includes both waking and sleeping.

Now is this Awareness the same as the primordial nature? Not so! It is a function and an effect of the Ultimate, but until primordial nature emerges, it is not equivalent. Once awakened, once primordial nature merges, then this Awareness will become equivalent to the Ultimate. Therefore, one has to train in power of Awareness. The less afflicted one's mind is, the closer one is to enlightenment.

There are four bases of mindfulness that the Buddha taught for Satipatthana:

- Body

- Feelings

- Mind/Citta

- Mental qualities/Dhammas

But for now, in this post, for the sake of better understanding, we will use a slightly different categorization, in terms of:

1. The Five Senses

2. Feelings

3. Afflictions (mainly Anger, Greed/desire, Ignorance)

# (1) Five Senses

When you hear a sound, how is it that you know that you hear a sound? When hear a gong, for example, immediately after the gong, you will realize that there is an Awareness that remains even after the sound disappears.

For most people, however, this Awareness is not reinforced. After one sensation, it goes on to yet another, and yet another, and yet another. One suddenly is in this auto-pilot mode, with Awareness thrown far behind in the background, forsaken. One goes from a sound, to a sight, to a taste, to a touch, and so on. Therefore, one should practice to be able to retain Awareness regardless - Awareness should be like a reminder, right in the foreground, it is there, just like a huge, infinite ocean of water. All of these sensations are just like salt that appear and dissolve away into the ocean. At no point has this Awareness disappeared - it has always been there.

# (2) Feelings

The problem with Feelings is mainly two: Pleasure and Pain. When one experiences some kind of condition that allows the rise of pleasure, when there is no awareness there, it allows pleasure to yet again plant a seed of Greed in the Alaya-consciousness. Likewise, when one experiences Pain, it plants more seeds of suffering.

Again, one should not focus on what has caused the pleasure or pain. Instead, one should stay with the Awareness or Knowing Nature immediately. No need to focus on why you are pleasurable or in pain, no need for the reasons or conditions that brought you there. For example, if you are in pain because of sickness, you do not need to focus on the sickness at all - rather, deal with the fact that pain has risen!

Now unlike Vipassana-teachings outside, which force you "look deeply into the nature of pain" and so on, this is absolutely not what I mean. What I mean is this - pleasure and pain is empty of self - they rise and they fall according to conditions. Therefore, you absolutely do not need to try to penetrate this pain or examine it or cognize it or analyze it. Instead, you simply know that it will pass away whenever it will, you know that it has risen from the Alaya-consciousness from a karma-seed, and you know that it will pass away! It is empty anyway, so why are you chasing after a reflection of a moon in the water?

Instead, one simply stays in bright awareness. Being absolutely present and free of discrimination. Free of past or future. At this point, Awareness will again be this vast, infinite ocean of water whereby these pain and pleasure phenomena will just dissolve away and will not surface again.

If however, one does not have awareness, then these phenomena will be grasped onto by the mind, and it will reinforce these seeds in the Alaya-consciousness as future phenomena.

# 3. Afflictions (mainly Anger, Greed/desire, Ignorance)

Anger and Greed are also both dealt in the same way. However, they are much subtler than ordinary Pleasure/Pain feelings. For example, greed can be as subtle as "what should I do later?" Anger can be subtle as "I don't want that".

How about Ignorance? Ignorance is the subtlest of them all, and it is the I-view. In fact, there are 41 grades or levels of Ignorance. When one ascends up the Bodhisattva bhumi grounds, one still has subtle levels of Ignorance! Therefore this is something that can only be talked about when one directly experiences it in meditation, and as one's wisdom grows.

---

Therefore, what exactly is it that I am saying here? It is exactly the Shurangama Samadhi, the Diamond-Prajna Samadhi. It is non-dwelling awareness that transcends even the need for the four jhanas.

The jhanas are a graduated stage of release of various things that are firstly seen as "desired" and later seen as "gross", which means that elements of jhanas should be let go of, until one reaches the fourth jhana and beyond.

In the first jhana, for example, there is no functioning of the nose or tongue consciousnesses. The joy that is experienced arises as an experience of bodily pleasure, from the five senses.

In the second jhana, this bodily joy is seen as gross and is discarded for a different type of joy. This joy arises from mental tranquility, a peaceful mental abiding. Here, the other three bodily consciousnesses of eye, ear and body stop as well. In other words, it has proceeded onto a completely mental stage from here onwards. As one can see, it is a series of letting go, a series of phenomena arising and passing-away after wisdom discerns it as "coarse".

This method starts with one understanding that all sentient beings are originally Buddhas - and that it is the afflicted mind that makes a Buddha a sentient being. In fact, these afflictions are completely empty, but it becomes a matter of "removing" or seeing-through-of these afflictions. This is also the same as the Dzogchen view of the Nyingmapa school, or the Chan school of no-method.

Therefore, do not underestimate all of these methods. Eventually, all the methods meet above the mountain and become a common-path.

There are many expedient means, and frankly speaking, there is an immense load of information out there that can confuse people instead of guide them onto the path. For example, the development of siddhis can lead people into wrong side-paths. They forsake the true precious liberation gold for scraps of cow-dung!

If it is too difficult to take all three categories, I implore one to start with just one section to strengthen one's awareness. No longer should one let one's awareness dwindle and chase sense-desires senselessly.

This awareness is not about trying to enter some empty state - No! This awareness is about destroying discriminative view. For example, seeing something as subject (self) and object (other) - seeing a "self" as possessing anger, seeing a self as practicing meditation, seeing self versus pleasure, or self versus pain, and so on. It is about seeing all of these dualistic perceptions as complete emptiness, just letting them dissolve like salt in the immense power of one's Awareness.

A happy new year ahead for you, and may you strive to be enlightened in this lifetime.

      > My understanding of this is that establishing mindfulness is like resting in the sense of peace.

      This is slightly wrong. Mindfulness (*sati*) is not dwelling in any form or phenomena. Instead, it is letting things be as they are, letting things rise and fall away according to their own conditions. It can certainly lead to a sense of peace sometimes, but mindfulness/awareness is being aware both in stillness and movement.

      For example, when there is anger arising out of the peace, if one is able to be aware of this anger, then this is mindfulness. Ignoring any conditions that led to the anger, you should simply be aware of the pure affliction of anger, and then allow it to dissipate by its ownself. All afflictions are empty - they come and go as well, just like thoughts, sensations, or feelings.

      On the other hand, if one is unaware of this anger, being unconscious, one will be dragged away by the anger without knowing that it has risen. Having been dragged away, it is often the case where one is continuously dragged by all sorts of afflictions subsequently, multiplying the manifestation of karma-seeds into the Alaya-consciousness to repeat themselves as future events.

      The key, again, is not to have any form of discriminatory function in awareness. There is no establishing of a 'self versus anger', there is no establishing of a 'self versus greed/desire', there is no establishing of a 'self versus ignorance'. Similarly, there is no establishing of a 'self experiencing warmth, coolness, pain, numbness, etc', neither is there an establishing of a 'self feeling pleasure or pain'. There is no 'thinker with thoughts'. There is simply purity in everything, with no discriminatory, categorizing, or processing - direct, pure, non-dualistic perception. What you take as an "I" are too, habits. Habit of being a body or formless body, habit of being a personality, an ego, or being.

      I have never said that cultivation means the balance between calm and contemplation. Instead, I have stated - that when *shamatha* or absorption or jhana is established, wisdom at the same time is established. When wisdom is established, jhana is too, established. This is the big difference - one regards shamatha and vipassana as two completely different practices, whereas the correct view is that they are co-existing and mutually dependent. From the moment one watches the breath, for example, one is *already* developing the wisdom of impermanence, one is already watching impermanence.

      As the Blessed One stated:

      ```

      "There is no jhana without wisdom

      There is no wisdom without jhana

      But for one with both jhana and wisdom

      They are in the presence of Nibbana"

      ```

      The main difference, is that jhana is the acquisition of several phenomena that happen in graduated stages. Wisdom on the other hand, is completely non-linear in development.

      Whenever the word contemplation is used, it is often used as 'recollection' or 'reminder'. It is a method of bringing one's awareness again to the forefront, through various means. For example, one contemplates the breath - this means that one reminds oneself to continuously attend to the breath. When one contemplates impermanence, one reminds oneself to continuously attend to impermanence, and so on. This is similar for the Ten Recollections.

      > The second part I'm stuck on is what's aware of this glob of water. Sometimes I feel like I'm the "I"-sensation appearing and dissolving in this glob of water. Sometimes I feel like I'm the glob of water itself, as if in a dream. Sometimes I feel like I'm an outside observer from this glob of water, watching things appearing and dissolving in it.       As long as you remain in duality, separating a 'this from that', then one will be constantly dwelling in such a confusion. It is not necessary to think of an 'I apart from X'. When one does this, there is an "I" (a subject) and there is too an "X" (an object). Doing this reinforces a discriminatory awareness which will lead to nowhere.

      Instead, one simply stays in non-dualistic awareness, and what is meant by non-dualistic is letting go of any tendency of wanting to separate this from that, or wanting to seclude one thing from another, of discriminating. As Buddha taught Bahiya:

      ```

      In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen. 

      In reference to the heard, only the heard. 

      In reference to the sensed, only the sensed. 

      In reference to the cognized, only the cognized. 

      That is how you should train yourself. 

      When for you there will be 

      only the seen in reference to the seen, 

      only the heard in reference to the heard, 

      only the sensed in reference to the sensed, 

      only the cognized in reference to the cognized, 

      then, Bāhiya, there is no you in connection with that. 

      When there is no you in connection with that, 

      there is no you there. 

      When there is no you there, 

      you are neither here nor yonder nor between the two. 

      This, just this, is the end of dukkha.

      ```

      What is meant by 'what is sensed'? It means the five senses of the body - How one experiences the body is simply through the five senses. If these five senses are purely perceived - as the Buddha says - "in reference to the sensed, only the sensed", this is pure perception of the five senses.

      What is meant by 'what is cognized'? It means that the sixth-consciousness does not dwell in any form of discriminatory function of feelings, thoughts or afflictions - as the Buddha says - "in reference to the cognized, only the cognized".

      From this point, there is simply no 'self' that is participating in a sensing or cognizing. There is no need to think of a 'self' that exists separately. Instead, the entire question of a 'self' dissolves spontaneously. There is no answer to an illogical question, therefore, one should develop non-dualistic, non-discriminatory awareness like this.

      When awareness is strengthened, the strength of afflictions will slowly diminish, and at the same time, one develops more wisdom. Awareness is strengthened throughout one's daily life, be it whether one is working, bathing, eating, drinking, urinating, defecating, talking, listening, and even sleeping. At no point should one be dragged away through the lapse of awareness.

      Training in this way, simply knowing at all moments, that what is sensed rises and passes away; Knowing what is cognized rises and passes away - One will develop a bright and pure awareness that leads towards the noble fruit.

              This is not the case. Awareness is always there. It is either discriminatory and obscured by objects, or it is pure. There is no degree, there is only a difference in function. Just like how a moon remains in the sky despite a cloudy weather, once the clouds pass, there is always the opportunity for the moon to shine through the gap in the clouds.

              Likewise, when thoughts pass, the awareness is always there and it does not appear and disappear. There is also no degree of awareness in actuality - this 'strength' of awareness is simply used to explain the degree of obscurity by thoughts. When there are many afflictions in the mind, it is like an extremely cloudy sky. When there are fewer afflictions in the mind, it is like a cloudless sky. At all times, the moon is always there.

              > I do believe the viewpoint that the practice develops both calm and insight in tandem. However, sometimes some readings I've done elsewhere make it sound like each one can be developed separately, so I'm trying to reconcile them together. For example, I recall reading in either Zhiyi's Lesser Cessation and Contemplation, or Tsong Khapa's The Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment, that excessively cultivating calming without contemplation leads to stupidity, and vice versa leads to mental derangement. In what cases would one be in such dangers?

              I do not suggest that you base your understanding off simply reading. Instead, one should investigate it by one's own experience. Genuine cultivators will all know this - that yes, there is certainly a sequence in shamatha-vipasanya, but this is a natural progression. For the sake of illuminating this aspect, I will try to explain this a bit more clearly now.

              As one knows, the Tientai sect speaks of the Six Methods - Counting, Following, Stopping, Watching, Returning and Purification. One should firstly understand that these six methods lead to a Hinayana fruit. Secondly, one should understand that the 16 Victorious Methods are included within these Six Methods, but the 16 Victorious Methods are much more detailed.

              Why is it taught in such a sequence? Why count, then follow, then stop, then watch, then return and then purify? It is simply because this is how the cultivation process works.

              Firstly, one **counts**. What is the purpose of counting? It is to decrease the afflictions. By counting the exhalation, one simultaneously trains the unification of the mind with the breath. What does it mean to unify with the breath? It means that the mind does not scatter along with all the distracting thoughts that arise in the mind. By constantly keeping with the exhalation, the afflictions dwindle in number, and the grip of the mind on the breath become stronger than on the distracting thoughts.

              Eventually, one does not lose track of counting and is able to easily follow up with the counts without being distracted at all. There is a self-that-counts, there is the number count, and there is also breathing. These three are not forgotten.

              After reaching a stable state where the mind is not easily distracted away from the breath, then the breath will slowly stabilize as well, reaching a state of faintness and subtlety. At such a point, counting will seem 'coarse' and it becomes effortful to keep up counting. Then one should let go of this counting and go into the second method.

              The second method is that of **Following** the breath. When the breath is warm, cool, long or short, you know that the breath is so. Also, you know where the breath reaches - does it reach the throat, the chest, the abdomen, or perhaps even the feet? It is not an intentional type of looking where you follow the breath down from the nose and see where it U-turns. Instead, it is a general type of knowing. For example, if there is an ant bite on your toe, you immediately know it is at the toe; If you have an ant bite on your neck, you know it is at the neck. There is no need to try to chase intentionally. The focus is on awareness and being aware.

              Eventually, one reaches a state where the breath becomes seemingly non-existent. It is as if there is a breath present, but yet there is no breath sensed. At this point, the body will straighten up, as if there was a force or energy that is pulling up the body. Even if one sits for seven days, there will not be the slightest fatigue. This is also known as the Samadhi of the Desire Realm.

              At this point of Samadhi, one has reached the Zhi-Xi, the Cessation, Shamatha. However within this **Stopping**, this Cessation, there are other types of Samadhis. For example, after the Desire Realm Samadhi, one must firstly prevent the loss of the samadhi state, and at the same time progress towards Not-Yet-Jhana Samadhi. One knows that one has reached Not-Yet-Jhana Samadhi when there is a spontaneous loss of bodily sense and external surroundings.

              The loss of samadhi can be caused by many things, such as the lack of precept-keeping, bragging about attainments to others, showing-off to others, external influences, hopefulness and longing for samadhi, doubtfulness about samadhi, fearfulness from samadhi, irrational happiness from a false-samadhi, greed and grasping for attainment, and even regretfulness and remorse from not attaining samadhi. These must be carefully examined by the meditator - and it is only when one is free of these that one will be able to enter samadhi.

              Even so, when one reaches this type of entry-samadhi, there is a problem of over-brilliance/exertion and under-brilliance/exertion:

              - Over-brilliance signs: Seeing of colours even though body is not felt, Seeing of suns/moons/stars or bright visual nimittas, Having no control over exiting the samadhi, Seeing of abnormal things like future events, etc. All of these can lead down side-paths and sometimes lead to mental illnesses.

              - Under-brilliance signs: One has no body-sense, but one sees thick black-lacquer. It is similar to sleeping, where one is completely unaware of things.

              Of course, after entering the right type of entry-samadhi, then it leads down a path towards First Jhana, Second, Third and so forth down the Eight Samadhis.

              But at the same time here, one has turned towards Watching/Vipasanya. What does one watch here? This is true *sati* in action here. One watches the four bases of mindfulness of body, feelings, mental-consciousness objects and mental-qualities/dhammas. Within body-mindfulness, there is also the watching of the breath. Thus, there are five to be watched and are as follows:

              1. Watching of breath - it is as if the breath is there but yet absent.

              2. Watching of body - layer-by-layer, just like in the Kayagatasati Sutta, from the skin inwards to the bones, layer-by-layer, you develop the realization that when there are no sensations from the body, there is no 'body' - the body has no actual feelings and only exists as a phenomenon in mind - it does not really exist. This is actually treating one of the Four Perverted Views with **foulness**.

              3. Watching of feelings - in particular, one watches pleasure. Pleasure arises throughout the entire four jhanas in response to various jhana factors, as explained in the Mahasaccaka Sutta. However, one realizes that these pleasure feelings are also impermanent - when they end, they produce suffering - and hence they are not true happiness. This is treating one of the Four Perverted Views with **suffering**.

              4. Watching of citta activities - Once a thought rises, it immediately disappears. This is treating one of the Four Perverted Views with **impermanence**.

              5. Watching of dhammas - when mind is no longer grasping at dhammas, no longer discriminating between good and evil, then the comparison will no longer be as strong. All the dhammas arise from conditions and hence are devoid of any self. This is treating one of the Four Perverted Views with **anatta/no-self**.

              You must realize that this watching is done at the same time as when one progresses through the various samadhis/jhanas. Therefore it is inseparable. Eventually, although the Four Perverted Views are treated with foulness, suffering, impermanence and no-self, there remains a problem - duality of foulness-vs-purity, suffering-vs-happiness, impermanence-vs-permanence, no-self-vs-self. As long as such a duality exists, then the true nature cannot awaken.

              Then we move on to the step of **Returning**. Now, there are two things that matter: Subject and Object. The subject is the capability of watching, of seeing, of knowing. It is what seems to know, whereas the object is whatever is produced as realms of phenomena that is perceived.

              Now there are times where in one of the samadhis, there is suddenly a point where both subject and object vanishes. Does this mean that one is liberated when subject and object are liberated? No. Instead, one must be able to prolong this period of being without object or subject, in pure non-duality. Then spontaneously, there is a chance of realizing the True Mind. This is also known as **Purification**.

              Hence, one attains the Hinayana fruit, the awakening. In the 16 Victorious Methods, there is a further step, but it is fruitless to explain it to anyone who does not have the capacity to understand it.
