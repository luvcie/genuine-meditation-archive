# How to understand "Form is Emptiness, Emptiness is Form"?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 8 points · 8 days ago

A person asked me about the Heart Sutra's famous line, what exactly does 'form is emptiness, emptiness is form' mean?

Firstly we must understand what we refer to as form. Form in this case refers to ‘rupa’, rupa meaning that of the form-realm. What has form? The body has form, other people's bodies have form, the world around us has form. If it is formless, we call that immaterial. What is emptiness in this case? Emptiness means the absence of form. It refers to the opposite of form in this case.

Now here, we must understand this principle. The Dharma is so confusing especially to a beginner in the Dharma. At one moment, the Buddha says there is form. At the next moment, the Buddha says there is no form but emptiness. And then again, the Buddha says it is both form and emptiness. And yet again, the Buddha declares it is not-form-and-not-emptiness. Repeatedly, the Buddha uses such a structure - such as consciousness not being outside, not inside, not in-between, etc.

But then again, one might ask - what does the Buddha mean by 'both emptiness and form'? How does that make sense, and how do people cling onto such a view?

Let us imagine milk to be form, and water to be emptiness. When milk and water are mixed, they mix quite well and form diluted milk. When one sees this diluted milk, they see the milk but yet also see the emptiness at the same time. At this point, both form and emptiness are one and the same.

Why is the Buddha being so confusing, with so many seemingly-contradictory teachings?

If it is confusing, it is because you cannot see how compassionate the Buddha is. Firstly, when the Buddha says there is form, it is to counter the minds of those who insist on nihilism. When the Buddha says there is only emptiness, it is to counter the minds of those who insist on eternalism. When the Buddha says it is both form and emptiness, it is to counter the minds of those who refuse to answer the question or choose a side without understanding. When the Buddha says it is neither-form-nor-emptiness, it is to counter the minds of those who cannot make a choice and decide to accept both views.

At all of these points, the Buddha is pointing towards the true reality, the true nature, your Buddha-nature.

It is like a balance-scale - if the Buddha sees that you are heavier, he makes it heavier on the other end. If you are lighter, he makes it lighter on the other end. If you are medium-weight, he makes it medium-weight. If there is nothing there, he makes sure there is nothing on the other side.

Did the Buddha tell you in the Diamond Sutra to not have a mind that dwells anywhere, even in Dharma? Did the Zen Patriarch Hui-Neng not write in his poem that there is no Bodhi-tree and that there is no mind for any affliction to rest on?

Now this is a realm that is untouched by most people. Unlike Hui-Neng who already cultivated in past lives, one's wisdom will need to be developed through practice. As such, this is a Mahayana scripture, dealing with the development of wisdom and right view. But having this understanding will make a clearer path for those on the Bodhisattva path.

Strive well and may you be enlightened in this lifetime.
