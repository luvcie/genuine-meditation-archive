# I Am Free!

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **orihh** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 5 points · 1 year ago

Hello everyone, I'd like to share what has happened to me just moments ago. Sitting in a chair, I was listening to a video of Moojiji called Laughing Buddha 3 when all of a sudden, I found a strong force pushing up from my body, tightening my face. I didn't know why, but my entire body began grieving very intensely. Though it would seem that I was crying because out of melancholy, I actually found myself to be in a state of utmost surprise and joy. This was just as if a doctor was sticking a needle in my arm, and although I expected it to be painful, I was pleasantly surprised to feel no pain whatsoever. I suddenly felt apologetic emotions, similar to returning to one's parents after being lost. It was a very contradictory yet familiar moment. When I stopped crying, I waited. Then I began laughing hysterically. And as I laughed, I cried again! Everything in my mind melted away, and I felt complete joy. There was no reason to laugh, no reason to cry, yet I found myself doing both very naturally. When I stopped, I found myself to be liberated, to be reborn, to be cleansed. I feel as though heavy, painful shackles have been broken off my limbs. I feel as pure and as innocent as a child. What more can I say? I don't feel like saying anything at all!

    - **orihh** · 2 points · 1 year ago

      I do not feel. My body and my mind might feel, but I no longer identify myself with them. In terms of day-to-day life, not as much as you might assume has changed. Shit still comes out my ass, breath still flows through my lungs, and noise still resonates in my ears. Consciously, my mind still produces thoughts and it still continues to change. My person still suffers. Only now I've come to witness that I am that which does not suffer, that which does not change. Changes to my perception, yes. I don't hold any concept, idea, belief, or form with reality. Someone once said that when you see yourself, you cannot see the world. What is meant by this is not that you go physically blind, but that the veil of your ego once placed on every aspect of your reality has shed from yourself, just as your mind as shed from your identity. For the first time, you see the world as it is: blank. "That is an apple. Nothing more, nothing less". Every person has their own reality, yet ultimately there is only one reality. The self knows this reality because it is it.

      I'd also like to note that self-recognition is a single, instantaneous moment. However, to reach the state of enlightenment is a much more gradual process. Today is the third day since my hour of awakening, yet I have only just "stabilized" my enlightened state. Once you've popped out, it's very easy to be lured back into your state of lower consciousness. You will beg yourself not to return there, and the desire to remain in awareness will be felt strongly. But that's just it. Once the desire to stay enlightened is developed, your ego Has already returned. This is natural. In order to stabilize your state, you must let go of the fear of losing enlightenment. Only then can you find yourself again. Currently, I am in an ongoing state of meditation because I'm still practicing discernment from the mind and myself.

      And one more clarification: enlightenment is truly a natural state. When I saw myself for the first time, I immediately recognized that state of consciousness as something my young-child self lived in. This state is quite slippery in the beginning because it's so subtle. The self is a very subtle sense of intuition. Like the wind, you cannot be sure it exists through sight, but you can be sure it exists through feeling and through the awareness that if affects other things. So far, the intuitive feelings arising from my freedom have been very much akin to a sort of jhanna. Though words have no purpose in this matter, the self is peaceful, still, silent, and at times, joyful. But there are truly no words to explain it - it cannot be described through thought either. The closest literal resemblance to the self would be emptiness.
