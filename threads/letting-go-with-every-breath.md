# Letting go with every breath.

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 1 point · 2 years ago

Recently I've been attempting meditation through the four frames of reference, as explained by Paramananda. The first step is to master the technique of discerning whether each breath is long or short. This, as I understand it, means to stay with the breath for its entire duration, from beginning to end--not to ask oneself whether each breath is long or short (which actually makes it impossible to answer the question, since you must miss part of the breath's length to ask it.) Neither are you supposed to obsess over the tactile details around your nose.

Yet today I tied myself up by trying not to control or check the breath in other ways, and wondering if I had my mind on results rather than the present moment. I was tying myself up about whether I was tying myself up.

Then I tried something a bit different. I remembered one of my very early meditation sessions, which resulted in jhana. During this meditation session, at a certain point I said to myself, "just breathe." I let go of trying to focus and just breathed, with no thought of whether I was doing it right. Somehow I was aware that the pursuit of doing it correctly would only drive me crazy.

Fortuitously, today I was reading the beginning of The Dude and the Zen Master, by Bernie Glassman (zen master) and Jeff Bridges, and they talk about just doing, without tying oneself up in rituals and expectations about the doing. It struck me that when I "just breathed," this is what I was doing--and in fact maybe "just breathing" is equivalent to focusing on the current moment, with no distractions or expectations. This also means that maybe the distinction between zen's "just sitting” (which implies breathing) and concentration meditation is just semantics.

So as an experiment, I decided to try to "just breathe" during my second session today. What resulted was that I heard a voice in my head telling me to "let go with every breath.” Every time I breathed in or out, it was a letting-go of effort, and soon I found that my breath got very faint.

Now, to my question: is this a good way to keep one's attention on each breath, from beginning to end, "discerning" its length? Because my physical body got very calm and my breaths short, is this actually something higher up on the four frames of reference? I only managed to meditate for 25 minutes the second time, so I'm not sure if this method would have resulted in jhana.

Thank you for your thoughts as always.
