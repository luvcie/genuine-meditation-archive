# The History of Buddhism

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 9 points · 1 year ago

The current state of Buddhism right now is a reflection of all the accumulated fragmentation of Buddhism since the passing of the Buddha into parinibbana. Therefore, it is an important topic for all genuine followers of the Buddha to know exactly how this religion and its scriptures have changed over the years, so that one can know the right path to follow. In this essay, I will talk about the development of the Theravadan and Mahayana paths, as well as the Vajrayana paths.

In the age of the Buddha, India was a fragmented country with varying states. However, religious faith has always been the top priority over everything else. The class of Brahmins had absolute power over the control of generations of society.

The highest degree of Brahmins would have no relation with the population and mainly studied the various forces of the universe - physical and supernatural.

The second degree had priests who dealt with divination, prophecies, spirit communication and supernatural ability, with the Atharva-Veda held as highest among sutras. This class had to study twenty years to get to the first level.

The third degree comprised of popular cults which directed ceremonies at pagodas, with knowledge of the first three books of the Vedas. This class had to study for twenty years before promoting to the second level.

Brahmins, too, had code of conducts, or precepts, such as: Patience, righteous action, regularity, sincerity, purity, senses control, thorough knowledge of Vedas, Thorough knowledge of Brahma, honesty, and good temperament.

Into this society, the prince Gautama Siddharta was born. His clan name was Sakya, and he was called Sakyamuni. In this age, even royalties paid respect to the Brahmans. The prince studied the Vedas and mastered many other subjects with an genius intelligence. Having the holy-man being revered, and one of the sights that the Prince saw when travelling outside the palace, apart from birth, ageing, sickness and death, Siddharta then left home for the ascetic life to find the answers to suffering.

The prince found no answer in all the religious theories he read in the traditional Vedas sutras, and abandoned all of that to search for the Truth to end suffering. He studied with the highest teachers of his time and mastered the samadhi of no thought and samadhi of neither-perception-nor-no-perception but yet did not find the answers to why there was suffering. He then practiced asceticism and starved himself to the point of death.

Just then, he found the "Middle Way", was challenged by Demon Kings, and finally was enlightened under the span of a few days under the infamous Bodhi tree. He was aged 35, and became known as the Enlightened One, the Buddha, the World-Honoured One.

Having achieved liberation, he gained the transcendental powers of remembering past lives, seeing the death and rebirth of beings, clairaudience, the mind-made-body, and the power of eradicating all impurities and all Dharmas.

The Buddha started his first sermon on the "Four Noble Truths" in the infamous Deer Park (Mrigadava), and did the first turning of the Dharma wheel. This event was so impactful that numerous heavenly beings and Brahma came down to listen to his teaching.

Now as the Buddha taught his disciples, at this point of time, there was no Papyrus or writing of any kind. Everything was given by oral instruction, which is why the discourse of the Buddha is quite often very repetitive - this is to aid his students in remembering the discourses. Ananda, his attendant, had a perfect memory and was invaluable in remembering every single discourse.

When the Buddha passed away, his enlightened disciples gathered under Kasyapa to form the first council, reciting from memory what each of them had remembered. Even here, there was no writing of any kind.

As a hundred years passed, more of his enlightened disciples had passed away and the Dharma became distorted by the unenlightened sangha who inherited the Dharma. Arguments rose and rifts appeared in the sangha, and all kinds of interpretations started to arise.

This split into two factions: The Sthavira (Teaching of the Elders), composed of the older, conservative monks, and the Mahasanghika (Members of the Great Order) with the younger monks who were more open-minded. This was the second Council and yet here, there was nothing recorded in writing.

There was huge enmity between the two and each side's masters fiercely attacked each side to promote their own philosophies, building huge collections of doctrines. After 400 years, King Asoka reined from 268 to 232 BC, and there were over twenty sects in total. At this time, the Third Council was established and the doctrines were finally written down.

North India wrote the doctrines in Sanskrit, while South India wrote in Pali. Here, Buddhism started to decline because of the rise of Hinduism and Islam. The sutras written in the North (Mahayana) spread to Tibet, china, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam. The sutras written in the south (Theravada) spread to the southern parts, going into Ceylon, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos and Burma.

As such, the Mahayana canon had these collections: Nikayas, the Saddharmapundarika Sutra, the Vimalakirti Nirdesa Sutra, Avatamsaka Sutra, Lankavatara Sutra, and the Prajnaparamita Sutras (Diamond, Amitabha, Nirvana, etc).

While the Theravadan canon had these: Digha-nikaya, Majjhima-nikaya, Samyutta-nikaya, Anguttara-nikaya, and Khuddaka-nikaya.

One can split all of the teachings into 5 periods:

1. Avatamsaka period. The Buddha remained under the Bodhi tree for 21 days after enlightenment to teach the Devas.

2. Agama period. The Buddha went to Deer Park, and upon realizing there was no one who could understand his teachings, he simplified them and preached the Four Noble Truths and Twelve Causes.

3. Vipulya period. The Buddha's doctrine started to conflict with existing Brahmanic groups, and the Buddha was forced to justify his doctrine with discussions.

4. Prajna period. The Buddha taught his superior disciples about the supreme principles of the universe.

5. Lotus-Nirvana period. The Buddha finally lectured the bodhisattva vehicle to his highest students. However, there were many arahants who left, thinking that the Buddha had lost his mind and that there was nothing else to attain after the arahant's nirvana.

Middle Way

The Buddha taught that one should avoid two extremes:

e Devotion to sensual pleasure. This was a response to the erroneous view of an Indian school, Uceda, which believed that the soul ended after death, and hence pleasures are real and there is no need to control cravings.

e Devotion to self-affliction (like extreme asceticism). This was a response to the erroneous view of an Indian sect, Sasvata, who practiced nudity, filthiness, self-mortification and asceticism.

Instead, the Tathagata found the Middle Way which leads to superior knowledge, self-awakening and Nirvana. This was when the Tathagata found the first jhana to not arise from the sensory pleasure, and to be the path to freedom.

The Buddha explained: "Birth is suffering, Ageing is suffering, Death is suffering. Sorrow, Lamentation, Pain, Grief, Despair are all suffering, not to get what one desires is suffering. The five aggregates are suffering.

“Now Bhikksus, the supreme truth of origination of suffering is this. It is that lustful craving that causes rebirth. Craving binds up with lust and passion, here and there to find fresh delights. This craving clings to sensual pleasures, clings to existence and for self-annihilation.

“Now Bhikksus, this is the elimination of suffering. It is the elimination, renunciation, relinquishment and letting go of such sensual craving.

“The Noble Eightfold Path is the path leading to the ending of suffering.”

The Middle Way might sound easy, but how does one balance not clinging to sensory-pleasures, and yet not to self-affliction?

In fact, Devadatta tried to disgrace the Buddha by splitting off the sangha through asking for the reinforcements of several ascetic precepts, such as:

e Monks should spend all their life in the forest.

* Monks should not accept meal invitations and live entirely on begging.

e Monks should only wear discarded rags from trash piles.

e Monks should live at the tree foot and not under roofs.

e Monks should be vegetarians.

As one can see, this doctrine is not easy to understand, even for the disciples of the Buddha.

The Theravadans looked at the Mahayana as "heretics” who have gone beyond Buddha's teachings, while the Mahayana think of the Theravadans as "small-minded"” and arrogant, unworthy to represent the essence of the teachings.

The Theravadans tend to think that life is filled with sorrow and that one must cut one’'s own rope of sensual cravings that binds them to rebirths. On the other hand, the Mahayana Buddhists built their dogma to prevent the pessimistic viewpoint and open the eyes to duty with society.

The Theravadans wish to separate themselves from the world to merge with Nibbana, while the contrary is seen by the Buddha himself, who opens himself up to society and teaches. Therefore, there is huge arrogance in the community which does not realize that even the great disciples of the Buddha had to cultivate many more lifetimes to be fully enlightened, and that included the coming of the next Buddha Maitreya. The Buddha himself gave prophecies for his disciples, predicting when they would become a Buddha in a different world-system. As we can see here, there is a huge clash in values taught and the facts shown by the Buddha.

Not to mention there have been all kinds of changes to the scriptures over time, which includes fabrication of suttas.

The Buddha had foreseen this, and this is why he has proclaimed that one should not believe simply just by reading a sutra, hearing a teacher, a master, or whoever it is that speaks, or to derive by pure logic, or by faith - but instead, to investigate personally what the Dharma is and how to end suffering. If it is seen to be good for one, and all, then this Dharma is the Dharma the buddha teaches.

In the end, one needs to realize that the Buddha is simply a title given to one who is awakened.

Gautama Siddharta became a fully enlightened Buddha - this means that he fully awakened to the Truth. There are countless Buddhas in the past, to which Gautama himself had been a student of. So realize that in every being is the potential of Buddha. Buddhi is truth, and to find truth, one has to investigate properly.

Strive on, and may you be enlightened.
