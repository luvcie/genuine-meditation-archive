# Introduction, Lust, and Doubts

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Neurogence** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 3 points · 6 months ago

Hi all,

I've been reading most of the posts here and I thought I'd finally introduce myself. I'd like to thank u/Sherlockian_Holmes again for the invite, and u/refugeinbuddha for all of his insights, and to everyone else here who has contributed as well.

I'm 23, male, and my background is a bit complicated. As a child, I was indoctrinated with traditional Christian teachings. In my teenage years, I became a "radical" Christian. I took the teachings of Christ literally. I would even avoid watching movies since I felt they were glorifying violence and lust, and therefore, sinful. I avoided lusting at the females my age at the time. My friends viewed me as an extremist.

But then, a few years later, after understanding evolutionary theory, I lost all my faith in the Bible and Christ, and I became a hedonist. In these years I indulged heavily in porn, masturbation, mindless entertainment, and other vices. Eventually, the atheistic perspective became grim and depressive, and I decided to restart my spiritual search. It's at that time that I discovered Eastern Spirituality and gnostic Christianity. Over the past years, I've read many books which motivate and inspire me, and I've dabbled in various meditative practices, but the lack of "results” have always caused me to doubt whether there is anything truthful in the “spiritual path.”

But, I am starting to think that my lack of results is due to sexual lust. I've not been mentally celibate at all in the past few years. In fact, one of the last books I read instructed me to transmute sexual energy by actively cultivate sexual thoughts, do pranamaya, but not to release the semen. In search of superconsciousness, I even tried dangerous practices like mantak chia's techniques, where I would try to ejaculate internally and raise the seminal energies up my spine and into my brain through deep breathing. So, I was stunned when u/refugeinbuddha mentioned that even the lustful thought alone zaps spiritual energy. And then I immediately remembered that I read those same words by Jesus, as a teen, through the Bible. I've been experimenting a lot with "semen retention” in the past 2 years. I've gone through long periods of not releasing semen physically, but it's doubtful that I've gone more than a few days without lustful thoughts. So I've decided to try actual mental celibacy from now, and so far it's going okay.

I am willing to practice meditation diligently, to think more loving thoughts, and to expel lust from my mind, but I am not sure how to deal with the doubts. I have a very scientific mindset, and I hold science in high regard since it saved me from Christianity. And as much as I love the teachings of the mystics, their writings have not been able to convince me that consciousness is beyond the physical. And even if one says that it's all mental and there is no actual physicality, it seems that the complexity of one's brain is intrinsically tied to the level of one's consciousness. The less complex the brain is, the lower level of consciousness there is. For that reason, I have serious doubts that the human brain/mind can know the answers to existence. What if scientists eventually manage to create artificial brains, that are thousands of times more complex than the human brain? Wouldn't that "being"” using such a brain be far more conscious than even the greatest yogis that have mastered all the jhanas? Or perhaps that is impossible, and scientists will never able to create "artificial” minds?

These questions may be ultimately irrelevant, and I will not allow them to stop my practice. But I can't deny that presently, I don't have much faith in my human brain. And by extension, I don't have much confidence in the great teachers either since their consciousness is tied to a human brain. I do know that if I experienced a siddhi, such as walking on water, I would let go of all my doubts, but focusing on the results could prevent me from ever getting there.
