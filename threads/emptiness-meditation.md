# Emptiness Meditation

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 10 points · 2 months ago

A friend here asked me: "How do I do emptiness meditation?"” It then occurred to me, that I have never given any advice on this. This is extremely important, and it was only when someone asked, that I realized that such an important message was missing.

This is the most profound, direct path - the single path that leads to illumination. Out of all the possible routes in the world, genuine Zen schools have made more realized masters than others.

What is emptiness meditation?

I quote Master Hongzhi Zhengjue, who explains it in the most wonderfully simplistic way possible:

> ' The correct way of practice is to simply sit in stillness, and silently investigate; deep down thereisa state one reaches where externally one is no longer swirled about by causes and conditions. The mind being empty, it is all- embracing; its luminosity being wondrous, it is precisely apt and impartial. Internally there are no thoughts of grasping after things; vast and removed, being just as it is—free from confusion. Alive and potent, all dependency and opposition are severed; one remains self-contained. This contentment has nothing to do with emotional feelings; one must not rely on anything at all. Being quite outstanding, [this state of suchness] is full of life and spirit, and does not fall into defiled appearance; one finds a resting-place. Pure and immaculate, it is bright; being bright, it is penetrating. It is able to respond smoothly in accordance with phenomena, where phenomena do not ob- struct each other. Floating effortlessly, clouds appear on mountain peaks; shining boldly, the reflections of the moon flow along mountain streams. Everywhere—there are radiant luminosity and spiritual transformations; clearly, appearances are unobstructed! Mutually responding, like a cover to a container or the tip of a sword meeting a shooting arrow’s point. With further training and nourishing, it ripens. Its essence becomes firm and stable; it penetrates every place freely. Cut off sharp corners; don‘t speak of theories [of right and wrong]. Like a white ox or a tamed ferret that naturally [and willingly] responds to any command — one can then be called a true person. Therefore it is said, "Having the Dao of no-mind, one can be like this; having not yet gained no-mind, it is extremely difficult!”

What is contemplation of emptiness? It is:

1. Letting go of the past

2. Not pushing towards the future

3. Not remaining fixed in the gap between past and future.

In other words, past, future and present minds are ungraspable. Non-dwell as such.

When one is intensely in the present, there is no past or future to attend to. At this point, the sensation of the body will gradually diminish and disappear - and one may not even feel a body. Here, even the 'present’ moment will disappear. As such, the mind should rest in this clear, open awareness, free from all thoughts. This is what is called the contemplation of emptiness.

At this point, there will still be a very subtle "I" sense there. When this falls away, this is exactly it - enlightenment.

So can one reach enlightenment through emptiness contemplation?

No.

There is actually no method that can lead a person to enlightenment. All practice methods are just a way of calming the mind down, freeing it of frustrations, afflictions and thoughts.

Simply stay in awareness. And for this reason, contemplation of emptiness is called Chan, and Chan is the "gateless gate" or the "method of no-method".

People think that there is a door to enlightenment to be found. They find all sorts of methods, all sorts of keys, but they cannot find the door. Here is the reason - There is no door. Enlightenment, awakening to the Tao, has no door!

What exactly determines if someone is enlightened or not?

One's karma, virtuosity, practice - these all determine whether or not one gains entry into enlightenment!

Now think about it - Ananda, the attendant of the Buddha, had been next to the Buddha for many years, till the Buddha passed into parinibbana. How did he not enter enlightenment?

Because Ananda had all the keys, but there was no door.

There is nothing outside you that will lead you to enlightenment. Drop all seeking, drop all grasping - sit in emptiness contemplation, the highest no-method of all methods, be patient - and spontaneously, one will awaken to Tao. One will suddenly realize that enlightenment was right there, its possibility was there all along!

One who does not hold the Dharma view, the Buddha's teaching of views, will end up in a different place from one who is holding the Buddhist view. Therefore, Right View is the foremost in the Noble Eightfold Path. In fact, it is 50-50 with your practice!

When the accurate view matches with one's emptiness experience, one enters into kensho, awakening to Tao, and seeing one's self-nature. This is the beginning stage of enlightenment.

All errant views are dropped instantly and one gains a thorough understanding of the right Buddhist view.

Therefore, a right view is established by reading the sutras and understanding their principles correctly. One must know the Noble Eightfold Path by heart.

There are 3 stages to meditation:

1. Mental and physical health - physical exercise, hatha yoga, qigong, energy-work, diet, and so on. This establishes factors of determination, peace, stability and good-will.

2. One expands beyond the Small-I (five skandhas) towards the Expansive-I. One gradually enlarges the 'sphere' of this Small-I, under the instructions of a master, eventually merging into the entire universe into one indivisible whole - also called Yoga. All that exists at this point is the universe - there is no 'you' in terms of body and mind. You are not a part, but the complete entirety of the universe.

3. Now in Chan, there is a third-stage. What one achieved before this was simply a Expansive-I. Now, one goes towards the No-I. This is the real Buddha Dharma, everything before this is something that many other traditions have! Here, there is no concept, no feeling, no attachment, no self, no 1. This is the wisdom of the Heart Sutra, the Diamond Sutra, and so on.

Enlightenment experiences are not just one - one must have it continuously, supporting them with continuous practice, until it is brought into full maturation.

Self is conditioned by Awareness; Awareness is conditioned by Body-sensation. Therefore, the body and the self both exist. When the body sensation is strong, the sense of self is equally strong. When the body dies, the sense of self is no longer there. However, the body's sensations are not the "I", the Self, which is produced by awareness. Equally, thoughts are object and awareness is subject. Understand that thoughts are not you.

No matter how high one fuses with - a godhead, a Brahma, a deva, and so on - one is still within the experience of a self. No matter how high the "yoking" is - of Yoga, union with God, and so on, there is still a trace of self.

Now Buddhism is this, but more. The true value and preciousness of the Buddha's dharma is that it goes beyond this. This is called Chan, Zen.

While it is considered good to have these experiences, and that they are called awakenings in other traditions, in Buddhism, this is not the true awakening, not the true enlightenment. Buddhism is the ultimate letting go, the ultimate selflessness, the complete liberation from samsara.

To have bodhi-citta is to have two aspects: Renunciation and compassion. One throws down all self-benefit and worldly clinging, as well as extending oneself to benefit others. Avoiding responsibility is escapism! One must do the Four Great Vows.

How important is this?

As I mentioned earlier, enlightenment has no door. One has to have the correct views, karma, virtuosity. In fact, the Avatamsaka Sutra says: "When bodhicitta is generated, one attains buddhahood ultimately.” There are too, five stages of bodhicitta - I leave that for you to investigate.

Wisdom is impossible without compassion! But yet compassion is impossible without renunciation. If one lacks both of these, then egocentricism will only increase!

Therefore, everytime one sits in emptiness meditation, one must firstly arouse the bodhicitta of renunciation and compassion. If not, why are you sitting for!

Master Hong-zhi then described the stage of Boundlessness. So firstly we had the body-sense disappear, next the environment disappears, and at this point, one is not clinging to self or no-self. This then is the genuine illumination stage.

With just internal infinity without external infinity, this is called samadhi, not the illumination stage. In samadhi, one is oblivious of the environment. In true illumination stage, the environment is vast and infinite, without no discrimination. If one can see this difference, one will be able to bring this state into the daily life, discouraging the idea of escapism.

This is a huge topic, I will continue at a later stage. Strive well my friends.
