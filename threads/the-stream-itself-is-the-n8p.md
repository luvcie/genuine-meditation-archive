# The stream itself is the N8P

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda*

The stream itself is the N8P

The Stream itself is the Noble Eightfold Path. So the stream itself is right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right sati, and right samadhi.

Therefore, is it possible that a stream-entrant does not have jhana? Of course not, because the Stream is not a seven-fold path, neither is it a one-fold path. If any of the eight folds of the path are missing, it is not the true Noble Eightfold Path.

But how does the true Noble Eightfold Path arise?

It starts first and foremost with right view. Without the right view, the Noble Eightfold Path will not be perfect. The enlightened person naturally executes the eight folds perfectly, simply because the opposite of these eight folds are the eight deviations that lead towards afflictions.

What is most important is wisdom, the understanding, the realization, the awakening. Until then, it is still good for one to abide by the Noble Eightfold Path, although this path will not be a perfect one. From the very start of turning the mind towards Dharma, one has already been getting rid of afflictions.

When the knowing is right, it is as if there was a strong spotlight that pierces the darkness, shedding a light on a path forward. Without the light of knowing, it will be walking in darkness. However, one who has already turned to the Dharma is already walking in semidarkness.

As Ajaan Chah very wonderfully put, "The true Noble Eightfold Path is within us: two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, a tongue, and a body."

It is from direct experience that you can truly see the Path.

It is not how deep one goes that affects one's wisdom. As I have always repeated, wisdom goes hand in hand with absorption. If right wisdom is developed, absorption will be effortlessly achieved. If right absorption is developed, right wisdom will be developed at the same time - they are one and the same.

So do not say:

I have some blocks in my meditation that may take a while to resolve.

Dharma forces you to have four qualities of wisdom:

Great - careful investigation

Clear - not concluding without meticulous investigation

Swift - thinking on one's feet as it goes

Penetrating - pursuing the full line of inquiry without stopping

What is blocking you from this meditation? Perhaps one would see that one is shrouded in a dark sphere of experience, sluggish, filled with sleepiness and torpor.

Seeing such a block with clarity, one can then move ahead.

It is this kind of wisdom that sheds the light on the path. When one continues to pursue this down to its root - what exactly has caused this hindrance to surface? If all is cause and condition, what is the cause for this hindrance? Where is it rooted in? Pursuing it like a hungry wolf chasing its prey, one would not let go until this answer is found.

This is the spirit of the Dharma.
