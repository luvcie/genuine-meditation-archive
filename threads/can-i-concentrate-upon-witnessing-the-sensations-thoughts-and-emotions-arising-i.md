  It is by personal investigation.

  There are two ways as Patanjali has stated: With-seed-thought, or without seed.

  When going along the day, one can stay in mindfulness of body, sensations, thoughts and mind quality. This is also known as *sati*, which does not involve a meditation object. There are various other similar meditations, such as the meditation on the seed sound "Om" which leads to the auditory jhana-nimitta.

  If one wishes to calm the mind, they should thus give the mind something to focus on and have a seed-thought, or meditation object. This object is a training-wheel.

        - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 2016-08-17

          The Sanskrit texts have never used the word 'concentration', instead it has used words like "vitakka" or "vicara" or one-pointedness.

          Let us imagine that your awareness is like a light. When one is very distracted, the light splits and shines on various other things, never staying put in one phenomenon. Being too distracted and scattered causes the light to weaken, and this causes the mind to lose energy and become sluggish. This also happens if one tries to chase too many things at the same time, causing agitation in the mind.

          If one places attention on just the breath (or meditation object), and observes just the breath, ignoring all other thoughts that arise, then it allows all the light to be placed upon the breath. This causes the light to become "focused" and strong, as if it was a spotlight.

          After some time, the attention will simply be absorbed into breath. In this state, we say that "thought" and "breath" have become linked. In other words, whenever the breath moves, then you see thought movement. When the breath does not move, thought does not move.

          Eventually, the physiology of the body calms down as a consequence of the mind calming down. Soon, when the body experiences full-body breath, the chi fills the body and one enters the state of Xi, where the breath stops completely. Since thought is linked to the breath, coarse-thoughts also stop at that point.

          In this emptiness comes the freeing of energy movement - No longer is it being directed by the mind. Therefore, in the stillness comes the rise of Yang energy, starting from the back channels and the front channels, opening up the "microcosmic orbit".

          As you can see here, the breath is simply just a training wheel that helps one reach a certain state. From then on, it becomes various degrees of letting go.

                - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 1 year ago

                  The "dark void" is a thought too, is it not? True emptiness is when even the thought of a "dark void" is not there. Exasperation is a perception of pain, is it not? Thinking of advancing is too, a thought. As long as the mind is not focused on only the breath, one cannot reach one-pointedness of thought.

                  There is no 'train of thought'. Just like frames make up a movie, each thought flashes by so quickly that one would think that it is like a "train of thought". However, each thought independently rises and falls away.

                  Look at your body now. You have so many organs. When you were in your mother's womb, you were just a tiny cell. These organs grew at different times. Your heart grew at a different time from your lungs. Now they seem to interact with each other, but they are simply carrying out their own functions. You look at your body as "one whole body", don't you? Now consider that the number of bacteria throughout your entire body far exceeds the number of cells your body has. Whose body is it now?

                  There is a tendency for one to group things together - like a train of thought - or one whole flesh body - or a belief - or discriminations. It is in fact, independent, yet interdependent causes and conditions rising and falling away at different times.

                  What universal energy if you cannot see how the entire universe is moving in a interdependent movement? Coming in from the emptiness, and falling away yet again into the emptiness - This is the nature of the universe.

                  Just like the universe, if you reside in meditation, simply watching things arise and fall away - becoming more aware of the things that you did not consider to be impermanent - letting go more and more - you return to your original nature as the universe, do you not?
