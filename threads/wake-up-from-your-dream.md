# Wake up from your dream

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 6 points · 2016-04-25

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/4gbcft/wake_up_from_your_dream/>

When you are in a dream, you aren't aware that you are in a dream. When you aren't aware, you believe wholeheartedly that the dream is reality. To you, at that moment, the dream is your reality.

In the same way, when you are in this waking life, you aren't aware that you are in an illusion. When you aren't aware, you believe wholeheartedly that this illusion is reality. To you, at this moment, this dream is your reality.

And so beings continue dreaming, for lifetimes upon lifetimes, never ending. They continue to dream, unconscious, playing their roles in this dreamscape.

In your sleeping dream, everything is more fluid. You can poke your finger through the palm of your hand and you cannot pinch yourself. You can breathe even with your fingers pressed against your nostrils. The dream-like quality comes from how fluid everything is, and how warp-able it is from influence of the mind.

When you wake up, everything is less fluid. You cannot poke your finger through your palm, you can pinch yourself, and you cannot breathe when you pinch your nostrils. That is how you verify that you are in a waking reality.

But other than that, how can you verify that case?

This reality of yours breaks too.

When you go to sleep, this reality of yours disappears, replaced by your dreams. How can you safely say that this, right now, is your waking reality?

Everything happens in front of you. All phenomena is before this witnessing.

When you recall a previous lifetime, you think to yourself, "Ah, how strange. It feels so ephemeral, as if it was a dream. But it was so real." Let me ask you then - What is the difference between that past life and the current life you are living?

Let us imagine you attain samadhi and obtain the mind-generated body. You fly to the furthest realms, even to the Brahma heavens and lokas. You meet the deities of faraway lands. What though, is the difference between witnessing your waking life and witnessing all of these?

Who are you?

Why do you have so many dreams?

Isn't it time to awaken?

      > When you realize you are dreaming, you may wake up in our normal reality... but when a person discovers that normal reality is also a dream, to where will this person wake up?

      Reality is but a very vivid dream. When one recalls previous lifetimes, one will also realize that those lifetimes, too, were like dreams.

      The Diamond Sutra the Buddha expounded said thus:

      > "So you should view this fleeting world --

      > A star at dawn, a bubble in a stream,

      > A flash of lightening in a summer cloud,

      > A flickering lamp, a phantom, and a dream."

      ---

      > If everything we perceive is a dream, how can there be anything other than the dream? If someone wakes up from the world of perception, where could this person wake up? Where would this person live? Endless nothingness, wich is still a perception? Unconsciousness? Is "dreaming" the right word to compare? Is this "dream" the same thing Plato talks about on the allegory of the cave?

      That is something you can only understand by experience, thus strive on with diligence and liberate yourself. Saying "nothingness" or "emptiness" is simply another mental realm, saying "unconsciousness" is yet another mental realm. As long as you think of a mental concept and dwell on it, you lose emptiness.

      True emptiness is non-dwelling. It is letting go. This is hard to cultivate, because you find that even the subtle idea of residing in this "empty state" itself is a type of mental realm on its own. Therefore, it can only come from cultivation and practice.

      Plato talks about the same thing - One who looks upon the shadows on walls might suspect something more than what they see, however, it is unimaginable to them as long as it is out of their experience. Similarly, the Tao is indescribable and the only way to truly show someone the Tao is have the person cultivate the Tao on one's own.

      In the Tao Te Ching, the first stanza is as follows:

      > The Tao that can be spoken is not the eternal Tao

      > The name that can be named is not the eternal name

      > The nameless is the origin of Heaven and Earth

      > The named is the mother of myriad things

      The Buddha himself says in his very first long discourse about the 62 wrong views of reality - This is because people tend to formulate mental conceptions and then dwell on them - however, their knowledge is not perfect and impeccable. They do not yet know the true Deathless. Therefore one should not speculate or try to form logical formulations on something they have not experienced yet.

      However, the Buddha described nibbana as the end of suffering. That is what you need to contemplate on and know. Know that the purpose of cultivation is to attain enlightenment and hence end suffering.
