On this first day, I've been accepted into this subreddit, and I will begin practicing meditation using the beginner's guide thread.

11/8/2015 Anapanasati, I practiced "Inhaling, exhaling, discerning that the breath is long/short".

First session was 10 minutes long, as I quickly realized that I confused discernment for ignoring! This was done in half lotus posture with my eyes half closed...

The second session was 15 minutes long, again in half lotus but I closed my eyes completely this time. I focused on my breath going from short to long, and as I did so, I eventually felt a very big space appearing in my mind. The only other time I'd felt this was when I was hallucinating during a very high fever, but this time I was completely aware of this emptiness. Soon I was seeing my breath as a visible form in this space, and the more I breathed, the longer the inhales and exhales got, and the smaller the form of my breath became in this space. It was an experience that sort of made it easier to concentrate on my breath, as I was able to see something happening rather than just listening or thinking about my inhales and exhales. I wouldn't consider this anything more though. I didn't feel very happy or in bliss - I don't think I was in any Jhana.

I'd like to note that before both these sessions, I did the complete body relaxation thing mentioned in the Complete Beginner's Guide, and although it was useful, I didn't feel very joyous or anything at the end. I'm probably doing it wrong. Anyway, I'm wondering if this step needs to be taken, because I'd like to make my practice more simple, and the thought of having to do something before I can actually get to meditating is a bit of a nuisance.

  Everything is a learning experience. One is only guided onto the right path by experiencing mistakes. That is the very nature of investigation and the Buddha himself explored many, many obstacles on his path. He then questioned, why could it be that the obstacles are as so? Then, he repeats the practice, each time troubleshooting what he had done wrong. Therefore, do not be worried about doing it unsatisfactorily the first time.

  The complete body relaxation is to teach you how to relax the body completely - This is in alignment with what is said in the Breath Meditation thread as:

  > 2) Inhaling, exhaling, sensitive to the physical body.

  > 3) Inhaling, exhaling, calming the physical body.

  Once you are able to do it more quickly, then you can let the body sleep while in a meditative pose. This can be reached to a point where there is no agitation, stress or tension found in the body.

  Once you can feel the peace in relaxation, and grow more sensitive to it, then you will feel bliss and joy arise in waves, each greater than before. Then this will be going away from the physical body reference into the reference of sensations/feelings.

  Another thing to note is that you want to minimize "doing" as much as possible. You are observing, so do not try to control the breath. Simply sit in a good posture, as still as possible. When you quieten the mind, the breath immediately stands out, so do the bodily agitations.
