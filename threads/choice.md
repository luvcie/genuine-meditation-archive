# Choice

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 5 points · 2015-10-27

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3qfqpg/choice/>

There is nothing that restricts a person from exercising choice. Every single person is free to do whatever he or she wants.

The only problem here, is whether this individual is conscious of the consequences the choice leads to. If you are conscious that a choice leads to a dissatisfying outcome, but still wish to do it, then nothing stops you. The universe is free like that.

There is nothing right or wrong in that manner. However, this does not mean that there is no "right path".

The Buddha once told a man that saying that the world is amoral, free of morals, without morals, is wrong view. Why is this so? Let us explore with logic.

The knife is a tool we use. We can use the knife to chop fruits, but yet we can use the same knife to harm ourselves.

Every single action leads to a consequence. However, is this consequence satisfying or dissatisfying? *That* is the question. Nothing else matters.

Therefore, if one's purpose is to eliminate dissatisfaction, misery and suffering, then one's efforts in this world is solely to permanently rid of all causes that lead to this dissatisfaction.

Anything that serves towards this purpose, is enlightening, is "right". Anything that deviates from this purpose, is sin, is "wrong". There is no right or wrong - it is just a matter of satisfaction or dissatisfaction - liberation from suffering or continuing to suffer.

So what leads towards satisfaction, liberation from suffering? That is by eight ways:

1. **Right View or Understanding** - It is understanding that doing things that lead to satisfaction lead towards liberation and doing things that lead to dissatisfaction lead towards suffering. The Eightfold Path and the Four Noble Truths are the gateways to this, leading towards jhana/samadhi, and eventually leading to release with the mastery of enlightenment factors.

2. **Right Thought** - It is renouncing everything that is dissatisfactory. Firstly, discipline to counter craving; Secondly, lovingness, compassion & harmlessness to counter all unwholesome and dissatisfactory mental states.

3. **Right Speech** - from falsity, slander, harshness and idleness - Instead, being truthful, generous, kind and teaching the truthful dharma.

4. **Right Action** - Abstaining from killing, stealing and sexual misconduct - Instead, being kind, generous and inspiring all to abandon suffering.

5. **Right Livelihood** - Living one's life and career, centered around the fulfillment of this path - So that one is able to end suffering in all aspects.

6. **Right Effort** - Exercising effort continuously, to firstly prevent arising of unwholesome mental states, and secondly to abandon any existing unwholesome mental states. Instead, one arouses and maintains wholesome mental states.

7. **Right Mindfulness (sati)** - Being self-aware and contemplating of the (1) body, (2) feelings - pleasant/neutral/unpleasant, (3) mental states, (4) Dhammas - four noble truths/factors of enlightenment

8. **Right Samadhi (absorption)** - Moving from first jhana to the fourth jhana, such that one does right meditation.

These 8 parameters, when incorporated, when practiced, when strived for continuously, leads to consequences that do not cause suffering, and instead, leads to an ending of suffering.

Therefore, do not see them as ethics.

Yes, there is black and white. There is "right" and "wrong". However, no one is correct or incorrect. It depends on what one consciously chooses.

All naturally choose to end suffering. If one is determined to end suffering, to be enlightened, then it is obvious that one chooses the path that leads to it, and abandons the path that leads away from it.

Having this understanding, you also understand the mundane meaning of the four noble truths:

1. **There is suffering**. There IS dissatisfaction/suffering, according to what one chooses.

2. **There is a cause for suffering**. This dissatisfaction comes from a root cause, which is mainly impermanence and craving.

3. **There is an ending to suffering**. There IS the end of dissatisfaction, leading to satisfaction.

4. **There is a path to end suffering**. By shifting away from a dissatisfactory path, and following the noble eightfold path, one ends this dissatisfaction.

Therefore, follow not a religion that tells you this is bad or that is bad. Instead, use your own freedom of choice. You are a profound being, a piece of this brilliant universe.

What do you wish to do? What is your purpose for reaching enlightenment?

Enlightenment allows you to access the original nature - the cosmic force. It allows you to reach satisfaction.

Some people try to strive for enlightenment, but they have an intent that is lacking. They do not understand why they are doing it - and it is only fueled by curiosity.

Intent is so important. In the Mahayana sutras, it is repeatedly stated that a Bodhisattva must have a vow.

Whether your vow for your purpose is to help all beings, liberate them, or lead to a healing of all species of the universe, of all planets, of all realms - That is up to you.

But this purpose should be the reason why you are seeking enlightenment. Otherwise, what is your purpose of even seeking it? You must also understand that even enlightened beings have the ability to excel in worldly affairs.

The world is about sensibility and conscious choice. It is about expanding your consciousness or contracting it. It is not about removing your intellect, but about expanding it.
