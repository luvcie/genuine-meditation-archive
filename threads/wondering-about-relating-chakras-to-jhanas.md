  It is absolutely important to understand that the chakras are not a cause and to cling on to manipulation of chakras here and there is being bound to form. Chakras are symptoms, not causes. Being bound to form, one will not attain enlightenment. That is the first thing to note.

  However, it is useful to understand it from an experiential point of view, once you have felt it.

  The bottom 3 chakras (root, sacral, solar plexus) are related to the "desire realm"”, while the upper 4 chakras (chest, throat, third-eye, crown) are related to the form/rupa realm, or rupa-jhanas. Therefore, one who cultivates the four rupa jhanas, cultivates being like a deva in the rupa realm. Having an imbalance can cause one to have problems in their experience.

  The first jhana is largely characterized by joy and bliss. This is often associated with the heart, and the ancient chinese used the words "open heart” to describe happiness. This literally means the heart chakra opening. A profound sense of love, or like a huge hole in the chest, is characteristic of this. Therefore, the heart chakra is associated with the first jhana. The different degrees of bliss also depend on the opening of channels and how well the energy can flow. Therefore, the fewer blockages, the higher the bliss. Stagnation of energy always causes problems, while smooth movement of energy allows possibilities.

  The second jhana is largely characterized by tranquility. What does tranquility mean? Like a calm lake, serene and without ripples. In other words, the internal dialogue of the mind has stilled. It is what Zen refers to as "no mind", or “the clear sky of a thousand miles". This means that the throat chakra, associated with the action of speech and mental chatter, has opened. Normally, this is preceded by some kind of choking or coughing sensation, where one may cough out a dark phlegm. One will exercise righteous speech after this.

  The third jhana is largely characterized by equanimity. Equanimity is generally the non-dualism experience, where there is either observer nor observed. This is associated with the third-eye chakra, which has various degrees of opening. Most people observe some kind of intuition, which helps one lose the fear of any kind of phenomena. A pressure in the forehead, or perhaps a magnetic blissful sensation in the middle of the forehead denotes a third-eye opening. If one is able to see beings, or auras, etc, then this is slightly associated with the third-eye chakra opening, which is a gradual process.

  The fourth jhana is largely characterized by the loss of pain and pleasure. In other words, any kind of sensation of the physical body has completely disappeared. One feels boundless. It can be possible to see the "inner light" at this stage. Many jhana-nimittas culminate at this point. But there is no more physical sensation whatsoever. Even if one slams the door, or a lightning strikes, just like the Buddha, one would not hear a thing. This is also associated with both the third-eye and the crown chakra.

  Remember, it is not the opening that allows the jhana, but the mental cultivation of the person. The physical-astral-energetic aspects always follow the mental. In other words, if one is bound by form, and unable to leave one's attachment to chakras, channels, nadis, etc, then this is someone who has not purified the form skandha/aggregate. If you do the right cultivation, the chakras naturally open as a consequence.

  Therefore, the Buddha did not use the chakra system to teach. Neither did Patanjali. Neither did Jesus. Neither did several other masters. If you notice, they always talk about the rigor of discipline, and the avoiding of sin, which is the avoidance of unwholesome states. This is the very basis that is required, before higher fruit can be attained.

  Open yourself up to compassion, love and gratitude. Work away the unwholesome mental states. Then this allows energy blockages to disperse gradually. The more this happens, the more cleansing takes place. How is this done? By watching the body.

  For the attainments of the immaterial samadhis and beyond, they do not necessarily involve chakras, but dimensions that are higher.
