# Just had some understanding on lust problem

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Freeman_Reddit** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 3 points · 1 year ago

I'm not confident to say if it's correct understanding or even insight into the problem. Along my way of cultivation, I have encountered a lot of occasion when I feel uncontrollable energy in my body, which I don't think is jhana, and revolutionary thoughts that look like enlightenment. I'm not sure if I was actually improving or I was developing a psychosis :() But I feel necessary to write down what just came up to my mind.

I have been fighting against lust for around a decade. That's longer than World War Two. I've tried countless methods. Being occupied, taking cold showers, going into meditation, being a vegetarian... None worked. I look at it as it's the biggest enemy in my entire life. It's the hardest thing I have ever tried to do. It's a devil from hell. I have gone through countless frustration and failure. Yet I'm still fighting this war.

Right today I just broke my streak of nofap. I'd been meditating diligently and didn't relapse for over 10 days. I felt my body is integrated. But I ruined it all today. Every time I relapse I feel the following week is going to be awful, since I have to rebuild and repair my body slowly. And during that period I will not do well, and other people will avoid me because of the terrible vibe relapsing has created around me.

As I laid down on the ground and face the sky and sun, I felt hopeless about my situation. But out of nowhere, some thoughts occurred to me. I started to wonder how exactly can relapse hurt me? What does it hurt? Well it hurts my body and health. But is my body really "me"? It is obviously not "me". All the books and sutras I read have clearly stated that our bodies are not our Self. None of the cultivation methods is about our body. It is all about our mind. Then how can relapse hurt me? Well people may not like me. But no way that can hurt "me" either. In fact, nothing can hurt my true Self.

As I realized that, I immediately threw away the burden. I instantly felt revived. I no longer felt sad or desperate because of the relapse. Instead, I recall how Buddha said "One becomes Buddha whenever he realizes; One becomes mundane person whenever he is lost."”

So shouldn’t I keep staying away from relapse? At this point the problem is a bit hard to explain. Although relapse can never hurt "me", but it is a sign that I am lost, that my mind is blinded by sensual desires. However, upon realizing that it can never hurt "me"”, I don't see it as a threat any more. The war is over. Whenever I want to win, there are chances I lose. But how can I lose if I'm not even in the game?

Please tell me if my understanding is correct or if anything should be improved :) <
