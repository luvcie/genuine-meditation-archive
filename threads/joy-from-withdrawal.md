# Joy from withdrawal

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 1 point · 1 year ago

I think I've discovered what is the beginning of the joy of letting go. I haven't been able to cultivate it into full-blown joy or bliss yet, but currently it's a pleasant gladness to be with the breath and nothing else.

I can see that this would turn into a kind of "light" inside the meditator's head. It almost feels like a light currently. I discovered this because Ajahn Brahm instructs meditators to begin with letting go of the past, future, and worldly concerns. I began each meditation with a few moments of this until I realized that I didn't want to leave that state and transition into methods that required me to "think" too much.

So here is my question: now I'm trying to find a way to note the lengths of the breaths, as instructed, but without leaving the light. It's almost like looking at the breaths through the filter of the light, or from entirely within it. I try to be glad and joyful to be with every moment--the whole length--of the breath, hoping that the more I notice, the more I'll be glad of, and the more the light will grow.

Does this square with how others have practiced? I have the same problem over and over again: I've achieved jhana in the past, and this light feels similar to the beginning of jhana, if cultivated, but what I'm doing doesn't exactly fit the instructions I've been given. Trying to somehow marry these two in my mind.

Thank you as always.

          > I was a little stuck trying to note the gaps the right way, because you're supposed to intuitively "know" them, but in a way it seems that this is the same as ignoring them.

          It's probably difficult to note the gaps right away when you don't have sufficient focus. If you asked me, i would probably transition slowly like these steps.

          1. Note the in and out breath sensation. When you can discern them clearly start step 2. 2. Note the starting and end of in-breath and out-breath. When you can discern them clearly, start step 3 3. Note the starting and the end of in-breath and out-breath and the pause between each cycle. Start step 4 when you can discern them clearly. 4. Note the breath related sensation that accompanied the in breath and out breath. If you could notice more than a 3 or more, try to connect all of them (simply be aware of them all at the same time).

          When you have reached 4th steps, you are quite closed to the first Jhana from personal experience.

          > If you note the end of one breath and the beginning of another, aren't you delineating a gap? Is that enough Cknowing® that gap?

          It's a bit hard to note them since the pause is short in the beginning but as you continue, it will then soon becoming a little bit more noticeable. Try the steps i listed above to see it it helps and personally, i feel it is sufficient to just know that there is a gap there or there is a pause before a cycle start or after the cycle end.

          > T've always wondered if I was practicing correctly but thinking I was doing so incorrectly just because what I was doing didn’t seem to match instructions I was give

          If your mind is not dull (Hazy, heavy or sleepy) and you could follow the breath's cycle closely, you are probably on the right direction.

                  This "breath-related sensations” has some close resemblance to the Grade 1 Piti or you could say it's one of them.

                  the "breath related sensation” becomes more noticeable when the mind is sufficiently calm and body is relax.                   When you have a large number of this sensations present at the same time and you have successfully connecting them (Be aware of them all at the same time), this sensation will pervade the whole body. (Grade 5 Piti)
