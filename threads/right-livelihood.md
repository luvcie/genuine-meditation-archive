# Right livelihood

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 4 points · 2 years ago

I'm reluctant to post this here because it's not directly related to meditation, but a byproduct of meditation: de-identifying with passions and interests that formerly were a part of the ego.

This has happened to me in graduate school. Now when I finish with my degree in a few months, I won't be going on the job market to pursue writing, which was formerly the most important thing in the world to me. I think I've realized how overly identifying as "writer" has caused me immense suffering, and I don't want to continue that cycle. I will write sometimes, when I feel like it, as the inclination arises, but I won't force anything.

However, this leaves a void of ideas for what I should do for livelihood. I currently teach, but I don't very much enjoy it--it's just something I'm qualified to do. Has anyone here experienced this confusion when certain elements of the ego (particularly interest in a vocation) are stripped away? To make it worse, it seems that every job in the market requires you to have a great passion for that kind of work, and that is precisely what I don't feel for any particular line of work at the moment. How to deal with this?

Thank you for your input, and apologies if this is a bit tangential.

  > 'Has anyone here experienced this confusion when certain elements of the ego (particularly interest in a vocation) are stripped away?

  You also have to realize that in the past, especially that of the age of Gautama, people revered and respected holy men and hence one could survive through the begging of alms. One could literally live as a holy man surviving on alms. These days, it is not as easy as society has progressed a lot. This confusion is experienced by nearly every person who has had spiritual progress. Many of these people hence retreat as a contemplative, hermit or even monastic life.

  However, the truth is that while you need to be able to give up everything, you also need the ability to survive in the world and feed the stomach. One of my previous masters remarked, "Feeding the stomach is the hardest thing to do in the world." Fortune itself is a result of past karmic merit. If you wish to remain a layperson, it is important for you to open up the insight stream through cultivation, then let this insight stream lead you. The most important thing is to adhere to the Eightfold Path and stay away from the Five Heinous Crimes. There should also be enough free time to pursue a contemplative life beside a right livelihood.

  To become enlightened, the intention is very important (I will do a post on it soon). If you truly wish for liberation in this lifetime, life should catered to the practice of the Dharma and not the Dharma catered to a life. In fact, as stated, “The intensity of practice indicates how close one is to enlightenment.”

  What I'm saying is to follow your insights and wisdom. Be honest with yourself in what you truly want to do.
