# The Seven Factors of Enlightenment

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 3 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/34ewvb/the_seven_factors_of_enlightenment/>

One of the biggest problems in reading the English translations of the original Sanskrit words is the experience of the translator. If the translator is not well-versed or experienced in the Dharma or cultivation practice, one can tend to give subtly incorrect words. It is my hope that this post can help to clarify the actual meaning of some of these words, as written in the Pali Canon.

Bojjhanga comes from the root words "Bodhi" and "anga", collectively known as "factors of enlightenment". Now let us loosen any prior knowledge attached to these. There are seven factors of enlightenment, and they are described, accurately, as such:

1. sati - This is the same word used as the 7th part of the Noble Eightfold Path. Although it is translated as "Mindfulness", it is often more of "self-awareness" as well as actual meditative practice. To understand more about sati, read the Satipatthana Sutta[1] .

2. dhamma vicaya - This is often translated as "Investigation". However, it means more than that. It is critical thinking, as well as unravelling our belief systems and philosophies. This is a siddhi, but can manifest in mild forms through insight streams.

3. viriya- This is translated as "Energy" but this does not do it justice. This is the raising of the kundalini - virtuous power. This is the energy that normally keeps one awake after meditation.

4. pīti - As previously posted, this is a factor in the first jhana. sukha, joy, is part of the jhanic experience, and pīti, bliss, takes over the experience soon after.

5. passaddhi - This is translated as "tranquility". This is a factor in the second jhana. It means directly, the "stillness" or "stilling" of the mind. All mental objects come to rest as a result of non-dwelling, allow the mind to be still.

6. samādhi - This is an actual altered state of consciousness. Samadhi is not simple concentration, although it is usually erroneously translated as "Concentration". It is better translated as "Ecstasy/Euphoria/Absorption".

7. upekkha - This is a trait that comes from the third jhana. It is yet another deeper and calmer state of mind.

Now understand that these factors can be utilized in various ways. sati is the weapon that treats everything. dhamma vicaya, viriya and piti are factors that treat a sluggish mind. passadhi, samadhi andepekkha are factors that treat an over-active mind.

What you must realize is that sati is used to determine the condition of the mind. It is the best weapon to prevent the mind from even entering any of these states. Prevention is always better than cure.

The others must be cultivated in jhana. There is no other way than to get to it either via living the Noble Eightfold Path.

    - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 2 points · 2 years ago

      Firstly, there is no difference between concentration or vipassana practice. The Buddha has never said that 'vipassana' is a practice, if one were to read all the Pali Canon discourses. He has only ever said that 'vipassana' is an attainment - or a fruit that one gets when correct meditative practice is established. It has always been that one engages in concentration practice through to the fourth jhana and insight is then established. Similarly, 'jhana’ is also part of the fruit (some of the traits are marked as factors of enlightenment).

      The Buddha has only ever established one meditation foundation - that is, sati, which can mean concentration or "mindfulness”. This is firmly elaborated in the Satipatthana Sutta. If you read the sutta, he clearly talks about the four foundations - Which can be found even in the Anapanasati Sutta. The Buddha has only ever used the word sati, not jhana nor vipassana to refer to meditation practice.

      Reflection is not insight - Hence, vipassana is not reflecting on the three marks of existence. However, insight can emerge from reflecting on the three marks on existence. It is all part of sati.

      Insight is, an intuitive understanding about nature, oneself and the path to enlightenment. It is a fruit, an attainment, birthed from proper sati, which is the 7th fold of the Noble Eightfold Path. Insight is not intellectual or logical understanding. In fact, vipassana is never mentioned within any of the discourses that has to do with sati. I hope this clarifies the misunderstanding.

              piti is more of the blossoming sensation within the chest, revealing the opening of the heart chakra. It is often related to what people might refer to being loved, or feeling immense love for another.

              Now sukha is more of what we discuss within this subreddit as the various jhana-nimittas. Most often, it is known as a tingling sensation. It can also be high-pitched ringing (auditory), aura/lights (visual), dizziness, sweet-tastes etc. While it translates into joy, it does mean a low-level in the lower jhanas. As you ascend in the jhanas, you get a higher degree of bliss revealed. They tend to become very intense until it overwhelms the external sensory domains completely. My experience is that this bliss is in fact a single field of bliss - which can culminate in various siddhis, such as clairaudience, etc. However, do not get engaged in them. Simply ride along with the bliss and allow it to unfold.

              Yes, there are various levels. Just like you would unwrap various layers of an onion and get to the part where it stings the most, the degree of bliss does increase as you go deeper into the higher jhanas. What you experience in the first two jhanas are very, very insignificant as compared to the deep equanimity states.
