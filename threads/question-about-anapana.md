- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 5 points · 1 year ago

  The important point is to have a clear, lucid mental state that is not overly agitated or sluggish.

  When a thought arises and your mind has been distracted by it, then you must know that it has arisen and that your mind has clung onto it. By simply knowing, your lucidity as the knowing nature does not dim. If however, you were not aware of the thought rising, such that it is dimly noticed of, then it is possible that your mental state has not reached sufficient power.

  At the same time, while one observes the thought, one does not cling onto the thought. The phenomenon that is being observed is impermanence.

  Therefore, the course of action is to firstly realize if your mind has wandered to a distracting thought. If it has, lucidity must be maintained by knowing fully that the thought has arisen. Following that, one mindfully turns attention back to the breath.

  There can be any kind of disturbance, mainly physical feelings and mental perceptions. In either case, when the mind turns towards them, simply know, then turn it back to the breath.

  For beginners, it is helpful to say in the mind, "Feeling. Breath. Thought.” Once familiar, one should use the knowing nature without verbalizing.
