# Ana-pana

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 8 points · 1 year ago

I have once covered this in a previous thread, but I found that many people have continued to have this misconception. Therefore this is an attempt to clarify doubts.

There are 4 levels of breathing according to the speed of the inhalation and exhalation.

Panting - fast and hasty, especially when the body is out of balance and requires oxygen.

Wind - The normal day-to-day breathing which people do. This is breathing to the level of the "lungs" and does not go into the dan-tian, especially after one has reached sexual maturity in puberty.

Chi - Subtle breathing that seems invisible. This is soundless, nearly undetectable, except for a faint sensation at the nose-tip. This is achieved in deep meditation and one can feel this enter throughout the skin and fill the body up in the lower dan-tien.

Xi - There is zero movement. No inhalation nor exhalation. Instead, the function of mechanical breathing shifts to what we call "fetal breathing" through the umbilicus. The lower abdomen contracts and expands, as if one was a fetus.

Now you must also know that before anything else moves, it is the mind that moves first. Therefore, this is all just a sign of the Sixth Consciousness becoming quiet of thoughts. At the stage of Xi, if one starts to have thoughts again, one breaks out of Xi.

Some of you seem to have problems sitting for long periods, so let me ask this question: When you wish to rise from sitting in meditation, what decides to get out of the sit?

You will realize that it is the Sixth Consciousness, the chatter-mind, that sends in thoughts. The five sense consciousnesses, the body consciousness, does not tell you to get out of meditation - it is often the sixth, discriminatory mind that does.

Therefore, when one arises the thought of rising up from meditation, it is an excellent endeavor to investigate this. Is this from the Sixth Consciousness? Or is this truly from the body? It is nearly all the time that the Sixth Consciousness is what is flooding your mind with distraction thoughts, and that is the very reason why you have slipped out of deep absorption states.

When the mind moves faster, the breathing becomes coarser again. The more silent the mind, the more linked the breath is to the thoughts, and the quieter the breath will become.

It is the same with fear. If one feels scared, the thoughts have come out of nowhere again.

In Anapana-sati, one watches the in and out breaths. However, it is not the in and out breaths that one cultivates. Instead, one is learning how to quieten the mind by slowing down the thoughts along with the breath.

When this happens, breath slows along with thoughts. Eventually, the gap between the inhalation and exhalation will lengthen slowly. This also becomes the gap between your thoughts.

Anapanasati is to utilize out and in-breath as phenomena to watch, to be mindful of. However, its eventual goal is to quieten the mind and hence reside in Xi, where the breath neither comes nor goes.

This is where energy starts to move unrestrained and uncontrolled by intentions. It goes where it should go, instead of what the intention forces it to do. The channels hence naturally open up, as if the dams have been lifted from a river and the water flows to where it should irrigate.

In the I-Ching, Master Nan taught this as the symbol of "flourishing". This means that when there is no consumption nor radiation, this is a state of beautiful harmony and growth.

When breathing in, one should also learn to pull the lower-abdomen inwards. Breathing in is a form of "consumption". Breathing out, one should learn to relax the lower-abdomen, and this is "radiation". Doing this, one separates the Yin and Yang dynamics accurately.

When there is a gap in the Sixth consciousness, or the discriminatory mind, then one suddenly has a chance for awakening. While this can also be done in various situations in your life, such as intense emotions - the one thing you must be able to do is to recognize it and go into samadhi immediately.

Strive on with diligence.

May you be enlightened in this lifetime.
