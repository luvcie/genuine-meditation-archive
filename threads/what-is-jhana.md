# What is Jhana?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 12 points · 2015-01-20

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2t1lm5/what_is_jhana/>

Jhana is an altered state of consciousness that allows for the factors of enlightenment to "shine" through. There are 9 Jhanas in total, 4 being that of Material (and 5 being that of Immaterial).

Edit: Please note that the Immaterial Samadhi's can be accessed without the four form Jhanas - But it is these first four Jhanas which are the most important and the road to liberation and insight.

How do you identify Jhana? Most of the time, people who go through Jhana experiences know it is extraordinary. However, there are exceptions by which people who have already experienced jhana think that they do not have attainments yet. This is the fault of poor translations of suttas.

First Jhana is characterised by:

Applied and sustained attention.

Joy. This joy is often felt throughout the whole body as a tingling sensation. Mental bliss arises as well.

Bliss. Bliss is the term used when you interpret this joy as pleasing. This is not the actual joy itself. Anyone who has sufficiently reached Jhana before is able to tap into this bliss and sustain it throughout the day.

Second Jhana is characterised by:

Applied and sustained attention => Unified Awareness.

Joy and Bliss from Tranquility. This bliss feels more that of tranquility, as compared to the withdrawing in the first jhana.

Third Jhana is characterised by:

Unified Awareness.

Faded Joy. The physical tingling has usually reduced by a lot by now, there might be remnants of mental tingling pleasure.

Pure Bliss. Bliss now exists alone and there is a very strong equanimity happening.

Fourth Jhana is characterised by:

Unified Awareness.

Bliss => Equanimity. Equanimity is an extremely non-dual experience - There is no pleasure nor pain experienced.

This marks the end of the four Material Jhanas. We say material because these are the rupa-Jhanas. Proceeding on, we reach the domain of the Immaterial, the arupa-Jhanas.

Now this Immaterial does mean something specific: It means things that are not of gross physical nature, such as subtle/astral matter, deva realms, deva beings, abstract concepts, archetypes, etc.

It is normally around the fourth to fifth Samadhi where a lot of strange phenomena happen, or even the birth of supernormal abilities. These abilities, known as "Siddhis", are normally regular faculties of the astral body generated by the mind.

Fifth Samadhi is characterised by:

Acquisition of Astral Body. This is also known as "Infinite Space". Upon exit of the physical body, you suddenly have heightened awareness - sensing the infinite boundaries of space itself.

Sixth Samadhi is characterised by:

Infinite Consciousness. This is reached by thinking of "who" is seeing these phenomena in the fifth Jhana. This is a subtle shift from "space" to "consciousness" itself.

Heavens/Deva realms. One transcends the physical space and reaches the deva realms, full of formless beings of light.

Seventh Samadhi is characterised by:

Nothingness. Omnipresence and love, one exceeds even the sights of galaxies, etc.

Eighth Samadhi is characterised by:

Infinite immaterial existence. Here, duality takes its end. This is often called 'neither perception nor non-perception'. There is literally no more 'movement' of the mind.

Finally, one reaches the Ninth - Cessation.

It is from this state where all your previous preparatory work is seen to be sufficient or not. The moment where you are almost liberated, any remaining fetters that are remaining will hold you back from enlightenment.

My advice to anyone who has never reached any of these Jhanas is to take it one step at a time. You should not look at these Jhanas as a goal, but more of a milestone tracker, such as one who measures liquid in a measuring cylinder.

Strive well, fellow Dharma friend.

    - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 2 points · 2015-01-30

      "Applied and sustained attention" can be put simply as a one-pointedness of attention. You are so deeply absorbed in attention that your mind does not fill with any other thoughts. In this case, there is a sorts of a mental effort in sustaining this attention. It might be on the subtle breath or the physical joy experienced by the body.

      "Unified awareness" is nearly automatic in a sense where there is no need to sustain the attention. In this case, it would be the full body tingling (joy and bliss) and a sense of tranquility. I hope this makes it clearer.

              Think this explanation will be valuable for you.

              **The First Jhana: Pleasant Sensations**

              The concentration begins with one-pointedness concentration and then when the concentration becomes strong you enter a sustained concentration which is a continuous concentration with no interruptions. You start to enter the remaining jhanic factors of aiming, happiness, and joy or rapture. You shift your attention from the meditation subject to the joy associated with your concentration. You do not cling to the sensations, but just watch them. The experience can include some very pleasant physical sensations such as goose bumps on the body and the hair standing up to more intense pleasures which grow in intensity and explode into a state of ecstasy. If you have pain in your legs, knees, or other part of the body during meditation, the pain will actually disappear while you are in the jhanas. The pleasant sensations can be so strong to eliminate your painful sensations.
