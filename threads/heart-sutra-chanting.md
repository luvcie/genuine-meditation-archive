# Heart Sutra Chanting

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 2 points · 2 years ago

The word 'chanting' came from the Chinese word 'Chan’ (or Japanese word 'Zen'). This came from the root Sanskrit word Dh'yan. However, this Chan then became a name for everything else grouped under it, therefore, it is no longer equivalent to Dh'yan.

One of the most powerful methods is literally the chanting of mantra. Avalokitesvara entered the stream by sound.

How does one chant a sutra properly? In fact, there is a secret behind this. When one chants, it must encompass fundamental tones.

In Yoga, there is "OM", which is pronounced in three parts: "A-U-M." "Ah" activates the root chakra. "U" activates the heart chakra. "M" activates the throat chakra.

However, when one chants AUM, when moving from "Ah" to "M", the fundamental tone of the "Ah" remains throughout the chant. Likewise, the "U" remains throughout "M". It does not matter how high a pitch one goes - The fundamental undertones must be there. This is why the voice of masters can sometimes seem like waterfalls.

Therefore, sit in Burmese (heel in perineum), half lotus or full-lotus.

Take any copy of the Heart Sutra (Sanskrit, Chinese or Japanese). It does not matter which version. The Japanese version is often used by Japanese masters as it contains many fundamental sounds within the language. Read each syllable, one-by-one.

Each note should vibrate at the bottom of your tailbone. If you sit in the correct posture, the note should vibrate throughout.

Breathe in fully, then resonate the syllable, vibrating it at your root chakra. It does not matter if you do it one breath and one syllable. You can go on to two syllables the longer your breath is. Then advance on and on until 5 syllables, and so on.

Enlightened masters can go on continuously for very extended periods of time in a single breath.
