# A light reminder: Practice versus Reading

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 4 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2tuct2/a_light_reminder_practice_versus_reading/>

One of the most unfortunate things about modern society is that due to the widespread movement of information, many people have also fallen into the trap of devouring information without truly processing it with experience.

I always use this modern example to describe it:

A basketball fan can analyze the moves of his favorite basketball player to the extent where he can say where this player jumps and takes off, his favorite moves, his playing style, etc. He knows the sport inside out, he is kept updated to the scores of the various basketball teams.

But here's the difference. Does this basketball fan play as well as the basketball player who does it for a profession?

Here is the metaphor that is easily mapped to the modern practice of the dharma.

Like the basketball fan, the Buddhist analyzes the sayings of the Buddha where he can recite concepts such as Interdependent Origination, karma or this or that. He might even know the discourses inside out. He is kept updated to the teachings of the various Buddhist teachers.

Has this person been enlightened yet?

Not so!

To reverse the effect for its cause is the biggest mistake in Dharma practice today. Do not forget that the Buddha never wrote his sermons down. He gave advice as if it was medicine, based on the condition and understanding of the person at that point of time.

Similarly, although there is no Buddha in front of you to give you direct advice, he has said that one looks to his teachings as the teacher.

How does one practice?

1. One looks at the suttas to get a basic guidance of the first steps to take practice. Looking the discourse, one understands the disease of delusion and understands the Four Noble Truths.

2. But does one really understand this yet? No! To truly understand the advice given, one will need to practice and encounter this question on their own. The Buddha was taught by his teachers but he investigated the phenomena on his own. With his huge compassion, he even laid out a roadmap to enlightenment.

3. Once the obstacles are reached, one then goes back to the suttas to seek the answer, almost as if you are a disciple going to the Buddha to ask for guidance.

4. This cycle continues until the point where enlightenment is attained. Once enlightened, one then seeks the suttas once again to verify if the true wisdom has truly penetrated and see if the experience is consistent with the other masters of the Path.

Do not forget this process. Any time you find yourself reading too much, ask yourself if it is truly relevant to your practice.

Let us strive on to the Dharma.
