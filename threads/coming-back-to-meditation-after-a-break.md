# Coming back to meditation after a break

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sylamatek** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 5 points · 2 years ago

Greetings all. I began meditating roughly a year ago- I didn't really keep track of when I started but I'm fairly certain is was during last fall. I worked up to 2 separate 20 minute sessions a day, one in the morning/early afternoon, and the other usually right before bedtime.

I remember being more relaxed, needing less sleep, less irritable, etc. However, I went through some rough emotions and eventually was unable to meditate anymore. A month or so passes and I eventually seek help and was diagnosed with depression. I think a big part of it was that not only was I filled with negative thought, but it was unending. I always picture the more and more fleeting thoughts in my head when I begin meditating as a boat drifting pass less and less drift wood, or a ball bumping into less and less pegs on its way down a board. But those last few times I had tried I was simply getting Isot in my own head and could not relax. I began treating myself but even over the summer I was afraid to try meditating again for fear I would still not be able to do it.

This semester started, and I was feeling ready to try again. I feel like my thoughts are harder to quiet down than when I was in full swing months ago, but I'm just getting back to the full 20 minute sessions and it hasn't been too difficult.

This post didn't have much of a purpose other than to say I will probably frequent this board more often now that I am practicing again, and that if you also have issues with depression and it is affecting your practices, I've been there, and there are ways to get past it, it's simply another obstacle.

So if you were able to recognize your thoughts, then that was already practising self-awareness. Think of it like a top spinning at full speed. As the mind becomes brighter, the top spins slower and eventually loses its speed. You don't have to put your finger on it to force it to stop. Just by watching it, and not giving it extra spin, it loses its momentum eventually.

  Another analogy is a cup of muddy water. After stirring, the muddy water will swivel around for a while and it will seem an opaque brown. But simply watching the thoughts and not identifying with them, you allow the swirling to stop, which brings the mud down. The mud rests on the bottom and the layer of water above (representing the mind) shines crystal-clear.

  Learn to be content. Put down all of your worldly worries and rest like a child in his cot, a wild animal in the safe embrace of the forest, or a fish in a safe ocean space. There is a certain power in gratitude or surrendering. When you feel pleasant, the mental state becomes focused and bright, allowing bliss and joy to rise. Just like how you would gather plasticine into a ball, bliss and joy accumulates within you, ripening in your heart and your body.
