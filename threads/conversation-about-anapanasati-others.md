Dear refugeinbuddha, I am doing anapanasati as per your thread but I find that my breath continues on and on even though I try to find the gap or stop it naturally as you said. Any tips?

> Suppose there was a child in front of a mirror. When he raises his arm, his reflection raises his arm as well. When he does not move his arm, his reflection does not move too. In the same way, your breath is the reflection in the mirror, while your mind is the child. When your mind moves, the breath will also move. When your mind is still and does not move, your breath will also be still. We call this Zhi Xi. Now you need to understand that there is a certain perspective that all beings have an inherent enlightened nature. But what does this mean? Everything is perfect. However, due to the seed of ignorance, everything is thrown out of disharmony. In the same way, nature has a form of unfolding and folding between harmony and disharmony. To be enlightened is to return back to perfect harmony - to reunite with the Big Self of the universe. To be ignorant is to return to disharmony - to dissociate with the Big Self and identify a piece of life, a body, as one's self. In the process of anapanasati, you understand life and death from every breath. When you return to the state where the mind does not move, the breath will naturally cease. Therefore, the task is to still the mind - and hence still the breath. If the body is agitated, the mind cannot settle either - therefore one should relax the body so that the mind settles. All of this is to settle oneself into a state of great stillness. From within stillness comes forth great movement. In other words, great Yang erupts out of extreme Yin. Samadhi is in fact a great movement. Therefore, do not mistakenly think that to still the mind means to reach a drowsy, sluggish state with no thoughts, because this is not true.
