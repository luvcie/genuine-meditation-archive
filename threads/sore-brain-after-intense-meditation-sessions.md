      Just because you leave the meditation matt does not mean that you leave a meditative state of mind. If you think

      meditation is only on the matt, then you will be unable to carry your practice over to waking life. When you sit, you do this to calm the mind and stabilize clarity of mind, disentangling from the conceptual aggregate that is the mind-stream; then you get up and you engage the world, resting in this clarity and space of mind that is not constantly grasping and entangled with thought.

      What causes one to get up, is that you must engage the world, rather than favor a dualistic perspective and choose to remain on the matt forever as if you can only maintain a meditative state of mind when hiding away from the world.

      What causes one to fall out of a meditative state of mind is being entangled in thoughts and falling into an unconscious reactionary state of mind, this is what ends the meditation, truly.

      So to clarify one last time. Meditation is not something that is done. It is a state of mind, centering yourself in a peaceful clarity and stillness that is ordinary awareness; disentangling yourself from the egoic mind-stream. In the beginning, this is difficult, you must learn to stabilize yourself when confronting thought and you must learn to not fall into an unconscious association with the thought-stream, which pulls you into an unconscious state of reactivity.

      When you can begin to disentangle and center yourself, then meditation becomes about disentangling from the thought-stream, you then apply this when engaging the world. It is only when you are caught up in thought, caught up in reacting, that you have fallen out of this meditative state; so then you recognize that you have fallen unconscious and center yourself again.

      This is the initial practice and this is called building a foundation.

      After you have built this foundation, then you can truly begin to work on other aspects that have been discussed, such as the energetic path. But first, you must build a foundation of clear awareness, if you do not build this foundation first then you will continuously find yourself falling into patterns of deluded mental activity; perpetuating karma and making no real progress.

      Edit:

      To clarify the steps to building a foundation:

      First, we recognize that our attention is chaotic and all over the place, so we practice increasing our will and ability to concentrate by practicing mindfulness of an object; this can be the breath and thus we just observe, passively and thus mindfully, the natural rhythms of the breath.

      Once we have stabilized mindfulness, then we can begin to practice disentangling from the thought-stream and resting in the peaceful clarity of ordinary awareness.

      Once we have been able to disentangle from the thought-stream and remain centered in a peaceful clarity, without grasping thought and reacting, then we can move onto the next step of building the foundation.

      This is when we then allow the mind to be entangled with the experience of the senses, with the experience of thought, but the catch is that we use the state of mind that we gained from the previous step; the state of mind that is the disentangled state of ordinary awareness; and we use that to seamlessly and mindfully engage our senses without being caught up in them. When we do this, we do not grasp at anything, we just allow it to be as it is without judging it or forming any opinions about it, for if we did do that then we would be grasping the senses and grasping the conceptual aggregate.

      So, first we stabilize mindfulness, then we learn to disentangle our awareness and learn the taste of awareness, the feeling of this state of mind, then with that having been known to us fully, we return to a detached mindfulness; the difference between the second and third is subtle, so I will explain again.

      The second step is when we detach from the 5 senses and the 6th aggregate, resting and learning the taste of ordinary awareness, the third is when we cease detaching from the 5 senses and the 6th aggregate but retaining that taste of ordinary awareness, allow our experience to pass by without judgement, without obstruction, just letting it flow.

      Once you have built these three pillars completely, we can say that you have truly begun to build a great foundation.

      From this, you can begin to work on jhanas, which are still foundational as mastery of them is required before we can move beyond the foundational stage and begin to truly cultivate energetically; in the long-run, this results in that ordinary awareness becoming extraordinary and from this vantage point, a lot more becomes possible.

      But first we must work on our foundation, from that basic foundation we stabilize ourselves in the jhanas, then from here we can truly begin to talk about working on samadhi.
