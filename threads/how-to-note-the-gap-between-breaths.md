# How to note the gap between breaths.

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 3 points · 2 years ago

Recently I watched Master Nan on youtube explain the true interpretation of the Buddha's instructions on anapanasati:

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KIqlZ6nn268

However, I'm having trouble knowing if I'm interpreting Master Nan's words correctly. Does one consciously note the length of the gap between breaths? And if so, does this not take the attention away form the current moment?

If you don't note the length explicitly, but instead simply experience it, then isn't this the same as following each breath from beginning to end?

In other words, do you need to know that you know the lengths of the gaps...or does it just happen naturally from following the breaths (in which case, why mention it)? Currently my interpretation is that noting the lengths of the gaps between breaths, without conceptualizing, is one and the same as simply noting when one breath ends and another begins. It is a technique that maybe helps the meditator stay with the breath for its entire duration.

As always I appreciate thoughts or corrections.

  > If you don't note the length explicitly, but instead simply experience it, then isn't this the same as following each breath from beginning to end?

  By following the breath from start to finish, is one not aware of this gap in the middle?

  At least, when I follow my breath, I feel the breath move in, it stops for a moment, then it moves out, stops for a moment, then moves in. I don't consciously say something like "breath stopping”, "breath beginning”, "breath ending”, but rather, it's just experienced as a cognitive "knowing" that the breath is now moving in, the breath is now stopping, and the breath is now moving out.

  As I get further along in my meditation, the breath becomes more subtle, and the "breath stopping” increases its length, and suddenly this bardo period is several second's long, instead of just a single second, and as I get deeper, this period increases until I reach a point where my breath stops and breath cessation occurs and a forced "breath hold" occurs until my body expels the air, and this keeps happening again and again.

  So for me, the way I understand Master Nan, is simply to be cognizant of this area of the breath, but not to explicitly note it. I am sure refugeinbuddha will help with the accuracy here, but at least, that is how I understand it.

        - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 1 point · 2 years ago

          Yes, this is correct. It is not noting cognitively, but knowing.

          When a thought arises in your mind, how do you realize it is there? Surely there is the content of the object. However, the function of the mind to know when it has risen - that knowing - that being - is what is there despite the thought being replaced by another.
