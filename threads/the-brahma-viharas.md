# The Brahma Viharas

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 2 points · 2015-03-19

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2zk7m8/the_brahma_viharas/>

As I've promised, this is a post on the Brahma Viharas in reply to a request by members of the subreddit.

---

The words "Brahma Vihara" directly translates as "House of God". This is also known as the "Four Boundless or Wholesome States". What does this mean exactly? In practice, it is good to call these the wholesome states that can replace unwholesome states.

The very first thing one must do is to *recognize* the unwholesome states as they happen. It is only by identification first, can you shift towards the wholesome states.

What are these four states or divine abodes?

1. **Metta** = Loving-kindness = In all directions, of all things that have shape or life, there is not one that one passes by or leaves aside, but regards all with metta.

2. **Karuna** = Compassion

3. **Mudita** = Sympathetic Joy

4. **Upekkha** = Equanimity

For 2-4, the description in the suttas is as such: To let the mind pervade one quarter of the thought with these thoughts, and then lastly to the whole wide world, above, below, around and everywhere, far-reaching and measureless.

How do you arrive at these divine abodes? It is only through jhana where you can attain them. They are products of jhana. Genuine metta cannot be developed mentally - It is only through the product of jhana where it arises.

Hence, it is erroneous to say that you can practice metta on its own, if you have never experienced metta by itself before. It is possible to feel a very filtered experience of it, such as having unconditional loving-kindness towards your loved ones - However, the full-blown experience is only within jhanas.

Metta meditation is in a way, simply following the Noble Eightfold Path and not a mental exercise. By using the "method", where one visualizes someone you love, etc, it is in a way, recognizing the inherent nature which is apparent in all beings.

Be mindfully aware in every moment. Endeavour to still the mind in every moment. Recognize every unwholesome state and replace it with wholesomeness in anyway that you can.

This is in a way, sila practice. To treat everyone with respect, as if one was a friend.

Now, how do these four brahmaviharas link to enlightenment?

It is only when these four are strong, where one is said to have established the basis for fourth jhana. One then has developed enough "power" to go into the samadhis beyond.

A simple out-of-body-experience is realized through an ordinary mind state. But true samadhi experience is realized through strong brahmavihara foundations.

I hope this answers some of the lingering questions about the four brahmaviharas. Please post if you have a question.

              Toxic friends can be lethal to the mind, as always taught by the Buddha. Yes, it does depend on your personal wisdom and the situation as well. As much as you can, do try to stay away from gossip and abusive chatter. There are reasons for upholding such discipline and you should observe the reason behind these rules.

              The key in talking is to ask oneself, am I:

              - Honest? Speaking the truth? Factual?

              - Speaking pleasantly?

              - Speaking divisively?

              - Considering whether the other person is willing to hear what I want to say?

              - Engaging in idle chatter? Speaking in accordance to the Dharma, etc?

              It can be hard for a layperson to adhere to the idle chatter rule, but you must realize that this wisdom must be cultivated through mindfulness and worldly wisdom (logic, analysis, etc).

              Is your mind settled when engaging in such activity? Is it conducive for your cultivation? If it is not, then perhaps you might want to think about it.
