- **elnoxvie** · 2 points · 8 months ago

  > What can we do when our bodies are very ill and no relief is in sight?

  Accepting that this is the true nature of this body. No matter how we want things to be otherwise, it will eventually lead us to death. By knowing that our time is limited, we have to put even more efforts to practice seriously to realize the dhamma.

  As we started to realize the dhamma, the suffering will lessen and eventually gone with our complete realization. Though, pain will still remain.

  > When it is difficult to meditate during these times, is it too late to cultivate further?

  It's not too late. Instead, it could be a good opportunity for us to practice the letting go of this body by using this body as an object for contemplation.

  Remember, meditation does not mean you have to sit down on the mat. Ajahn Chah well known saying "As long as you can breath, you can meditate". Meditation is not about what you do externally, it's what you do internally with your mind.

  Here is a story told by ajahn brahm: "Do you see the leaves on the tree?"

  "are they only old leaves falling down?" “The truth is no. Whether young or old, they all fall down to the ground" “This is the law of the nature”

  We all have to learn to accept them as it is and while we still have this vessel / this body, we should strive to realize the dhamma as far as we can.

  I hope this helps.

          Mm, it kind of depends on exactly what you're experiencing and I'm definitely no expert, but essentially yes.

          If you think of your body in third person as though you're not actually connected to it and then have the intention to know what it feels like at that point (e.g. the end of the nose, just what it feels like in the flesh there) and as you notice it, stay with the feeling and watch it intently - but not forced, when you're really with it - you'll feel nothing else for that moment.

          Calming down is not so easy. What has helped me today is - notice the observer 'you' - that is - if you notice the flashing grey fuzz in your vision, the you that is noticing that stuff, not the light itself of the eye-nerve-cells, but the observer.

          Notice that then notice your discomfort / the tension making it - and suggest yourself to use 50% of the tension only and for a few seconds feel the difference, and keep repeating.

          If you are more specific we'd direct you better. I'm definitely not an expert or good advice giver
