# Another Description of the Four States of Breath & How to Count Breaths

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 7 points · 2 months ago

It is taking me a while to answer all the inbox messages, I will reply them one-by-one when I find the time. For now, I will post general tips and hopefully they help your cultivation.

This is yet another method of understanding the Wind-element and how it relates to the breath. As from previous posts, there are four states of breath: Wind, Panting, Qi and Xi. This is another way of understanding them.

wind (Feng) is when the breath is noisy and coarse, which can be heard in the nostrils.

Panting (Chuan) is when the breath reaches only the lungs, whereby one is struggling between the inhale and exhale - unable to fully perfuse the body with adequate chi.

Qi is when the breath reaches the Dan-tian (below the belly button).

Xi is when it is as if this breath has fully stopped.

Now when I say it reaches the lungs or abdomen, do not just focus on the inhalation - the inhalation must be natural and not clung onto like an object in any way. There will come a period where you take in an inhalation and in this pause, the body starts to 'fill with chi’ as it sinks into the dan-tian, followed by an exhale to purge the dirty-chi within.

How to understand this? In the Tien-tai sect, one starts with Counting when the breath is irregular. Therefore, the mind is tied to the breath - and one counts on the exhale. Therefore:

e In... out (count 1)...

e In... out (count 2)...

e In... out (count 3,4,5,6,7,8,9)...

e In... out (count 8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1)...

If you can complete this sequence (1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1) without a single interruption of mind going to other thoughts, then you can abandon the Counting method and move on to Following Xi - at this moment, your mind will be released adequately.

Therefore, the counting of breaths is only used as a tool as well as a gauge - to unite the mind with the breath. The moment the mind and breath are unified, there is no need to count breaths anymore - just follow the breath! It will evolve from panting towards gi and then finally to Xi (which is the state of cessation).

At this point, the chi will be saturated within the haemoglobin and myoglobin of the body, and all the cells will seem as if they are breathing together, with extreme bliss and lightness of the body, as if one could levitate. If one does levitate, this is due to the anapana gong-fu accomplishment of ridding the body.

The internal-breathing system of the body kicks in - with no dependence on the external-breathing system utilizing the lungs or external oxygen. The emphasis has shifted from post-heaven chi usage to pre-heaven chi usage. What the Buddha talked about in the sutra about knowing whether anapana was long or short, permeating the body, and so on, is exactly this!

Note that the breath must not be controlled in any way. Mind should be held not too tightly (straining muscles) nor too loosely (becoming distracted) during counting. If the mind is already easily paired with the breath without any form of distraction, then just go on to following Xi.

One of the easiest ways to progress from counting to following is simply to be aware of the breath during the entire day. Knowing when it goes in, knowing when it goes out, this is already the goal of doing counting - tying mind with breath.
