# Having difficulty living the precepts as a 21 year old

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sylamatek** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 1 year ago

I've just recently returned from Japan and Singapore. Before the trip, I was a relatively firm atheist/agnostic. One of the professors from my university that went with me was a Buddhist and between seeing lots of Buddhist architecture, to my professor finding lots of Buddhist pamphlets and free books in english. Now I'd say I'm leaning towards Buddhism spiritually, but definitely philosophically.

I enjoyed reading the material and immediately started applying some of the knowledge to every part of my life, finding some deep roots of un-compassionate thought or behavior and starting to rectify them.

However, it is difficult to apply some of these precepts in reality. For instance, as a young college student, most of my peers are the type to talk about people behind their backs, wish ill upon others, etc. Part of my difficulty is in regards to how I feel about their actions to me. This is mostly in the form of exclusion and active unfriendliness. I know it is probably best to keep a polite distance from these people but it is difficult to not feel some bitterness on any scale.

But one of the more things is seeing people being cruel to others. I usually do not chastise someone out loud but there are a great many times where I get a angry or annoyed feeling towards that person for inflicting cruelty on someone else.

I also have not been practicing meditation much at all lately. I was unable to practice during my travels because of a strict schedule, and I have not made much effort lately, something I will attempt to change this weekend.

To make a long story short, I'm having difficulty practicing in a very un-compassionate sangha.

  There are many who don the yellow robe but are unworthy of it, just as the Buddha had pointed out. Similarly, there are many who have the label of being a Buddhist but do not practice the Noble Eightfold Path. Only one who practises the N8P can be called someone who seeks Dharma.

  As for their actions, perhaps there is a phrase in the Bible that might fit here: "Forgive them, but they know not what they have done." Similarly, these people who have not cleared their minds and moved away from unwholesome states do not know what ails their mind. It is like how a mad person does not think he is mad. However, the way to treat a

  mad person is not to ostracize him. As long as you stay in your own wholesome state, being equanimous and unbothered by others, then you will continue to teach the Dharma by exemplification. That is the highest teaching.
