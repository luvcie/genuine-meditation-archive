# Moving Meditation

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 3 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3jy1b0/moving_meditation/>

One of the most important things to understand is how to allow oneself to sense without acting in sensing. One should see without the act of seeing, hear without the act of hearing, taste without the act of tasting, smell without the act of smelling and feel without the act of feeling.

In the Bahiya Sutta, the Buddha says:

"Then, Bāhiya, you should train yourself thus:

In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen.

In reference to the heard, only the heard.

In reference to the sensed, only the sensed.

In reference to the cognized, only the cognized.

That is how you should train yourself. When for you there will be only the seen in reference to the seen, only the heard in reference to the heard, only the sensed in reference to the sensed, only the cognized in reference to the cognized, then, Bāhiya, there is no you in connection with that. When there is no you in connection with that, there is no you there. When there is no you there, you are neither here nor yonder nor between the two. This, just this, is the end of dukkha (dissatisfaction/stress).

You must learn how to untangle yourself from the senses, and stop identifying it.

In walking meditation, the eyes can be left open. However, we should not gaze and perceive things as distinct. Instead, we must retreat away from distinctiveness such that the eyes "soften". Instead of perceiving objects, you perceive light and how they enter your eyes. As such, it can become blurred and luminous. By learning how to dissociate yourself from the senses, there is no "you" in terms of sight.

You can also do this with other senses, such as the auditory jhana-nimitta. However, for safety sake, since you are moving about in a space, visual would be a better option.

To start walking, one can take lessons in Tai Chi, for it is based off moving meditation. A good video that shows how to do basic tai chi walking is here: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aKra_KJOiKk [1]

When doing the walking, let the energy that rises guide your movements, even in the arms. It is said that monks in deep absorption sprung into various mudras and forms that you see in internal-based martial arts. By learning how to do so, you can even incorporate this meditative state in your everyday life.

If you want, you can also do Anapanasati while moving. Keep silent without talking and use the breath to guide the motions. For example, when moving your arms up, one can breathe in - and vice versa. This also has its grounds in hatha yoga.

This is especially useful for people who are tired after a day's work and tend to slide into torpor-filled states. Perhaps this is a good lesson to show that meditation should not just be done on the mat, but it is a continuous effort.
