          When having a stiller mind with one-pointed attention, one becomes much more sensitive to movement in sensory fields. Therefore, it is ideal to practice in places where sensory stimuli are not distracting. This is especially so for the first four jhanas. The Buddha described himself being unable to hear the crash of thunder while meditating, and that was because he was past the form-jhanas and in the immaterial samadhis. Otherwise, being within the body, it is still very much possible, especially if one is in the 1-2nd jhanas, to be startled by loud noises, or be affected by external influences.

          It is great to meditate in nature, especially with contact of one's perineum against the ground. However, try not to do it in a place where there are many people around if you are unable to go into deep absorption quickly.
