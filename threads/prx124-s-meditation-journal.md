01/30/15: Today I started off less than optimal. Later in the evening I felt agitated a little for no particular reason. I caught myself being irritated by minor annoyances, luckily I was mindful of arising impulses to act on them. I wanted to start my journal tomorrow but I felt the urge to mediate tonight. Before my session I couldn't help but notice the barking dogs outside and the sounds of the passing train. These annoyances reassured me that it's important to practice in a quiet place, or at least I understood better why people would want to seek seclusion. Before, I practiced Shikantaza, I just sat for the sake of it, no concentration and no stillness of the mind, whatever came internally or externally was alright with me. But now with starting Samatha, I was not ready for all the noise, so I decided to cheat a little and use ear plugs and headphones. It was silent now and I could hear my breath and the heart beat. I had a feeling that maybe this is there is all to me, this breath and this heart beat. It's nothing to contemplate about but it's the thought that came to me because of how loud it felt to listen to my own heart beat and breath. I focused on the breath and I did not have many intruding thoughts. I can't say that I've achieved pure concentration but my heart beat slowed down with my breath and normalized, it felt natural and easy to sit. I didn't time it but I think it was close to 15 minutes or so. I meditated with eyes closed but closer to the end my eye leads felt like they wanted to open, so I didn't fight it and switched to eyes open meditation. I had my 108 mala next to me and I decided to do one round of beads for timing my breathing and call it a session. In retrospect, even though I didn't achieve high concentration, I've calmed my mind and it was easy to sit, after the session I couldn’t care less about the barking dog outside, I forgot about it and it felt like a petty concern. I also find Samatha meditation to be more enjoyable than Shikantaza. Since I never had a teacher, there is a big possibility that I was doing Shikantaza wrong but it felt like it was too much because I was just observing my thoughts and any noises. It was a calm and neutral observation but breath-concentration is definitely more enjoyable because it felt more immersive. Overall, I didn't have high expectations for the session because I was agitated from the start but at the end my mind was calm. It was a good session.
