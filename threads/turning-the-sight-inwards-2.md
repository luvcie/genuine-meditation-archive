# Turning the Sight Inwards

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 21 days ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/6c0uxt/turning_the_sight_inwards/>

This is a response that was due a few months ago, so I apologise for its lateness. What is internal seeing? In the Mahayana teachings, it is very easy to see this phrase, but what exactly does it entail? There are concepts known as 'turning the hearing-sense inwards' as well.

There are various levels, and I will not describe all of them here because it will only be counterproductive - one should find out for oneself and not intellectualise it too much.

Now I want to ask you about the sense of sight and hopefully a short exercise will help you understand more about it.

When one receives light onto one's retina, it translates into an electrical information that eventually brings up an image in your mind. Throughout this process, there is no additional thought added upon it. It is simply seeing.

However, for most people, the seeing becomes ornamented with all kinds of interpretative thoughts - It says 'this is an apple' or 'I hate this' or 'this is good and this is bad'. This is what we call the process of feeling, clinging, birth of thought, leading to suffering.

Now I would like to invite you to simply see. It can be a tree, it can be any scenery, perhaps even a white wall as Bodhidharma did - Simply see.

What you will notice, just like anything you place your attention on, is that other distractions and residual energies will start to arise, trying to draw your attention away from just the pure act of seeing.

It is almost as if in the present state, it is what some new-age teachers call 'being present' - and it is reaching out for these extra ornamental thoughts that obscure the pure state of seeing.

You do not want to interpret what you are seeing, neither do you want to add anything extra above that of seeing. Just see - it is what it is - it is not what you think it is. Just like a camera, what it sees, it simply sees without editing any of the contents.

There is no being, self, personality or ego that is doing the "seeing". It is just pure seeing. Nothing else should be in the mind.

When you let go of all of these distractions, not through suppression, but through the simple realisation that all of these ornamentation around seeing is unneeded - then your visual field will start to become more saturated.

You are not sending your vision outwards - that leaks the spirit - neither are you trying to drag visions in. It is already there in your mind without effort. Nothing to push out to, nor pull in.

Now if you have done it properly, then you will see that the function of seeing is just the function of seeing. It is unadulterated, it is unhindered.

Now close your eyes.

If you wish, you may "internally see" the energy of all the Buddhas of the ten directions gathering above you in a disc of brilliant light. There is no need to visualise - simply know - there is no added process to this.

Now this disc of light rains down upon you, until the entire body dissolves in this brilliant light. One is no longer the body - one is in union with the Buddhas of the 10 directions. Now there is only a dense pillar of light connecting the disc above you and the ground.

At this point, you no longer need to generate any more imagination or visualisation - dissolve the sense of the body and become one with the light. That is all you need to see from now on.

In the same way as you have done above, you are to simply have the function of pure seeing - only internally now. Affix this 'image' and purely see without adulteration.

If you do this correctly, you will immediately feel the blessings of the Buddhas of all 10 directions. Joy and intense bliss may arise - one's energies will be cleansed.

Again, this seeing is not obscured by anything - not a mental wobble, not a thought arising, not a mental quality arising, not a sensation arising, not a body-sense arising. Just simply let it be.

Depending on how much merit you have - you might not have a connection at first. Our original nature is one with the Buddhas, but our lack of merit and our karmic blockages are what determines whether we can be one with them.

The lesson to learn here can also be applied to any meditation sitting. For example, when one watches the breath, is it pure 'seeing' or 'hearing'? You will realise that it is 'impure' seeing most of the time, because of the arising of false-thoughts.

When the Buddha raised his flower, why did Mahākāśyapa smile? This is a hua-tou (meditation enquiry) that is good to reflect on.
