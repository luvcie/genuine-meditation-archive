# What differentiates a Buddha and a Bodhisattva?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Question` · 6 points · 1 year ago

This was asked as a question by a sangha member. Previously, I have mentioned that the main difference is the mastery of the faculties, but here is the full answer.

This answer can be found in the Mahaprajnaparamita Shastra as 18 values.

1. Perfection of Body

2. Perfection of Speech

3. Perfection of Memory

4. Perfection of Impartiality

5. Serenity

6. Self-sacrifice

7. Unceasing desire to liberate all sentient beings

8. Unceasing energy to liberate all sentient beings

9. Unceasing intention to liberate all sentient beings

10. Unceasing wisdom to liberate all sentient beings

11. Powers of liberation

12. Mastery of the powers of liberation

13. Perfect wisdom in deed

14. Perfect wisdom in word

15. Perfect wisdom in thought

16. Perfect knowledge of past

17. Perfect knowledge of future

18. Perfect knowledge of present

Along with these 18, comes along the ten great powers of the Tathagata, as expounded in the Maha Sihanada sutta, including the ability to see "the manifold and different elements and the systems of the world".

Now one might ask then - what is the significance of a Buddha?

One can firstly look at the Maha-samaya Sutta, where there was a great assembly of over ten thousand world systems (especially devas) who arrived to hear the discourse of the Buddha.

But what is a world?

The Buddha described the six elements as such:

1. Earth

2. Water

3. Fire

4. Wind

5. Space

6. Consciousness

And in all of these worlds, one does not see anything apart from these six elements. Man is these 6, the world is these 6, so is the entire universe.

The Buddha can extend his vision to thrice-a-thousand world systems, first by sending light, then his voice. In modern scientific terms, one may refer this to the span of a meta-galaxy. To understand this, one can refer to this as a guide:

1. Once-a-thousand world system (Minor World-system): Galaxy

2. Twice-a-thousand world system (Middle World-system): Galactic cluster

3. Thrice-a-thousand world system (Major World-system): Thousands of galactic clusters to form the Universe.

However, note that these systems are approximate, and they refer instead to the spheres of consciousnesses that sentient beings inhabit across the universe.

When one gains mastery of fourth dhyana, with faculties fully functional, one leaps into the the fruit of the holy life and attains the mind-made body, visiting the various worlds.
