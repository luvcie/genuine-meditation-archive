(1) The Five Aggregates

Where does Maya reach? Maya only reaches when the Ego exists. How does the Ego exists? When the inherent nature believes that it is the five skandhas (or aggregates). What are the Five Skandhas? They are:

1. Rupa: Physical form, body, matter

2. Vedana: Sensation (interpretation as pleasantness, unpleasantness and neutral)

3. Sanna: Perception (recognition of an object)

4. Sankhara: Volition (mental inclinations, habits, forms [thoughts, ideas, opinions,...])

5. Vinnana: Consciousness

In the Maha Malunkya Sutta[1] , jhana is shown to be a way to abandon the five clinging aggregates of cognition (vinnana).

As long as the five aggregates exist, they are called the Self. This also called "Jiva" in Hinduism. Jiva comes from Shiva, which is the word for the inherent nature that pervades the universe, also known as Purusha, the "Formless Spirit". It is this Self, this Jiva, this Ego that is subject to Maya. Although you are the inherent nature, you are conceived as a body due to the allurement of Maya.

At first, there was Akasha or Space. When it started moving, there was Wind. Due to Wind, Fire was born. Due to Fire, Water was born. Due to Water, Earth was born. As such, the five elements were primordial and manifestation. The five elements are separate from the Formless.

If one does not know Maya, one is already the inherent nature and Life is the very property of one.

(2) The Shining One

When the Sun shines down onto the Earth, it is only by this light where all things can be seen. Similarly, the One illuminates the five skandhas and hence allow the senses to take form and make contact.

What is ignorance? Ignorance is when you believe that you are a body which does the eating, drinking, fun things, thrilling things, sexual intercourse, etc. Ignorance is believing that the sensory objects give pleasure, when they are clearly not eternal and permanent. These sensory objects are mere nerve excitations that brings messages to the physical brain.

(3) The Inescapable Game of Delusion

It is truly insanity and delusion. One starts playing the game of the senses, and then starts to have pleasure/pain and rides on one's own fantasies, digging oneself deeper into the delusion. Even if you kill your own desires for the sensory world, the killer of the senses still exist. That must be removed as well.

One thinks one is the character is the game. One thinks one is the body, bearing the merits, bearing the sins of the body. This is the Ego's game. It is like claiming the fireworks to be caused by yourself simply because you were near the fire-starters. By being attached, one cannot escape the cycle of samsara.

What are results? What is progress then, if the ego is a delusion? Progress can only exist when there is an Ego. Hence even the desire for results is grasping at Maya. Desiring results is the same as claiming, "Yes! I am the body! I am delusional! That is exactly why I need progress!" The Ego suffers all of one's own accord, not the inherent nature.

(4) The Trap of Intellect

The more logic you treat it with, the more mental formations you will create. The Ego becomes stronger - Sankhara, or Volition (the fourth aggregate) becomes far more expansive. You cannot think your way to enlightenment. If anything, you are moving further away from enlightenment.

Those who are simple minded but full of faith reach the inherent nature. Those who think they can reach it by logic are self-deceiving. This is also the reason why simple faith can bring one into the inherent nature. By having faith in the Buddha, there were many people who were enlightened on the spot.

(5) The Mind is Contrary

What you say and what your mind thinks is contrary to each other. On one hand, you may think that you are not the Ego, but the mind certainly experiences the Ego. On one hand, you may think that you are in emptiness, but the main certainly experiences the thought of "emptiness". All sorts of experiential realms are within the mind itself.

There is no goal to reach when there is true non-duality. In other words, you are the inherent nature. To overcome the barrier, spiritual practice must be intense. By losing all sense of separateness, one merges into the embodiment of enlightenment.

(6) What is the inherent nature?

It is what shines upon and activates the five skandhas. The five skandhas are not it. The life-energy itself is the inherent nature. There is life-energy within the living and not in the dead.

It is not found in the physical body, neither is it found in the astral/subtle body. Neither is it in "nothingness". This is known as the causal body. When all is forgotten where nothing remains, that very "nothingness" is also given up. One who remains is the knower of the three worlds.

Where is it?

Things rise and pass. Knowledge comes and goes. Sleep comes and goes. Thoughts come and go. The body comes and goes. But what is eternal is the one that is changeless throughout all of this. The one who witnesses all of these. The one who is not depleted nor added when these things come and go.

It is within all beings. It cannot be represented by "it". Recognize that it is within all, and behave with respect to that. Treat all beings as the same inherent nature.

The "knowing" is the Pure Consciousness. The consciousness with objects is the bounded consciousness. However, even the knowing consciousness creates a duality and is also within Maya.

To see One is knowledge. To see many, is Ignorance.

But to be One, it does not mean that you do not know when your stomach hurts, when people call you bad names, and when you taste certain flavors. The One knows all.

The body is not separate from the inherent nature. The body itself changes. Matter leaves the body everyday, matter enters the body everyday. It is not separate from the inherent nature.

The body is not a body, but it is simply called a body. All is "Self".
