- **ColinPlays** · 2 points · 2 years ago

  Set a strong intention to vividly observe as many distinct sensations at the tip of the nose (or your upper lip, or just within the nasal cavity --- wherever you most easily detect the breath) as you can while you breathe.

  More specifically, take note of the temperature of the air as it passes in and out, the sensation of the air moving, any changes in pressure in your skin, etc. Note the inhale, pause (if you detect a pause after the inhale), exhale, pause, as separate yet rhythmic and cyclical events. Lovingly observe the life-giving breath with as much detail as possible. And try noticing when these details change, and whether and how your level of concentration, tranquility, emotional tone, and so on change as well.

  If and when you notice your attention has wandered, take a moment to appreciate the gift of consciousness that allowed you to wake up to that fact. Then, without judgment, reaffirm your intention to vividly observe the breath and return to doing so. With this behavior you positively reinforce your faculties of concentration and encourage mindfulness.

  Edit to add: I used to struggle because I had a hard time observing part of the breath, specifically the exhale, when the breath would become very subtle. I could still detect it at the abdomen, but wanted to continue to concentrate on the sensations at the nose. It seemed as if there was no sensation whatsoever to detect. Since the breath continued

  to move, however, this could not be the case; once I increased the sensitivity of my faculties of observation in the above manner, I was able to detect clear breath-related sensations even with very smooth and fine breath.

  This ability can then be developed and refined to observe the cycles of the "breath” throughout the body in non-obvious places such as the extremities, the top of the head, etc. In other words, one develops the ability to detect the flow of subtle energies, and so on.
