Namo Buddhaya Namo Dharmaya Namo Sanghaya

I had been doing sati of thoughts for more than a year quite irregularly, plus some other practices, recently I made the decision to change most of it, and begin anapanasati. With the sati of thoughts I have cultivated very little stable and directed attention, and maybe an habit of giving too much of it towards thoughts, so it's like I'm starting from scratch, but I have faith in the method of anapana. I also thought I could enter into first jhana but I was just overestimating my progress because I could feel piti. At most I must have caught some extremely brief glimpses of it.

This is my current practice schedule (edited):

Wake up at 5Am:

-9 Bottled-Wind Pranayama

-Anapanasati

-Before noon, the 5 tibetan rites and some asanas.

Afternoon:

-Cundi Mantra

-White Skeleton

Evening:

Optional loving-kindness meditation

-Anapanasati

-Offering for hungry ghosts

I still have to learn and implement the white skeleton practice, and am slowly augmenting the breathings I'm able to complete with the bottled wind practice, because it feels extremely intense to me. The current length of my anapanasati sessions is 35 minutes, I'm adding 5 minutes to every session every day until I reach 50, then I will perhaps wait a bit and again slowly work up to 2 hours per sitting. I also will strive to be always aware of my breath during the day.

Right now I've been doing anapanasati for three days and am noticing a slight improvement in directing and sustaining attention towards the breath. I will update this from time to time with my general progress, experiences, questions and possible changes.

Lately I've had this question, should I fall asleep while watching my breath and doing no other thing? What is the best way? I used to fall asleep while visualizing things or enjoying the dreamlike imagery that my subconscious projects when I'm close to being asleep.
