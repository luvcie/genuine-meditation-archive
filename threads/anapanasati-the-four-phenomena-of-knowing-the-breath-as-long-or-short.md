# Anapanasati: The Four Phenomena of Knowing the Breath as Long or Short

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 7 points · 13 days ago

I have realized that there are some sangha members who consistently ask me about the length of breath and how it is confusing them. As such, the instructions on the sticky thread seem to be insufficient, therefore I have elaborated it as follows.

The first thing to understand is the difference of Buddhist teaching from other traditions. The largest focus of Buddhist teaching is on discernment, on wisdom development. It is not about attaining samadhi - all kinds of traditions can attain samadhi. It is about developing one's wisdom along with samadhi - this is what truly makes the difference between a Buddhist and a Taoist master, for example.

Now this step that comes after "knowing the breath as coming in or out”. I had initially left out these elaborations on the step because I wished members to find out for themselves. Some members have intuitively went past this stage, however, I realized that many others have still been stuck, but either have not raised their concerns or questions, or they are not even aware that they have this problem.

Before one enters this stage, one must have continuously been using the "knowing nature” to know as the breath coming in and going out, preferably throughout the hours of the day, culminating when one sits on the mat. This is synonymous to the Counting method in the Tian-Tai Sect. The sign that this most basic stage is completed is that the “mind and breath has unified” to a certain extent and that the counting has become troublesome instead of a help to the meditation. As such, counting is abandoned, and the mind has developed strong attention and staying power with the breath.

The second sign is that the spine has suddenly straightened up by its own, as if guided by a force. The third sign is that the thoughts are relatively empty and is it very easy to stay with the breath. There is still the sense of the body. This is also known as the Desire-Realm Samadhi.

When this happens, one is to throw away this method of counting the breath, or simply 'knowing the breath as going in or out’, and to move onto the next step of ‘knowing the breath as long or short', or Following of the breath.

As mentioned many times before, this breath does not simply mean the 'air-movement’, it refers to the sensation of the breath which is not limited to just the respiratory system. This breath refers to the Wind element, Anapanasati is the cultivation of the Wind Element.

There are four clear phases in this step, and it is very important for someone to recognize where they are.

(1) Simply know that the inhalation is long, and that the exhalation is short.

This is a short-cut for you to understand the initial step of reaching the Desire-Realm samadhi. Due to how calm the mind is, the body starts to take in long inhalations and the exhalations are relatively short in comparison. It is not about precisely timing how long the inhalation is, it is knowing in comparison. For example, if you hold in your left hand some ice, and in your right hand a heat-pack, you can immediately know that one is hot and the other is cold without additional logical-processing. Similarly, you can immediately discern the difference without trying to think about it.

(2) When the inhalation is long, realize how subtle the mind is. When the exhalation is short, realize how coarse the mind is.

When the inhalation is long, you know that the breath takes a long time, slowly moving from the nose downwards. At the same time, you may realize that the mind is a lot calmer, subtler.

When the exhalation is short, you know that the breath takes a shorter time and feels more hurried/urgent. The mind at the same time, feels very coarse and rough in comparison, less tranquil and calm.

Slowly, this phenomenon will start to flip around as the mind becomes much, much calmer. One will hence notice the 3rd kind of phase.

(3) When there is both short inhalation or exhalation, there is a subtler mind. When there is a long inhalation or exhalation, there is a rougher mind.

This may become confusing to you - why is it that in the previous phase, a long inhalation gave a subtler mind, but now a shorter inhalation gives a subtler mind?

This phenomenon is interesting, because as the mind calms down further and further, the breath starts to disappear at various places.

For example, when you inhale, you may find that the breath comes in normally but suddenly disappears from the throat onwards. Or when you exhale, you may find that the breath comes up but suddenly vanishes at the chest. As such, the breath seems to be much 'shorter’.

On the other hand, if your mind becomes less calm, then you are able to feel longer breaths both in inhalations or exhalations.

After a while of this, we then move onto the fourth phase.

(4) In subtler-samadhi, the inhalation or exhalation is shorter but feels longer; With coarser-mind, the inhalation or exhalation is longer but feels shorter.

Now this becomes more and more complicated if you have not achieved the above stages. This is a training that is based on going from phase to phase, therefore, if you have not achieved any of the phases above, reading this will not help you much.

What this is saying, is that when your mind is so calm until it enters a subtle form of samadhi, then your breathing lasts for a short time and distance, but even though it stops, there is a feeling of it slowly continuing.

An example of this is that when you inhale, the inhalation may stop at the chest. Even though the movement of air has stopped, there is this sensation of the 'breath' slowly continuing to move down into the abdomen. This is what is meant by a ‘short breath that feels like it is longer’.

On the other hand, if the mind becomes coarser and moves out of samadhi, then even though the distance of the breath from the belly up to the nose is so long, the breath moves very quickly and finishes very quickly. An example of this is that if you breathe out, the feeling is from the belly all the way to the nose, but the time for it to happen is very quick.

This concludes the 4 phases of this step of the 16.

One should also incorporate the contemplation of the last few steps of the 16. When watching the breath, one already knows that it is impermanent and ungraspable. At the same time, the thoughts are impermanent and ungraspable.

When one leaves the samadhi, one also contemplates and reflects on how this samadhi is impermanent and ungraspable. In this way, one will not grasp onto attainments, thinking that they are permanent.

One can easily see how powerful discernment is inside Buddhist meditation. It is not simply the attainment of states, it is the careful investigation (discernment) of the states which ripens wisdom. This is what leads to investigation as the seven factors of enlightenment. Strive on well my friends.
