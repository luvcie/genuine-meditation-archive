- **Sherlockian_Holmes** · *אֲהָבִים Ahaviyahu* · 1 point · 1 year ago

  I believe you answered your own question.

  > Let's say I understand that I should stop all "I am X" identification with my body, thoughts, emotions, etc.

  Do you apply this in your daily life? Mindfulness is to be kept at all times, even off the mat. When you are hungry, do you feel "I am not hungry, but my physical body needs nourishment” dissociating yourself from your body? When you have negative thoughts, do you let them pass not associating with them, simply observing them?

  When you feel tired, or angry, do you not associate with those feelings and know that you are not tired nor angry?

  > How do I "apply" that besides focusing on my meditation object?

  You apply it by using it. You say you understand it - but do you really? If you had truly penetrated into the wisdom of the non-identification, would you not already have achieved the goal of undoing?

  I believe that it is something that is properly understood and applied as you come to experience it. In the beginning, it may be cognized that you are not your body, thoughts and feelings, but you have not properly penetrated into this insight before you experience it as thus. Thus, one must become absorbed into one's divine nature. Then, one easily knows, "not that, not that" or neti, neti.

  That is why it is the whole of the Noble Eightfold Path that brings one to liberation. Not just right concentration! It is about all the aspects! Right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness and yes, right concentration.

  Strive well to become absorbed into your divine nature. A sutra is a finger pointing to the moon, but not the moon itself. The Tao that can be spoken is not the Tao.

  C'est ne pas un pipe.

          As I've understood it, if you check out the Correcting Wrong Concepts in Breath Meditation, you'll see that insight meditation is part of breath meditation (anapanasati).

          > Inhaling, exhaling, mindful of Inconstancy Inhaling, exhaling, mindful of Dispassion Inhaling, exhaling, mindful of Cessation Inhaling, exhaling, focusing on Relinquishment

          In jhana, when you've become absorbed with one-pointed attention on the meditation object, and you're in the first jhana filled with blissfulness and joy, it is then easier to get insight into those concepts because of the strong awareness of the jhanas. Right now, as I mentioned, you might have a cognitive understanding of them, but you've not internalized the ideas or truly comprehended them. I believe this is only something that comes as you go deeper into the absorptions, and investigate for yourself.

          It is like me saying I've read the Diamond Sutra, and understood it, before enlightenment. You may understand parts of it, but it is the degree to which you understand it. The divine nature within you already knows, it's just that we're so entangled with matter (the five skandhas) that we have forgotten.
