# The Purpose of Meditation

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 16 points · 1 month ago

What exactly is the purpose of meditation? If one can express it in one single sentence, it is this:

To tear the aspiring-seeker away from oneself.

Many people try to bring their conditioned selves along with them into the spiritual path, not knowing that what they are doing is expanding this conditioned-self and including ideas of religiosity.

The spiritual-path is no religion - it is a condemnation.

To step on this path, you must be prepared to let everything go. All the habits and ideas of personality that you have accumulated ever since you were a spark that entered the embryo in your mother's womb.

Lao-Tzu, the Taoist sage, said - are you able to return to the state of a newborn baby?

If we look at the Zen halls today, we see 'Buddhists’, 'Taoists’, 'Spiritual people', 'Atheists’, 'businessmen’, ‘lawyers’, ‘doctors’, 'nurses’, 'salesmen’, 'drivers’, so on and on. They have carried their identities with them into the hall. What Zen do they even speak of? The moment they start Zen is not when they step inside the hall, but when they drop everything they have. Renunciation, especially internal renunciation, is so important.

Unfortunately, this habit of self perpetuates due to how strong and multi-factorial it is. Some struggle for years to catch a glimpse of this emptiness.

When one succeeds in prolonging one's meditation, one will feel the expansion of one's consciousness, as if it was infinite, luminous, ethereal and transparent. One no longer feels the dense, corporeal, weighty form of his physical body.

Every time one comes out of meditation, one experiences the disconcerting sensation that one's consciousness is shrinking back into this form of matter, sinking back and sleeping.

One starts to ponder - whether all of the celestial bodies in this universe are merely consciousnesses condensed into matter.

The beginner however, will be in the elementary stages of trying to keep in the present. One finds one's mind flickering back and forth between wandering in the past or worrying about the future. Only in rare dangerous situations can the mind be absolutely intense and in the present.

The beginner must learn that if he allows his thoughts and emotions to flow passively, flowing along with them, impurities will collect - that is to say, habits and tendencies are accumulated as one progresses into old age towards death, which brings huge consequences for oneself in later lives.

If one desires with all of one's being - Satori, Grace - one must have the vigilance like a secret-service agent guarding an important figure. Guarding in this way, one prevents any kind of unwholesome, undesirable and harmful thought/desire from festering inside the mind.

To make things worse, thoughts flow like an endless river. Sometimes, people do not even see how thoughts are separate blocks that rise and fall away, giving way to the next thought. One then naturally fails to see that there is a space between the thoughts.

This space, this void - is a very subtle consciousness. If one remains with this when it happens - not looking for it - one must try to prolong this emptiness until that transparency of being permeates one's entire body.

Likewise, when one is between waking and sleeping, at the point of waking, before the awareness stretches from the head down to the neck, torso, legs and feet, one should prolong this state of being between waking and sleep.

One point worth of note is that the head is the "root" of the body. When waking up, it is the head that feels first, before the rest of the body. When dying, it too, is the last part with sensation and consciousness.

However, this head is full of thoughts, ideas and habits. The aspirant wants to keep himself while embarking on the spiritual path to attain the 'best of both worlds'. That is absolutely foolish.

This chatter in the mind tells the seeker to stop meditating, that the path is too difficult, that it isn't worth it, or that the time is not ripe, etc.

You must, with Manjushri's sword, cut down these thoughts.
