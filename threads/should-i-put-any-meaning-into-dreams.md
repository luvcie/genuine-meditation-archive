# Should I put any meaning into dreams?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sylamatek** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 1 year ago

I very rarely have dreams that I remember. When I do, they have usually been nightmares. The dream I had last night felt like a nightmare at first but. . . now, I am not so sure.

I wrote down some details, but dreams are always hard for me to recall with clarity.

In my dreams, I am on death row. I am unsure why, but I was fairly certain I was innocent of any crime. They kept botching my execution, and I kept pleading to be let go, to not be killed. It feels like this took a long time, where 1 had a discourse with my executioners and they refused to budge.

Eventually, I think I came to accept that I was going to die, and I let go of my body and my attachment to it. I even remember saying out loud in my dream "Just let me become Buddhist again in my next life." After I said this, I wasn't Killed.

I'm wondering if this may have any meaning. It feels relevant to my pursuit in ridding myself of attachments, but it almost seems a bit on the nose, considering dreams are usually random neurons firing off in the brain. My spiritual friend that I confide in said that maybe its about maintaining a Buddhist outlook in the midst of temptation, which I have been struggling with in some aspects of life recently. It seems like I am so close to abstaining from some things, only to suddenly backslide, but I think that means I am close to making true progress.

- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 4 points · 1 year ago

  It depends on the degree of lucidity and awareness you have in your dreams. Most people, untrained in deep mindfulness, tap into the collective thoughtforms of unconscious dreamers. Most are steeped in torpor as they browse the dream space.

  In my past dreams I have relived entire lifetimes, sometimes to wake up and find that this too is yet another dream. Sometimes I woke up interspersed between lives, wondering which lifetime was real. This life, as well as all the other lives you have lived, past or future, are simply part of the turning within samsara. As long as one is not liberated, suffering will continue.

  Perhaps it might have been a memory of a past-life. Or perhaps it might simply be a collective thought within the collective unconscious of man.

  When one is free from the body and engaged in deep meditative states, the thought-waves and heart-waves can flatline. In other words, one is perceived to be clinically brain-dead and dead. Therefore, there is certainly a difference between physically detectable activity of the brain with regards to dreams, and the undetectable which is outside the range of the physical body.

  Therefore, release your notion of a self being contained in the body. Your body has changed radically since you were young, but you still are - Nothing changed within your sense of awareness. You are not this impermanence.

  I'm no expert, but I don't think you should put much stock in your dreams. I've had a few strange dreams myself and according to my understanding of dharma teachings we should view them in the same light as our waking experiences - as illusion-like in nature and to have neither attachment or aversion to them.

  However, if you can use them as motivation to strengthen your spiritual practice, I could see that as an application of skillful means and as a positive.

  About your current situation about abstaining from some things, I'm dealing with that struggle myself.
