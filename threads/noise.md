- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 1 year ago

  Itis hard for a beginner since one has not learnt yet how to retreat from the senses and go into deep states. Sounds do not disturb you, it is the mind that clings onto sensory impressions and disturbs itself. True meditation practice is when one can keep mindfulness and right jhana when one is off the mat, even in the busiest cities with loud sounds.

  Sounds are passing waves. Notice them and take no notice of these external sounds. They are just more visceral in sensation. Just like more visceral images that you see, or visceral smells.

  The Buddha once sat in deep meditation and there was a loud thunderbolt which crashed close to the abode. The Buddha did not hear a single thing.

  Therefore, when one is well established in mindfulness, withdrawn from sensuality, one delves in wholesome mental states and joy and bliss arises from seclusion, rising from it comes tranquility, then equanimity and eventually the voiding of pain and pleasure. Losing the identification to the body and senses, one then transcends the skandha of form.

  This is to highlight that a skilled meditator can easily meditate in such a place. However, if you are beginning, and still honing your skill in withdrawing, then you should try to find a quiet place.

                  One thing that helped me to taper off with masturbation (though I will admit I cannot completely shirk it myself) was to realize how easy it can be at times to separate yourself from the urge if you maintain awareness and be mindful of the sensation as soon as it arises. Does this mode of thinking lead to a positive action? Does the action and thought help you, or others? If not, it is an unskillful action and clinging to sense pleasure.

                  This is the same method you should apply to any form of attachment. When you recognize the thought, try not to force yourself away from the thought, this is simply repression. Instead, recognize the source of that mindset right then and there. Confront it and try to reason to yourself why it is something you do not wish to continue thinking of. In this way, instead of pruning the thought, only for it to bloom again later, you are attempting to remove the train of thought at the root. Plant a seed of compassion in its stead and abandon chasing the sense of pleasure.

                  This is more difficult than it sounds, especially when surrounded by external stimuli that continually try and reinforce lust, or selfishness, or envy, or gossip, et. al.
