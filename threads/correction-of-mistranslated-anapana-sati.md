# Correction of Mistranslated Anapana-sati

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 7 points · 10 months ago

Friends, this has been an issue I have been thinking through and suspecting about the suttas, and am now willing to put it to the front for theoretical discussion.

For years, many English translators have defined ana-pana as:

e ana - breath moving in

e apana - breath moving out

In recent months, I have been more convinced that this translation is not entirely correct. Therefore, I am raising this to the floor in the Sangha so that people can test this out.

As a result of this contradiction between the scriptures and experience, many have become confused. I have realized that while trying to fit my experience in these mistranslated suttas, the entire process has become confusing as well. I have tried to use the phrasing of these suttas, and phrased training as such:

e Following the inhalation and exhalation, not controlling it, letting go of hindrances until one's mind is peaceful.

Now, I wish to correct it as follows:

e Following the pause before the inhalation and the pause before the exhalation, not controlling it, letting go of hindrances until one's mind is peaceful.

There is a problem in breath meditation, which is taken from scriptures. You see, the original language comes from Pali and Sanskrit, but the translations have been skewed horribly and as a result, nearly every single breath meditation instruction has been following suit, with very little progress.

Why is it that Rahula, having learnt Anapana from the Buddha, achieved enlightenment in a faster manner than most people? There are many factors, such as one's own merit and karma, but another problem is that the instruction has not been passed down correctly through scriptures.

Pali and Sanskrit may also not be the original scripts, they have been rewritten, and this has distorted the message over the years.

Friends, ana-pana is the original Pali script for breath meditation. Ana is translated as 'air moving in', while Apana is translated as 'air moving out'. As time has passed from experience, I have realized that these words "ana" and "pana” have a very different meaning from inhalation and exhalation.

Instead, I urge you to practice anapana as the following:

e Ana - during the gap between inhalation and exhalation

e Apana - during the gap between exhalation and inhalation

As many members here, as well as I, have found, the key in breath meditation lies in the moment where no external breath is found. This is also called "Xi".

At this very moment, there is no movement of false-thought. When a thought arises, this restarts the movement of inhalation and exhalation.

Why s0? Suppose there is a certain person is sleeping. In the early stages, one's chest rises and falls, indicating light-sleep, where dreams and false-thoughts are rampant. In short-periods, one's false-thoughts are momentarily cut, and one appears to not breathe very lightly or not at all.

As such, the evidence appears to point that the translations for ana and apana refer to the gaps, rather than the moving segments.

At this moment, this is still a conjecture and up for discussion. However, you may choose to test it out and see if the results are more fruitful than your current practice.

One key to this, is that in the Chinese Agamas, where people translated Sanskrit directly into Chinese, skipping English, there is a term known as "Chu Xi" and "Ru Xi".

Xi, in the chinese language, holds many meanings, but its predominant meaning is that of resting, of non-movement.

Therefore, "Xi" should refer to the resting period between either inhalation and exhalation, or exhalation and inhalation.

For one's training method in the Tian-tai school of Buddhism, as taught by my teacher, it is recommended to count from 1 to 6 on the gap before the inhalation, without being interrupted. It is also possible to do it before exhalation.

It however, also appears that many Chinese meditators who use the Agamas, translate "Xi" as 'out-breath’. My teacher had also critiqued this, saying that it is a mis-translation as well.

If this is accepted in the Sangha and practice-to-theory, then I will amend my past posts. Please feel free to share your experiences and thoughts.

Extra thoughts:

e The mind can be independent of the breath. It is Intention that straddles the horse (mind), so that the mind stays together with the breath.

e Holding in air is not real Xi, because this can actually drive sicknesses. Air that enters the body is clean, upon contact inside, the air becomes dirty and unpurified, hence the secret is in exhalation. In exhalation, one expels impurities. Therefore, one counts on exhalation instead of inhalation.
