# He_of_the_Hairy_Arms's meditation journal

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Journal` · 5 points · 2 years ago

I've just finished meditating and would like to write down the experience. I'll write a brief background at the start of this journal in the near future.

2/7/2015: Did a lot of meditating today. Saturdays are nice in that way. Around noon, I meditated for one hour, trying to simply stay with the breath from the beginning to the end, every inhalation and exhalation. My attention was still choppy. Towards the end, I felt calmer, but honestly I did not achieve any significant concentration.

I knew that this was probably because of stressful events in my life lately, which were in the back of my mind at the start of sitting, so I decided to stand up, stretch, walk around my apartment for ten minutes, and sit down for another session. So I meditated for another hour. This time it was a bit easier. Before I started, I reminded myself to follow each breath all the way, every inhalation and exhalation. Very quickly I knew I was doing a better job. I wasn't thinking of my breath as some part of a process towards jhana. I was just breathing. At a certain point I felt that I had very easy access to the sensation of my breath and could hold my attention almost effortlessly. I briefly laughed to myself that this was why they called it access concentration--easy access to concentration. I stayed there for a while and started to get uncomfortable from sitting, and my attention got unruly again.

Just now I sat for another hour. I didn't achieve a consistent access concentration this time, but instead I experienced short stretches of good concentration. Overall it was less consistent. However I felt a very distinct pulse at one point. It felt a bit like someone sending an electric shock from deep inside my brain and through my body, causing tingles and warmth. To use an analogy, if jhana is full immersion, then this felt like sticking my big toe in. Then towards the end of the session I began to get lethargic. I think I thought to myself, "I've already done a good job," and my effort faded. Still, I think today was good. There's a subtle difference between concentration on each moment of the breath, and concentration on the breath interrupted frequently by thoughts of jhana. I think I was reminded of that difference today.

2/8/2015: Just did an hour-long morning session. Concentration was weak, but then again, I haven't eaten or drank coffee yet today (I'm quite dependent on coffee.) Grip on the breath was soft and I got interrupted a lot by memories, half-formed thoughts, etc. Not the last session of the day, though.

2/10/2015: Yesterday and today I had sessions that felt a lot better. Just now, I finished an hour-long session in which at the beginning I was thinking much less of jhana and concentration than I have been recently. I was simply feeling the sensations, perhaps unintentionally visualizing them a bit. It seems that the irony of all this talk of how to get to jhana is that talk must go out the window in order to achieve it. Any conception of "how well I'm doing” or "how close I am" has no place in the process. I recognized this in my session, and I think this caused me to think about how to keep doing a better job. Of course this interrupted the process, but even still, I was enjoying it and the inside of my head felt bright. No strange jolts or tingles or other signs of jhana, though--just a steadily pleasurable light feeling. Every now and then I noticed I was doing a "good job" and thought to myself, "keep doing this and you'll end up you-know-where." It's difficult not to have these thoughts.

At one point my breath got very, very faint, and I ended up controlling it in an attempt to keep feeling it. I wasn't experiencing any discernible nimitta at that moment, either, so that was odd.

2/14/2015: The last few days were interesting. I never was able to meditate at the ideal time, however I never failed to meditate for at least forty-five minutes each day. I felt myself achieving brief moments of good concentration--better than in previous weeks--but through a haze that I was always fighting. Yesterday I meditated in my university library, on the normally quiet third floor. In the middle of my session an electrician set up a steel ladder and removed one of the tiles on the ceiling to do some sort of repair. Still, I achieved moments of good concentration, but it struck me how easy it is to start off concentrating and end up thinking. The shift is so subtle and insidious. I also found that I was using my recent improvement in concentration as fuel for thinking. I would try to conceptualize what I had done before and whether I was doing it again. Even that, I realized, was thinking. It frustrated me a bit to realize this.

Just now, I meditated for an hour and achieved access concentration. Suddenly it was quite easy to hold my attention on the breath. Around this time in the process is when my thoughts usually become uncontrollable. I begin to anticipate and hope for jhana. It occurred to me that I'm maybe a bit afraid of continuing to concentrate on the breath, because of what my mind might do to me if I "fail" to achieve jhana anyway. I realize that my mind often punishes me and I seek to avoid its punishment as a result. It's for the same reason that I avoid lots of things in life, probably--it's not that "failure” is so bad, it's that I'm afraid I'll use the failure to make more global and personal beliefs out of the failure. I'm afraid I'll become convinced it means something it doesn't mean; afraid of my mind distorting the experience and having to engage in a losing battle with it.

2/15/2015: My meditations today (one hour-long session and one half-hour session) felt very struggle-free and focused. I was close to access concentration on my first try, and on my second I felt yet another brief, but exhilarating jolt, which basically felt like jhana but only for the quickest moment. It felt like something opening up in my mind--I have a hard time explaining it any other way. It felt as if some part of my brain that desperately wants stimulation, received stimulation, if only ever so briefly. It was a kind of pleasantly cold feeling, although it had nothing to do with temperature; the sensation was totally mental. In the past when I experienced jhana, it felt like a cold (but immensely pleasurable) shiver running from my forebrain to my brainstem to my spine. This felt like a zap from the forebrain to the brainstem. (Of course I have no idea what my brain was actually doing, but it felt that way.)

2/20/2015: I've been very busy, so it's been hard to update here, but I've managed one hour a day of meditation at the very least. What seems to have suffered a bit is how much I was able to read and think about meditation over the

past couple of days. I had to pause my reading of the Bhagavad Gita and limit my perusal of reddit, and perhaps that caused me to get a bit too bound up in my daily grind. In any event, my meditations got a bit less focused and felt more like work. Or I would start to fall asleep, which was a bit frustrating. One thing I've noticed is that when this happens it takes me a full Saturday morning of meditating possibly two or three hours to let go, both mentally and physically, and start using my mind the right way again. I hope to do that tomorrow.

This evening I tried an experiment and paid close attention to how much I craved to inhale or exhale. It was a strange sensation, almost as if I was controlling the breath, but since I was just following what my body wanted, it was my body that was in charge. I was only controlling my breath in the sense that I would "decide" to breathe only according to my body's feelings, in which case it's perhaps debatable what is doing the controlling. I felt very at peace doing this for fifteen minutes on my zafu, as a kind of warm up session, but started to feel tired and bored at the end of an hour on my chair. (Which I use to meditate for longer.) Not much pitisukha that time, but I'm not really sure why. Maybe just fatigue or discomfort, but in general I wish wish it were easier for me to identify my mistakes.

2/28/2015: Over the past several days, my meditations have been just so-so. I've wondered previously in these entries if my so-so meditations, or weak focus, is a result of being flooded with preoccupations relating to my graduate studies and other everyday stresses. It seems to me that when I have less time to read for inspiration, my focus suffers. This is just one possible explanation for why I have this pattern of "getting it" in meditation, coming closer to jhana and feeling tranquil and happy after sessions, and "losing it" at other times--feeling like I just couldn’t get a handle on my concentration and that my mind was doing too much the entire time.

It is amazing how different it is to "Get it" and "not get it." For example, a few days ago, I was in a terrible mood, without even exactly knowing why, while sitting in a waiting room before an appointment. I took about ten minutes to meditate and ended up paying attention to each breath effortlessly, as it naturally happened, and my mood was totally changed after those ten minutes. It was so much different from what I do when I sit at home, timing myself for an hour, with the hope of achieving jhana, and the concern of doing meditation the correct way. Yet it's also amazing how automatic and difficult to control is the reflex to try. So much of my meditation is just forced effort and controlling and trying. Much of what I think is switching back and forth between the "Correct” and "incorrect” way is simply moving from one wrong way to another--"wrong" meaning an attempt to control the breath instead of letting it happen and watching it. And it is so, so easy to slip back into trying to control from having let go.

Yet here's the thing: I know from my reading that jhana is very important to spiritual development. How do I not think about it, or wonder if I'm practicing in a way that will lead me to it, when I sit to meditate? The reason that I find it difficult to let go is that I fear it will not reap results. Just like people who close their eyes to go to sleep are "letting go,"” and aren't meditating, so too will I not be doing anything to progress in meditation--or so I fear. How to reconcile this paradox?
