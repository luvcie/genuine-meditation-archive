# Some Tips on Anapana

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 9 points · 2015-11-05

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3rl7vi/some_tips_on_anapana/>

Breath meditation is one of the most powerful methods to go quickly into jhana, along with the body mindfulness techniques.

I just thought to create a thread to settle all the FAQs:

Anapanasati is sati of anapana = Mindfulness (sati) of Contraction-Expansion (ana-pana). Mindfulness is not straining, but settling into stillness, so that attention on things that are not still, such as the breath, can be observed.

Therefore, it is not about concentrating on your nose. Neither is it about concentrating on every single detail of the breath such that you become overwhelmed. It is about ease, letting go, and in that process, things naturally stand out against the stillness and you are aware of them.

And it is in this letting go that you must feel open to blissfulness and joy. The original nature is full of bliss, and going into stillness, you allow it to rise - You become more sensitive to it. Your channels open.

The stiller you get, the more your body and mind follows. It is like letting a stirred cup of water spin on its own, without stirring anymore. You then watch it naturally fade in momentum, swirling until it no longer moves. That is when the body and mind has become still and free of agitation.

The breath is the one key that brings you from the contraction-expansion movement into stillness of the body.

After which, the thoughts, which are linked to the breath as well, cease to give a subtle state of emptiness. You do not need to force linkage between thought and breath - simply knowing that the breath is going in, or going out - That is perfectly fine.

From this cessation bursts forth yang chi.

There is no "individual" awareness watching these things. There is only one awareness (the moon in the sky) that is reflected in the myriads of beings (millions of lakes reflecting the moon).

No need to count. Just be still and the breath stands out from the stillness immediately. Just be aware of the length of inhalation and exhalation.

No conscious control. This is the fastest way, and you do not interfere with the natural movement of the breath. The more you control it, the more stirring you cause, the longer it will settle.

Tightness in body? Observe it, end the added agitation, let it dissolve with the ebb-and-flow of the breath.

Tip of nose is good for beginners, but it is not concentrating at your nose. If you are more advanced, go for middle of nose, bridge of nose or where the third-eye is.

It is perfectly natural. If you are straining, it is still not right. If you are counting, you are on training wheels and it is time to take it off. It is something even a child can do. Become like a curious child who wants to remember what one was when one was born. Like a child who wishes to explore one's own mind and body. No effort. Let go.

It is very, very simple. And all it requires is training. If one does it right, it will not take weeks or months to go into first jhana. The Buddha taught Rahula the method and on in a short period, he accomplished the four jhanas.

Intensity, intent and the willingness to know: "Where am I from? What did I forget?" or "Who am I?" All these are important.
