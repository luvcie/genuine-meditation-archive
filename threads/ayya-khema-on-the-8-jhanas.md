    - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 2 points · 2 years ago

      Yes, there are types of meditation that do not lead to Jhana. This in particular, are meditation practices that shirk away from the joyfulness of jhana. As expressed by the Buddha after his decade of erroneous practice, it was the fact that he suppressed enjoyment of the joyfulness birthed not-of-the-flesh that he failed to attain the rupa-jhanas.

      There are practices where you can indeed attain "siddhis". But these are not necessarily the real path of the dharma. In old India, there were many schools that claimed to do out-of-body samadhi, or produce various siddhis. However, none of them were the right path.

      Let me first say this: There are literally thousands of types of samadhis. It does not mean that if one is in a deep state of mind, that one has attained the fruit. There are many paths to go wrong.

      It is crucial to not treat jhana as simply something that one can do without. Jhana IS part of the Noble eightfold path.

      However, if you do experience jhana, but do not pay attention to it, it is not wrong. Jhana should be a passing road-sign to tell that you have been doing correct meditative work.
