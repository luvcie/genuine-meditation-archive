# My Experience Journal

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **RandomShortStory** · *(OCR)* · `Journal` · 2 points · 1 year ago

Hi everyone,

I'm not sure how best to start my journal so I will just begin. First it's a privilege to be allowed to participate and have access to this information so thank you. I felt the desire to create a journal to keep track of my experiences and listen to the guidance that anyone would be willing to provide along the journey.

I would like to say that I would update this often, but I'm not sure how realistic that statement is. It could be extremely frequently, it could be sporadic, but I think it would be most safe to say that this will be updated every time at the appropriate time.

So let me begin...

I began meditating consistently around a year ago. I found it extremely easy to “calm” my mind even though I tend to walk around daily life not very mindful or present as to what's going on. I always feel during the day, especially when interacting with other people, that I am not fully there or even listening to them, I am completely absorbed in myself. This doesn't bother me persay, but when I have those moments of clarity that come after a meditative experience, it's very analogous to cleaning a dirty car windshield and saying "Wow! This is what it's supposed to be like?"

I never was taught how to meditate so I "intuitively" created my own method. I would follow my breath and focus all of my attention inside my head, and eventually I found my breath would become extremely faint and shallow (moreso than it naturally is because I have an extremely faint and shallow breath 24/7 to begin with).

I am always aware of my body vibrating constantly. I feel tingling, vibrations, all the time. At first I thought this maybe a problem with my nerves? I realized over time however that this just meant I was calm and actually feeling my body for what it is.

My meditation experiences at this time consisted of my attention jumping back and forth between observing the vibrations of my body (which could become extremely tremendous when focusing on it) and focusing in my head in which I would see what appeared to be a bright light, or swirling patterns, never any color but maybe sometimes the most mildest of purple hue, although the color may be just me misremembering or a deluded image at the time.

The vibrations are 100% real.

To continue on I kept meditating around this time and I found I particularly enjoyed meditating in my bed underneath the sheets. I found I could focus on the sensation of vibrations EXTREMELY easily while in bed and at certain times the vibrations would become so strong the bed would start (I perceived it to be) shaking extremely violently.

The seminal moment happened for me when during one of these bed meditations I found my breath just completely stopped. ceased. and all of a sudden, I found myself ABOVE my body (again all through perception) staring directly at the wall in front of me (not the ceiling, but the front wall). It was as if I was floating in the middle of my room staring at the front of my wall. I have a very distinguishable poster on this wall and I remember seeing it in the most bright of HD. 20/20 complete crystal clear vision, better than any normal eyesight I could have ever seen it with. Everything had a very purpleish hue to it. At this point I felt a feeling of absolute panic, fear, terror, anxiety, you name it, i got SCARED, and I immediately came back to my body and got out of bed, almost "woke up”, and was trying to process what just happened to me!

That experience made me stop meditating for quite a while because, simply put, I got spooked.

Now I'm not sure whether that was a jhana, samadhi, whatever, but maybe someone here can illuminate? What I do know for a fact is that I'm ready to go back and continue meditating and give it a shot because I feel comfortable knowing that I now have a venue to express my experiences and get guidance and feedback. This is a wonderful, wonderful, thing and I am extremely lucky/blessed to have this opportunity.

The final thing I note is that people talk about the benefits of meditation, extreme bliss/joy, etc. I haven't really experienced that. Sure when I'm meditating I can get very calm and sometimes even out of nowhere feel uplifted and get a smile on my face, but these effects are often short lived and I've never had an experience (besides the one I mentioned above) where I said "WOAH. Something just happened here."

So thank you guys for listening to my "brain dump.” I spilled the contents of my mind and experience so you can know exactly where I am, especially since you all have more experience than me.

I hope someone can point me in the right direction for my continued growth?

I will commit to updating this to share my experiences, comments, etc. as appropriate based on the progress.

Thanks again and I'm excited to see where this goes!

  Personally, I have not had the experience you're talking about, but it sounds like an out of body experience. It's not a Jhana or Samadhi.

  As far as feeling bliss or even a state of certain degree of happiness beyond the regular state of mind off of the cushion, it comes after a while (few months at least) of diligent practice. Depending on what degree of joy you're expecting off the cushion, it can take anywhere from 30 days to 30 years or 300 lifetimes. It's all dependent on your practice, your state of mind. Buddha, after all is not a person; it's a state of mind.

  Having said that, it is very possible to start feeling and noticing the change in our mind after about 30 days of daily sitting practice. The benefits are greater and more noticeable when you practice off the cushion. Meditation is working with our mind. It is the process of becoming familiar with our mind. That's all. So, the more we get to know it, the better friends we can become with it. Ultimately, with proper practice, we realize the true nature of our mind, and that really is what we're trying to do here: recognize the true nature of our mind, of reality. In order to get there, we study, contemplate, and practice on and off the cushion. We constantly be aware of the movements of our mind-as much as possible. What is the mind doing, thinking, feeling? This is also known as mindfulness of mind.

  Stay on the path, keep moving on. :-)

  Vibrations are a sign of deep absorption. Here, we call them tactile-like jhana nimittas, meaning signs of deep absorption.

  It is very normal for one to leave the body if you reach a deep enough absorption. This is what I could call incomplete samadhi. However, as you mentioned, you were fearful and not equanimous. The power of equanimity only comes from a strong foundation of developing jhana before leaving the body. One who has true samadhi and leaves the body is one who is able to be absolutely equanimous, full of bliss and joy - With a certain stability that allows one to go as far as the Brahma realms and not merely the lower realms.

  > I hope someone can point me in the right direction for my continued growth?

  Developing jhana will be extremely beneficial for you. The first step is always to move away from unwholesome states, such as fear, worry and anxiety - this is normally handled by what we call discipline, or sila - through right speech, actions and thought. This in turns allows withdrawal from the external senses, allowing you to then experience bliss and joy within.

  This all happens with a certain opening of the heart and vulnerability. One must be able to be sensitive to that blissfulness and joy, and this can be developed through goodwill and compassion towards others.
