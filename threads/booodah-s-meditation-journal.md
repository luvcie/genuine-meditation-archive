3/6 2015 I had a very interesting session. I were very tired coming home from studying all day and started out with 10 minutes spinal breathing as this always impact my sessions in a positive way.

Then I sat my timer for one hour and started to focus on my breath.

It wasn't long until my body felt like a soft fluffy cotton ball, a feeling I have felt earlier - but not this strong. My eyelids were flickering a lot and felt tense, which they usually do when I have drinked coffee during the day. I also experienced unusual lot of light under my eyelids, purple and white mainly. My focus were effected by the flickering and all the light and I had trouble maintaining it. I caught myself thinking about the annoying flickering and the marvelous lights more than on my breath. It felt like my body was spinning in a weird way on and off. And I started to smile very hard, so much that the muscles in my cheeks quivered.

I sometimes experience a purple ball of light that comes from the area of my nose and floats away into the distance, over and over again. This session it happened, but at one point it stayed infront of me and "swirled" around making weird moves and just seemed to play around. At the same time it was growing bigger and bigger. I didn't know what to focus on because my breath were very shallow and my eyes were focusing on the purple ball swirling around, sparkling and growing bigger. But at the same time I had trouble knowing what to focus on, my mind were clear and I

were very aware.

During the whole session I were filled with satisfaction.

Then my timer rang and I got up, feeling happy and super energized.

          Please look at this and this for more elaboration on the jhana traits, as well as samadhi.

          The first jhana might not be immediately obvious, if you have always been experiencing bliss while having a wholesome mind-state. It usually starts off with a pleasant satisfaction, as if one was enjoying meditation (this is known as piti). One then has sukha, which means joy - in the form of rising tingling pleasure as well as other jhana-nimitta such as the things you have mentioned. Lastly, these two will not arise if one does not have sustained and applied concentration (vitakka - applied; vicara - sustained; ekaggatha - one-pointedness).

          When one moves to the second jhana, the need to sustain attention is removed. sukha, piti and ekaggatha continues, but a "tranquil” quality emerges (passaddhi).

          Moving to third jhana, one adds on more factor: Equanimity, also known as upekkha. You still retain sukha, piti and ekaggatha.

          In fourth jhana, one drops sukha, experiencing no-pleasure-and-no-pain. This is associated with no physical sense contact. Although you may recognize it, you feel detached to the physical sensations and do not interpret them. Upekkha, piti and ekaggatha continues.

          Moving beyond fourth, one experiences vigor and vitality - Also known by yoga texts as kundalini. Please note that the word "kundalini" does not appear in the Buddhist texts, but is called "virya", meaning virtuous energy. At this point, sensory stimuli drops off completely as one moves into the mind-generated body.

      Firstly, determine that the breath stopping is not because of a sluggish mind-state. If it is because the brain is sluggish and hence causing a state of oxygen deprivation, then perhaps you can do pranayama to help open up the airways.

      There is no trouble with the breath becoming shallow if your mind is clear, with your attention sustained and applied, together with bliss and joy arising. There is a certain point where the breath stops all together. When the breath stops, the external respiration stops, but here is when the internal energy channels awaken. This is known as primordial chi (or precursor to kundalini). You might feel pulsing (not of blood) from the perineum, etc, with heat/blissfulness of the whole body.

      For now, I suggest that you do the pranayama suggested before every meditation session, in attempt to clear up your airways and open up energetic channels of the body.

      Lying down meditation is certainly possible, which gives a slightly different experience of meditation, often resulting in out-of-body experiences or visions. If you are able to do so without a sluggish/stupor-disturbed mind, then it is perfectly okay. Mixing both sitting and lying-down meditation is good practice.

      > I've had some trouble recently with my breath becoming super shallow, sometimes it even stops, and I have a hard time knowing how to handle the situation.

      The trick is to know when to switch the focus from the breath to the jhana-nimitta (signs of jhana) that arise. If it stops, and you know that you are in jhana, then simply watch the jhana-nimitta arise and ride the flow into deeper levels of jhana. You can switch away from the breath to the factors, such as the bliss.
