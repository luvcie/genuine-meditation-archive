  Yes, suppressing thought puts you at the level of insentiency. Mastering thought, however, brings you to a higher level of sentience. This means choosing what thought you want and entering silence whenever you wish, allowing higher intelligence and intuition to occur spontaneously.

  To the average person, the mind continuously clings from thought to thought. This is done subconsciously and often, unknowingly. Sub-personalities arise, archetypes arise, one switches from one role to another as if one was mad. Therefore, for one to truly master the mind, one must learn how to quieten the mind first.

  Thought is recycled. If a thought rises and falls away, how can it be you? The problem arises when one identifies oneself as the thought process, especially when this thought process has been running on, untamed. Pain comes from the lack of mastery.

  True samadhi is not stillness, but in fact, a great movement.

- **Emdaer** · *净力 Jing Li* · 2 points · 2 years ago

  Thoughts happen. They arise, change and fall away. Thoughts also make emotions appear in the body. Constant mindfulness grants you the choice to act in wholesome ways in every moment. If you are not aware of this while a thought is happening, you may react in unwholesome ways to a thought. This may affect your mood in negative ways, and therefore possibly others mood. Thoughts while unattended can make you say things or act in ways that are damaging to your relationships or yourself. Let me give you an example from my own life. I would often scroll down my Facebook feed, not mindful of thoughts. I might see some picture or event that make a thought like ‘they have fun, and I am sitting here alone' arise. Or I would look at pictures of a crush I had and think 'she is sweet, but she doesn't like me in the way I want her to'. These thoughts, unattended would cascade into similar thoughts, most of them sending emotions such as loneliness or jealousy into my body. If the emotions arent attended too, this would probably make me act in unwholesome ways that the ego thinks will compensate for negative emotions, such as overeating, watching TV, play video games, activities not conducive to mindfulness and clarity.

  Being mindful of their arisal and change, thoughts and emotion pass away much faster, and leave you grounded with the choice of how you want to act.

  In meditation, thoughts are a hindrance meaning something that draws your focus away from your object. What you should work on is learning to be mindful of the arisal of thoughts. This will give you the choice to instantly return to your object, instead of mindlessly fueling the thought stream. A good technique is to use a meditation word such as bud-dho (meaning awake). For inhale think 'bud’ for exhale thinking 'dho'. With practice and intensity you can be mindful of this for every breath, and you will most likely be mindful of a thought arising too.

  Because thoughts aren't our true nature. Paramananda covers this quite well in his explanation of the five skandhas.

  If you observe your thoughts, why would this make you a mindless zombie? Is it not that when we simply have thoughts, and react, without the gap between stimuli and reaction, we are no better than mindless zombies?

  Meditation allows you to step in between the stimuli and response, and really take a look at the thought as you become conscious/mindful of it before it actually instigates the development of more thoughts of a particular nature in a particular direction. However, as I understand it, thoughts aren't meant to be forcefully reduced, it is more that when you sit still, and you simply watch the thoughts, as one would watch clouds on a sky, after a while, the clouds disappear, and the clear blue sky underneath becomes more visible.

  As we go deeper, and let go more, more is revealed to us.
