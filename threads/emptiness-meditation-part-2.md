# Emptiness Meditation (Part 2)

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 10 points · 2 months ago

Dear friends, this is a continuation of the first thread on emptiness meditation.

True Non-Dwelling

Now friend, what is emptiness? Does this emptiness refer to a vacuum? Does this emptiness refer to an absence of something? Does this emptiness mean blackness? Does this emptiness mean light?

Emptiness is actually just this: Things are empty of self.

Do cars exist? Does the chair you sit on exist? Does the table exist? Does the universe exist? Does your body exist? Does the environment around you exist? Does the building, society, country, oceans, rivers - all exist? Yes, they indeed exist.

But why does the Dharma say - what is said to not exist, nor not-exist? This is pointing to the illusion of the self. The Self does not exist, but yet it tries to manifest itself in myriads of ways. Wherever it enters, we say Mara has entered.

Now I wish to direct you towards this very important point right now. What is called "samadhi” is in fact not the true Ch'an. Is it valuable? Absolutely. But is samadhi the true Ch'an? It is not.

Let me give you a very, very simple example to illustrate what I mean.

Many cultivators shut their eyes and lock themselves away from the world. They switch the presence of light to absence of light, they switch the movement of body to stillness, and they sit in absolute absence. If one enters a state, it will be that of samadhi.

When one stands up from the meditation cushion, the samadhi state becomes lost - Why? Because one had not practiced with presence! When one has one's eyes open, one's room filled with loud sounds, one's floor vibrating from a neighbour’s subwoofer, one's nose smelling the scent of barbequed food from the next-door BBQ-pit, or one's mind filled with torrents of agitation ---

At this point one becomes swirled around in conditions. This is what the Ch'an Master Honazhi referred to as:

> ...to simply sit in stillness, and silently investigate; deep down there is a state one reaches where externally one is ; no longer swirled about by causes and conditions.

The proper practice is to open one's eyes, let them be half-open, without strain. One is not gazing at any object or trying to discriminate or identify any object. One is not engaging in thoughts about what one sees. Simply gazing, one accepts the presence of light into the eye-consciousness.

Whether one sees white or black, does it really matter? In between white and black, one sees the 7 colours. If one can only meditate when the eye is presented with blackness, then how can this be said to be Ch'an?

In this way, one can easily see - that when one is in everyday life, life itself becomes the meditation. Whatever one sees - be it the sight of ill-will, the sight of death, the sight of all sorts of things - one will be unmoved.

The typical person trains in absence of conditions - But when these conditions arise in their presence, they startle the mystic! The mystic is caught off-guard, and this becomes another step for development of equanimity.

Likewise for ear, tongue, nose, body and thoughts. However, I want to remind everyone that it is not about seeking outwards, but allowing things to be as natural as they are - and not grasping onto it in anyway.

This first point, therefore, is on the difference on inner-samadhi and the Chan samadhi. Inner-samadhi focuses on absolute silence and absence, voiding conditions and escaping, whereas the Chan samadhi does not discriminate between absence and presence, and thus does not escape, suppress or cling. This is therefore called non-dwelling.

Bodhicitta

The second point is that of bodhicitta. Now what exactly is this, and why is there a need for bodhicitta in emptiness meditation?

When the typical cultivator looks out into the world, one thinks oneself as separate from the world. What one sees outside the body, is external - what one experiences inside the body-area, is internal.

However, they do not realize that this separation is again an action, a volition of self! When one looks out into the world and sees pain, demons, corruption, misery, anger, and so on, this is because one is projecting one's own discriminatory processes into the world.

If an enlightened master is asked whether a murderer is a Bodhisattva - one will certainly say, "Yes".

Now this is shocking to so many people, because people are clinging onto their notions of self. They believe religious-notions to be part of their identity, and immediately the self rejects anything that is against that identity.

To the master, the master only sees Bodhisattvas. The master does not see him-/herself liberating other beings, the master acts spontaneously, and sees that it is the dharma itself allowing beings to save themselves. Therefore, while there is a being that is said to be liberated, there is no being that is liberated!

To the master, the master only sees the Pure-Land. The Pure Land is not elsewhere. There is a Pure Land on the earth, a Pure Land in heaven, a Pure Land in a different galaxy - wherever the sage steps, that is a Pure Land! Why? Again, it is the difference of one's mind - to the enlightened being, there is no discrimination caused by any notion of self, ego, personality, or being.

Therefore, when one shines forth bodhicitta, the light of compassion, it is as if one is a candle, allowing other candles to light up, without depleting itself. By lighting other candles, suddenly the room becomes especially bright. It is a very similar principle. What one sees in others, certainly speaks a lot about how the notion of self in one. When a genuine Chan practitioner opens one's bodhicitta, one will strive to see everyone as Bodhisattvas.

If a Bodhisattva enters a pirate ship, he or she becomes a pirate. The Bodhisattva then converts all of the pirates with expedient means.

No Samadhi?

Ch'an practice, non-dwelling - it is not one-pointed absorption in the sense of excluding everything around, achieving it only when one is in total absence of conditions. However, while one says that Ch'an is not samadhi, jhanas do arise.

The Buddha is always in samadhi, whether teaching, washing his feet, walking, begging for alms, talking, and so on. One may simply see him as a dirty beggar and pass him by completely if one has impure perception. One may not even know a Buddha has passed by them!

Now the Chan-samadhi, jhana, that we speak of, is when the mind is not stirred by conditions - pleasant or painful, good or bad, positive or negative.

The mind is luminous and ever-bright, tranquil and equanimous. Even though one is virtuous, one does not even perceive merit-making. Moving dynamically, not a trace is left.

How is it that people become enlightened just hearing a discourse from the Buddha? The answer to this is that it is like a snowball that vaporises instantly - the burden of the 'self' is suddenly dropped.

All Methods

The Buddha taught anapanasati (in/out-sati), kayagatasati (body-sati), metta, Pure-Land, non-dwelling, and so on - which is right? There are in fact 84,000 Dharma Doors that the Buddha has taught. Which should one use?

What matters a huge lot is one's Right View. When one corrects one's view, no matter what one practices, one will move towards the right goal. Therefore, set right your view.

Each method is like medicine - for each sickness, one uses one type of medicine. Using a cough medicine to cure a diarrhoea may not work very well.

For example, when one examines the Dharmatrata Dhyana Shastra, the in-breath is used to energize one's sluggish mind. The out-breath is used to calm down one's agitated mind. There are countless remedies out there, but one should understand that one must develop one's own wisdom to find out what works best for you.

If you do not yet have the wisdom, simply sit in this emptiness meditation as described, with renunciation and bodhicitta. When one masters one dharma-door, all the other dharma-doors will be instantly known.

Thus concludes this 2 part series of Emptiness Meditation. My friends, I hope this was useful for you. Strive well for enlightenment.

  I am in no way competent to respond but what has helped me in approaching emptiness is the daily practice of body contemplation. I contemplate the body parts, their permanence, their dependence on causes and conditions (food, water, oxygen). I ask ,which of these parts is pure, permanent, which of them is a self, part of a self, which of these parts am I, <name>, or is part of me? Then leave a pause after these to reflect. Then I contemplate the stages of decay of this body and from time to time rest the mind in that knowing. Especially useful at the moment seems thinking about how after all the soft tissue has decayed and the bones seperate each other and then wither away. Until at one point there is nothing left anymore of what one could call a bone. I then reflect on this body and think: »This body which seems so solid, real and permanent is bound for complete disintegration, destruction, bound to dissolve." To conclude I contemplate the aggregates and how they too are dependent upon causes (as in the case of the aggregates - on each other). The mental aggregates depend on the rupa. Without consciousness there can be no contact, without contact no feeling or perception can arise. Thus no impulse. I contemplate how the fading away of one leads to the fading away of that for which it is the cause. How can there be sense contact without a body? How impulse without feeling? And how can something be a self that is so volatile, arising and passing away so frequently. So dependent upon causes and conditions?

  This contemplation has been very helpful to me.
