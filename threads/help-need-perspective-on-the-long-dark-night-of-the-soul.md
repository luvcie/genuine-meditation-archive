# Help? Need perspective on "the long, dark night of the soul".

**r/Meditation** · by **gcmorar21** · 10 points

<https://www.reddit.com/r/Meditation/comments/3a69nh/>

> **[post was deleted on reddit]**

I've been meditating for about two or three years. More consistently recently this past seven or eight months (five-ish days a week, ~15-30 minutes a session). I alternate between mindfulness meditation and Taoist meditation. Fairly similar just slightly different flavors.

I've come to a stumbling block: the long dark night of the soul. Full disclosure: nonbeliever, the soul is just the most useful concept for describing this. I feel wither. It's akin to depression but tastes a little different. From what I can tell, it seems to be fairly common among mediators.

But it is bad. Well, it's intense. It's pervasive and consuming. It's dark. This happened lightly before but I stopped meditating for purpose and it faded away. I used to sit to "get enlightenment", now I'm not sure what that even means. I used to sit to "gain insight". But when you go looking, it's eludes you.

Now I'm in a kind of dark mental patch. Meditation seems to soothe it and also power it. When I sit, it settles. When I am walking around it grows.

Any insight into what this is and how to approach it constructively?

Edit
I also start each session with the simple Metta practice from Mindfulness in Plain English. If insight is a knife that can cut things out of you, Metta us the bandaid and salve to heal you. You have to pack the cuts so they heal.

  The dark night of the soul can only be filled in with wholesome states, the 4
  brahmaviharas. However, before this can happen, you must investigate the unwholesome states.

  Read about the unwholesome states through
  the five hindrances of meditation,
  the five lower fetters, the five upper fetters
  and the sixteen defilements.

  The unwholesome states described above are the Buddhist's dark night of the soul. You should not forget that the Buddha cultivated in asceticism before realizing the middle path. In this ascetic stage, he had also described the dark night to a certain degree, talking about "pain" when he shut off the "wind entering his nose, ears, etc...".

  Anybody who does not understand what a dark night is, might either be a practitioner who has yet to face their own subconscious psyches or a practitioner with fewer of these unwholesome states as a result of having done meritorious/virtuous deeds of the past.

  thread that I have posted
  in the past about this, it may help you.

          A lot of people become afraid of the dark night and repel themselves away from it. However, they do not realize that the dark night is facing our own neuroses. They are inner demons of our own ignorance.

          Meditation is a rehabilitation process that reveals these things to you. However, the idea is to recover from it, not indulge in them. It is not to repress them either, but to face them with courage and turn them for the better, saturating oneself with the wholesome states (brahma viharas).

          As stated by many others, loving-kindness is something that can arise as a result of samatha practice. However, it still requires a mastery of equanimity to be able to navigate the deeper phases of this "dark night". In fact, all four factors have to be strong.

          It is in fact, a good sign that you have faced this. Do not fear it, embrace it, for it is of your own karma. However dark it will be, nothing can beat the light of enlightenment.

- **lxl** · 2 points · 2 years ago

  First step is making sure this is the dark night phenomenon, not another psychological issue. And in reality there is no good way of doing that and these things are convoluted.

  My approach is brute force it. Attack the problem with as much strength, resolve and a variety of tools as possible.

  From the meditation perspective you are right in doing metta. Just sitting with the unpleasantness is also the obvious but difficult to follow advice. I wouldn't make concentration a priority. What worked for me was complete surrender and open and accepting awareness.

  Things like exercise, doing things you enjoy, being in good company and taking it easy in general goes a long way in addition to things you do on the cushion.

  Basically get support whichever way you can. Writing on these forums is one way of doing that since there are others who've been through similar things.

  I also believe a lot in professional help and support. They may not be open to things like "dark night of the soul", but they'll understand fear, depression etc.
