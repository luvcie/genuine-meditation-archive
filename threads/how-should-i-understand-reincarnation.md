# How should I understand reincarnation?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 2 years ago

I would love some discussion on reincarnation, specifically how literally we should interpret the claim that we are reborn many times.

How do we know this is true? I ask with an open mind and out of curiosity. Some zen practitioners claim that freedom from the cycle of rebirths is a figurative way of saying freedom from identification with thoughts and feelings, which come and go. Yet the Tibetan tradition seems to understand it more literally as the next physical manifestation of the same consciousness. (And this introduces the question of "what is getting reincarnated, if not a soul?")

In Paramananda's welcome to the virtual sangha, he states that "...1 year, 5 years, 10 years or even 50 years...is insignificant before the face of the numerous lifetimes you have gone through in this cyclical nature of suffering.” If I were deeply convinced of this instead of taking someone’s word for it, then many of my current doubts and attachments would vanish, and I would possibly have the faith to commit to enlightenment in this lifetime. My cravings for material wealth and romantic love--not lust, but Love with a capital "L"--would no longer matter, because I would know that this life isn't purely material and that whatever I crave, I have attained before and lost and craved again.

Until now, I've viewed meditation and spiritual practice through a very scientific lens. I've trusted its ability to transform people and bring them lasting happiness, but I've also wondered if that transformation requires belief in things that I will never be able to believe. What attracted me to Buddhism was its empirical approach, yet until how I've had difficulty deciding the matter of reincarnation using this approach.

So, any help achieving the right understanding of reincarnation is truly appreciated. If belief is required, I want to genuinely believe.

      > I think most importantly is actually to forget all this stuff, don't believe it, but don't disbelieve it either. Stay ‘neutral, and with a steady heart on going through the jhanas, more will be revealed in time. It is only natural.

      Well said, this is Right View.

      To OP, look into the Digha Nikaya 1 of the Pali Canon, and you will see that the Buddha is trying to make this point. Do not form a rigid view out of the limited things that you perceive. This is speculation.

      The ancients believed that the world was flat because it appeared exactly flat to their ability to observe at that time. However, later this firmly-believed stance was replaced by the sun revolving around the earth. Then it became the sun as the center. The more we are able to perceive - the truth can change to huge degrees.

      Therefore, be a brilliant, flexible scientist unto yourself for your own mind. Hold the resolute intent to find out.

      The moment you have fixed yourself in a speculative mental construct, that is where you have settled - you no longer have the drive to find out. One of the masters of the past used to say, "Great doubt, great enlightenment!"

      The thing about rebirth is that you can prove it for yourself. Probe. Find out.

      It is absolutely true that you do not know anything about rebirth now, so why should you believe in it? When you see it, then you do. What you do not know, you do not know. What you know, you know.

      When in meditation, simply drop all of those thoughts. No thought matters because what you want is direct experience.
