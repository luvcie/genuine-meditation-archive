# Mummified Monk - truth or fiction?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 5 points · 2 years ago

Source: http://mongolbeat.com/mummified-monk-not-dead-rare-meditative-state-says-expert/

> Dr Barry Kerzin, a famous Buddhist monk and a physician to the Dalai Lama, said: ‘I had the privilege to take care ; of some meditators who were in a tukdam state. ‘If the person is able to remain in this state for more than three weeks - which rarely happens - his body gradually ! shrinks, and in the end all that remains from the person is his hair, nails, and clothes. Usually in this case, people who live next to the monk see a rainbow that glows in the sky for several days.** This means that he has found a ‘rainbow body”.** This is the highest state close to the state of Buddha“.

Apparently there are accounts that this monk is not dead and possibly in samadhi.

In Tibetan Buddhism, practitioners often produce a rainbow body when dead, as a result of "light" contemplation or Dzogchen practice. Now this is in no way different from a Zen master who dies without effects, an esoteric master dying to leave a pure skull (such as Nan Huai Jin), etc - What matters is how the elements of the body were cultivated.

If this account is true, then let it not be that case where you idolize this person. Instead, treat it as an inspiration to your dharma learnings.

In fact, you might be surprised to know that being in a long state of samadhi is not in fact, a right path to being fully enlightened. However, it does show you the fruits of one who has attained considerably high wisdom.

It is said that certain disciples of the Buddha are still in deep samadhi under directions of Gautama Siddharta to await the return of Maitreya.
