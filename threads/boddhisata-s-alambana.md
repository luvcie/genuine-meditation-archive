Boddhisata's Alambana

A Bodhisattva's alambana is closer to that of a Buddha's because it is true non-dwelling, while an Arahant goes for a mini-vacation, where he will come back after aeons and restart. The forest-dwelling Arahant was a teacher I met before I received Master Nan's teachings in full. He was extremely wellpractised, and taught the way to liberation through Anapanasati as well.

However, the Arhat state is that which one dwells in "residual nirvana", meaning that there is still habitual energy that remains. As long as the conditions are there, one sits peacefully and is able to enter endless samadhis. However, upon the conditions collapsing, one would return back to the world with the same habitual energies as before.

In my perspective, however, I have found my Arahant teacher to be more like a Bodhisattva, since he has often emphasized the importance of cutting away fetters and burning away addictions and habitual energy. The more prominent one that set him as an Arahant though, was that he constantly clung onto the notion of being in "emptiness", as Master Nan expressed.

Therefore, you must understand that an Arahant does not necessarily leave samsara for good, while a Bodhisattva is able to go as he wills. I believe Master Nan had an excellent commentary on the Diamond Sutra, so perhaps you can take a look.
