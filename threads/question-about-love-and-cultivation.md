# Question about love and cultivation

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Emdaer** · *(OCR)* · *净力 Jing Li* · `Question` · 3 points · 2 months ago

Dear Sangha I have some questions that are relevant to what happened in my life recently. I am very grateful if you have something to share.

I recently went apart from a person whom I love. I love them in the way that I desire being with them, the conversations, the laughter. I want to make them happy and the relation made me happy as well. I can see now, how in way that lessened my motivation for cultivating the path. I was supported in my practice but I just didn't feel the same urgency to cultivate as I do now, because being with them made me feel happy and content to a degree. Now I'm experiencing emotions like loneliness and grief, which strengthens my resolve to cultivate. My concern is that being a layperson with a family would be a hindrance to the path for me, seeing how it made me care less about cultivation.

So the question is how could one love someone and be loved, and still be motivated to find the inner happiness in meditation?

Another question I have about this topic : Is there such thing as true, genuine love towards another being? And is it found by cultivation or by meeting the right person? If it is the unconditional love in Buddhism, I don't see how it would work in a exclusive relationship. That said I probably don't understand since I can't say I have experienced it. Last question I have: Is unconditional love and compassion empty of self and impermanent like the other things mentioned in the Heart sutra?

Thank you. It's been a pleasure to follow the subreddit and to me it is the Sangha that motivates and support me on the path.

- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 6 points · 2 months ago

  Firstly, one needs to define what love is.

  Love, as interpreted by modern society, is a pleasantness of experience - of body and mind. For example, when a mother comforts a baby, the baby feels nurtured and safe, pleasant and comfortable. When a son takes care of his mother, the mother feels blessed and pleasant.

  Before we go into this, you need to understand that a lot of things have been learnt in society. As a baby, you did not have these ideas of romance - it was only through the watching of television and emulation of other adults around you that you developed the mental conditioning of romance. First and foremost, you must understand how societal conditioning can reshape a person's mind.

  If you ask many married couples, for example, the Disney-like romance that is often idealized on mainstream media is very unrealistic - and worse, it brings about a negative message - that one's happiness must come from another.

  In the 21st century, this is the age where the marital construct will fall apart in many developed countries, giving way to a culture of short-term flings. As one can see, this trend is evolving for many reasons - one being that women do not require men to navigate in society as it was in the past for a patriarchal society - likewise, males feel pressurized by this rebound due to the suppression of one's own rights and do not find the benefits of engaging in a marriage.

  In short, marriage is a contract between two individuals, sometimes more, depending on the society. If one were to look at it with wisdom, one will see - it is a covert contract for most people so that each are expected to provide the other person with pleasurable experiences.

  As such, the Buddha declared for many laymen the responsibilities for the wife and husband as shown in his era:

  > The wife: love, attentiveness, family obligations, faithfulness, child-care, thrift, the provision of meals, to calm him down when he is upset, sweetness in everything. The husband: tenderness, courtesy, sociability, security, fairness, loyalty, honesty, good companionship, moral support.

  In modern terms, when a husband does not give a wife pleasurable experiences, the wife thinks, "He no longer loves me." In the same way, when a wife does not give her husband pleasurable experiences, he thinks, "She does not love me the same way as she did." It is hence a continuous, up-hill battle if both sides demand pleasurable experiences from the other side. It is a poisonous relationship that stresses both sides out and encourages suffering.

  Now what is love in the Buddhist context? This is tricky because there is no equal term.

  Firstly, one takes care of one's own suffering - one firstly realises that pleasantness based on the senses is unsustainable, as all conditioned things are impermanent. Having understood that, one turns to pleasantness that arises not of the senses.

  Secondly, one takes care of others' suffering - having reflected that this suffering is too, present in all other beings. These beings are all too, seeking pleasantness through their senses - it becomes natural then, that one becomes compassionate and kind in intention, speech and actions to them.

  Thirdly, marriage is a societal construct. To do this, one must understand what the reason and motive it is for one to have established such a rule. The reason is that marriage is also a contract for sexual relationships - Impregnating another person’'s wife will certainly cause huge suffering in her husband, therefore illicit sex is a heinous crime. To establish order in society, based on the society's culture, the marriage construct is set up to reduce conflict. This also created a stable structure for families without crossing-over.

  From a Buddhist perspective, one can take to this attitude - It is difficult for one to perfect one's conduct towards everyone. However, one's spouse whom one sees everyday is a perfect person to practice one's conduct on.

  Therefore in a Buddhist couple, both should understand firstly their responsibilities in compassionately fulfilling their role as per the marriage contract and not cause the other side suffering - and secondly to take one's own happiness in one's own hands, not rely it on the spouse.

  What one calls love is complicated in modern society, but it is merely this: A pleasantness of experience. This is why sex is often grouped in together with love. This only confuses people.

  Why do people love inanimate objects like their beds, sport clubs, movies, music and so on? Again, pleasantness of experience.

  One should always make it simple - cut away the poisonous, gradually-growing societal conditioning that seeks to increase one's dependency on external causes for happiness, and turn away from the senses to find one's own nature.
