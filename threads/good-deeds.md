# Good Deeds

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2uxx77/good_deeds/>

What are good deeds?

In order to understand what this is, you need to understand the dhamma of disengagement (viraga-dhamma).

Often, when performing any deed, when one sees that it is good, one claims it as one's own. When one sees that it is bad, one rejects it as one's own.

Now, look at this and compare it to the states of your body and mind. One often grabs and clings onto what seems to be good or pleasurable and rejects what seems to be bad or not pleasurable.

When you claim something as yours - it becomes a problem. You end up having to guard it, having to prevent it from wearing off and you become engaged in maintaining and preserving this thought.

It is the law of nature that anything that appears has to disappear. When a person has eaten, he needs to pass out waste in the future. Similarly, a good deed, when done, has to be spit out.

Think of it like an earthworm crawling through the mud. It eats the dirt and passes out dirt right after it is done. If it were to store the dirt within itself, it would become too heavy and bulky to move.

A good deed, when done, is not claimed by yourself. Even grasping at your past deeds, is fattening yourself with dirt, just like a foolish earthworm.

Where is Nirvana?

It isn't at some heavenly realm, Brahma realm or deva realm. It is not a place which is not reachable.

What is nirvana? It is deathless, it is birthless. It is truth that is not added to or subtracted from. It is not subject to change and is eternal.

Nirvana is right at your nose.

It is so blindingly simple. When you can truly stop clinging on to anything, and whenever pleasure, pain, neutrality, emptiness, form, good, evil, wealth, poverty comes your way, you say, "No thanks."

Where can Mara find you? Nowhere.

But you see, the hardest part of realizing Nirvana is not this principle. It is the mindfulness and capacity of doing so, of being this simple.

How many people can throw away their lives and everything they have owned? The Buddha abandoned kingship, unimaginable wealth (his country was extremely rich at that point), lust - And eventually he abandoned the Six Masters (sight, smell, ... , thoughts). Looking up at the star, he was enlightened.

Do you think this enlightenment is really sudden enlightenment? Zen students have been awakened by cherry blossoms, being hit by sticks, being thrown off a boat into the water to drown, etc. Many people disregard the fact that they have been studying the dhamma for years even before having this revelation.

Our elementary nature is imbalanced. Our minds are thrown into disarray. For the many lifetimes we have been here, we have constantly been clinging onto this complex.

We say "No thanks" with our head, but our hearts are still clinging on.

What is difficult, is the examining of one's own mind - or evaluating it, of being mindful at every single minute, even when you are sleeping. If you cannot even control what you are dreaming, what makes you think that you can control your mind and tell it to say "No thanks"?

Do good deeds, and then let them go. Do good deeds without any inkling of receiving, because who is really there to receive?

A person should be compassionate by heart and do deeds for the good of all - but never should this same person cling on to what one has done.

      > ' What is difference between astral projections and other astral travels in consciousness states?

      This is exactly what the Buddha had asked before he attained enlightenment. He was taught by various masters to reach realms of emptiness and the various astral realms. However, he still saw suffering in all of these realms. In order to find the answer to this, the first clue he received from insight was the experience of first jhana in his childhood.

      Take careful note of this. The liberation starts from the first jhana to the fourth jhana, also known as the rupa-jhanas. These are extremely important. If a person does not go through these, they do not develop equanimity or any of the factors of enlightenment. They do not develop sufficient "power” or emptiness to receive the insight stream in whole.

      The higher samadhis - such as reaching various astral worlds - the realms of devas, brahma, etc - These are all simply experiential realms. If an adventurer has this ability, it is only due to one's past merits and meditation work in another lifetime that has allowed this to ripen. However, this does not mean that one should not develop the first to fourth jhana.

      > May you, for example , reach higher states of consciousness and higher astral worlds by projecting only , without meditation background ,or ego is obstacle?

      You are right in saying that the ego itself is a form of obstacle. This ego is consciousness. Even if you are able to experience the whole world in the various samadhis, if you do not develop the first four jhanas and attain the right factors, it is impossible to attain liberation.

      In sleep, dreamlike states where a person is not completely lucid can also be a form of astral projection. However, this is a state that is alike to "oblivion". The person is not aware. It is not the right state of samadhi. Even in lucid sleep, this is still a limited experience. Some refer it to an "astral tube experience". Even in near-death experiences, the “intermediate yin body" is still nhot able to truly reach all of these realms.

      What we are doing in first to fourth jhana is to allow the faculties to fully ripen (please note this is a layman description to just give an idea). It is like developing the ability for one to receive wisdom. Vipassana is not a type of meditation technique, it means "insight", which comes along with absorption.
