# What is Samadhi of No-thought?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 7 points · 9 days ago

Before the Buddha did his ascetic practice, he was trained by two masters. He thus started his training by doing what is known as the Samadhi of No-Thought.

So what is the samadhi without thought? This is what it means to forcefully suppress and crush-down thoughts so that they do not rise. Even if one stifles the thoughts, the afflictions of the Form Realm still remain, and therefore if one dies, one will go to the Thoughtless Heaven in the Form Realm.

Due to this suppression, the 6th-consciousness does not arise at all. However, because of this, the practitioner reaches a dead-end because one does not realize that there are subtler workings of mind. As such, it is very easy for one to mistake this as 'liberation’, as Buddha's first teacher did.

Therefore, do not try to suppress thoughts. By developing wisdom, you can see that thoughts by themselves are empty in nature - they rise and pass away - at the very moment one is even aware of it.

The second samadhi that the Buddha trained in was the samadhi of Neither-Thought-Nor-No-Thought. What this means is that firstly, there is no thought, hence no coarse thoughts arising, which is similar to the first. The addition is ‘nor no thought' and what this means is that one is still able to be aware.

Now the ultimate problem here is what Shakyamuni raised as a question - Is there a self or not a self? If there is a self, how come this form and concept of self is not abandoned? Therefore Gautama left his second teacher and tried to seek a way to remove those afflictions.

He thought to himself - let me practise austerities, because by restricting the desires of the body and mind, I can abandon the affliction of self. Alas, he did torturous practices - starvation and extreme fasting, extreme breath-retention exercises, and crushing-mind-with-mind.

Again, he experienced the painful feelings, however, even though he trained his mind to be impervious to pain, such that it does not remain or invade his mind, he still did not find liberation. He hence realized that the pain was fruitless and gave up on the practice.

Upon accepting gruel from a village girl, he recovered his physical strength. He then sat under a Pipala tree near the Nairanjana river and declared the famous statement: "I will not rise from this seat until I attain bodhi."

Here is the crucial key - the Buddha had years of experience in meditation, in the various forms of samadhi, and it clearly took a very high aptitude to master even the samadhis of no-thought or neither-thought-nor-no-thought. Already experienced, already wise in meditation, he sat there for 48 days and then entered a profound state of meditation. He went through the jhanas one-by-one and many afflictions arose which he had not seen previously in his prior practices.

At that point, mara-states of meditation arose in his vision. He saw his afflictions for craving of sensory pleasure, for wealth, his fears of birth and death, and lastly even lustfulness. Hence, 'Mara's hordes' were defeated and he emerged without wavering.

He then attained the knowledge of spirits such as devas - firstly being able to travel anywhere in a mind-created body, secondly clairvoyance, thirdly clairaudience, fourthly telepathy, fifth the knowledge of past lives, and lastly the clear-wisdom that allowed the penetration in the four noble truths. Upon attaining these six, he experienced a brilliant light. Later on, the Buddha saw the Sun appear in the sky. At that very moment, there was an opening - and he attained anuttarasamyaksambodhi, perfect supreme and unexcelled enlightenment.

At this point, Buddha wanted to enter nirvana. However, numerous devas spontaneously appeared and implored the Buddha to carry on to teach. After seeing how there were indeed beings that were close to enlightenment, he then resolved to teach the path.

Therefore, remember that one should not be suppressing the 6th consciousness, forcibly trying to ‘'remain in silence'. Instead, one should recognize if afflictions arise and simply allowing them to go. Without suppressing, without inviting or clinging, just allow thoughts to settle. This is what is meant by non-dwelling.

              No, the samadhi of no-thought is different from the samadhi of nothingness. Samadhi of no-thought means there are no thoughts due to suppression but remaining afflictions of the form realm. Samadhi of nothingness means that there are no more form-realm afflictions or thoughts left.

              The samadhi of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is also different in that way, where the afflictions of the form realm are no longer there.

              In a way of speaking, they seem similar but are quite different samadhis. It is not just a qualitative difference, but a completely different samadhi at that point. Yes in a layman manner of speaking, it is because the jhanas have burnt away the afflictions of the form realm. The word "jhana" actually refers to the meaning of "burning”. What does it burn away? Afflictions of the form realm.

              Now afflictions of the form-realm burnt away, the six supernormal powers that arise include clear-wisdom. This is one of the keys to penetration into the four noble truths. As such, one can enter the stream after the four jhanas.
