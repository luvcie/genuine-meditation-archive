# The Meaning of "Turn the Five Senses Inwards"

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 7 points · 1 year ago

Can a camera observe itself?

A camera captures all of sound and sights that it can see. But can a camera become modified by what it captures?

Can a movie-screen observe itself?

A movie-screen can show movies of all kinds of genres. But can a movie-screen cry when it is a melancholic movie, laugh when it is a comedy, or be afraid when it is a horror movie?

Can a sky observe itself?

A sky can contain clouds, birds and planes. But can a cloud touch a sky? Can a bird shit onto the sky? Can planes touch the sky?

Can a being observe itself?

A being can capture all of sound, sight, taste, smell, touch and thoughts. But can these sounds, sights, tastes, smells, touches and thoughts touch the being at all?

Even with a comedy movie on a screen, the screen cannot feel pleasure and laugh. In the same way, a being always finds comedy movies in its mind. This function of attachment to a self brings pleasure.

When there is a horror movie on a screen, the screen cannot feel pain. In the same way, a being always finds horror movies in its mind. This function of attachment to a self brings pain.

Your five senses bring you the perception of a body, and this body, you think to be a self.

Let me ask you:

If you do not have a body, nor an etheric body, nor a Yin body, nor a Yang body, nor an astral body, nor a spirit, nor a "big self”, nor a "higher self”, nor a nirmanakaya, nor any other bodies that you can construct in your mind, with no location and no space to take up, where can you find a past, present or future?

Without a body, how can past and future even exist? These things only exist as sensory impressions.

There is no present moment in your five sensory perception. Each present moment that you perceive is already a past.

What is the name of your body right now?

I am called "Paramananda”.

This name means nothing to your own identity. But why does this name mean to much to "my" identity, while it does not mean anything to you? Why is your name so significant to you?

That is because there is a perception of a "you" to grasp that history, to possess that name.

You are like a camera that has tried to become the very image that it is capturing. You are a dreamer that has forgotten he is dreaming, and has imagined himself to be the dream.

The problem is that you are claiming everything as "you".

You are trying to imagine that enlightenment is a journey, that you can go from one end to the other end. You think you go from one dhyana to another, you want to climb a ladder, step by step.

What are you thinking of though? Only bodies can move through journeys. You are thinking again, in terms of the body awareness.

The body certainly goes through steps.

But you are not your body.

You are not jhana.

You are not anything that you see, hear, feel, touch, smell, think.

Avalokitesvara taught the best entry: Listen to the sounds of the world. What do you hear? You can hear the sound of things near and far.

Now listen more closely, what comes before the sound? You may start to hear the sound of blood moving, the sound of your heartbeat, etc.

Now what comes even before that? You hear the high pitched humming.

But even that is not it.

What else?

Go on.

A camera cannot find itself.

If you imagine yourself to be a "self" trying to find another "Self", then you are simply finding another way to continue playing the game.

Advaita, it is simply another game of finding an alternative Self to place your content in. This is completely different from the Dharma the Buddha teaches. Sever it completely. There is no self at all. Do not even play the self's game. Nothing. Everywhere you perceive, the self that is clung onto is there. However, so is what enables it.

There is a process that is going on at every single moment, because the origination is timeless, it is everywhere. It is not a 'something' or a 'somewhere'. It is what enables the clinging. It is not even a 'it'.

The moment you even think of something being the "it", you've lost it and started playing another game of "catch the enlightened self".

All you need to do is stop.

That's it.

Reside there.

                      No guru can enlighten you. Enlightenment is not something done onto a body.

                      A guru can try to point. But most people will simply follow the finger and try to find yet another concept of the self. A true guru will point his finger directly at you, into your eyes. Why is your seeing enabled? Why do all five senses, separate from each other, culminate into a unified experience when they simply do not mix?

                      It is as if sand and water mixed into mud, and we only see the mud, but do not see the sand and water.
