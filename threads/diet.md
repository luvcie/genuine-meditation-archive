  I've had some extended conversations with Paramananda about this, so permit me to share a bit.

  The first thing that should be mentioned is that every individual is different, and due to various physiological reasons, some diets are a better match for some rather than others. To that end, experimentation is warranted. As one continues to meditate, one becomes increasingly more sensitive. This includes more sensitivity to what we eat, and how it affects us.

  At first, one may find that there a particular cravings; perhaps those are a symbol that the body needs something (a particular vitamin or mineral for instance) more than something else. Maybe one starts to feel aversion toward certain types of meat, and how they, when ingested, dull the senses and the mind.

  Some general rules of thumb, which I am sure most spiritual seekers can attest to:

  e Try not to eat too much nor too little. The middle way is indeed the right way. Fasting is helpful. Gautama suggested one meal at noon. This, for most people, will however be too little, as one is not in spiritual retreat nor living a Yogic lifestyle, but intermittent fasting is an excellent idea for many health and spiritual reasons. See Paramanda's excellent post on the subject.

  e Stay away from meat, especially red meat and pork, if possible. Animals take more energy to digest, and it can make both the mind and body lethargic. Meat also has the propensity to awaken the passions, and are thus not a very good choice for the meditative lifestyle.

  e Vegetables and fruit are to be the stable of the diet. These are very conducive toward meditation, and cultivate serenity and peace in the mind.

  There are numerous benefits to a vegan diet. To understand better the impact of food on health, there's an incredible book on the topic, that delves into these things in an advanced way. I wholeheartedly recommend it. Healing with Whole Foods.

  Most of our food today, unfortunately, possess an absurd number of GMO, artificial sugars and carcinogens. It is therefore very important to educate oneself about things like this. There are of course also elemental balances to take into account, and that is indeed, where systems such as Ayurveda, or the Traditional Chinese Medicine's knowledge can be taken into account. For instance, if you are eating foods or drinking alcohol, which affects the liver negatively, do not be surprised to see anger. There are many examples like this.

  I hope this was of help to you. Strive well and may the Path bring you to liberation in this lifetime.
