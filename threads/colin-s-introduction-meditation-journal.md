Hi friends,

The introduction thread (and others six months & older) seem to be closed to new comments, so /u/refugeinbuddha suggested I introduce myself in a meditation journal thread. I'm going to use the introduction and "How is your cultivation?” threads to help shape my initial post. Before I start, I'd like to thank Paramananda for creating and all of you for participating in this virtual sangha and providing a place of learning, sharing, and mutual support.

My background

My name is Colin and I'm 33 years old. I live in San Francisco, California, USA. I came to meditation through a lifelong interest in fantasy, mythology, history, philosophy, spirituality, and magic/k.

My first investigation into the supra-mundane began in my teens as I became curious about druidism, and from there I explored and dabbled in Western ceremonial magick, Chaos magick, kundalini yoga, shamanism, psychedelics/entheogens, etc. I have also practiced lucid dreaming and astral projection off and on for many years. I am not adept in any of these areas, but have had enough unusual and inspirational experiences to keep the flame of curiosity alive.

I've also practiced Emotional Freedom Techniques, Whole-Hearted Healing, Quantum Touch, Matrix Energetics, audio-visual brainwave entrainment, HeartMath coherence techniques, self-hypnosis and more in an effort to find peace, physical & emotional healing, and self-improvement/evolution.

I've struggled with chronic depression since my teens and I've been searching for ways to break through the despair, hopelessness, and self-loathing I've felt so often and for so long. This has been a major factor in my wide-ranging search for the extraordinary. I do feel a bit like the cliched "seeker" stereotype in this regard.

I took a few classes on Buddhism at university and something about the teachings resonated very strongly with me; while I've continued to explore other spiritual philosophies including Taoism and Advaita Vedanta, Buddhism seems to be the most detailed, well-explicated, and efficacious way out of suffering to me.

Prior teachings & teachers

I have studied the teachings of the Buddha Siddhartha Gautama for many years, though I take part in no official sangha and have taken no official vows. Among many others, I have read the works of Nan Huai-Jin and his student Bill Bodri. I was formally initiated into the kundalini kriya yoga system of Yogiraj Gurunath Siddanath, though I have not undertaken those specific practices in many years.

My current practice & discipline

Currently I meditate for a minimum of 30 minutes daily, often sitting for this length of time (or longer) twice in one day. I recently transitioned from counting my breaths to noting them using the recitation "Bud-dha" thanks to advice found in this subreddit. I have been familiar with the 9-step bottled wind pranayama for years thanks to the writings of Master Nan, but I have only recently integrated it into my daily practice directly prior to sitting. I also mentally recite the Cundi and Vairocana mantras throughout the day, usually between (approximately) 300-500 times per day.

I try to remain mindful throughout the day and, though I do not officially keep the precepts, I refrain from lying, stealing, sexual misconduct, intoxicants, and killing as much as possible. I do eat meat and sometimes Kill insects, and I do occasionally partake of alcoholic beverages, but typically cease prior to the onset of heedlessness. I am not currently in a sexual or romantic relationship.

My goals, doubts, and questions

In the short term, I wish to experience vitality, happiness, peace, connection with others and our world, and a sense of belonging and purpose in this life. Ultimately, I desire to realize true nature in order to end suffering for all beings.

I aspire to be a friend to all creatures and a source of aid & comfort to those in need. I struggle with anger, contempt, impatience, and hateful thoughts, and seeing those as antithetical to the previous aspirations, wish to steer the ship of "my" personality to a different course using compassion and loving-kindness, which I don't actively cultivate at this time.

I currently take antidepressants to help keep me on an emotional even keel and boost my energy levels. Though I experience essentially no side effects from this medication, I'd like to wean myself off of them and believe that the proper practice of meditation and the Buddhist path should naturally have this result. I'm curious if anyone else has experience and thoughts on this matter, i.e. took antidepressants, anti-anxiety medication, etc., and were able to cease taking them due to their practice.

On a more mundane level, my body has tight hips which preclude me from sitting traditionally on a cushion. I also experience back and shoulder pain. So, I would like to include some kind of physical cultivation in my practice in order to lessen or alleviate those hindrances to meditative absorption.

I suppose that, ultimately, I view myself as fundamentally selfish and unworthy of experiencing true satisfaction, so I'd like to get past that for the sake of those around me as much as myself.

I have a few doubts/questions/curiosities about my current practice about which I hope I can gain clarity:

Currently I readily experience jhana-nimittas such as tingling, phosphenes, high-pitched noises, etc., both in sitting practice and in daily life, but my mind never seems to really settle for very long. I realize that suppression of thoughts is not a goal with this practice, but I do feel like I'm pulled into thought-streams fairly easily. At the same time, I rarely ever lose track of my meditation object (typically the breath) while this goes on, so I'm not sure if or how I could improve here.

I've also noticed that, contrary to what Paramananda and others write about breath cessation, my body tends to pause between exhalation and inhalation, not vice-versa. Only very recently (within the last week or so) have I on occasion experienced something akin to cessation between the in-breath and the out-breath. What I find is that my body expands with the in-breath, but then feels full and either breathes out slowly or (rarely) not at all, at least for a few moments. But then I feel as if I'm influencing the breathing process and losing track of a natural rhythm.

A few days ago I experienced very strong waves of bliss moving through the body while meditating. I've experienced similar sensations before, but only mildly when meditating or relaxing. I've only experienced sensations that strong in conjunction with the use of substances like cannabis and psilocybin mushrooms. Now, and similarly with the breath cessation, I feel like I'm trying to force these experiences when I sit. I suppose I'm struggling with the distinction between allowing and craving/clinging, and would appreciate some guidance here.

That's about everything I can think of for now. Thank you for reading and for any and all feedback you wish to share. Strive well!

Edit to mention: This will start as an introduction and I welcome your responses as such, but I will use this thread as a meditation journal moving forward.

        - **infinite_sustain** · 2 points · 2 years ago

          I've encountered that "having to force yourself to breath” thing. You're right in that I don't recall ever having seen it specifically mentioned by anyone anywhere, either.

          My take on the situation is that due to letting go and deepening relaxation, the breath becomes subtle to the point that it wants to settle and rarify, but the body's channels aren't yet clear enough to fully handle the lack of external respiration. This leads to a mounting sensation of breathlessness as the in-and-out current moves toward the zero point.

          My experience is that if you just mentally say "screw it" and consent to allow the breathless sensation to remain, without interfering with it at all or indulging the desire to gasp or resume coarser breathing, then a certain sequence of events follows: your back will spontaneously straighten up (you may hear cracks and pops), your lower abdomen will pull further and further inward, you'll feel an internal pumping "breath” in that same belly area, and you may feel (and even hear) your nasal passages "pop" open with little bursts.

          After this occurs, when you resume with windy breathing, your posture should feel strengthened and more relaxed, and your breathing might feel lighter and more subtle as if the air passageways had been dredged open to some degree by the force of pressure.

          I don’t think this whole phenomenon is desirable in a way that it ought to be cultivated, since it's rather clumsy-seeming and violent (and is definitely not real "xi" or kumbhaka -- breath cessation) but I don't think it's a bad thing either. My current feeling is that it's just the body taking care of stuff on its own, preparing the infrastructure for future states of more pervasive stability.

          (These are all just notes, not conclusions of any sort. If anything, I wrote this stuff just so you know that you won't pass out or explode if you decide to "ride out" that breathless state for the sake of science!)

          Good luck.

You can experiment with the switching.
