# Questions about Pranayama

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 2 years ago

I've heard a few people here say that pranayama accelerated their meditation progress, but I'm still having problems with it that I'm not sure are normal.

For instance, I'm not sure if my body is just built strangely or what, but placing the backs of my hands on my thighs and raising my shoulders up still hurts my wrists quite a bit and doesn't really hold up my shoulders like it's supposed to. When I'm done, my wrists feel unnaturally strained, and I don't know if this is just something that everyone experiences at first but gets better, like leg numbness in meditation.

Also, I find it nearly impossible not to breathe between long breaths. When I try not to, the second long breath is much, much shorter, and involuntarily so.

My Biggest problem here is that I don't know if the discomfort I feel is what I'm supposed to feel, or evidence of incorrect practice. Any help would be appreciated.

  The shoulder muscles have to be trained to hold the position up, so that lesser effort is placed on the wrists. Perhaps try to rotate the wrist such that the fingers point forward. The main aim of this practice is to train the point of breath cessation and holding it. It also helps to open up channels. This is especially for people who have not reached breath cessation like /u/Sherlockian_Holmes has.

  The key is to bring this practice into breath meditation, where it stops spontaneously. You do not force it, but when it happens, this practice allows you to stay in that cessation for a longer time.

          The pranayama is forceful, therefore it is uncomfortable. However, one should try to maintain relaxation and gradually increase the period of holding the breath. You may even do this with a timer. It is like training wheels, so that one may clean one's channels to allow spontaneity of breath cessation to happen in breath meditation. Therefore, the answer is to hold until it is at the border of being strenous, and gradually practice until one can hold it for extended periods of length.
