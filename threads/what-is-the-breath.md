# What is the Breath?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 9 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2t5e1g/what_is_the_breath/>

To many of us, the breath simply means air moving into our lungs and out. Within this breath, oxygen is taken into the bloodstream through the membrane of the air sacs (alveoli). Similarly, carbon dioxide is expelled to flush out acidity of the blood. This is one such case of what the breath means.

The true esoteric meaning of the breath is what is known as "internal wind".

Now, it is easy to be confused with the terminology and you should think of it as the characteristics of "wind". For example, you know that when the wind moves, it gathers heat and it loses heat. It moves from a place of higher pressure to a place of lower pressure. When it is in equilibrium, there is no wind. Rather than actual wind that of air, the breath refers to the philosophy behind wind.

Similarly, if people talk about internal fire/water/earth elements in the body, they are referring to the various philosophies of energy and laws that govern the body.

For example, fire refers to the warmth phenomenon of the body, as well as desire and emotions that flicker with heat, as if they were fire. Water refers to the endocrine system and how the hormones flow, along with the unpredictability of "tides" and cycles. Earth refers to the less malleable aspects of the body, such as the bones and flesh.

Out of all of these four elements, the most versatile is wind. This is followed by fire, water and then lastly earth. This is the reason why certain enlightened masters prefer to leave coarse earth elements of the body behind, such as fingernails and hair.

This principle is seen throughout the world. If you study cosmology, it is seen that the movement of huge rocks refers to wind. The whole planet becoming molten indicates fire. The cooling process of fire refers to water. Lastly, the water recedes and earth surfaces. This is also consistent with the birth of our own planet Earth, just as our own physical body has developed.

So now that you see from this point of view, the philosophy of wind itself is the foremost, the most versatile of the four and the easiest to grasp.

When the human is in the womb, how does this concept of wind occur? When the human is an embryo, oxygen itself is limited to allow organogenesis, preventing reactive oxygen species from attacking the development of major organs. How does one breathe at this stage? Think about it.

Next, through the umbilical cord, one receives nutrients. It is only in the second trimester where the fetus starts to receive increased oxygen. How does one breathe at this stage? Certainly not through the lungs.

When the fetus is born through the birth canal, he/she gives a slight sigh, releasing compressed air within the lungs, after which there is a huge intake of air. This is the point at which the human switches wind movement to that of external exchange with the environment.

What is this philosophy of wind movement then? Can you begin to see what the 'breath' really is?

If you close your eyes and become mindful of your entire body, you can sometimes feel the tingling of blood-flow under thinner skin of your body, such as the finger tips or feet.

Don't be deceived, this movement is exactly as what it is: It is blood flow. However, where there is movement, there is wind movement along with it. It is also interesting to note that while the nerves are at the surface of the skin, you are able to feel the blood flow, which are normally in a slightly deeper layer of the skin. Why is that so?

Let us go yet even deeper. When you breathe in or out, the mind itself moves as well. When the breath does not go in or out, what then happens to the mind?

The mind temporarily ceases in movement. However, the untrained person will not be enlightened as a result of that because as soon as he feels this cessation, his logical mind kicks in and he starts to conceptualize that gap as "without breath". The very moment a concept is grasped, it is no longer it any more.

Now the transition that you see here, from embryo to fetus to human, you will start to see in reverse as you meditate in anapansati (human to fetus to embryo).

Upon high attainment, the human ceases his breath. The wind philosophy switches to the internal wind inside the body, especially at the point four fingers below the belly button. Do not focus on this area though, if untrained it can cause unneeded stress on the body.

Reading this, what have you learnt about the breath and its usage in breath meditation?
