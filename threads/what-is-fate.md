# What is fate?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 5 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2thpyb/what_is_fate/>

One of my teachers' fortune was read by a blind fortune teller at the age of 24. He felt the bone structure of my teacher's face and said, "It is unfortunate, but in 2 years you will die."

Being told in the face that he was about to die, having had an aspiration in the past to abandon everything and seek enlightenment, he went up to the famous Taoist mountain in China, Mt Emei to meditate.

What happened? He meditated for a total of 3 years, attaining enlightenment. He had escaped his predicted fate. Coming down from the mountain as a Bodhisattva, he went back to the same fortune teller who was still tending his shop. The fortune teller nearly went into shock touching his face structure and he stuttered, "It cannot be. Why are you still alive?"

The story above was given to illustrate how fate binds a person. One who has attained true emptiness through the Dharma is void of all elements, consciousness, feelings, sensations, etc. As such, one becomes free of conditional effects. He/She becomes the master of one's own fate.

Now before you claim that the fortune teller was wrong and just deluded in the first case, it is also important to note that this particular fortune teller had a limited form of clairvoyance (also known as psychic ability), born in exchange for his external sight being blinded. He had predicted many events, all without fail.

Does fate exist then? To the enlightened being, fate no longer grips him/her. To the unawakened being, one is bound by fate.

Let us look at a building sitting in its glory. It did not come from nowhere. It came from the construction through placements of cement blocks, glass windows and metal frames. How did this idea of structure arise? It came from an architect's thought. What was the condition for this thought to arise? It was the city-planner who gave the green-light to build it. But where did this idea even come from? It came from the people's demands and the perception that the building is needed.

You can link this cause-and-effect chain to aeons in history. When something happens, it has no choice but to follow that route, just like a domino. As such, you can say that if one thing happens, it is inevitable that everything else follows and arises simultaneously at the same time.

People are merely blind to the conditions that cause "luck" or "misfortune" incidents. There is no luck. It is as if the whole world is a giant mathematical structure, one component cannot exist without another. One change affects the rest of the web of effects and causes.

But does this mean that everyone has to follow their own route blindly?Not so. It is however, not easy to change one's route from fate. Just like my teacher had demonstrated, by achieving true emptiness in Dharma, one can achieve mastery of one's own fate - One becomes the master of one's own life and death, even fate.

What are the things that may change a person's fate? It is that of decisions. This is what is said by the Buddha when he talked about practising right morality in interaction with others.

To an unawakened being, one latches on to a thought that has risen from one's "consciousness". However, he/she does not realize that this "consciousness" of theirs has been a mix of conditions - from society, from parents, from dharma teachings, from books, from media, from previous sights/sounds/tastes/smell/touches/mental objects. It is the consciousness here that controls the unawakened being's decisions.

To the awakened one, one knows and is not controlled by these. He/she makes decisions that are not birthed from the control of these decisions. As such, he/she comes from a state of profound emptiness. Fate cannot touch him.

Out of compassion for the deluded being, the Buddha then gave his instructions to emulate the essence of actions of enlightened beings - that of being virtue, samadhi and insight.

From this, the deluded being breaks out of the pattern, out of the vicious cycles throwing them like clothes in a washing machine, out of the fire of desire that burns them. It is almost like a parent telling his child to not play with fire. You can see how compassionate the Buddha really is from this, to have taught the way out of harm.

The Tathagata likened it to a poisoned arrow. When an archer shoots you with a poisoned arrow, do you run after this person or treat the poison immediately?

The whole gist of this post is to show that in order to break out of the unmeritorious, unfortunate future effects that can be produced from wrong conditions set as of now, following merit, one follows in the footsteps of an enlightened being and sets up meritorious future seeds. Such is the nature of karma.

With this, we can also appreciate how deep the Buddha's compassion was, that he persisted in teaching and guidance for so many years before going into parinirvana. And not to forget, that he asked three times before going into full enlightenment and passing away from the material realm.
