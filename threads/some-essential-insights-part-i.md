# Some Essential Insights (Part I)

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 5 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2x6yie/some_essential_insights_part_i/>

Maya is Illusion.

To be pulled by Maya is to fall deeper into the ensnarement of Maya. To identify with the objects of Maya is to fall deeper into Illusion.

How does one escape Maya? There are a few ways in which Maya drags a person back into its depths.

(1) Pride

The most subtle of all, is the Self. By having an ego, one becomes selfish and deluded. All it sees is separation. It sees the need to differentiate water in a jug and water in the ocean. It sees the need to differentiate the prince/pauper, and the male/female. Is a gold ring a "ring" or "gold"?

We are foreign guests who take host in an organic body with a brain within this physical world of Earth. However, we claim this physical body as ours, how absurd! It is as if you walked into a foreign town and declared the whole street to be yours, because you walked on it.

Your body is not You, neither is your ego. You are the knower of the world, the knower behind the senses and consciousnesses. When a doer has no sense of being the doer, one becomes free of the action. When you decide someone is bad, you yourself become bad. When you see everyone as friends, when you see everyone to come from the same inherent nature, you yourself become the inherent nature.

When there is nothing to be seen, how can there be a seer? Everyday, say to yourself: "There is nobody. This person does not exist." You are a guest, not your body itself - Stop harboring so much pride.

You, the moon, the Sun, stars and all sentient beings are One. Your life's energy gives life to all. "You" are the one surfacing and disappearing from behind the faces of all the myriads of beings around the world.

The "I" is not the inherent nature. The person projected by your imagination is not it. Wisdom is its only slayer.

(2) Desire

Desire and anxiety/restlessness come together as a pair. Where there is desire, there is anxiety. To demand something is to insult and disrespect. To not demand something is to honor and show respect to. The person who renunciates, who does virtuous deeds, and then turns back onto himself and says "I did that." is not a renunciate.

Giving no value to things gives you full access to them. Having no value to Siddhis allows them to surface in full magnitude. Having no value to wealth and riches, having no value to any desire whatsoever, brings the kingdom to you.

The object in your mind at the point of death decides the nature of your next birth. Live every moment, even right now, as if it was your last minute. Stay in jhana, stay in equanimity, stay in non-duality. The one who says "I don't want" purely and sincerely, gets everything. When in the inherent nature, there was originally nothing, what is there to let go of?

The ever-changing mind can never have peace. It is as if mud water was continuously stirred, and the mud in the water is never given the opportunity to rest. Hold on to the thought "Nothing is mine", and this mud will sink to the bottom, leaving a clear and pristine mind.

Charity is desire's opposite, to say that "Nothing is mine. How can anything belong to me?" When you have valuable things, you have no desire to protect or hoard them. That is charity. Behaving righteously does not mean that you have truly renounced. By being able to say "This is not mine, take it if you want." to anything of yours - That is true charity.

If you are greedy, you will lose everything. The one who is not greedy gains everything. One who is non-greedy becomes bored when looking at precious gems, to valuable possessions, to property and money.

(3) Excuses

The one who says "Later" is one who does not have an intent to see the truth. It does not matter if you are a layperson or a monastic. If you are able to stay in jhana constantly, whether you have a family or not, whether you have a wife/husband or not, being diligent in your spiritual practice is following the dharma.

Changing your attitude and mind is far more important than relocating yourself physically. There is no difference between a monastery and your own house. One who practices the dharma and has entered the stream, has already donned the yellow robe and is worthy of being called a Bhiksu. If a person does not practice the dharma, but walks in the company of monks, this person is not worthy of the yellow robe.

Do not imagine that you need to be in the presence of a highly attained one in order to have high attainments. It is just an excuse for the Self to take over and allow Maya to claim your mind.

(4) Intellect

There is only a single duality: The seer and the seen. Everything seen is Maya and unreal. One who sees the seen as real is one who perishes. One who meditates on any object becomes that very object. One who worships the seer becomes the All-Knowing One.

One who is intelligent is busy hypnotizing himself by creating sandcastles in the air. This play of imagination drives a person deeper into delusion. One who takes this earth as real, belongs to the earth in a next lifetime.

The world is seen from "the Eye of Illusion". When one truly experiences that one is in everything, omnipresence results. One who is enlightened does not see from that eye of illusion. Experience depends on one's concepts.

To a husband, a female might be his wife. But this same female is a "sister", a "daughter", a "sister-in-law", a "teacher", a "friend" to many other people. What remains is that this female is a structure of bones and meat, that will eventually return to dust on death.

To meditate is to renounce all mental activities. It is not suppression, because suppression is a mental activity. It is the allowance of these temporary things to fade away naturally.

(5) Negligence

Maya is devious. She finds the smallest crevices to sneak a hook into your mental orifices. The moment you lapse your attention, you are hooked and dragged into her illusionary realm. Cultivate the third jhana, remain in equanimity and be unaffected by these fleeting phenomena.

Do not live your life waiting for death. If you look around, that is the fate of every sentient being isn't it? Whatever is subject to birth is subject to death. There is only one destination for everyone. What differs is what you make use of within this rare human birth. This life is short. You cannot afford to waste the time.

(6) Limited Wisdom

The progress of one who is elated by achieving some form of virtue or Siddhi is one who has stagnated and become stalled, falling prey to the demons of delusion.

That which is generated from an element eventually returns to that element. If it were to continue increasing, imbalances and disasters would ensue.

In the spiritual life, spiritual powers are ensnarements. Do not be entangled by these powers, or you will become a bear caught in a hunter's trap.

One behaves according to the attitude and feeling one claims. What we "know", we become. Day and night, you should meditate on the inherent nature. Make it become a habit.

This is just a first part, I will write up a second part soon. I have not posted much content these few days. The main reason is that practice is much superior to conceptual knowledge. Do realize that the answer does not lie in the amount of things you read, but what you discover with your own practice.

These insights can be understood, but you need to use them as guideposts. Do not reverse cause and effect. You need to investigate on your own and get it from practice. Please feel free to ask any questions or share your insights as well.

      > One part of my realizations I know/partly know to be true insight. These realizations mostly comes to me through meditation or mindfulness.

      Insight must be matched with critical thinking and active study. At lower absorptions, you can certainly get insights, but they are often filtered through levels of conceptual understanding. Sometimes, you are unable to distinguish between what is real insight and what is delusion. Hence, one must be able to compare his own insights with that of wisdom shared by others.

      > : In the end I realize that practice has the most important role in gaining insight.

      Yes, absorption comes hand in hand with insight. Practice is extremely important, however, study and observation of several guidelines should not be forgotten as well.

      > I have a question here. Is practice sufficient for enlightenment, or should one write down and recite his insight and realization?

      Right practice is the practice of the Noble Eightfold Path. That is what is sufficient for enlightenment. In order to understand what this practice entails, you need to study first and accumulate mundane knowledge. Just like an archer, one needs to know where the target is, and how to use a bow and arrow before aiming. While practising, one then verifies these concepts by experiencing them.

      You should not worry about forgetting insights. If these insights are true, they are accessible to you any time in the insight stream. There is never an insight gained, nor an insight lost in the first place.

      Just like how a cave man sees the expanse of the world outside his cave and cannot forget it, you certainly cannot “forget” supramundane insight - rather, you merge and become the very embodiment of it.
