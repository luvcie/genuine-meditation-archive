  Piti itself is only disturbing and of no benefit. This can be confusing, so here's an analogy: Turbulence when you're flying an airplane. Piti is a good sign, because you don't get piti unless you're successfully forming a jhana (turbulence is a good sign, because it means the plane is flying through the air). But while it's a good sign, it's of no benefit in itself, and can even be distracting/disturbing. If you focus only on trying to create piti then you've missed the point; it's generated as a side-effect when you successfully concentrate attention. It may be necessarily to temporarily increase the amount of piti you experience in order to concentrate yourself more. That doesn't mean that the piti itself is what you should aim for, you should keep aiming towards concentrating your mind, just don't be disturbed that piti may greatly increase for a while as you increasingly concentrate.

  I'd like to know exactly what you mean by "waves." Do you mean that you feel the pleasure move from place to place through your body in a wave like motion? And/or do you mean that the pleasure surges for a while and then weakens before surging again?

        - **mungojelly** · 4 points · 2 years ago

          OK gotcha, sounds like what happens is that when it surges it throws you off of your concentration somehow. If you think about it that'll tend to produce that bucking bronco feeling where it surges in chaotic waves for a while before throwing you off completely. It's just logical really: The piti increases and increases, until at some point it's able to distract you. At that point, since the distraction pulls you off your focus, the piti ebbs. Then as it ebbs you can stop being distracted and regain your focus, but regaining your focus again causes the piti to surge enough to distract you, and the loop repeats.

          All you have to do is steadily focus on something. It doesn't matter what. The lists in scripture of which meditation objects to use is really basically mostly just a list of random objects: You could try using a rock, maybe try focusing on your hand, how about breathing that's always there, or you could focus on death, or how about the color blue. Doesn’t matter, just pick one that works for you.

          So here's three possible strategies for how to deal with the surging piti. There's nothing complicated about them, we're just looking for a strategy that ends up with you maintaining a steady undistracted focus the whole time, that's all. Strategy one, probably the one you should go with, most straightforward: Keep the focus on the breath (or whatever object), the whole time, no matter what happens. No matter what kind of piti comes up, give it no attention at all.

          Strategy two: When piti starts, switch your focus of attention to the piti. This is a trickier maneuver, but can you see how it can work if you do it right. You have to be concentrating on something in order to produce the piti. You start off with your breath or whatever, which begins to produce some piti. Then when you notice the piti you immediately switch to focusing on it. As long as you don't change the depth of your concentration, the same piti continues to be produced by the focus-- it doesn't care what it's on, your breath, a rock, even the piti itself, that's just another object, no special rules. So there's a tricky thing there obviously where you swing your attention from object to object in the middle, which is why I'd recommend strategy one, the "just ignore it" strategy, as an easier simpler way to go generally. This second strategy might be easier if you find the piti just so distracting that you just can't attend the breath instead while it's there. But if you don't really need the medicine it's easier to avoid having to do that trick of swinging your concentrated attention around without losing focus.

          Strategy three: Focus on the piti the whole time from the beginning. This is a different weird trick, because of course the piti isn't actually there at the beginning because you're not focused yet. But the object doesn't matter, it doesn't matter so much that you're allowed to have an object that doesn’t even exist. So it's possible to focus on the piti until you reach a concentration on it that the thing you're focusing on starts to exist. But I mean if that's not easy for you or doesn’'t make sense to you don't worry about it, it's just another idea, the very simple theme is this: Concentrating intently on ANY FOCUS produces piti. Losing focus and producing discursive thought as a result of ANY DISTRACTION breaks it. No exceptions for the content of the focus or distraction-- it's not an intellectual thing and it just doesn't check on what things mean on a meta level, any more than walking cares what you mean by each of your steps. Take one step after another for whatever reason with whatever feeling and you keep going.

                - **mungojelly** · 1 point · 2 years ago

                  The style and intensity of the concentration is independent from what you're concentrating on. The concentration occurs in the pattern of thinking, not in the content of the thoughts. So you can slip the content of the concentration around while maintaining the same degree of concentration. The object doesn't have to be simple in the sense that it would be simple to explain it, just the pattern of attending to it has to be simple. The object could be something like “the sensation of my breath and also the sensation of pleasure in my forehead considered as one sensation” and the fact that that explanation is complicated doesn't matter if the sensation is a simple sensation and the pattern of attention is just to loop around repeatedly attending it.

                  Expanding the piti around your body is exactly what the instructions in the suttas say to do. It happens naturally as you've noticed and it should be encouraged and given room when it appears. But don't let it get too interesting, do you know what I mean by that? Don't explore different flavors of pleasure zooming around your body. I mean do if you like, do!! That's fine for what it is, a dance of pleasures around your body, God yes, do that, but that's not what concentrates you is all I'm saying, that's distractingly fun and interesting. Instead to become more concentrated you should just like follow a boring rule.

                  Here's what I do when I'm trying to help my piti to spread without distracting me: When I notice piti, I allow it to spread instantly effortlessly not just everywhere in my body but everywhere in my awareness, to every everything. Then if some piti comes along and says, well, I'm an even more pleasurable better piti then I just say fine, spread that instantly everywhere instead. No mess or fuss, most importantly no decision to make about it, no wondering where exactly what sort of pleasure should go. If there's really some sensation that's better for me now or whatever, OK I accept that sensation as the piti. But there's no drama about it, just waves of better-better-better-better-better with a very boring overall character to the practice of it.

                  Then generally it can pretty quickly find a better that no betters are ready to challenge and settle there, some people call that the "true"” first jhana, call it what you wish but let the pleasure be boring enough that you mass enough a giant boring mass of it such that you rest in the boring sufficiency of it.

                                  Here is some advice, I have found useful: Slow down and do one thing at a time! You can find great joy in simply being aware of what you are doing in the present moment.

                                  When walking for example, you can attend to the rythm of how you move. Too often, when we walk around, we are chasing thoughts without even knowing. Mindfulness or self awareness is about knowing what you are doing.

                                  When talking to others, it is an act of virtue to practice awareness of what you are about to say so you don't say anything abusive or divisive about others or not just talking random. Of course it is OK to small talk with others to create a harmonious atmosphere. But try to be aware of when the small talk becomes unnecessary. If you can stay mindfull while speaking, you can also decide to always speak in a gentle tone, no matter who you talk with. The practice of wholesome speech is beneficial because it lessens your mental chatter and you avoid making mistakes, harming yourself and others. Also when conversating, you can practice compassionate listening. Giving all your attention to another person is very generous and they will notice too!

                                  Self-awareness of the thoughts that come and go is a also a great tool to have. Thoughts can be very deluded and irrational, a good example are thoughts that belittle your self esteem. Or thoughts of anxiety and worry. You didn't subscribe to these thoughts but still, they might arise. Notice how the negative thought sparks a counterpart negat feeling in the body ( often in the belly or heart area). If you are not aware of your thoughts, you might create more suffering than necessary by getting caught up in such negative thoughts, possibly creating a negative feedback looj (neurosis to some extend) . When you notice an unwholesome thought you can think: 'do I need to have this thought?' and return to any meditation object. This will counteract the negative feedback loop.

                                  Mindfulness /self-awareness is like a weapon. It will prevent unwholesome things if you can wield it properly. The breath is a powerful tool here, because it is always there. Whenever you find yourself not grounded/not stable you always return to the breath. By practicing you are building the most genuine and noble refuge there is.
