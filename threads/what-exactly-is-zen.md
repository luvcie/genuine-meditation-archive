# What Exactly is Zen?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advanced` · 3 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2uphel/what_exactly_is_zen/>

Many people have a huge misconception on what Zen really is. Zen is in fact, a transmission of the Buddha's Dharma, the method of no method - For one to understand the direct truth.

The very first Zen patriarch was known by the name Bodhidharma. Bodhidharma explained that there were two methods of entry into realization of the truth:

1. Entering through principle - Acquisition of mundane wisdom (intellectual wisdom from studying of Buddhist principles).

2. Entering through practice - Acquisition of perception ability of supramundane wisdom through right meditation (right effort, right mindfulness and right jhana)

At this point of time, the recommended sutta for understanding this truth was the Lankavatara Sutra.

Zen is renowned for the quirky behaviour and unique teaching style of the propagators of the Dharma. It is not unusual to see Zen teachers shout, slap or do extremely unusual things with the aim of awakening a student to the truth.

However, the unfortunate problem about modern Zen is that instead of embracing the second entry of entering through practice, many people tend to fall into lip-service, debate sessions or emulations of the old masters, without truly practising the principles.

Can one awaken to truth by engaging one's consciousness? Certainly not. Consciousness itself is grasping onto a mental object, a mental state. It is far away from liberation.

Let us not forget that one of the factors of the Eightfold Path is right resolve/intention to study the Dharma and be liberated from suffering.

An example of how this was reinforced in the past was shown in the Second Patriarch cutting off his arm to show his resolve to Bodhidharma in wanting to study the Dharma. He was a great scholar of the I-Ching, yet he abandoned everything to become a monk, stood in the snow for three days before Bodhidharma accepted his resolve for wanting to understand the truth. It might seem extreme to you, but in the past, people had a lot of determination to study the Dharma, and it can come across as a huge difference in culture.

The very first thing you need to be able to do is to give up everything. Not just physical things, but everything - even your ego, your identity, your thoughts, etc. Only by doing this does one strip the layers of delusion and get to the truth.

One of the most classic illustrations on what the Buddha's dharma truly is can be found in this interaction:

Second Patriarch: "When I look for my mind, I cannot find it."

Bodhidharma: "i have already pacified your mind for you."

Did you awaken by reading this?

If not, you have not reached the right level of understanding yet. Many people memorize meditation sayings and know them inside-out, but they do not realize that depending on your level of insight, you will interpret a simple conversation like this to varying levels of understanding.

How did Zen patriarchs study Zen? The fact is that there is really no "Zen". All of these Zen patriarchs studied the whole of the Buddhist canon and mastered the concepts and principles to firstly attain mundane wisdom. After understanding the one principle, they abandon these teachings and focus on the mind.

It is also worthy to note that in old China, all the greatest scholars and even emperors were required to study the morality of Confucianism and the Buddha's Dharma. In that age, all the talented people studied this, instead of economics or the popular studies of today in wanting to make money.

It was only in the time of the Fourth Patriarch where the basic scripture for wisdom was switched to the Diamond Sutta, a text that allows a simpified understanding of inherent emptiness.

The Problem With Inherent Emptiness

Many people think they understand emptiness. They cling on to the concept of "emptiness", without realizing that the very concept of "emptiness" they have is birthed from the reversed polarity of their concept of "form". Their "emptiness" is a thought, a concept.

It is not true emptiness. If you are able to perceive true emptiness, this is already the stage past consciousness. The mind itself has stopped moving. You are already fully enlightened.

You see, there is a very strong word here. "Enlightenment" is not used simply. Compassionate light itself is the true nature of mind. But the moment you cling on to this concept, the true mind has already disappeared. You know where the Moon is, but it is difficult without cultivation, to reach the Moon.

Look into your mind.

Where does a thought come from? Where does it return to?

What exactly is a thought? A thought is any mental object. In fact, the sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches that you are experiencing are all mental objects.

Where do these sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches and thoughts come from? Where do they all return to?

Aha. Have you suddenly have had that slight pause of realization? This is exactly what all meditation techniques that advocate jhana is trying to reach.

Now what is a breath made of? Movement, yes. But how is it perceived? Tactile sensations. This is also a mental object. What happens when the breath is not felt in inhalation or exhalation? There is this gap, isn't there? Now this gap is not complete emptiness, but a simple taste of it.

Through your daily mindfulness, observe all of these mental objects - your speech, other people's words, stereotypes, habit patterns, sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, sensations, feelings - Observe all of them arising and passing away.

Where, do all of them pass away to?

What is the self? What is this identity of yours? They are habit patterns, clinging on to a past, clinging on to sight, sense, sounds, smells, touches and thoughts. So what happens when you pass away? What happened before you came into being?

Have a think about it. Realize that this is not so different from the breath.

All Thought Exhausted Returns to the Source

Everything that comes into being eventually passes away. This is the concept of interdependent origination of the Buddha. Anything that is birthed is also subject to death.

The very first spark of delusion is what generates birth, and after it is exhausted, it would surely pass away. However, due to our delusions, we continuously cling onto a myriad of things.

Now what does it mean by the saying: "All form is inherent emptiness, Inherent emptiness is form"?

All of the Buddha's methods are catered towards this truth. Yet, it is difficult for a deluded being to realize that they are deluded. Now study the following from the Diamond Sutra:

"The past mind is ungraspable, the present mind is ungraspable, the future mind is ungraspable."

The thoughts of the past are already gone. The thoughts of the future have not come. Both are illusions. What about the present? The moment you grasp at it, it is already gone.

The Buddha once compared our life to that of a river when talking to a king who was concerned about old age. He asked the king if the 3-year-old self was the same his current self. He then elaborates that the whole body has already transformed and aged, and is already no longer the same person. However, what is it that has not changed? Now, I leave that to you to contemplate and apply in your practice.

Remember. Practice is not the same as simple knowledge. To move to supramundane wisdom, one must engage in practice.

The Three Bodies

Inherent nature, inherent emptiness, inherent truth - This is pure, illuminated with light and exists independently to the world. This is known as the dharmakaya, or truth body.

The second body, the sambhogakaya, is derived from the dharmakaya, and is known as the physical body that manifests for your usage. Once enlightened, this is transformed into a fully-enlightened body, where spiritual faculties are opened fully from wisdom.

The third body is the nirmanakaya, derived from the dharmakaya as well, also what is used for teaching others. By means of the nirmanakaya, all Buddhas throughout eternity perform transformations of infinite differentiation.

i hope you have found something useful in this post.

Zen is not intellectual babble, lip-service or studying meditation sayings. It is exactly what the Buddha has taught - Jhana practice and attainment of wisdom.
