# The One Method that Encompasses All Pranayama

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 5 points · 2015-02-04

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2uppex/the_one_method_that_encompasses_all_pranayama/>

Firstly, what is the meaning of pranayama? Many people immediately think "yoga". But that is not its real meaning. To understand it, let us break the words up:

"Prana" is inherent wind.

"Ayama" is extension.

What exactly is an extension of inherent wind? To understand this, read the thread "What is the Breath?". There are four elements in the body that come together without mixing as a process of birth - and separate in their individual paths without mixing as a process of death. While as a physical body, this wind element can be stimulated via mind.

In fact, anapanasati is really a pranayama technique - It is just that you do not realize it. What it really aims to do is switch the wind movement from the airway into the chi channels of the body.

It is said that "chi and blood" is related. By opening the chi, it is inevitable that opening the various blood vessels will come as well. This usually results in a much healthier circulation.

In this post, I will share one of the most powerful methods that is used in Tibetan practice.

Before this, I want to tell you that in the case that you have truly switched over to the inherent wind, it is still not the peak of cultivation. You might experience light of sorts, but it is merely the mind becoming bright as a result of the opening of chi. It is not enlightenment.

Also, by withholding the breath, modern science has also found that the increase in carbon dioxide in the blood will cause a dilation of the airways, allowing a more efficient oxygen intake. All excessive mucus will be drained or expelled. There should not be any blocked nose or unequal breathing through the nostrils.

1. Sit in a lotus pose (half-lotus or sage pose).

2. Use the Simhamukha Mudra by pressing the middle and ring finger against the thumb (See this picture for an example.). Holding this mudra, place the back of your hand onto the outer edge of your thighs.

3. Now straighten your elbows such that your hands become straight "pillars" to spread your ribs vertically. This is aided by raising of the shoulders. The final form will be the back of your hand (with the mudra) as the base on the thighs, followed by the entire arm being erect and vertical, leading to raised shoulders. The head and two shoulders will form nearly a triangle. Now place your tongue behind the incisor gum and swallow to create a vacuum.

4. The reason for spreading the ribs vertically is to maximize the amount of air that can enter the ribs. By elevating the shoulders to that height, it takes off the pressure of the increased air volume away from the internal organs and allows maximization of the breath.

5. Now having done so, firstly turn your head to face about 45 degrees to the right. Lift your right hand such that it is extended fully to the right. Ensure that still in the mudra pose, the index and pinky finger are pointing upwards.

6. Bending at the elbow, you will find that this finger can reach the nose. Press against the skin of the right nostril and secure it in a way that only breath can pass through the left nostril. Ensure that while doing this, the right shoulder is relaxed and the left arm is still erect and propping the body up.

7. Breathe in slowly, as slowly as you can. Esoteric masters can breathe in as extremely slowly, but if you are new to this, do is as slow as you can. You might find that you cannot even last a few minutes and it can even be painful. But bear with it.

8. When breathing it, feel the area four fingers under your navel contract inwards towards your spine. The upper region of your body should expand while that area below contracts.

9. Once saturated with breath, as if your whole lungs are filled with air, hold this breath. Hold this breath as long as you can, to the point that you can no longer hold the air anymore. Masters can do this for this for hours. In fact, when you are in deep samadhi, you can hold the breath for extremely long periods.

10. In this period, you must realize that the mind must be still. No thoughts, no mental objects at all. No sensations, no physical feelings. The body must be relaxed and the mind calm.

11. When you can no longer hold it in, expel this force through the left nostril with all your force. Expand the area that you have previously contracted. Then draw the breath in again slowly and repeat steps 8 to 11 for another two times, a total of three cycles.

12. Now switch over to the left side, doing the complete opposite.

13. Lastly, switch over to the centre, using no fingers to block any nostrils. Use both hands to prop the body up.

14. A total of 9 cycles will done at the end. It is normal to have heavy sweating or even itching over the body.

You should not be tensing up your body.

When inhaling, you must contract the area four fingers under the navel!

Do not try to rush if you are new. You will find it difficult. Do not force it.

Keep the mind still, or you find it very difficult.

The shoulders might ache if you do not exercise well. This means your shoulder muscles are in poor health. Keep practicing. When erect, do not let the shoulders down.

When you breathe in or out, know that along with the thought, the mind has moved.

At the point in which the breath is held, the mind becomes "still", but do not try to conceptualize it. Just remain with as few thoughts as possible.

It might even be possible to reach samadhi through this technique.

The benefits of this practice is to allow you to get into states of deep samadhi, where the breath is stopped, more easily. It also forces the chi obstacles to "pop" open, as well as increase blood circulation.

You might find all kinds of physical phenomena happen, such as heat, itches, etc. These are all due to the sudden increase in blood circulation.

This can be done before any meditation session. It should be done daily, so that the body can stay healthy.
