  > 1)

  You have sat for 1.5 hours doing anapanasati but you are yet unable to progress further - It is highly commendable for understanding that something is lacking in your practice.

  Empty the cup first. Unlearn whatever you have learnt, and let go of everything that you think you know. What is currently in one's experience, start from there. What is not in one's experience, one does not know. Do not be fearful of claiming that you do not know. The Dharma is a scientific, step-by-step, observational system - it is only from recognizing that you do not know, that it opens up the possibility of knowing.

  Do not be so concerned over first jhana, observing this or that, achieving milestones, achieving 'access concentration’, and so on. On a side note, 'access concentration' is a made-up term, from the commentaries, particularly the Vissudhimagga, and has never been found inside any sutta. If the Buddha had deemed it necessary to use such a term, he would have used a synonym, or a term like this.

  Later on in this post, hopefully you will get a clearer understanding of true Anapanasati. If you understand well, or clarify any confusion by continuously asking questions when you are unclear, as well as practising accordingly, it is mevntable that you will progress easily. Such is the power of the Buddha's Dharma if he is understood correctly.

  > 2)

  What is wisdom? It is not a "gaining" of knowledge, but a re-discovery of what has always been there, right under your nose, but has not been perceived due to ignorance. For example, a spotlight shines on a spot, but unless the spotlight moves backwards, it will be unable to show what lies in the dark peripherals of the spot it shines on.

  True wisdom is not of the intellect. It can certainly act through the intellect, also known as mundane wisdom, but its source is from the Dharmakaya, supramundane wisdom, the body of the Buddhas, the Unborn, the Undestroyed, the Immutable the Eternal, and the Deathless.

  While one may feel that they are growing sensitivity, one is actually removing subtle forms of ignorance - of subconsciously numbing oneself. Again, they have always been there. For example, if you simply stop entertaining your thoughts and hear the sounds around you - You might suddenly realize the continuous sound of cars in the road, the chirping of birds in the distance, or the sounds of machinery around you. Sensations do not disturb you, they do not come to you, it is you who goes to the sensations.

  The reason why one is not able to feel compassion and love is because one's wisdom has not been developed rightly, and that one's view is not set rightly on the Path.

  Now contemplate on this - When you were an unborn fetus, inside your mother's womb - you fed on her nutrients. Whatever she ate, you took a portion of it. Whatever she breathed in, you took a portion of it. As you grew bigger, you stretched her abdomen - causing her great pain and sleepless nights. No matter how painful it was, no matter how much you continued to grow, to kick her while inside her belly, she bore it completely. Regardless of the suffering she bore as a result of your growing process, slowly eating at her sources, making her limp - she loved you regardless - she continued to eat, to take care of you, to radiate loving-kindness towards you - until you were born. After you were born, you continued to grasp at your parents' resources - and they continued to feed you, bathe you and shelter you from a society that was hostile to the vulnerable. What fortune! What love! What blessings!

  The Buddha said - it is impossible for a child to ever repay the kindness of one's parents. Such suffering they bore, but yet they held no ill-will towards you. Such loss they had, but they continued to give to you. Such blame they had, but they continue to praise you. Therefore, the karma of ungrateful children is heavy, for they have failed to understand that they were given a chance of a precious human birth as a result of the fruit of their parents' sacrifices.

  This is the first spark of love that a child learns while they are growing up - as such, this must be understood and contemplated well. To firstly understand loving-kindness, one must first understand the suffering of others. To truly love one's parents, for example, one must understand how wonderful their love has been for oneself. To truly love your wife and children, one must understand what pain and suffering they too have gone through in their lives. To truly love other sentient beings, one must understand how much they have suffered, how lost they have been for aeons and innumerable lifetimes, and how disastrous this whole mess of samsara has been.

  Gratefulness and empathy, therefore, are the basic steps towards the cultivation of wholesome states. Being grateful and empathetic, we can then take on the suffering of others, taking on their suffering, we understand how painful it is, understanding their pain, then comes forth the spark of understanding, of love, of kindness.

  The heaviness in the heart you feel is due to many things, and they will slowly unravel to you as you start to develop bodhicitta, the aspiration to benefit others.

  Another way to cultivate love, is to firstly train oneself to radiate love to others. For example, if you have a child, how would you love the child? Seeing the child being naive, innocent, and a reminder of the state that you were in when you were less conditioned - or perhaps a baby animal, or a plant - someone or something that requires help. In daily life, doing good deeds for other people, and seeing that kindness immediately reciprocate in the form of gratefulness, these are all training forms that lead to a loving mind.

  If one successfully trains in continuous giving and loving, regardless of what is reciprocated, I declare to you, that one will be protected by devas and powerful beings, unseen or seen - in myriads of ways. Help will arrive when one is in trouble and unfortunate events will be eased into resolutions. This is the power of the Dharma.

  It is not that one does not have intention - there is a difference between right intention and wrong intention. Intention that leads towards liberation is right; Intention that does not lead to fruit is wrong. Intentions that impede one's own progress, by grasping onto imaginary fruits like jhana or kundalini, will not lead to fruit. What is right intention?

  In the Buddha's own words:

  > “"And what, bhikkhus, is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The thinking, thought, intention, mental absorption, mental fixity, directing of mind, verbal formation in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this is right intention that is noble...a factor of the path.

  In other words, intention that focuses on the Noble Eightfold Path is right intention.

  > 6)

  What you are cultivating in Anapanasati is the wind element. What is the wind element? Air is still wind. Wind is moving air. What is it really air that is moving inside the body? Actually, this 'wind’ is referred to as a current, a movement, a sensed energy that seems to act like wind. One can certainly feel it in the form of sensation. For example, some breaths can reach only the throat, some can reach the chest, some can go as deep as the abdomen.

  wind in the atmosphere is formless, shapeless and unseen. Its presence is only detected when it generates friction against something. When it enters holes, it makes musical notes. When it blows chimes, it causes tinkling sounds. When it enters the body, it generates sounds and sensations that can be felt.

  However, it is like when you open a window in the room. At first, there is a current of wind due to the difference in wind pressure. Over time, the wind disappears and the room's air becomes as still as before. In the same way, this is what happens to the wind element inside your body. When the wind element in your body harmonises, it will quieten down into stillness. Naturally, the breath will seem to disappear at the nostrils.

  What is the window in this case? It is actually thoughts. When there are distractions and thoughts, the window is opening and closing, it does not open fully and allow the Wind element to harmonize. However when the mind is matched simultaneously with the natural breathing, such that it does not run off to other thoughts, then this mind becomes united with the breathing. Over time, just like the window opening, the Wind element will naturally harmonlse and come to a certain stillness without the slightest need for deliberate exertion, effort or intervention.

  4 am is just another hour on the clock. It is instead, a difference in conditions in the environment - different temperatures, movement of wind, distribution of yang and yin chi, and so on. In the body, the chi flows to different areas of the body at different times. That is all - nothing supernatural about it.

      Continued.

      You are grasping onto too many ideas and all of these are destroying your progress. As such, one might sit for even 5 hours and have not an inkling of progress.

      When one sits, be absolutely in the present. No past, which means no memories, no identity, no personality, no conception of who one is, nothing is brought over from the past. No future, which means no expectations, no assumptions, no theories, absolutely no fictional imaginations of the future.

      Not even the "present moment”, because at the very point of perceiving this present moment, it has already moved from future to past. It has already taken birth and died. Even if it is still there, it will die. Therefore, there is absolutely nothing that you should try to cling on to, because they are all 'empty of self’, temporary, hence empty in nature.

      Sit like a newborn infant, with no memories or expectations. You are not practising meditation, you are not practising this or that. You are merely sitting and being absolutely free of future or past. You are absolutely free of clinging. Just sitting, will you attain supernormal experiences? Don't be so wishful! Will you attain jhana? Stop thinking of the future!

      So what if one attains jhana? Jhana is still within the Form Realm. Jhana itself is not liberation! What is more important is the mental development. How does one’s mind react while in jhana, for example? As you sit there normally, still not feeling any bliss whatsoever, but your mind is focused, this itself is a type of Samadhi! It is a Desire-realm samadhi. Jhana is simply a samadhi that is within the Form realm. Whether it is this or that, what does it matter? Stop looking for the person who shot an arrow at you, and treat your own wound first!

      Now that you are in this state, fully present, then turn the awareness towards the nostrils and simply notice as the breath comes in, or the breath goes out. Do not follow the breath as it goes all the way down to the lungs. This is considered a distraction, a thought. However, in the background, you are aware of any sensations that surface. In the foreground of your attention, you are constantly paying attention to the coming in and going out of the breath at the nostrils.

      Paying attention does not mean scrunching one's eyebrows, tensing one's scalp or muscles, and generating a vice-like grip on the breath. Neither does it mean controlling the breath itself. I often use the metaphor of holding a jelly - Too tight and it is broken; Too loose and the jelly falls down.

      Let the breath be absolutely natural, let the body do what it needs to do. Sit up straight, let the whole body's weight rest stably on a correct posture, so that none of the organs will feel squashed. Just sit there and pay attention to the nostrils.

      If your mind is filled with thoughts or distractions, pay attention to the exhalation. While the breath turns around and is starting to exhale, feel the aches, pains, discomfort, thoughts, distractions, frustrations, and so on - and let them follow the exhale out of the body. At this point, the body is suddenly emptied.

      Then simply follow the breath. Know that it is coming in, know that it is going out. In the background of one's awareness, one still knows where the breath is going to. It might go to the chest, and suddenly one might feel very uncomfortable in the chest. What should one do? There are two remedies:

      1. One is to simply breathe the ache outwards together with the natural exhale.

      2. The second way is to use one of the Six Sounds, as referenced in this post. After one or two breaths, if done right, the discomfort will vanish.

      When the blockage vanishes, one simply goes back to the natural breathing. Eventually, this breath sensation will go deeper and deeper. It will then become as if this body has become recharged like a battery.

      When this body is completely recharged to the maximum, it will be just like a modern charger, it shuts off. Just like the breath, the breath shuts off. There is no need for any external source anymore. It is not completely gone, the breath comes and goes, but has an interval of a very long period before it takes in another breath. In modern analogy, it is like a phone going to 98% and being recharged to a 100%, then going to 98% and again being recharged to 100%.

      At this point, the entire body seems to be breathing individually through every single cell. The Kundalini is activated and opens up the various channels.

      When the body's blockages are completely emptied, the sense of a body completely vanishes. What sense of qi? Remember - Wind only makes itself appear in form of sensation and sounds when it hits friction - therefore, when there are no blockages in the body, there will not be any sensation of gi. Zero. In fact, there will not be the slightest sensation of the body, it is extremely light, as if it was cotton-wool.

      At this stage, one arrives at the Lao Zi's saying: "Becoming as soft as a new-born baby."
