# Concentration meditation failing but awareness meditation comes easy - what am I doing wrong?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **zuiper** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 4 points · 2 years ago

My goal is to master concentration meditation, so I have been practicing it for a week now. I don't think I have succeeded in holding focus on the object for even a few seconds.

Today, after another frustrating day of trying to build concentration, I whimsically decided to just become aware of anything that happened in my field of consciousness and not focus on anything in particular deliberately. Doing this felt automatic, and I could really tell that I was getting the feeling of 'being in the zone' that I've NEVER had with the former type of meditation. All in all, for my first attempt at awareness meditation EVER, and given my history of terrible attention span and derealization, the session went GREAT, whereas, the concentration thing, despite having experimented with tons of techniques and put in the required effort, I've been failing at it.

Does anyone have any idea what might be causing this? Also, I've heard that getting good at concentration meditation automatically helps the awareness practice, but not the other way around. How true is this?

  The goal of any type of meditation is to calm the mind, as if you were slowing down the currents of a pond, allowing the dirt swirling in it to settle. Once the dust settles, the mind becomes clear and light shines through. Some methods (like Tibetan visualizations and rituals) add so many things that eventually the mind overloads and falls into a calm state. Some subtract. Some add or subtract nothing, which is what you have described here. This is sati (mindfulness), with the frame of reference on mental objects. One can use either body, sensations, thoughts or mental qualities all as frames of references. If this works for you, I do not see why you should switch. It has been used by many cultivators, even that of Confucius.

  When you are “concentrating”, are you allowing joy and bliss to unfold itself, or is it a mental straining exercise? Are you distracted by myriads of thoughts, sensations and sensory objects? If one's object is the breath, then simply tie the attention to the breath and become sensitive as it allows bliss and joy to rise in the body.

- **mungojelly** · 2 points · 2 years ago

  My intuition is that awareness practice can help improve concentration. But I suppose it depends on how you approach it, what you take from it. They're both different ways of holding the same thing. So you can generalize lessons either way about directing attention, or also you could fail to generalize and just learn to control attention within one context or another.

  I feel like with the wider focus you might be more able to take energy for the meditation from the investment you have in the things that pop up, whereas with a tighter focus you'll need to put energy directly into the focus. A lot of people forget at first to put in energy. Even though all you're doing is focusing on the focus, you still have to do that, you have to apply your energy to doing that, or it's too slack. Don't just let go of your mind and hope it floats magically towards the focus-- that won't last a second, as you've seen. You need to put in a bunch of energy holding it continually to the focus (until you have lots more experience avoiding distractions).
