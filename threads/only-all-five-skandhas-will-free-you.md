# Only All Five Skandhas Will Free You

Only All Five Skandhas Will Free You

When you focus on the body, cultivating the body, you generate a habit that is centered upon the body - A bad habit like this can transcend lifetimes. This is one thing that one of my enlightened teachers, Master Nan, warned heavily against. Master Nan had the title of "Vajra Master", which means that he fully mastered all tantric teachings - and despite this, he never teaches tantra and often points to Zen as the most direct route. And this includes things like kundalini, chakras, etc - anything that can be perceived as a form. When one enters transcendental stages of meditation, their third-eye can open and see all of these structures as if they were tangible like our gross body structures. Therefore, this is what we call the skandha of form.

When any yogi tells you that they have opened all their chakras, have kundalini fully awakened, purified all their elements in their body, or anything of that like, they certainly have accomplished one thing: Purifying the form skandha. The bigger problem with this kind of method is that it forces consciousness, which is non-local, to be localized into the habit of being a body.

However, the Buddha taught that there are 5 skandhas in total. This simply means that the cultivator has reached a very low level of attainment, if one were to use the Buddha's standards.

What are the rest? They are the skandhas of Sensation (I like this, I do not like this...), Perception (function of recognizing and labelling), Mental formations (imprints conditioned by an object, or any process that makes a person act... including even speed, etc.) and Cognition (that which discerns and supports all other experiences).

These five, in total, make up the small Self. If one were to investigate into this Self, you would see that they are a mixture of things that seem like a tangible entity - But it cannot be further from the truth.

Just like how you would mix mud and water, but yet only see a brown mixture, when you mix the five skandhas, you have the illusion of an ego or self.

Therefore, if one is confused, then simply cultivate mind.

Your body cannot be taken along with you into your next life, neither can your chakra/kundalini transformations. Let go of all of these erroneous habits before they get imprinted. Simply go straight into the root source of Mind, and all of these will happen naturally.

If one follows Vajrayana Buddhism, then Mahamudra and Dzogchen are good paths. If not, Zen or the Chan sect of meditation are paths that will lead you straight to Mind.

It is only when one sees the Truth, then can one start to cultivate their bodies properly, as well as the other five skandhas, removing all imprints and letting go until one is fully enlightened.

            - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 2 points · 1 year ago

              > If this skandha is freed what remains?

              When the skandha of form is freed, four other skandhas of sensation, perception, mental formations and cognition remain. In other words, one has only attained the lowest level of purification.

              The skandha of form is the way an object appears to the mind, therefore it includes whatever "form" that can be perceived - be it the various bodies, chi, chakras, etc - which can be seen in higher stages of meditation, and hence are considered form.

              > Equanimity to pleasure/pain would be cleansing sensation, yes?

              Yes, that is correct. But "who" is experiencing equanimity? If you look deeply, there is no entity there to experience equanimity. There are simply thoughts that arise - "This is pleasure”, "this is pain" and "this is neutral”.

              The skandha of sensation is hence the way a perception is evaluated as pleasurable, painful or neutral.

              > I assume cleansed perception would be along the lines of "in what is seen there is only the seen..., etc".

              The skandha of perception, or conception, is the quantitative evaluation of perception. For example, by grasping the characteristics of an apple as "red", "crunchy”, "round”, we then have a deliberate mental discrimination which we form from what we perceive through sense contact. This skandha is involved in mentally classifying and labelling.

              > My understanding on mental formation is not proper so I won't deign to speculate.

              The skandha of mental formation, or volition, involves any kind of factor that impel us in various ways. This includes the "will", gravity, anger, greed, interest, habits, intentions, reactions, etc. In particular, it is possible to say that this aggregate is the core component responsible for karma. This skandha involves impelling behaviour, compounding karma that one clings onto and also projects content into future mental states.

              > The 5th skhanda vijnana (experience in sanskrit I believe) is called "consciousness" by many people who use it to say sat-chitta-ananda's consciousness cant have a self. But the term in latter is chitta and not vijnana... Do you have any views on this?

              This is what I call cognition, because the term consciousness is simply too loaded to carry its actual meaning in its use here. The skandha of vijnana is what is involved in the cognition of things. This is also the skandha to which the mind-only school talks about the Eight Forms of consciousness.

              The first five consciousnesses are the five senses and are unregulated, meaning they can come and go as they please. The sixth is known as the conscious mind, although there is also a shadow side. The seventh is the egoic mind, which is a delusion that "binds" all the consciousnesses together. The eighth is the alaya-consciousness.
