# Refine Your Practice

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 3 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2xof9b/refine_your_practice/>

If you are doing Anapana, here are some insights and reminders:

• Let the breath be natural. Do not force it. Bare attention.

• An inhalation is a thought, so is an exhalation.

• The reason why you say "out"/"in" or associate a word with the breath is because you want to become mindful that the movement of breath is linked to the movement of the mind.

• Once mind and breath synchronizes, you should lay your bare attention on the gap.

• The gap between the inhalation and exhalation (or thoughts) is where your bare attention should be laid on without interpretation or clinging to. This is "true anapana".

• You cannot treat this gap with logic. The only way to experience it is to notice the start (the ending of the inhalation) and the ending (the start of the exhalation).

• Remember, bare attention.

If you are hitting first/second Jhanas, here are some insights and reminders:

• Treat every meditation as a new experience.

• After every meditation, recall what were the conditions that brought you to that meditation and note it down, maybe in the Sangha journals in this subreddit. The conditions, such as what you ate, what time you meditated and what state of mind you had prior to meditation are all very good things to note down.

• You do not go into a jhana expecting a jhana. Instead, you go into jhana once conditions are met. That is all. This is especially so for the ones who have not mastered the jhanas (being able to freely enter and exit at will).

• If the breath has become feeble, then it means that the gap between the inhalation and exhalation has increased. This means that the number of thoughts have significantly decreased. You should be able to feel physical, pleasurable tingling. Switch your focus to this.

If you are reaching the third Jhana, here are some insights and reminders:

• Do not be frightened when you experience a sudden surge of intense sensory experiences. You might see unrecognizable faces, hear loud voices and sounds, etc. These are all the jhana-nimittas surfacing. There can be visual, auditory, tactile and all kinds of signs of absorption.

• What you need to do is to develop equanimity. Become unaffected by these things by simply not interpreting them. The moment you interpret them, and do not treat them with a neutral, bare attention, you are absorbed into them and you lose equanimity.

If you are in fourth, here are some insights:

• Your breath would already have stopped. Try not to be frightened and try to go back to your "safe zone". Many people stumble right out of the fourth because they are afraid of many things or become distracted.

Here are some insights for practice for now. Continue practising, and strive hard for enlightenment.
