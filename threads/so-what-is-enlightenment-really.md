- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 2017-08-11

  When you understand that everything is only Mind, you are awakened. To understand is not an intellectual understanding, but a direct seeing.

  1. The alaya consciousness is the embodiment of the original essence - but also where the fundamental flaw of ignorance arises.

  2. When the six sense faculties do not interact with the six sense objects, then these six consciousnesses will not arise.

  3. Not having perceptions of pleasure or pain, positive or negative perception - this is when the seventh sense, the discriminatory sense does not arise.

  Now when the first to seventh consciousnesses do not arise, the original essence will spontaneously reveal itself - pure, radiant, omnipresent - undying, unborn, uncreated, undestroyed, unchanging, perpetual - as such the three realms are ended and here is no more (unwilling) birth and death.

  What the Buddha awakened to under the Bodhi tree was dependent origination - the fact that all things exist through conditions. This is the ultimate goal.

  In the Chan teaching, there are Three Gates.

  1. When one penetrates the koan and realizes its reality, this is the First Gate - the first bhumi of Bodhisattvas.

  2. When one breaks through the second Gate, this is the state of birthlessness - the eighth bhumi of Bodhisattvas.

  3. When one breaks through the third Gate, this is the state of Buddhahood. All frustrations are ended - ignorance is uprooted - this is the state of ultimate tranquility.

  Koans in such a case is replacing all wandering thoughts - keeping the ignorance from arising - increasing 'conscious energy' - stopping oneself from indulging in pain and pleasure.

  This is not *using* the conscious mind, it is reaching a state of mindfulness without "effortful mindfulness". One enters the state of constantly pure thought - one forgets their mind and body, the chanting, and all conceptual thoughts cease to rise. In other words, it is an effortless mindfulness.

  Let me ask you then: Do trees, mountains and rivers have the wisdom of Buddhas?

  Yes, they indeed do! As the Buddha proclaimed under the Bodhi tree:

  > "All mountains, rivers, and the great earth possess identically the wisdom and virtue of the Tathagata. However, due to conceptual thoughts and clinging desire, they cannot attain such a realization."

  Matter and mind are One. As the Heart Sutra declares:

  > Form is emptiness, emptiness is form. Form is not different from emptiness and emptiness is not different from form.

  All things that have form and appearance are intrinsically empty in nature, manifested from the original essence. They have *all* begun from an arisen conceptual thought of ignorance - subsequently generating the six elements. From thus, the mountains, rivers, earth, moon, stars and ten thousands things are created.

                        - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 2017-08-13

                          Yes, there are four levels of enlightenment towards becoming an Arahant. These are related to the relinquishment of fetters - when the higher fetters are completely released, one is an Arahant.

                          When one is a Sotapanna, you no longer come back to the Earth - you ascend to the Pure Abodes. Once the life in the Pure Abode is finished, one then returns to become a human being - we call this a Returner. As to whether this incarnation is male or female, or to have certain characteristics - this is unfathomable - because this is simply to come back to pay back karmic debt. Thus, they return seven times in total. If one comes back as a Sotapanna, they often do not know that they are simply repaying the debt.

                          They are also said to be 'stream-winners'. What stream? It is the stream of the Noble Eightfold Path, the stream of sages, the awakening of the Tao entering into the flow of the Dharma nature.

                          To the common person, they are ensnared by pleasant realms and experiences and dragged away. However, to the Sotapanna, they do not enter sound, smell, taste, contact, dharmas. This is only like a stone pressing on grass - when the Sotapanna meets certain negative realms, the problems in the mind stream will reveal itself.

                          Then, we have the second fruit, the third fruit, and the last fruit being the Arahant. It is only reaching the level of the Arahant that has a significance.

                          The Arahant is 'birthless' - without any frustrations or mara obstacles. The mind is eternally pure and bright.

                          However - does this mean that the Arahant does not come back to the Desire Realm? One still does. The Great Arahants go into a deep samadhi of about 84000 great kalpas - in this time, the Earth is destroyed and rebirthed. However, when one leaves this samadhi, one still needs to move towards the Mahayana vehicle, using wisdom for liberation to become a Buddha.

                          It is only when one has become a Sravaka through the Hinayana - that one can then become a Bodhisattva.

                          In the genuine emptiness, there is no 'empty' realm that can be obtained. If one perceives an 'empty' realm, that is a side path. If one says it is 'boundless', that is again a side path. It is not emptiness! It is a hole! Why do people perceive it that way? It is only a matter of wisdom.

                          When a great Arahant is rebirthed, they are not muddled in the womb, however when they leave the womb, they are still muddled! On the other hand, a great Bodhisattva (eighth bhumi ground and above) will not be muddled in the womb or out of the womb! The difference here is one's samadhi-power.

                          Therefore, the Arahant itself is an extremely Noble fruit. By itself, it is as you say:

                          > ‘Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being.’

                          There is no actual "birth" of an entity - one is already in the stream of sages. The Buddha-nature is birthless and undestroyed. A Buddha manifests multiple emanations to the boundless Buddha realms and planetary systems.

                          The question is - "Who" exactly comes back? To the Arahant, there is no "being" that comes back. There is no "state of being". One's mind ground is pure and bright - without any rising of frustrations, mental agitations or fetters.

                          The only difference is one's wisdom - which we can say is divided into the ten grounds (bhumi) of Bodhisattvas to become a Buddha.

                          > So in essence, we must all eventually become Buddhas? Through however many kalpas and aeons it takes

                          Yes.

                        - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 3 points · 2017-08-13

                          When one truly enters into the Noble Eightfold Path, the stream of sages, one has awakened to the original nature.

                          > Does that mean that the Arhat's enlightenment enter a samadhi state of cessation that simply appears as if it's outside?

                          The Buddha-nature is birthless and deathless. The Arahant has indeed freed the fetters and stopped the 'birth' of the ego, relinquishing all frustrations and agitations. One enters the samadhi of nibbana with remainder - this is not the same as a samadhi of nothingness or samadhi of no-perception nor non-perception - there is no 'empty' state there to perceive at all.

                          > what/where do they come back to?

                          The question should be changed to: "Who comes back?"

                          And the answer is: "Who is left to come back?"

                          > Do they have to take rebirth again due to not having fully purified the alaya and the seeds thus compel them to rebirth; and if so, which of the realms?

                          Yes, one returns to "pay back" karmic debt. For one who has entered the stream, they will return as humans or devas in the pure abodes until the debt is cleared, and attain Arahantship within seven lives or fewer.

                          > Does that depend on the seeds of the alaya, or do they still have a choice when they return?

                          "Who" is there to make a choice?

                          > Can they for instance go to the Pure Abodes like the Bodhisattvas and continue their cultivation there instead?

                          Yes, when one is awakened to the Stream, they are certainly take human or deva incarnations in the various levels of Pure Abodes.

                          I explained more in the reply to /u/erickaisen, perhaps it can make things clearer.
