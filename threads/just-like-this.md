# Just Like This

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 10 points · 2016-10-03

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/55md6p/just_like_this/>

So goes the saying:

> > Before enlightenment, carry water and chop wood.

> > After enlightenment, carry water and chop wood.

In fact, after enlightenment, you might enjoy washing the plates and doing laundry even more. Simply cut out all that is unneeded, and do what is needed.

When moving, move. When sitting, sit.

What you call enlightenment is reverting back to the usual immensity of being human.

The five superpowers that arise from the four dhyanas, they arise certainly from the power of samadhi, but all of that can be used to serve the ego, if one is led on an errant path.

Yes, one can indeed go back to the age of the Buddha and be in the presence of the World-Honoured One, and listen from him directly. One can bend space-time, rearrange the elements, do all kinds of things. But all of that is useless, if you do not at least have this wisdom of enlightenment.

What is suffering?

> Birth is suffering, aging is suffering, sickness is suffering, separation from the beloved is suffering, not getting what one wants is suffering. These five clinging categories are suffering.

The Buddha taught this in his very first sermon, and he imparted a deeper wisdom: How to end suffering?

Friends, it is the Noble Eightfold Path.

Speak kindly, act kindly, think kindly, live kindly - Be loving in anything that you do. Be selfless.

On top of that, pay attention. This is sati. Whatever arises, you know that it arises. Just know. At least note it. Whatever goes, you know. Just know. Note it.

Whatever you do - Be it sitting, walking, eating, talking, exercising, shopping, working, reading, etc, your state of mind must be supreme. Know.

If you are unconscious, you are not living, you are like a stone. If you are conscious, you *know*.

Meditate, this is needed. As much as you can, find a retreated period of time of around 2 weeks and meditate. Even in your daily life, no matter how tired you are, simply sit for more hours. It is not difficult, it only requires effort and dedication.

If you meditate for 2 hours, extend it to 3. At least 4 to 5 hours everyday. If you are free for the weekend, meditate the entire weekend. If there is any excuse, then one simply does not want cultivation badly enough. Or perhaps one does not know suffering in its face yet.

An Olympic athlete has to train many hours to utilise their body and mind. In the same way, one has to train and strive. At least until you reach the first stage of enlightenment! Otherwise your human birth has gone to naught and waste.

When you love, you are present, you are keeping watch, you are intensely focused on whatever may arise. When you do not love, you cannot be present, your mind loses its grip and it slides into drowsiness.

Pick one Dharma-door, and do the best that you can out of it. You may mix just one or two more tantric methods to help speed up the process, but otherwise, it is perfectly fine to just use one proven Dharma-door.

There is no other way around it except practicing it. Remember that earlier I mentioned that Anapanasati is done throughout the entire day and even night. Sati is never abandoned by the disciple of the Buddha, for the excellent student knows that sati is what prevents the uprising of unwholesome states.

You do not need to go into the wilderness to be enlightened. That can remain when you wish to accomplish the trikaya and go beyond. For now, even the first stage of enlightenment will suffice. Many people cannot even reach this, simply because they are distracted all over the place.

The world is like a shifting desert filled with moving sand-dunes that never stay. Your body ebbs and flows with each breath. The heart pumps and relaxes every heartbeat. The entire world is filled with rhythm of rising and falling.

And along with this diligence comes equanimity. If you are eager, frantic and agitated, you have lost the point. When you are sitting in the middle of a traffic jam and getting agitated, you have lost the point. When you are agitated by a friend or relative whose behavior you do not condone, you have lost it. If you follow the dualities of the world, you have lost it.

It does not matter what it is - body, sensations, thoughts, mental qualities - all of them, simply know that they are there. If itchiness arises, know that itchiness is there. If you are a beginner, you can say "itchiness". But if you are advanced, simply know. Be actually conscious and *know* that it has risen.

If you use a meditation object like the breath, then simply go back to the breath after knowing that something has risen.

Simply like this, continuing like this, you will reach the dhyanas.

Do not fear, as a student of the Dharma, you are never alone.

      The jhanas in themselves are not liberation, they raise you to an altered state of consciousness which correspond to the various form heavens. However, going into the jhanas are required for one to develop strong enough "mindfulness-power" to gain insight.

      The first stage of enlightenment is stream-entry. This means that one associates with people of wholesomeness and virtue, listens to the true Dharma, practices appropriate attention and practices accordingly to the Dharma.

      One who has truly entered the stream (the true Noble Eightfold Path) will have the opening of the Dharma Eye (the direct seeing of that whatever arises will also cease). The three fetters of self-identity (in the five skandhas), doubt (about nibbana) and grasping of rites and rituals are cut.

                    - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 2 points · 2016-10-05

                      > But why is it that holding just one object is skilful?

                      In a sky with many clouds, you cannot see the blue sky behind it. When the sky clears and you see just one cloud, you easily see the blue sky behind it. In the same way, this object is like a training wheel - it helps you to eventually "cease the mind", as if you were letting a cup of muddy water rest, such that the mud rests on the bottom, and the water becomes clear as a layer on top of the mud.

                      > Why not multiple objects, and why is there even a matter of discussion of how many objects the mind sees?

                      There is much more insight to it than simply this. It is not about disregarding whatever arises. Instead, if things arise that distract the mind, one should *know* that it has risen, before going back to the meditation object. It is more about the state of mind.

                      > But in themselves alone, despite being the hallmarks of the path, what truth is in them?

                      You need to develop insight through deeper absorption. When the mind is free from thought, it becomes like a very strong magnifying glass that allows you to pick up deeper, subtler insights.

                      > No-attachment has been easier for me to accept as attachment is ridiculous and faith supports it.

                      The idea of "emptiness" is itself a thought. True emptiness is non-clinging. There is no idea of "rejecting phenomena". When something comes, it comes. When something leaves, it leaves. In this true kind of 'emptiness' meditation, one eventually reaches a deep stillness (extreme yin). And out of this extreme yin, yang energy will burst forth and bring about transformation, eventually going through the jhanas.
