# Time?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 4 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2vnhri/time/>

What exists now? Definitely not the past, definitely not the future either. If you choose to be in the past, well, you are in the past. If you choose to be in the future, you are in the future.

Worries, expectations, stereotypes, debt, anxiety, sadness, disappointment, regret - how do they exist right now if not by your own discriminative ability?

Our past is an entangled ball of thoughts. We say, "I love x.. hate x... support x... believe x... doubt x..." Then we think to ourselves, "That ball of memories and inclinations is me. That is my life. That is my history." What is the point of clinging to a story that is now just a dream? What did you eat for lunch last week? Why would you remember events when you think they are more important? Why remember a name? We even forgot previous lifetimes, why hold onto something within this life?

Our future is probably a slightly smaller ball but it is still a mess. Fears of the future. We say, "what if... then... if... but..." These are not there. This does not mean that you should not project to the future to think ahead of things. It means that you should recognize these thoughts as part of the whole dream. Once done, let it out.

Even the now cannot be grasped. If you cannot hold one thought in mind right now without it slipping from your mind, you have wandered away. That is the nature of things, they fade away.

There is a difference between isolation and non-attachment. In the same way that you walk through a crowd, you notice that the crowd is there. You still walk according to the gaps. People are still there, they do not vanish away. But once they have passed you, they are no longer there. Why be so serious?

Be an earthworm in life. Eat what you have, let it out in the other end. But know that you should not be clinging on to these things. The taste of foods, the dharma teachings, the methods of meditation... all of these are things that you bulk along with you. The reason why the Buddha advocates against these things is because you can find it helpless and want to grasp at these things subconsciously. The grasping cannot be underestimated. It was strong enough to give rise to a birth.

If you commit a crime or act unvirtuously, it weighs on you. You literally become helpless to the conflict of the enlightened nature within you. That becomes grasping. What is true repetance? Putting down the cause of the guilt. Sometimes we even go so far as to atone for these unwholesome acts.

In deep samadhi, you reach states where you forget yourself, the ego and even your name.

Think about it this way. If true nature is an ocean, we are waves that rise out of it and then disappear. As a wave, we think, "i am called a wave. I have travelled past so and so. I am born in the indian ocean. The other waves are always breathing down my back. What if i die on the shore? How much time do i have left?"

Never did this wave realize that it was the ocean itself. It cannot die. It forms the high waves, the tsunamis, the whirlpools, the high and low tides. But these are all merely names. Stop naming things, and find the thing that cannot be named.

      Excellent insight. If one can find a self, that self will be able to control elements of itself. However, we cannot control, but only see everything as a stream of cause and effect and impermanence - That is why there is no concept of a self anywhere in these phenomena.

      The past mind cannot be grasped, the future mind cannot be grasped and neither can the present. The moment you conceptualize the present, it has already gone into the past. However, as you say - There is something non-conceptual in "being". However, even "present” is not the word to describe it.

      If one were to remember a past life, it can appear as visceral as one's present life. What then differentiates the current experience from a past experience? The difference is how visceral the appearance of the sense objects are. In memory, things can fade and are less clear. In future, things are hazy and uncertain. The "present” is simply a case of where sense objects are at their strongest.

      Another example is this. If you were to go into the dream realms while sleeping, what differentiates the waking reality from the immaterial reality?

      You certainly have identified a layer that "seems" to stay regardless of change, but it is not completely condition-free yet - Anything subject to condition is compounded and hence subject to decay. It cannot be found in thoughts or words. It is ineffable.

      You are on the right track of investigation. Do remember that leading the eightfold path as one’s lifestyle is truly the way to the Dharma.
