# How do you know the truth?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 11 points · 1 year ago

When you read my words, how do you know if it is the truth if you have not experienced my words as truth? If you follow the Buddha, how do you know if what he says is truth? If you follow Jesus, how do you know? Same for Krishna, or any other religious leader, founder, teacher, guru, master, sage, yogi, etc.

Can you know the taste of an apple without tasting an apple?

If you read something from a book, do you think you realized it? If a master says it is like this, how can you know for sure that what he says is the truth?

You see, this journey of spirituality is not a journey at all. There is no journey. There is nothing at all. Nothing. You are either awake, or you are not. If you were in a dream, whatever you do in that dream vanishes when you wake up.

When asked who the Buddha was, he replied with three words: "I am awake."

Rather than read books and continuing to fill one's head with knowledge - instead, ask yourself if you are experiencing this truth or not. If it does not exist in your experience, ask why. Until then, do not believe it, do not disbelieve it - leave judgement out of the window. Not by logic, not by guru, not by attainment of the master, not by books, not by scriptures, not by anything - just you alone.

How can anyone know your mind better than you?

Yes, the masters see it. They are one with everything. But are you? They have tasted the apple, but you have not. That is why you should not be forming theories or trying to decide the end-point of something to which you have no idea about.

Instead, stop struggling. Let go.

Grasp on to nothing. Not even to the idea of "nothingness" or "emptiness”. Those, too, are experiences. Grasp not to the idea of "achievement” or "purification”. Grasp not to the idea of accomplishing anything. Just let go.

Let the mud fall to the bottom. Do not shake the glass anymore.
