# Being as Natural as Possible

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda*

Being as natural as possible

It can be very difficult to tell what is natural and what is not natural. Now the key here is the relinquishment of ignorance and delusion. Any delusion is in itself a form of insanity - a sickness - a poison - a vice - it indicates that one is grasping onto the notion of an idea.

If in one's whole life, one may have spent forming an identity, collecting bits and pieces from things around it, building a little scrap-man Frankenstein that they call themselves. On birth, parents give them a name to call themselves, slowly based on conditions, they slowly associate themselves with larger and larger concepts - "Mary does not do this, Mary does this."

Now all of these things in themselves do not have any harm. For example, a knife can be used to chop fruits or it can be used to harm others.

The narratives that arise in one's own mind about the situation, usually based on the clinging onto pleasure and aversion to pain, lead to problems. They aren't actual stories - if you examine them, they are individual thoughts that are linked together word by word.

For example, if you take apart the words "Mary had a little lamb", the words "Mary", "had", "a", "little", "lamb" all have their own meanings. But if you change the order, "Lamb had a little Mary" or "A little Lamb Mary had" completely distort the chain of interpretation in one's mind.

The thing that changes significantly is one's response to it. If the perception of your stimulated experience changes, it is usually the case where your response significantly changes.

Again, I should probably state again that none of the words in this thread are meant for any intellectual philosophy of any kind. If anything, they are just simple invitations for you to look into your mind.

Any neurotic obsession comes from repeatedly pulling back the same stimuli again and again - just like an ear-worm repeatedly and randomly surfaces in your mind's ear, or that favorite food appears from random triggers, etc. Repetition itself causes habit - in some people, they take familiarity to be what they like and are comfortable with.

Certain patterns of mind continuously arise spontaneously - with conditions - and if the response to it is continuous clinging, then it will be certain that such a pattern will only continue to arise repeatedly until the clinging is resolved.

Therefore, your practice is just one.

It is just one principle, however, it can be facilitated in many different ways, dimensions and perspectives regardless of religion or belief systems.

It is non-dwelling.

It is however, not grasping to the method of 'nondwelling', but truly letting go of whatever arises - body or mind - until all clinging is resolved.

Non-dwelling does not mean rejection or avoidance. Non-dwelling has no action in itself - it in fact has a huge action - a retreating away from the entangled ego-illusion into its original function of luminous emptiness.

This applies to any level.

For example, if you train in Anapanasati, you watch the breath. The breath should be natural and uncontrolled. The thoughts should be let go of. The mind should focus on the breath so that all activity ceases. Nondwelling in this case refers to simply watching the natural phenomenon without trying to 'add' anything - if your mind starts to move with false thoughts and you engage with them and entangle yourself with them again - this is not natural.

If you have awakened Yang Chi, and it moves through the various centers of the body, the mind will try to cling and respond again. Without knowing, you get entangled and the mind gets busy with delusions again. Not natural.

If you train in Zazen meditation, watching the stream of thoughts, not engaging in them, until they trickle to nothing, this is also non-dwelling. Trying to grasp onto 'nothing', this is dwelling. Trying to grasp onto the method of 'letting go', this is dwelling.

As you can see, no matter what part of the Path you are in, the idea is to stop the entanglement of the "Big Self" with whatever is impermanent. Let them come and go, their conditions have arrived, their conditions fall apart - it is all the way it has been.

This however, comes with many pitfalls - for example

- one must not mistake 'dead Zen' to be non-dwelling. If one finds themselves falling to sleep or getting sluggish, this is dwelling in 'oblivion'. If one seeks for temporary samadhi, this is dwelling as well. There are many ways to lose the Path, and these are all written in the Surangama Sutra.

Now there are stages along the way. Certain samadhis show that you are slowly unraveling the puzzle - they are milestones. As long as you do not think it is any kind of achievement, and simply let it be as it is, then you will cross that barrier. Again, one should not think they are bad or good - whatever it is, let it be.

Life should be as natural as possible. The ego is neuroticism, it goes against the natural function of compassion because it fails to realize that it is an illusion - that the real nature is the entire whole, unified with all sentient beings, sages, masters and Buddhas of all ten directions, instead of a constricted limitation.

Therefore, the instruction is simple, but it is very difficult to understand. It is to disengage from the illusion and return to the Big Self - the Ultimate - the Source - the All - God. In actual fact there is no return - there was no departing from it in the first place! Practice again and again. With patience will they be undone."
