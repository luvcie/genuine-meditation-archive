# The Right Grip on the Meditation Object

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 3 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2tlryq/the_right_grip_on_the_meditation_object/>

Whatever your meditation object is, it is always the case where attention has to be placed on it for absorption states to arise.

This meditation object can be anything: A koan, breath, mantra, visualization, flame, visual kasina, divine sound, etc.

All of them require the same kind of balancing of mindfulness. The problem with many people is that they do not realize that a certain degree of mindfulness exists.

If you let your attention too loose, the mind will drift and be distracted. If you exert your attention too tight, you start to engage muscles physically and tighten up the whole area, causing a headache.

What is the right grip?

Imagine grasping a jelly in your hand.

You are holding the jelly with the right amount of force so that the jelly does not slip out of your hand. However, you are also not holding the jelly so tightly that it becomes squashed into pieces or deformed.

Another metaphor that might be helpful is like dancing with a partner. It is akin to being sensitive to the meditation object and not mistakenly being too forceful that you start to exert unneeded stress on your own body and mind.

In this way, you should hold the meditation object in view. Doing so, as if doing nothing, dwelling in nothing but just the meditation object, it opens the route to deeper states.

May Dharma take you to peace.
