  To answer your question in regards to Jung, Jung's theory of the Collective Unconscious is not the same as Buddha Nature. Not at all. If you're trying to find parallels between the Collective Unconscious and Buddhist philosophy, the closest parallel will be karma. Karma has two influences; personal and collective or group. However, group karma is not the same as Jung's CU. The only similarity is that they both require more than one individual to function.

  When Buddhists say everyone has Buddha Nature they're only saying everyone has the seed of perfect enlightenment. Some scholars may say Buddha Nature means the true nature of self/mind. It's something we experience if we recognize it. We all can experience BN if we attain perfect enlightenment, and the experience of BN is the same because it is the true nature of reality. We all experience empty space the same way.

  As far as your personal experience is concerned, that's because of personal karma carried by the personal ego. That karma is in your 8th consciousness (alayavijnana). You see a window and 1000 others see that window as well, and that's because of group karma. We've all been "trained" to call that thing "window," however each of us are experiencing that same window differently. Your experience/perception of that window will not be the same as your friend's who's looking at the same window at the same time.

  As for consciousness and BN or enlightenment, your consciousness transforms into what's known as the 5 wisdoms at the time of enlightenment. There is no consciousness in enlightenment.
