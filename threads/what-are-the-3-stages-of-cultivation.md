# What are the 3 stages of cultivation?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 6 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2t9dgw/what_are_the_3_stages_of_cultivation/>

Let us talk about the three layers of cultivation. The Buddha has talked about this numerous times in numerous suttas and it has never changed its form. In fact, other schools of cultivation such as Taoism or Yoga have also understood the importance of this.

1. The first layer is Śīla (morality). Described in the Eightfold Path as Right action, right speech, right livelihood, etc, what exactly does Sila do on the Path? Morality can be described as something that is naturally done when a person has already attained liberation. By following Sila, one follows in the footsteps of an enlightened being and through emulation, is able to calm the physical and mental fetters. In Taoism, Christianity and Yoga, morality is also highly regarded as one of the "high practices". By practicing Śīla, one maintains a clear mind. As mind and body are linked, practicing a clear mind inevitably allows a healthy body void of problems for cultivation. In a way, Śīla clears the path and frees the road for the troubled dharma-seeker. There is a lot to be said about Śīla, but I will leave it to another thread to discuss about it. In fact, just by practicing this, one can, with ease, tap into advanced stages of the second phase. In Taoism, practicing Sila gives rise to Jing and physical health, which transmutes to Chi, giving willpower and vitality.

2. The second layer is Samādhi (jhana). Described in the Eightfold Path as well, what exactly does Samadhi do? In Samadhi, the mind becomes still. Just like how when muddy water rests, the mud sinks and there is clear water, the deluded mind rests and the mind becomes clear. In order to attain right samadhi, one must at least reach the fourth jhana stage, as described in this thread[1] . Now what does this concentration really do? While it can produce strange phenomena such as an "out-of-body-experience" and siddhis along with it, the real purpose of this concentration is to clear the muddy waters of the mind at various layers, allowing enlightenment to shine its factors through. Doing this, one starts to get insights naturally from absorption. In Taoism, practicing Samadhi will transmute existing Chi into Shen, giving insights.

3. The third layer is Prajñā (panna/wisdom). Also described in the Eightfold Path, what does Prajna do? Just like how in delusion, one starts to forget who one originally was, we come into being due to such delusions. This is also known as the Christian "Sin", which originally comes from the archery term of "missing the mark". Undoing these delusions and coming to the real knowledge (Dharma), one starts to uncover the layers of enlightenment. It is also for this reason that there is such a thing as "sudden enlightenment", where someone suddenly conceives of this Dharma from guidance of an enlightened being. This is the principle of the Zen school. In fact, what this "enlightenment" means is being enlightened to the dharma-wisdom. In Taoism, practicing Wisdom will transmute Shen into emptiness, the nature of No-Mind.

Have you noticed? The Buddha himself gave perfect instruction: The Noble Eightfold Path is amazingly broken down into simple and practical steps. Just keeping Sila, one makes entry into Samadhi. By keeping Samadhi, one enters Prajna. By keeping Prajna, one enters Enlightenment.

There are some misunderstandings to be straightened out though. For example, in Zen meditation, one ponders a koan. This aim is actually to get the Zen student straight into Dharma-wisdom. However, it is the case where even masters who become enlightened from Zen have to cultivate their own bodies and go through the layers of Sila and Samadhi. In fact, if a Zen student is not getting anywhere, it is mostly due to the malpractice of Sila and Samadhi.

All methods have their pros and cons. Meditations based on Samadhi can only get you that far, although they provide a stable base. You still need to progress through wisdom once Samadhi is reached. The right method is that of "no method". However, we are just too deluded to see the light on our own, hence we require a gradual systematic approach.

Knowing this, have you incorporated the Noble Eightfold Path into your life? Here it is again:

Sila:

1. Right speech
2. Right action
3. Right livelihood
Samadhi:

1. Right effort
2. Right mindfulness
3. Right jhana
Prajna:

1. Right view
2. Right intention

The N8P is the clearest way. If you are confused, come back to this path. Contemplate the Four Noble Truths.

Strive well and may peace lead you to Dharma.
