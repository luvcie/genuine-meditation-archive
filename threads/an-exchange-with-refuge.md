# An exchange with Refuge

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Gallo-Andrew** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 8 points · 1 year ago

Hi. I'm new here. I happened upon one of Refuge's posts and saw insight in it, so I messaged him. He eventually notified me about the subreddit. I'm joyous to be able to practice and discuss with you all. Once in a while I will be posting my progress, questions, and vision into what I'm seeing. Below I'll be posting an exchange that I had with Refuge maybe a week back. My hope is that there's something that everyone can pull from it. Maybe it can serve as inspiration, maybe it arouses questions, maybe you can even help me by pointing something out that I may have missed.

Strive on with heedfulness and diligence; release is in every moment.

> I spent the last few days devouring the posts on your subreddit. I sampled the meditation technique you prescribed and almost immediately experienced explosive bliss that suffused my entire body. Sustaining attention on the breath was effortless as I sort of "married" the breath and the bliss together. Combining these two things with a sense of contentment with the breath allowed me to sit with the pleasure for a very long time; I actually lost all track of time. There simply wasn't a need to go anywhere else. As the bodily pleasure began to fade what I was left with was this deep sense of contentment, and a feeling that everything was ok and would be ok no matter ! what happened, but then even that contentment faded. It "dropped” into this sort of state that was neither this nor that. It was quite luminous in terms of awakeness, but it felt as though someone could hit me in the face or scream at me and I would have not even responded to it. Is this the equanimity that you have been describing? After that I had the urge to get up and write you this message.

You can go deeper, simply continue to do so again and again. Bring the practice of mindfulness to every part of your

> I also feel like I awoke into some insight. It's about letting go. Let go of my concept about the world and about what I'm supposed to do in it. Words have only ever created more complexity in me and what I want is to be simple. I thought it was about achieving something, but I was wrong. It's about going beyond achievement. , Andrew needing to awaken and become a buddha is a concept creating by mental proliferation and language. What if I take all language and internal dialogue away? What is left is this awareness that illuminates anything that it looks at. The words try to describe it, but they're ultimately inaccurate. What I have without those words is raw datum. I'm going to stop trying to be anything because trying to be anything has only created problems for me.

Yes, that is a good insight. The purpose of language is useful though, since it includes the transmission to "point out” the Mind.

> Now the problem lies in sustaining it I suppose. I've been in this place before. This place where I've been incredibly compassionate and not trying to be anything. While the convergence points seem to be increasing in number and likelihood, I almost always drop back into trying to become Buddha; trying to be enlightened. How does "Andrew" die once and for all with nothing to take it's place? If I'm to trace the steps backwards, it was the extreme bliss of the meditation that allowed the mind to move into this state of super cognition of reality. It's almost as if the mind suddenly realized how to do away with problems and simply readjusted itself accordingly. Things from this point of view are far clearer than when I am not able to access the meditation states with bliss and equanimity. I am beginning to understand how you can describe everything so vividly and simply. Your mind has less burden on it and so can easily access what it needs to access. I want to let go into the same awakeness and understanding of Buddha.

States rise and fall away. What you witness, you are not it. You are experiencing samadhi states, but ultimately these states are still a realm of experience. It can be a realm of 'nothingness’, but this is still not the real emptiness, but simply a form of one thought over-powering the rest. There are myriads of samadhi states, but the main thing is simply to non-dwell and realize that all realms of experience are temporary. When the "you" concept leaves, the bliss naturally arises. It is simply the natural state of emptiness to which all things arise, but this emptiness is not the emptiness that you think of, or experience as a state. There is no "you" in these realms. There is no "you" in terms of cognition. There is no "you" in terms of your body or five senses. There is no "you" in terms of perception. Simply none. Excellent work, continue to strive.
