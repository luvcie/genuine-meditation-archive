# Miracles

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **dharmadhatu** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 3 points · 2 years ago

Hello all! I've been a student / practitioner of various "awareness-style" traditions since I was two years old (I obviously had no idea what I was doing at that age, but spent every Sunday praying with and learning from my guru). At the time it was Advaita, and now it's Mahamudra and Dzogchen.

Though I've been given assurances from respected teachers that I'm on the right track (supposedly having had some relevant insights), I feel that two things are missing from my practice: joy and wonder / mystery.

Recently I've come across an idea that thoroughly intrigues me. Here are a few takes on it:

e Ramana Maharshi's biographer, David Godman, writes that radical gratitude can result in miracles (see "We all think that we are in charge of our lives"). e A Course in Miracles teaches that radical forgiveness results in miracles. e This neat book suggests that radical self-love also leads to miracles. o A few posts from the ACIM and Law of Attraction subreddits suggested similar ideas. I'm not drawn to LoA because it seems to be oriented toward mundane and selfish purposes.

Could it really be that seemingly physical reality is not at all what it seems, and that "my" love has the power to directly influence it in seemingly impossible ways?

Buddhism of course talks about siddhis that come from the various samadhis, but the above idea is even more curious to me. By forgiving someone, I not only stop seeing them as bad or wrong, but they start behaving in ways that they wouldn't have before? And this is possible even without me interacting with them? Could the quickest way to end all suffering lie in this direction?

It's possible that Buddhism avoids mentioning this fantastic possibility for fear that students will become deluded or corrupted. Or maybe all of the above is nonsense. But I want to explore this possibility fully. It sparks a deep recognition I can't put my finger on, and that's usually a sign to dig deeper.

I've been told that some people here can possibly point me in the right direction.

With peace, D

  The first thing to note here is that people tend to attach too much significance to their thoughts. Your thoughts are merely a small speck within existence. To realize existence, one has to go beyond thought. Therefore, the first thing to note is that the 'gratitude’ or 'forgiveness' that you speak of is something not associated with thought, neither is it the emotion that many are familiar with as hormone-regulated.

  Then one might wonder - is it possible to love, be grateful and forgive without thought? The idea here is not to suppress thought, but to realize that there is a source of being behind thought. The original nature is boundless and undifferentiated - being undifferentiated it does not distinguish you and me, giving and receiving, or here and there. Therefore, unity is an expression of the original nature. When one surrenders, one gives up clinging to thought and goes into the natural flow of things, or what some will call "Tao".

  > Could it really be that seemingly physical reality is not at all what it seems, and that "my" love has the power to directly influence it in seemingly impossible ways?

  The love that people play with nowadays, is a hormone-fuelled love. However, if you are truly able to know love, then you will know that it is an expression of the very nature of the cosmos itself. This can only be realized by cultivation.

  > Buddhism of course talks about siddhis that come from the various samadhis, but the above idea is even more

  > curious to me. By forgiving someone, I not only stop seeing them as bad or wrong, but they start behaving in ways that they wouldn't have before? And this is possible even without me interacting with them? Could the quickest way to end all suffering lie in this direction?

  By forgiving someone, it is not about the other person, but more of oneself. It is treating yourself of poison before even trying to take revenge on who poisoned you. See this. Your entire life experience, is all about your perception, isn't it? When one bears hatred against another, one holds a heavy stone and feels tormented oneself. This is what is important. By simply abiding in the laws of nature, you avoid an endless cycle of war between you and your enemy. The repercussions are not easily seen by one who has no wisdom or insight, therefore, this point is difficult to express to you.

  However, by surrendering yourself to the voidness, the original potential of what it means to be human opens up. Wisdom, particularly, opens up. As you say, siddhis are merely a manifestation of one's original abilities, but it is very easy for siddhis to hinder one's wisdom, so they are often treated as side-shows.
