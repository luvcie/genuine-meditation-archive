- **Mailliam** · 1 point · 2 years ago

  You experience what is happening now through the sense doors. The truth is you can never 'stay' in the present moment because you are always in the present moment.

  So your question is actually about how to keep attention where You want it to, instead of getting caught up in thinking. I'm not familiar with what Ajahn Brahm recommends in the book but it sounds like it could be a general mindfulness exercise. Whatever arises in the moment, you are aware.

  Thought arise, you are aware, ah thoughts are arising. Sounds are being heard, you are aware, ah there is sound. Feelings are felt on the body, ah there is a feeling on the body. Images are seen in the mind's eye, ah there is image etc.

  You are not interested in the content of what is arising. Eg A thought like "I need to make sure I make lunch for tomorrow" is seen for what it actually is - thought.

  For posture, I use a cushion and sit in a Burmese style. One good tip is to have your hips slightly higher than your knees, so use as many cushions as you need to get there. Also I find a pillow folded in half works perfectly for me.

  Hope that's useful!
