# The Question of Vegetarianism

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Question` · 8 points · 1 month ago

This is a reply to /u/Emdaer and refers to the understanding of vegetarianism in the Surangama Sutra. I believe this will be useful for many people.

Again, I do encourage others to post questions on the forum, although I do respond to each message when I have the time, unless I have missed them. There are certainly quite experienced cultivators among the sangha and many will benefit from an open discussion as opposed to a private-message.

He asked a particularly interesting question, about this passage in the Surangama Sutra:

> ' "Bhikshus who do not wear silk, leather boots, furs, or down from this country or consume milk, cream, or butter can truly transcend this world. When they have paid back their past debts, they will not have to re-enter the Triple Realm. 6:27 "Why? It is because when one wears something taken from a living creature, one creates conditions with it, just as ' when people eat the hundred grains, their feet cannot leave the earth. Both physically and mentally one must ' avoid the bodies and the by-products of living beings, by neither wearing them nor eating them. I say that such people have true liberation. 6:28

The following is my reply.

There are a few cases that have to be absolutely clear here, and most of it comes from translation problems and misinterpretation. What does it mean to be vegetarian?

The Chinese words used for this is "Chi Su'. There is a specific meaning for this. For example, when you look at the Surangama Sutra, 'Chi Su' means to simply not eat the five pungent spices of onions, garlic, chives, green onions and leeks. Why so0? Because these foods are highly stimulating. This 'Chi Su’ did not include meat.

The part about not eating meat refers to the precept of 'not taking life’, not 'Chi Su'. Therefore, do not be confused. Not eating meat is for the sake of cultivating compassion for all beings, including animals.

First and foremost must be this - the mind must be absolutely pure and freed from greed.

The Buddha declared in the Brahmajala sutta:

> - B "A disciple of the Buddha shall not himself kill, encourage others to kill, kill by expedient means, praise Killing, rejoice at witnessing Killing, or Kill through incantation or deviant mantras. He must not create the causes, conditions, methods, or karma of killing, and shall not intentionally kill any living creature.”

He also allowed 5 types of pure meat, on the condition that the individual is not able to eat meat on a long-term basis due to reasons medically or due to one's environment:

1. one did not slaughter the animal personally

2. one did not instruct others to slaughter

3. the slaughter was not committed for one's sake

4. one did not witness the slaughter

5. one did not hear the cries associated with the slaughter

Another criteria used is as follows for the 5 kinds of edible meat:

1. One did not see the slaughter

2. One did not hear the slaughter

3. One did not expect the slaughter

4. The animal committed suicide

5. The leftover meat eaten by fauna

One of the most important things here is to ask oneself: Can you call yourself a disciple of the Buddha if you eat meat with the intention of enjoying meat?

One should know that for someone who continues to eat meat in such a manner, if one should stumble upon a type of samadhi realm, they will still only be a demon, a wandering evil spirit. When one's positive karmic reward is over, they will sink into a sea of bitterness and no longer will be a Buddhist disciple. One will not be able to escape samsara. It is like binding oneself with tight rope and pulling it tighter while trying to escape from it.

With this same principle, one shouldn’t use the blood and meat of these sentient beings as clothes to wear. Why? Because whenever you use a portion of an animal’s body, it will still have a karmic-relationship or function by which it has injured its biological life.

Take note of this! It is subtle but exceedingly important in one's cultivation.

Strive well and may you attain enlightenment in this lifetime.
