  You can note it, but it is simply like training wheels. What you want to pay attention to is the "knowing nature" which knows whether the breath is moving in or moving out.

  So if you have trouble with knowing whether the breath is moving in or moving out, then noting it can be useful. But after a while when your mindfulness has increased, you will find it natural simply to let go while residing in the knowing nature.

  I suggest you also read the thread here and see the post here as it may clear some things up for you.

  > Also, as i go deeper by watching my breath go in and out, what will I be eventually one pointed in?

  I find it is more about the mental state than "what you become one-pointed in". That is to say, the mental state itself becomes one-pointed.

  In fact, concentration is by itself a state of letting go, of surrendering to the object of attention. Think back to when you were a child watching the television. You felt it was fascinating and attention naturally happened. Similarly, when we watch the breath and let go into the breath, observing it as it moves in and out of the whole body, watching it with a similar form of awe or fascination, love or gratitude, mindfulness naturally happens.

  And as the breath quiets down, the thoughts quiet down; as the thoughts quiet down, the breath quiets down. Eventually, you reach a point where the breath stops. From here, one simply lets go and rides the phenomena that occur as part of the absorption residing in the knowing nature.

  That has been my experience. I hope that helps. I hope others here may shed some light on this as well to help you, in case my explanation has not helped. I can recommend looking through the archives on the subreddit as there are many posts that go through this as well. Strive well!
