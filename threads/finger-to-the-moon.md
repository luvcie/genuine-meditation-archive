# Finger to the Moon

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 6 points · 2016-07-24

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/4ucw8j/finger_to_the_moon/>

All phenomena have no inherent characteristics. Any characteristic that is defined to make up an appearance of phenomena is conceptually constructed by the mind.

Energy appears but has no independent existence that separates itself from the mind which gives names. If you were to see energy as something without a name, what would it be? This is what is referred to as "all phenomena are inherently empty".

You can call a plot of land by its country name. However, it is the mind that has designated borders and placed a name on it. Without the concept of a country, the land is simply the land. Emptiness is in a way, the appearance of phenomena without its fictional labels and descriptions.

The Dharmakaya is that which knows. It has no mental or perception object of attention. It never holds an object of attention, nor is it capable of doing so.

No matter what objects of inner experiences appear, the Dharmakaya is what's knowing without being "stained" by those appearances. As such, practice cannot *improve* the Dharmakaya, neither can anything cover or obscure it. It is what you are, this is your true nature.

Imagine a vast ocean of Mind. From this mind arise waves of all kinds of shapes and sizes. After a while, they return back into the ocean without a trace. Then other waves arise, and then return. They do so in an interdependent manner, one wave leading to another. A Buddha arises momentarily and then disappears back into Emptiness, just like all momentary waves.

The distinguishing unique shape of a wave that rises gives a temporary sense of being an "independent existence". However, give it time and it will merge back into the Emptiness.

When at its peak, the wave sees itself as independent and with an identity. It doesn't see that its power comes from the ocean of Emptiness. It sees the other waves peaking in apparently independent existence as well, and does not see how all of them are momentary expressions of the same ocean.

Each wave then becomes desperate and intent on maintaining their forms. However, they continue to change and fall apart. Slowly, they then die, not knowing that their nature has always never changed - They are the ocean's water, they are identical to all the other waves in the ocean, and they have never been separate.

When "you" as the wave realizes that it is emptiness, the karmic mind self-liberates.

One might say: "I had it, but I lost my state of pure awareness."

In that case, that was not the Dharmakaya, for the Dharmakaya cannot be lost. However, thoughts of "being it", "not being it", "being in it", "losing it", "gaining it" do arise and fall.

The contents of what it sees changes, but the Dharmakaya does not change the slightest bit - it is empty, so how can it change?

The subconscious mind of karmic suffering brings together all kinds of characteristics to make you believe that you are a self, bound together by things such as memories, conditioning, feelings and self-images.

If you were suffering from a complete loss of memory, and an inability to think of the future, who are you then?

"Who" lost it? You cannot lose the Dharmakaya, because you can only lose something that is seen by the Dharmakaya. If you lost it, then you cannot even know that you have lost it.

There is no self, no identity, no personality, no being!

Now how does one reach that realization? This is what meditation strives to do!

In the crown, the white sphere has the sound "Om", which sustains the formation of the energetic body. Our seemingly physical body is how we express our actions. This represents the Nirmanakaya.

In the throat, the ruby sphere has the sound "Ah", which expresses meaning through vibrations in phonetics. Our chakras, kundalini, etheric body is how we radiate the radiant subtle dimension of Mantra and Light. This represents the Sambhogakaya.

In the heart, the deep blue sphere has the sound "Hung", which is Emptiness itself, from which all phenomena arise from. This represents the Dharmakaya.

All three make Buddha-nature.

The path is a regression from the Nirmanakaya to the Sambhogakaya, and then to the Dharmakaya. They seem separate but arise from the Dharmakaya, and that is why all of them are also empty in nature.

The emptiness of Dharmakaya is the pervasive, non-dual radiant experience with awareness. The emptiness of Sambhogakaya is how your emptiness illuminates as a creative meaning. The emptiness of Nirmanakaya is how emptiness has the ability to make things appear solid and real, from which samsara magically appears.

This instruction is a method for those with wisdom - vibrate "Om" at the crown on the out-breath, then "Ah" at the third-eye, and lastly "Hung" at the heart. This is an introduction to the luminous clear mind.
