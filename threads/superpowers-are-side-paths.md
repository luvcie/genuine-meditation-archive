# Superpowers Are Side-Paths

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 13 points · 1 year ago

This is just another post to reiterate a very important point.

I have repeated this many times but this cannot be emphasized enough. In fact, my teacher used to add this disclaimer after every time he talked about some kind of siddhi.

All it takes is just one shift, one misunderstanding, at the higher levels, and one falls into the demon horde of Mara, blinded by them. As long as anything has form, you must see the emptiness in it.

Once, I knew a mystic who had very strong siddhis. He could manipulate physical nature, changing the volume of a music player just by focusing on it. He healed people just by sending his intention. In meditation, he felt a qigong master bestow upon him powers. He could 'absorb' powers from the sun and trees around like spirits do. He could see the various different lights above the heads of people. He then aspired to achieve the power of the Eight Immortals of Taoism. He even practiced the skill of leaving the body. However, along the way, his morality became horrendous and vile. His life started to take a turn for the worse. Eventually, he became suicidal and depressed, and eventually abandoned his entire ten plus years of gongfu work.

Why did this all happen?

It is because of the mindset that one has. This mystic became prideful, arrogant and believed that he was acquiring powers. His ego grew as Mara tempted him to sink into that trap even more, promising him 'eternal life' and to rise above all other men.

This has never been the goal of enlightenment. Enlightenment is to see clearly why we suffer, and to end this suffering.

In Buddhism, we seek to eliminate the illusion of a self, because it is in this self that suffering arises. The difference between this and yogic art is in the wisdom that we seek.

There is no 'person’ that becomes enlightened. We live lives virtuously not for ourselves, but for others.

The moment you chase a siddhi, you are going down a side-path. Look at the impermanence of all things. What has form and appears in front of you cannot be "you".

Do not shift your need to cling to something as the ego to spirituality. Spirituality is about destroying yourself and serving the greater good. There is "no one" that helps, and there is "no one" to receive.

Practice without the mindset of acquiring any supernatural powers. Throw it all away. All of them are fantasies.

Even if the Buddha appears in front of you, you chop his head off.

Before my teacher passed on, he said - What supernatural powers? In my entire life, I have never seen supernatural powers. These are just delusions.

The Dharma is right here, right now.

Honoring your loved ones, honoring your friends, honoring your society and the world.

Do not see yourself as hero to save the world. Save the world? That must be a joke, because nature does not need you. Thousands of humans die everyday. Thousands are reborn. Take care of your own ignorance first!

In fact, we are more stupid than others. We don't know anything, we don't even know ourselves. But it is also because we don't know anything, and that we drop everything, that we know everything.

Only an empty cup can be filled with water.

              You achieve mastery through spending more and more time dedicated to deep samadhi. This is something that comes over time, like any other skill, the fourth jhana is the completion of your absorption, its perfection if you would, which is why it serves as a spring board for other things; if you cannot enter into the lower jhanas at will, sustain access concentration (at the least) throughout the entire day and control how long you're in jhana, able to move through them with ease, then you cannot really attain the fourth jhana; which again is the product of mastery...

              This comes like any other skill, through time. You can work on both insight and concentration without accessing the fourth jhana, it just is further refined and deepened in the fourth jhana. Awakening can occur through a variety of things, I've spring boarded to the fourth jhana in the beginning of my experiences with them and then fell back down to the first/access concentration afterwards and then slowly have worked my way back up through them, really saturating myself with them and allowing them to become more baseline.

              You can have moments that are sporadic and result in higher levels reached, but really the key is to make this sustainable, reliable, and ultimately effortless.

              You can lose them through the desire for them and the subsequent attachment to them being hindrances to accessing that deeper state of mind. If you place them on a pedestal, there is a chance that you will be blocked from progressing deeper and deeper in jhana as well.

              Insight can and should be cultivated alongside concentration, this keeps both balanced, otherwise you end up with either dull concentration or unfocused contemplation and thought becomes disorganized.

              Reach states of concentration then begin contemplation and insight meditation, you do not need to perfect concentration before you start insight cultivation.

              Jhanas should be the result of surrendering the self, surrendering completely to effortless being, without any frame of reference and without any contrivance; this is possible in deeper and deeper levels only through the cultivation of insight. If there is no insight into the nature of being, the nature of reality, the nature of mind, then there can really be no true jhana. Jhana should ultimately be jhana with suchness, with mind, as its object, which is without the sensation of subject observing and object observed; this is the perfection of jhana, the samadhi of suchness. This is only reached with insight.

              Anything else is just another form of conditioned samadhi, another form of contrived jhana; this is thus distinguished from jhana [proper] which is unconditioned samadhi or vajrasamadhi, samadhi of suchness; which is the result of true mastery of samadhi, of jhana, through insight.

              but anyways it all starts with first learning to abide and stay there more and more, exposing yourself to it more and more, and letting it change your mind more and more.
