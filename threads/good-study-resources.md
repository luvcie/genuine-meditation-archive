# Good Study Resources

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 5 points · 2 years ago

While practicing sila to the utmost discipline, remaining in mindfulness throughout the day and night, you should also study the words and teachings of enlightened ones. Some have asked me for good resources to read, and I have compiled a few (which is not complete yet but good reads).

Remember that reading something does not mean that you have come to the realization by yourself. Thinking that you believe the words and hence thinking that you have realized it yourself, is a reversal of cause and effect. In the words of one of my late teachers, this is completely erroneous and detrimental to one's progress.

This list will be updated in future and expanded upon.

The Discourses of the Buddha (All 5 Volumes)

Mahayana Suttas (particularly the Heart Sutta, the Lotus Sutta, the Vimalakirti Sutra, the Surangama Sutta and the Diamond Sutta)

Tao and Longevity by Master Nan Huai Jin

Diamond Sutra Explained by Master Nan Huai Jin

Yogacarabhumi-sastra

The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali

Vijnanabhairava Tantra by Kashmir Shaivism (Methods on emptiness cultivation)

Six Yogas of Naropa by Glenn Mullin (Basic tantric yoga practice) - The particularly valuable part of the book is the commentary by the enlightened Lama Tsong Khapa

Be As You Are: The Teaching of Sri Ramana Maharshi by David Godman

I Am That: Talks With Sri Nisargadatta Maharaj

Consciousness and the Absolute: The Final Talks of Sri Nisargadatta Maharaj

Pointers From Nisargadatta Maharaj by Ramesh S. Balsekar

Master Key to Realization by Shri Sadguru Siddharameshwar Maharaj

Three Texts on Consciousness Only by Numata Center for Buddhist Translation

Traditional Theory of Evolution and Its Application in Yoga by Manmath Gharote et al.

The Awakening of Faith by Asvaghosha

Fundamental Mind by Mipamgyotso

Vasistha's Yoga by Swami Venkatesananda

Vijnana Bhairava by Kashmir Shaivism

If you want to find more resources, you should look into the various books by Christian mystics, Sufi sages, Kabbalists, Yogis, Taoist sages or philosophers like Plotinus or Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, and perhaps the Shamans.

What you want to know in the books should be as such:

e What is suffering?

e What causes suffering?

e What is the end of suffering?

e What is the path to the end of suffering?

As you well recognize, these are the Four Noble Truths. Using them, investigate the root of ignorance, the destination and the method on how to get there.
