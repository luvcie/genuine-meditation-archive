# What is an object of meditation?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 7 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2t1m5a/what_is_an_object_of_meditation/>

Think of this object of meditation as a type of "anchor". It is the only immoveable thing among the sea of compounded things. It only serves one function: That is, to hold the attention of the meditator.

Not all objects are equal, however.

Certain objects "cease" on the way up the Jhanas. For example, the breath itself stops when you reach higher form Jhanas - This switches to the circulation of chi within the body.

A mantra ceases when the sound sense field ceases. A visualization ceases when the sight sense field ceases.

Most objects of meditation serve just one function: To get the person into right samadhi (That is, the state of Jhana). When one attains Jhana, the contemplation becomes that of the factors of each stage of Jhana itself.

For example, if one is using the breath, when the breath exhausts itself, one switches to the physical and mental tingling joy in the first Jhana. It then becomes a very systematic approach in the Jhanas.

This is the reasoning behind usage of an object of meditation.

Also, this is why the Zen school of Buddhism teaches a strange object of meditation: To ask koans such as "Who is meditating" and hold it there. In such a practice, this object of meditation does not cease at all until the very last Jhana. However, in the end it still ceases. The biggest problem with Zen is that they often do not realize that they do need to get through the Jhana stages as well. Both physical and mental cultivation must be achieved.

In such a way, you may also see why using different "forms" of meditation can allow someone to reach similar experiences.
