# Dependent Origination & True Practice (Against Dry-Insight & Concentration practices)

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 7 points · 2017-03-14

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/5zdpaj/dependent_origination_true_practice_against/>

This post is in response to a lack of understanding of the 12 links of dependent origination.

---

This post will be a seal towards liberation through the Arhat's nibbana, the fruit of the Hinayana, and will cut through misconceptions and present the right teachings of the Buddha.

The Tathagata declared The Four Noble Truths are as follows:

1. There is suffering.

2. The cause of suffering is *craving*.

3. The cessation of suffering is the cessation of *craving*.

4. The end of suffering is reached by the *Noble Eightfold Path*.

Listen very carefully to this, because the key in practice is already demonstrated inside of these four Noble Truths. We will come to this later on.

In the age of the Buddha, there were many Brahman views and practices. All of these erroneous views have been described in the Brahmajala Sutta as the 62 views of 'self'.

One of the Buddha's teachers taught him how to control his mind and develop one-pointedness. It was believed that by forcing the attention upon one single object, this controlling would overcome desire, suppress it, hold it down and subdue it. This is what many people erroneously practice today as **'concentration practices'**.

The Buddha then realized that it was impossible to attain genuine enlightenment from such a practice, and started to practice physical austerities, thinking that if he could control the body, he could then control the desires. He fasted extremely, deprived the body and believed that this disciplining of the body would control and hence subdue desire. This is what many people practice today as **'discipline practices'**.

They believed that there was a Higher Self, an 'I', that had the power to subdue desire and have total control over it. These practices had one thing in common - **The idea that desire must be subdued by control or suppression**.

**However, when one gets out of meditation, why is it that the desire comes back?** No matter how blissful the states he entered were, why was it that he could not eradicate this desire entirely?

At this point, the Buddha realized that these were not the answers to his question, and infamously sat down at the Bodhi Tree, swearing to find the answer and never get off his sit until he did.

In the wake of a full moon, the Buddha entered nibbana through the Middle Way. However, what was it that the Buddha awoke to?

The Buddha awoke to Dependent Origination.

**Do not take this lightly.** Wherever Dependent Origination is not found, it is absolutely difficult to find true liberation.

Dependent Origination is the perfect description of how desire arises, how it leads to suffering, and also how cessation of suffering can be attained.

All things in body and mind are conditioned. What does this mean? It means that it has a previous cause. Just like dominoes, one cause inevitably leads to the next.

The truth is that there is no such thing as an 'ego' or a 'self'. This thing that you call a self is something that happens without control or intervention. There is no 'self' contained within it that can intervene and produce things in body and mind.

"No one" makes a decision. There is no 'self' behind that decision. Modern science has shown that decisions are made subconsciously for a few seconds before the person is consciously aware of it. These decisions are conditioned by past actions. There is no 'permanent self' that is making a decision!

Therefore, one has to recognize this, not take it as 'self' and hence not to grow attachment towards any step of the process.

The body and mind has to develop together - as such, absorption and insight has to be yoked together in practice. They should not be split.

Here are the 12 links of dependent origination:

1. **Ignorance**: The lack of realization/understanding the Dharma.

2. Formations

3. Consciousness

4. Nama-rupa (Name & Form)

5. Six-fold sense bases

6. **Contact**: For example, the contact between a sound and the ear-base allows the ear-consciousness to arise.

7. **Feeling**: After the feeling associated with the sound, perception arises as 'Pleasure', 'Unpleasant' or 'Neutral'.

8. **Craving**: Following perception, craving arises as 'Like', 'Hate', 'Disregarding'. There is a physical manifestation of tension that can arise from this, which is especially felt around the scalp and other areas of the body.

9. **Clinging**: This is where thoughts arise, building a story that gives a reason for the choice of craving.

10. **Habitual tendencies**: Here, there is a arising of an urge towards doing something, following the story that has risen in your mind. This is the arising of the 'involuntary decision' that we have discussed earlier on.

11. **Birth**: This refers to the birth of actions. Having the drive and made the decision, now the action is made. This can be in form of body, speech or mind.

12. **Aging & Death:** This is sorrow, lamentation, grief and despair.

This goes in a cycle, therefore, after 12, it would revert back to 1 again, and this repeats without cessation.

---

Now here it is important to list the reason for which one may enter a semblance-jhana through 'tight concentration', and how this differs from the Dharma that the Buddha has taught.

In tight concentration, we call this a yogic, one-pointed, concentrated state where one loses senses externally. You are only aware of the mind in this state. When attention wanders away, the practitioner grips onto the object even more tightly, banishing away the hindrance. Such practices take years to develop, however the biggest problem is that craving still remains within the practitioner, subtlely.

One can even do an experiment on one's own - Enter this tight-concentration state, and one can feel its power very strongly. It radiates, shines, and one can feel as if it is very exuberant. However, when the session ends, the experience disappears after a few days, weeks or months. There are hardly any permanent changes that happen to the meditator after such an experience.

If one does this well, one might observe that this tight-concentration practice leads to tension, tightness, or even other hindrances like torpor, anxiety, lust, etc.

This however, was not what the Buddha taught. This is the method of the Brahmans!

I have often used the word 'one-pointed attention' here. It is repeatedly stressed that this does not mean concentration. Instead, the meaning of *sati*, mindfulness, was repeatedly emphasized.

*Sati* is the function of the sixth consciousness, the knowing-nature. This means that simply *noticing* something is already knowing it.

For example, do you have to exert your force to know that a sound has contacted the ear-base? No, the ear-consciousness arises, and then 'knowing' arises. **There is no need for any effort there!**

In anapana-sati, one notices the in and out movements of the breath. However, one does *not* exclusively focus on the breath without being mindful of anything else!

This is because your tendencies are going to rise up in meditation. This is where *sati* comes into play.

In anapanasati, one is supposed to calm down both mind and body - not 'excite' it! One relaxes, tranquilizes, releases - Simply knowing. When tension arises, one releases.

This release of bodily tension is actually combined with the release of the craving! Try it out for yourself.

When one reads the Anapanasati sutta, it is very clear that the Buddha did not intend just the breath to be focused on. He introduces many other things, such as being mindful of the bodily agitations, whole body, mental agitations, etc.

Therefore, there is a huge mistake in translations of the Pali suttas. In the Sanskrit suttas translated to Chinese, samadhi is translated as 'steadiness' or 'even-ness', meaning tranquility. On the other hand, Pali suttas translate samadhi as 'concentration'!

In tight-concentration states, one can enter highly blissful states. They do not understand that this is simply temporarily forcing the mind to revert 'nearer' to its original state. It does not allow the hindrances and desires to die away. **The moment they stop forcing it, the hindrances and desires arise again, obscuring their original nature.**

Using this method of releasing, relaxing and calming down the bodily agitations and subsequently the mental agitations, through simply 'knowing', allowing the desires and hindrances to die out, this practice will not take years to accomplish. One can enter within this lifetime, months, weeks or even days.

What is most important is that **it produces changes**. The developmental power of the jhanas is what makes it highly valuable. The mental state of one who enters a sati-type jhana is a highly-balanced mind that is free from hindrances, lucidly clear, bright, unblemished and pure.

This is known as 'Shou Yi', or adhering to the natural function of sixth consciousness that is unbound by objects.

---

On the other spectrum, there is also the pure dry-insight or vipassana crowd. In the same way, their practices do not lead to changes. The experiences can be very intense, but after some time, one would revert to one's original personality without any change physically or mentally.

The common advice given is "Investigate pain, go into it, see its rising and passing away". However, this is highly cognitive. Not simply 'noticing' the pain, one has to enter the pain itself, force upon it and try to 'investigate' this pain.

However, what is right there is already empty! What is there to focus upon? The conditions are what created the pain, not the pain itself! What one chases becomes the shadow, instead of the source itself.

This is also the source of the 'Dark Night of the Soul', or spiritual crisis. This is the fear of dying, of annihilation of the self. However, due to the nature of the practice, one enters up with a highly unbalanced mind - the craving arises, takes on the form of great fear, and the self grips onto it.

This is because the agitations are not calmed down. The missing step in vipassana is the recognition that one should let go of the hindrances, not trying to use cognitive power to crush, suppress, 'investigate' them. One should relax into it, acknowledge it as it is, let go, and give rise to the joy of letting go.

---

**Experiences are not what matters.**

What you can observe and 'know', it is only a transformation of the five skandhas!

When you see blinding light, see holy figures, see all kinds of things, or even see various worlds - what do you think they are? **In the end, they appeared in front of you**. You have not taken a single step towards true development.

What you are in is simply a different state, a different samadhi, a different phenomenon, a different interaction of elements - they are all realms! Realms change with conditions!

But what is the conditionless? This is what we have to find!

As mentioned repeatedly, samadhi is reached like a spinning top. By letting the top spin on its own, and letting go of the hindrances, the top will eventually stop spinning.

There is no need to try to force the top to stop spinning. Neither do you focus on the hindrances that agitate the top further. Relax into it, let it by the natural process.

Use 'sati' to watch, an easiness of watching, of noticing. When distractions arise, that is fine. The function of noticing has not stopped at all, that is what is more important!

Simply acknowledge that yes, this distraction arose. Calm down the mind agitations. Calm down the body agitations associated. Let go of the 'tense' feeling and give rise to joyousness, with a sense of expansiveness (mind is non-local!). Then gently rest the mind on the breath again.

It is a waiting game. One should learn to stop trying to do anything in meditation except letting go, releasing, relaxing, and surrendering. Afterall, the sense of joy is not different from relief!

                      > Could you go into more detail about releasing craving?

                      When you release your body's tension through exhalation, that too, is a form of relaxation. Carrying that idea forward, it is the idea of giving, of letting go of tension, of letting go of grasping, of giving, and of charity. The nature of hindrances by themselves is empty, they are illusions. Just by seeing through them like this, letting go of grasping onto them, the hindrances will naturally empty themselves out.

                      > What is the next step when you have perceptual clarity and you want more tranquility and joy during the day and motjust when your sitting?

                      You already know the answer - When going about one's day, see everything as empty. There is only one thing that is permanent, and that is Nibbana - In fact, it is not a thing, it is nothing - And it is only from true emptiness does it not pose any friction towards the substantiated.

                      > Is "letting go" basically relaxing and loosening tension in the mind-body?

                      There is a big secret here, and the secret is that the mind and body are not separate. The body itself is in the mind. The mind is one and the same as the five great elements. Just like the mind, elements do not deplete or increase, rise or fall, take birth or die - Neither do they fuse into each other.

                      What is the difference between letting go of mind and letting go of body? It becomes that of observation. How can one release anything that they are not aware that they are gripping hold of?

                      > Edit: Would applying the relax/release step in the 6rs Bhante Vimalaramsi mentions here be correct cultivation? http://www.dhammasukha.org/the-6rs.html

                      Yes, this is correct.

                      However, one should understand how this relaxation and release comes about. For example, if one pumps in air in a tyre and does not let it out, the pressure inside the tyre becomes very high. However, if one lets go of the air inside the tyre, the pressure disappears and equalizes with the atmospheric pressure.

                      In the same way, one lets go of the pressure of holding in one's chi in a certain area - this gripping action is done by thoughts of the mind. As a result, the elements start to react and congeal in a certain way, causing tension. By letting go, one disperses the 'hardened’ chi in the area, allowing it to flow as normal.

                      Now we talk about chi as part of the wind-element. How does one normally intake the wind-element? We take it through the breath. And we expel it though the breath as well. Therefore, the secret to removing the 'impurities’ or hardened chi in these areas is in the exhalation. While exhaling and relaxing, one can visualize all the chi dispersing from the area of tension and leaving the body through the pores or through the breath.

                      When this happens, it is usually because the kundalini has started working its way throughout the body and cleansing out the energy channels.                       The sense of hearing is the last to go, even in death. Therefore, it is unreasonable to say that one cannot hear anything.       This is of course, only my perspective but...

      Let me explain from my perspective, based off of my practice and what I have learned from others:

      In the spiritual path, there are three components. There is the vessel/tool of conscious embodiment, there is the awareness that "rides" this vehicle so to speak (a different discussion in itself, but the awareness is essential nature) and then there is the "light" or "energy" which animates this vessel/tool and is thus the energetic function of this "awareness".

      Now, when we say "the missing step” we mean because most people turn to dry insight, which only engages the mental aggregate.

      When we say, do not use the cognitive power to concentrate, crush or suppress, we mean do not use the mental aggregate, do not engage it, as best you can.

      When we say, you must let go, we do not mean a passive letting go wherein one surrenders to everything; that will come later on, but for now, you are entangled in the body-mind of the material sense; so you cannot let go without falling into unconscious mental activity that arises in the form of habitual tendencies.

      Instead, what we mean is that awareness has become entangled in this unconscious embodiment (unconscious, because it was created unconsciously) of consciousness; this has happened because the "light" which we can work with a later level, has been unconsciously channeled and directed towards this vessel/tool that awareness has become entangled with.

      Now, what we need to do is first lay the foundation which allows us to eventually access the subtle body and its proper cultivation; from this, we gain access to will, intention and vitality, these three are our tools for cultivating the subtle body.

      Thus intention works as a steering wheel, directing us towards inward cultivation and away from engaging sensory perception or the conceptual faculty.

      Will works as the engine, we use it to power our turning away from the unconscious habitual tendencies and instead turn towards actively engaging the light and "turning” the light of awareness (where there is focus, there is energy, where there is awareness then there is "light") inwardly towards this dormant consciousness; again, to reiterate the idea of surrender, here there is no concentration in the form of the mental aggregate, it is actually a more passive surrender to the light and letting go of the phenomenon manifestations that arise during our cultivation.

      Finally, vitality or the light itself is the fuel of our journey and is what eventually allows us to restore this dormant consciousness to fullness, allowing us to begin exploring the spiritual path on a more genuine level, free from the body-mind of the material sense.

      It is from here, that we go into a deeper form of cultivation, but before we can even begin this we have to refine our tool for the process.

      Thus, we start off with an embryonic (unconscious) conscious embodiment, this is the awareness entangled with the material sense and is the result of an unconscious identification with the conceptual identity; we start off with the 5 sensory aggregates and the 6th conceptual aggregate as our tools for cultivation.

      When we begin to lay the foundation, we will reach a point where we realize the tools we have at our disposal will only get us so far, so we need to refine them and thus ascend to a higher, more subtle level of consciousness in order to cultivate further in our intuitive understanding; this is where the restoring of the subtle body, the dormant/atrophied aggregate of consciousness, comes into play.

      From having restored this, we are in a much better position to cultivate further, through this mind-made body; as we are free from the conceptual faculty at this level, free from the material sense and are thus able to explore the dharma on a deeper level through the faculties of this subtle body.

      Later on, there is further refinement in terms of the "tool" that is being used, but this is the first refinement that needs to happen or else one can never break free from "dry insight” meditation and one cannot begin to genuinely cultivate, free from the material senses or free from the sway of unconscious mental tendencies.

      Edit: to clarify, we can do this through breath meditation, it is the breath that has a hidden power, it is subtle and nuanced, when we cultivate the breath in this way of surrender; such as anapana-sati, then we begin to explore and open up this subtle body along with the animating energy, which can be called prana, and this comes with the breath (at first).

      So, the path is one of refining the tool of meditation (the vessel), the refining is done through the light or prana and the path that opens this up to us is anapana-sati.

      Thus, there is the union of absorption and insight. We gain insight into this essential nature of infinite awareness, which is the "substance" or "essence" of our consciousness; but we gain this insight through the refining of our absorption, which is this energetic function, thus we cultivate nature and life (vitality, light) and with their union we can reach complete realization.
