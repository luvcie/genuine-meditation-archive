# How to find a real-life sangha

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 5 points · 1 year ago

Hello, sangha,

I am finding it extremely difficult to find a real-life sangha in my city that belongs to a tradition that acknowledges the importance of concentration. There is a Theravedan temple half an hour away from me with a teacher I like, but even he seemed inexperienced with instruction of jhanas, as the temple is overseen by more senior monks who are frequently traveling. More importantly, the sessions I've attended emphasized the core Buddhist teachings for beginners, but not so much the particulars of right samadhi.

I also met a Zen teacher through my work, and visited the center where she teaches, but have been disappointed by their way of meditating. They don't believe that prolonged concentration is possible, and the whole practice feels disconnected from the original teachings of Buddhism. They claim they teach the real heart of Buddhism, but it doesn't seem that way to me, so even if they do, I'm not sure I feel comfortable following their path when my intuition tells me it's not quite right.

Meanwhile I've been meditating for an hour every morning, checking in here frequently, and reading a book of the discourses of the Buddha. I feel that this is the right way for me, but it is somewhat lonely. I'm curious what people here have to say about the process of finding the right teacher, with whom one can roll up their sleeves and really start to work intensively and in person. I've heard it said that when the pupil is ready, the master will appear. Well, I feel ready!

I guess maybe there is nothing to do but keep searching and waiting?

- **Sherlockian_Holmes** · *אֲהָבִים Ahaviyahu* · 2 points · 1 year ago

  As the aphorism goes, and as you said yourself, "When the student is ready, the Master appears.”

  In this Dharma Ending Age I think you will find it extremely difficult to find genuine Masters. As Paramananda said, it is exceedingly rare to find any genuinely enlightened people around anymore.

  That said, we have an online Sangha here, we have an online Slack chat which we could make more use of; it doesn't need to be a lonely path, but it is often said by the Masters that the higher one moves along the mountain path, the more lonely the path can appear to be.

  As for finding a teacher, Paramananda has given good advice in the FAQ section. I will copy-paste it here:

  Follow the teachings, and not the person. Follow the sutras before you follow commentaries. Do not take the teachings as faith until you have investigated it for yourself and found it to be for the benefit of one and all.

  When looking at a teacher, ask: Does one encourage personal investigation? Does one talk about how the right noble-eightfold path, in its various ways, lead to liberation?

  If a teacher dislikes sacred mantras, his teaching is questionable. If a teacher teaches to chase sensory impressions as law, his teaching is questionable. If a teacher teaches supernatural abilities as the goal, rather than for liberation, his teaching is questionable. If a teacher discourages investigation and enforces doctrine, this teacher is questionable.

  Lastly, one should seek whether this teacher is enlightened or not. A teacher who is not enlightened can teach you mundane wisdom, but this teacher cannot point you in the right direction.

  Can this teacher bring out teachings from the insight-stream, and be able to answer questions with deep wisdom?

  Can this teacher understand any Buddhist scripture, even though it is the first time looking at it?

  Can this teacher be unobstructed by any situation, but yet be able to have perfect conduct?

  Can this teacher be unobstructed by evil realms?

  Does this teacher have the 6 extraordinary attainments, the fruit of right practice?

  Additionally, as for helping you find a teacher that lives up to that, you could recite the Cundi mantra focusing on the intention of finding a teacher to guide you toward the genuine Dharma and see if that helps.

  One thing that Paramananda always told me is that there is a huge teacher-dependency from students, and it is not always helpful. What is important is to investigate for one self: Does this lead to liberation? If not, what can I change? It is said there are only two ways to "fail" in reaching enlightenment (1) not beginning one's search, thinking one has already found truth (e.g. dogmatic atheist-materialist), and (2) stopping one's search before one has found the liberating Truth of the genuine Dharma.

  I've struggled a lot with this too, wanting a real-life in-the-flesh enlightened teacher, but I realized that this was another form of grasping. It is far better to work on developing oneself and to build self-reliance than from the very beginning relying on someone else. No one can hand over enlightenment, not even the Buddha. It is only ourselves that can realize it through cultivation work. So let's strive well!

  I hope this helps!
