  Many people erroneously believe that if you require to become a vegetarian to become a follower of the Buddha's teachings. Let me first dispel that myth from your mind. That is not at all true. In fact, many enlightened teachers have also expressed the exact same answer to me. This is a very complicated topic but I will try to explain it clearly here.

  Firstly, you are right. Being a vegetarian is a compassionate path and doing it can be a good solution to the suffering we cause to the animal kingdom. However, this does not necessarily mean that everyone should become a vegetarian.

  The problem comes because of the insensitivity that people have with regards to the state of their body. Most people do not pay close attention to their own body. For example, can you tell if your body lacks energy? Are you sensitive to how the body responds to things around you, particularly food? Can you tell how energy is moving in the body?

  One particular problem, is sleep itself. Many people believe 7-8 hours to be an optimal sleep time. However, this is in fact a sign of the body not being optimized to its optimal efficiency. If one is truly healthy, there will be a radical drop in their need for sleep. There are so much problems with the modern diet due to the ignorance of the body's needs. This is also why the Buddha stressed sati (mindfulness) with reference to the body.

  This body is largely mechanical. It is just a very complex physical machine. However, it is essential for us to use it for cultivation. That is why we should hold this body in great respect, particularly with sincere gratitude for our parents for producing this body with their seeds.

  Now the problem with being completely vegetarian is the fact that everybody's body is different, even genetically. For example, certain vehicles run on diesel better, while others require a different kind of fuel. There are organisms that run on nitrogen while others run on oxygen. You have to understand this function as mechanical.

  As such, it is not that meat is not recommended at all. It is in fact, okay for one to eat meat if it has a "medicinal” purpose. In other words, if the body requires certain nutrients in meat to maintain its health, then this meat should be taken. In fact, the Buddha himself said that it is a violation of the path to refuse to eat meat if it is used as medicine for the body. If meat is to be consumed, one has to treat it with gratitude before consuming it.

  It is usually the case that meat is usually "dead"”, while vegetables and fruits are "fresh". If you want to look at the esoteric side, when you consume things that are "vibrant” with life, their energy is taken into yours. This does not mean eating animals alive, do not get me wrong - Eating them alive will take in their karma of suffering and it will not do any good. Eating vegetables and fruits, however, tends to have a refreshing quality to the body.

  It is also generally a healthier choice, given the way animals are treated before being slaughtered. Injected hormones, along with the farming process, can make meat less conducive to the energy body.

  Always treat your food with gratitude. This is not said religiously. If you have the eye of wisdom, you can certainly see that the mind affects the energy of things. Science has shown that if you treat water a certain manner, its molecules change drastically. It is just unseen to the flesh eye.

  When consuming water, breathing air or eating food, all of this should be done consciously and with blissfulness.

  To conclude the answer, it really requires an eye of wisdom to accomodate your diet to your body. Food should not be taken for satisfying cravings, but to satisfy the bodily needs.

  Setting aside the issue of compassion and morality for one second, I would say that the question of how the food we eat influences us on a more "mechanical” level, as relates to meditation and mental states, is a huge and important topic.

  The quality, quantity and timing of food and drink intake; any usage of medicines, nutritional supplements, recreational drinking/drugs, etc.; sexual habits; sleeping habits; the condition of the breath; our emotional thought-world on a daily and long-term basis (stress, joy, hopelessness, anger, calm, etc.) -- these things all form together into one complex and interrelated mass, all mutually influencing one another.

  The physical and mental sides respond back and forth and set up multiple feedback systems which require a sustained process of dispassionate, keen observation to suss out and get a firm grip on. All of this comes together to form the total state of body and mind each time we sit down to meditate.

  Based on my experience, my own recommendation for navigating these waters is to become familiar with the traditional medicinal schemas of Chinese Medicine and Ayurveda (Indian medicine), supplemented with modern nutritional theories. Although very useful when applied judiciously, modern theories do not offer as much utility specifically for meditation practitioners as these older systems do, in my opinion. There are also some errors you have to watch out for.

  Finally, I would like to say that it is very possible to be healthy in the long-term while not eating animal products. This statement can't be applied universally without cautions, though, simply because it's always a question of more factors than just food.
