# The Big Confusion of No-Mind

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 6 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3cww3q/the_big_confusion_of_nomind/>

There is a lot of confusion going about stopping the mind, suppressing thoughts and detachment from everything. From personal experience, this is a big perversion of what it means to meditate. Some people talk about meditation as stopping the activity of the mind. Some say that if you do not stop the brain, you cannot meditate. Some say that if you do not detach from everything around you, then you cannot seek enlightenment.

My dharma friend, I feel sadness when people talk about it that way, because while the words mean a certain meaning, the understanding of these words have been changed. I will try to explain my experience with better clarity.

Firstly, the body is the body. So if the stomach works, the liver works, the heart works, the kidneys work - You are perfectly fine in meditating. But when thoughts happen, you suddenly cannot meditate. Most of these thoughts are patterns bound as memory within the brain. The brain is going to function, as long as you are alive. Hence, do not try to repress thoughts. The brain can go on and do whatever it wants. All you need to do is either calm it, through binding attention onto a single thought-object, or just entirely watch the whole thoughts flicker by, as a separate entity. Afterall, what you can witness, you are not it. What you witness, it is not-self.

You must see this. The brain is telling its own story, it is relating its memory. If you swing a pendulum, it goes to and fro, as a memory of its initial force. Let it tell its story. Don't try to push away your intellect. If that happens, you are working towards becoming a mindless existence. What is the difference between a wandering ghost and you then?

What is meant by no-mind then?

No-mind does not really mean the absence of mind, although its activity can indeed become very minute. It is in fact, the separation between the "I" and mental sphere. Your body, your thoughts, your emotions and the "I" are all in different places.

The "I" is outside of the five skandhas/aggregates. The five aggregates are: body, sensation, perception, mental formation, consciousness. They all function on their own - powered by conditions. They are all not-self. Hence, the "I" is outside all of this. If the space between the "I" and these five skandhas increase, then you will see what it means to be no-mind.

It is not detachment either.

Detachment has been interpreted in wrong ways, especially when people use it with phrases such as be dispassionate, be uninterested, see everything as foul, etc. This is a method that has been used to let people increase the "separation" between the five skandhas and the I.

However, if you live in the world, detached in such a manner - You will be a lifeless person.

The key is to become mindful. Mindfulness is very hard to explain. It is like you are aware of everything. You must be aware of everything - Of your body, of the earth, of the solar system and beyond. It is only when you are aware that you can then choose how you want to deal with it.

Mindfulness is the antidote to being trapped in cycles. For example, when you have a certain habit, you continuously do it again and again. It is like a cycle that never stops. As long as you remain unaware of the cycle, you will forever be in the cycle.

Now the whole mind has been conditioned by many thoughts. So many thoughts, so many memories, so many patterns - All burned into your consciousness. As long as you are unaware of them, they will continue to control you. This is what we call karma.

When you walk in the world, you must live in the present moment. The present moment cannot be grasped, because by the time you try to hold or conceptualize it, it has already become the past. If you are thinking of the past memories or imagining the future, then you are already living in imaginary illusions.

The only thing is what you experience in the now. Even if you access past memories, it is still within the present. Even if you access future memories, it is still within the present. Even if you time-travel, in your experience, it is still in the present. What is this presence? It is "being". This being is active mindfulness. Time and space do not bound this "I".

When you experience the now, you should be aware of everything in the world. You must live life vibrantly. When you perceive everything in the world as your one awareness, then what happens to another becomes within your awareness. Whether you choose to help another or not, then becomes your own choice. They say ignorance is bliss, but that is awfully wrong. It is ignorantly living in suffering, with absolutely no liberation.

Detachment and no-mind means "separation" between the I and the five aggregates. You must not identify yourself as the body or the thoughts. But even if you disidentify, you are NOT pushing them away.

Think of the five aggregates as clothes or a tool. You must become a master of them. For example, if you were not aware that electricity existed, you would not be able to master its use.

The Buddha talked about his method as sati, mindfulness. This sati is not simply sitting down in meditation. It literally is a meditative state where the I is completely free from the five aggregates. You can be talking and doing sati. You can be running and doing sati. You can be sleeping and doing sati. You can be eating and doing sati. You can be shitting and doing sati. You can do anything, and yet remain in sati. As long as you are in sati, you will be conscious when things happen - and it is from this awareness that you are freed from compulsive cycles and finally have a freedom to choose.

What happens to the body, you might not even suffer from it. You can cut off your arm, but this pain does not translate as 'suffering', because you are no longer identified with the body. Similarly, whatever situation or stimuli that you perceive, it no longer translates as pain or pleasure. This is the ending of pain-and-pleasure, a trait of the fourth jhana.

Perfect equanimity allows one to function in the world, being untouched. However, this does not mean he is detached. He is grateful for everything, because he knows everything is one, and one is everything. Form is void, void is form - As the heart sutra said.

He sees the beauty and amazing miracle of life. How does he do it? By seeing it for what it is, not what the cyclical afflictions and fetters tell him it is.

He does not have multiple selves, and he knows that as an individual, he is indivisible. When something bad is done by him, he recognizes that he allowed it, and it was not some "ego" which did it. Hence, what he experiences, is only by himself, with 100% responsibility for everything that happens, whether rationally or irrationally. Yet what he experiences is the entirety of the cosmos. It is not logical, because how can you imagine that the whole of creation can be experienced? How can you fit the entire universe or cosmos into the I? It does not make sense. But if you experience it, it is so.

Not-self is not-self. This is disidentification, not detachment. Know a pencil as a pencil. Know form as form. Know a thief as a thief. This is honesty of the highest virtue. This is what the precept meant in its highest order, when the Buddha said not to lie.

Living is living. When you are living, why do you want to act lifeless? Having mastery over the tools, you can then be involved in the world without being tangled up in it in a cyclical nature.

I hope this sheds some light. Strive well for enlightenment.

              There is no realization until one gets it. This is either it or delusion. There is no in-between or small-enlightenment.

              Imagine that you have your feet chained to 9 anchors and you are at the bottom of the sea. You have never seen what is above the sea. Now, when you enter first jhana, you are released from an anchor. When you enter second jhana, another is released. This continues until one has reached the 8th samadhi. After all that, you are still left with the Sth chain. You are still in the sea, and you have not seen what is outside of it. This 9th chain is realization of the

              truth, becoming one with the cosmos and becoming beyond that of birth and death.

              The jhana states bring you to less-denser realms so that you can dis-identify with the coarser realms such as your physical body. If you never had a jhana experience, you would never know what it means to be free from the physical body. Hence, all the various degrees of absorption allow you to experience the different realms in the cosmos - from the form realm to the formless.

              But ultimately, enlightenment is not within these three worlds of desire, form and formlessness. All these three worlds are still bound by birth and death. It is only in different degrees.

              We say that enlightenment comes in various degrees, why is that so? There is the stream-winner, the once-returner, the non-returner, the arahant. Lastly, there is the arahant that transcends clinging to nothingness, as a bodhisattva that consciously comes back to do deeds in the world. That is when one understands the use of the Noble Eightfold Path, and all doubt is erased from experience. That is what you call the various degrees of enlightenment, not the attainment of samadhi.

              It is possible, that when you experience the subtler realms, you become more conscious of the existence. That is why the higher you go, the more virtuous one becomes - Virtuosity is simply a natural expression. For example, nature cannot lie, for it does things as it is. It is the mind that deceives. It is an approximation that allows you to get nearer to truth, but you cannot call it enlightenment.
