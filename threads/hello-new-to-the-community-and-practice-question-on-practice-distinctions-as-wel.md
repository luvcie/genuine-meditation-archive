  > 1)

  I'll just do the honors of reposting what /u/refugeinbuddha, Paramananda shared to a similar question in the subreddit around a year ago:

  Firstly, the Buddha has only prescribed a single method to right cultivation - He called it sati. Translated, this means “mindfulness”. But mindfulness to what? He describes in the Satipatthana Sutta the four foundations of mindfulness.

  When one uses Anapanasati, one firstly uses the breath as a method to still the mind. This brings about the one-pointedness that can allow the other factors of first jhana to arise. With reference to the breath, one then becomes mindful to the body, sensations, thoughts and mental qualities.

  There is no "Vipassana meditation". This is all commentary, which often brings about a distorted interpretation of the original scriptures. If you do read the actual scriptures, without subscribing to any of the various schools, you will find that the only way Vipassana is used as a word, is a fruit/reward of having practised the right path.

  Vipassana means insight, but there is no such thing as Vipassana meditation. In the Samannaphala Sutta, the Buddha talks about attaining a jhana state, which then gives birth to insight. There is no jhana without insight and no insight without jhana - Both are inseparable.

  What is this right practice? It is sati. How is this explored even further? The Buddha elaborates it as the Noble Eightfold Path.

  The Buddha describes many ways to obtain liberation. Some individuals follow a lifestyle of virtuous actions, thoughts and speech, eventually reaching a state of liberation. Some talk about various methods of liberation through various types of meditation. However, the main premise is always sati. This is described as the maga (practice) which leads to phala (fruit).

  However, despite the erroneous name given to it, there can be credit for the technique itself. It is in fact, sati again, but simply paying attention to the thought objects that arise in the mind instead of having a discrete meditation object. It is not, though, a meditation used to reach merely insight, as is expressed in the usage of the word "vipassana". In fact, any right meditation technique should bring a cultivator into jhana and samadhi. Wisdom or prajna can only be reached when one is in a deep, still, equanimous and altered state of consciousness.

  The general thing to take-away, is that insight cannot come without absorption - And the opposite is also true. Prajna can only arise from right meditation, which is based off right sila. If you notice the Noble Eightfold Path, it is often divided into three sections: Sila, Meditation and Prajna. The three are indivisible and come together in one's practice of the Noble Eightfold Path.

  > 1)What is the distinction between samatha and vipassana meditation?

  Samatha Meditation is the practice of calming the mind e.q. sitting, walking or lying down meditation by anchoring onto one object or multiple to achieve calm state. By achieving this calm state, we create a condition for the insight to

  You could also say 'samatha’ or 'calm’ is the result of the Samatha meditation and when 'samatha’ is achieved, ‘'vippasana' or Wisdom or Insight may occur.

  As for Vipassana Meditation, it is the process of trying to gain understanding of how our mind works by having sati / mindfulness with us all the time. Over the time, we could see a certain pattern.

  e.q. Our feeling towards someone changed as the thoughts pop up or even though we want to sit and don't think, we can't and etc.

  We could call it a lesser insight since it won't or have lesser effects on how we perceive things. If we keep watching the mind in this way, the mind becomes very sure of it and if the cause and conditions have matured, we may entered a period of calmness where a true insight may happen and thus your world view may change as a result of the insight.

  Both of the practices are like two different roads to Rome. They all start at different point in space but will eventually meet at the same location. (achieving both ‘'samatha’ and '‘calm’)

  There are however the reasons for both meditation. Imagine they are so many different types of people in the world. Some are very good at concentration, some are not.

  Those who are good at concentration could practice samatha meditation perfectly without any issues and likely the fastest route.

  Those who are not so good / bad at concentration may need a little more wisdom to aid into their toolboxs to allow them dive deeper when they sit down. This is where Vipassana excels. E.q. Once you get a glimpse of the light such as you have no control over your thoughts. when you sit, you could suddenly realize that these thoughts are beyond my control so you don't get angry / upset and just watch them.. you might dive deeper into samatha meditation and achieve the 'calm state' where a true insight may occur.

  Both Samatha and Vipassana Meditation could work hand in hand in this way. As a result ‘'Samatha’ and 'Vipassana' or 'Calm' and 'Insight/Wisdom' is achieved.

  Trying to say which meditation style is good or try to dismiss them may not be accurate as well. It's like calling taylor swift is everyone's dream girl but in fact, it's true to some but not true for others.

  Here is a summary of a route. 1. Samatha > Vipasanna 2. Lesser Vipassana > Samatha > True Vipassana.

  Here is an explanation of the great master of therevada's tradition how Concentration and Wisdom are the same

  > It is quite simple. Concentration (samatha) and wisdom (vipassana) work together. First the mind becomes still by ! holding on to a meditation object. It is quiet only while you are sitting with your eyes closed. This is samatha and eventually this samadhi-base is the cause for wisdom or vipassana to arise. Then the mind is still whether you sit with your eyes closed or walk around in a busy city. It's like this. Once you were a child. Now you are an adult. Are ' the child and the adult the same person? If you examine how concentration and wisdom arise, you will know the truth for yourself. These days many people cling to the words. They call their practice vipassana. Samatha is looked down on.
