# My main obstacles to spiritual progression

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Neurogence** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 4 points · 2 months ago

Over the past few months, I have been trying to take my spiritual life seriously, with mixed results. I've identified two things that have impeded my progress: doubt and "material circumstances.”

In regard to doubts, I basically have doubts on the idea that I am a spiritual being, with a purpose, and that I have been reincarnating cyclically over the ages. If I knew for sure that this is the case, I would take my practice a lot more seriously and have less attachment to the things of the world. When it comes to more practical things, like the importance of celibacy, I also have doubts. I've read many convincing scientific articles describing how frequent ejaculation/orgasms is good for the health of the body and immune system.

I wouldn't take this seriously if it wasn't for personal experience. I seem to sleep better and get colds less often with frequent orgasms. And one study even found that men who frequently ejaculate live about 4 years longer on average. Not significant, but interesting. It's not that I don't have enough willpower to conserve the sexual energy, it's just that it would be much easier to do if I knew it was absolutely necessary for genuine cultivation. Many seemingly enlightened yogis and gurus like mooji and sadhguru seem to downplay the importance of these practices. For the former, he suggests that no effort at all is needed for one to "awaken.” No pranayama, or sexual transmutation needed. Not even meditation, except for "just being/allowing” and not thinking. I am grossly oversimplifying his message, but hopefully you all can get the idea.

About the material circumstances, it's basically balancing the "householder" life with the spiritual. I haven't been able to balance it at all. I feel like I am trying to serve two masters at once and it's not working out. School, work, and just figuring out how to make a living in general seems to have a strongly negative impact on my spiritual endeavors. Sometimes it feels like I can't justify time on meditation when I have "pressing” matters to attend to. At times I feel like I have to focus on only one. And, I'm more leaning on focusing on my material life, even though I care more about the spiritual.

I feel that entirely focusing on the latter would just be me admitting defeat. Basically, me being afraid to face the "real world." It's tough out there. But on one hand, I wonder if all my difficulties in the material aspects of life are just so I can let it all go and focus on the spiritual? The monk life does not appeal to me, because there is no guarantee that I'd make any genuine spiritual progress. But I would certainly trade it for my current life if spiritual success was certain. But then again, if I had enough material resources, I would have peace of mind enough to dedicate a lot more time to my spiritual life (I know it's all one life, including the "material,” but sometimes the distinction is necessary). Is it wrong to use intention to better your material circumstances?

I would appreciate it if anyone could give me some clarity.

If this is not reversed through cultivation exercises, I believe that this "unused” semen can cause harm to the body like a bottle being filled up continually without release.

  Instead we should learn to use the Essence/ling - not semen. Semen is created from essence. In Ayurveda it says: "Semen is the last Dhatu that is formed out of food. Out of food is manufactured chyle. Out of chyle comes blood. Out of blood comes flesh. Out of flesh comes fat. Out of fat comes bone. Out of bone comes marrow. Out of marrow

  comes semen. These are the Sapta Dhatus or the seven Dhatus that support this life and body. Mark here how precious is semen! It is the last essence. It is the Essence of essences.”

  From a physical point of view - why is it important for our cultivation? From what I've come to believe, it is because ejaculating activates certain neuro-chemicals and hormones that put a damper on our internal 'heat' and blissfulness. In particular ejaculation releases prolactin which puts a damper on dopaminergic signalling along with reducing testosterone, and it takes many days to recover from an ejaculation. Try to meditate just before and after ejaculation and you will be able to notice an enormous difference if you are sensitive in your body and mind. In meditation we want a body with supercharged hormonal levels, we want the water element (e.g. hormones) in the blood to be at an equilibrated level so that you only need to bring the switch and then ignite those energies on fire.

  > I wouldn't take this seriously if it wasn't for personal experience. I seem to sleep better and get colds less often with frequent orgasms

  Sleeping better could be due to the relaxation that comes from lowering your arousal level. I've experienced it times too when the mind runs amok with sensual desire. You need to learn to rein it in, learn how to breath consciously and relax the entire body including the tension in the lower body - horse stance, full lotus and deep diaphragmatic breathing and kumbhaka training (9-bottled breath) are very important for these purposes. Otherwise you will feel tense and anxious and that can definitely inhibit sleep.

  As for getting colds less often with frequent orgasms, that is a bit tricky because it could be that you think you are getting colds but you are actually just experiencing withdrawal symptoms from sensory indulgence - and you are beginning to cultivate your energies.

  In fact, in my own experience as you pass through various cleansing stages in meditation, it can feel very much like a common cold sometimes. Shivering, sweating, random aches, brain-fogs, etc. You should also make sure you cover your neck and knees when you meditate because as your energy channels begin to open you become susceptible to ‘wind-invasions' which can definitely feel like common cold, so there are a couple of factors there that play a role.

  In the end, it is up to you. Perhaps for now you find that it plays a more positive role than negative one - so there is no problem to do it, is there? It is simply that we must become conscious of how we feel at all times and how our mind and body reacts to all things.

I personally find it a literally 'painful’ endeavour, but it's still difficult to abstain - those habit energies are difficult to break in my experience.

  > Meditation is not to leave the world, but to be better in it. Meditation is not loss of mind, but mastery of it. Crying is fine, fear is fine. You simply need to be able to know when it happens, don't lose consciousness. Know that it happens, accept and receive it with love. Whatever comes, allow it to come. Simply accepting gives you the space of forgiving and letting go. Why do you want to be apart from the world? Friend, running away does not solve anything. Graciously accepting things as they are, knowing them to be as they are - this is the first step. One still needs to be able to master worldly wisdom. Without knowing how to live the worldly life, one becomes useless in the world. Therefore, you cannot abandon the material world. You need to know how to bring the dharma into the world, and that very first step perhaps is to understand that every deed, speech and thought that you exert into the world should be kind and loving. The Buddha still had clay feet. He lives a simple life, washing his feet, going out for a walk, coming back to meditate, then teaching his disciples. Nothing fabulous or miraculous.
