# Samadhi and a Fan

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 2 points · 2 years ago

When you look at a fan, it is spinning at such a fast speed that your eyes can only see a circle. Another analogy is a fast-spinning top. The colors are mixed, and you see a homogeneous color in the circle.

If you put a rod onto the fast-moving fan or top, you see that the rod immediately is deflected away. Within this stillness holds huge power.

In the same way, samadhi appears still. However, it only appears still. As the esoteric, Taocist and Zen master Nan Huai Jin said, "Samadhi is a great movement".

It is not a fan that fades away in motion and comes to a stop. That is the complete lack of energy. Neither is it a fan that is moving at a fast-speed but jerking and sputtering.

When energy and calmness come together - when blockages of chi dissolve, the channels within the body become an interconnected whole. There is no front or back channel - There is only one.

There is no itching, no pain, no misery, no ache, no cold, no heat, nothing - the body becomes light - its burdens vanish. It is like opening the windows and doors to a room - At first, there is movement. Eventually, it equalizes and there is no more movement. However, the air pressure of the room has clearly changed.

If you use the Hindu system, the iga and pingala control the sympathetic and parasymthetic nervous systems. One excites while the other calms with the vagus nerves. The Yogi say that when they are balanced, the energies of the sushumna become that of sattvas (the most purified of the three gunas).

When you stress the body, that is extreme asceticism. When you calm the body into deep torpor, that is extremity as well. Therefore, do you understand the meaning of a Middle Path?

It is moving out of fluctuations, from dualities.

Like a tuning of a harp, a stringed instrument - One has to tune one's practice.
