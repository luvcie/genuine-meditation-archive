# On Knowing the Breath with the Whole Body

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 2 points · 9 months ago

Hello, sangha,

Lately I've been pondering the question of exactly when to transition from the first step of the Anapanasati series (knowing "long/short"), to the second (breathing while experiencing the whole body.) In doing so, I might have understood the second step better.

Two options presented themselves. The first was to switch to full-body breathing when the breath was faint enough, leaving the question of "how does one know when it's time?" The second was to switch when the breath can't be felt at all, leaving no option but to switch to experiencing the body (or as Paramananda puts it, "internal breath.”) But a thought occurred to me just now, during meditation, that perhaps answers the question.

Perhaps the "breathing in/out experiencing the body" is simply experiencing the body's response to the fluctuations in oxygen that happen as a result of breathing. In other words, taking as the object of meditation the body's movement from craving for oxygen to satisfaction (in-breath) and then from satisfaction to craving again (out-breath). This would mean the question of sensation at the nose depends entirely on how well the oxygen-hunger (or satisfaction) can be felt. In order for the oxygen-hunger to be a distinct enough object of meditation, the sensation at the nose would have to be weak enough that it wouldn't drown out the oxygen-hunger, but wouldn't necessarily have to be gone entirely.

This understanding does seem to align with the descriptions of this stage. The fluctuations in oxygen-hunger is a whole body sensation, which does indeed affect all of the body's cells, and when the breath is faint enough, it feels like respiration is all happening internally. Just wanted to run this understanding by the group.
