# Discourse on Karma

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 4 points · 8 days ago

One may have noticed that this subreddit started heavily on Hinayana cultivation, then moved gradually to Mahayana cultivation, and now gradually we are moving towards the building of Mahayana wisdom. When this is completed, then perhaps the work here is done. As such, all vehicles are just one vehicle towards Buddhahood, all vehicles are still just illusions within samsara.

So what exactly is karma? Everyone who studies Buddhism knows about karma and they give all kinds of definitions. One says it is inconceivable, the other says it is action, and yet another says it is habitual energy. All of these answers have their truths but they are just textbook answers, regurgitated because another person has told one about it. Perhaps one has experienced a little of this, but one cannot grasp where this karma comes from, it is as if there is a barrier and one cannot see beyond.

Why can you not be liberated?

Is there anyone who is restricting you? Is there anything else that stops you from liberation? Does Mara stop you from liberation? The answer is very simple. There is no one stopping you from liberation, it is you who has stopped yourself.

All sentient beings are originally liberated, but why is it that they seem not to be liberated, as if they were trapped in samsara?

There is a force that binds you to the Six Realms. A virtuous force brings you to the three virtuous paths; A non-virtuous force brings you to the three non-virtuous paths. What is this force called?

It is Karma. But where does Karma come from? They come from one's afflictions. So what exactly are afflictions? They are also called upakleshas and they can be classified into three types: The large-upakleshas, the middle-upakleshas and the small-upakleshas.

Karma is only created when upakleshas arise!

If one does not have these afflictions, karma will not arise. If the whole problem of being bound to samsara is because of karma, then the main problem are these upakleshas.

So where do these upakleshas arise from? If you trace these afflictions back to their cause, what is this cause?

One realizes that these afflictions all arise from the "self view". They can be understood in this way:

e When the "self" likes, it is called greed.

e When the "self" dislikes, it is called anger.

e When the "self" neither-likes-nor-dislikes, it is called ignorance or foolishness.

e When the "self" likes the "self", it is called conceit.

e When the "self" compares itself to better qualities of others, it is called jealousy/envy.

It is from this "self" where all of these afflictions arise, it is this as the bedrock which allows all of these afflictions to arise, hence leading to karma.

But where does this "self" arise from? Why is there a "me"? The moment you were born, you already had a feeling of a "me". Where does it come from? This is important because if you do not trace these problems to the root cause, you cannot get rid of karma at its root source.

When an infant is born, there is already a feeling of "me". It is just that one's 6th consciousness is underdeveloped. However, as the infant grows up, the 6th consciousness will become more and more discriminating, and this notion of a "self" will become stronger and stronger, with a stronger grasping and clinging. When this infant becomes an old man, at this time, the "self" identity has become extremely rigid.

Now this "self" - Where does it come from?

It comes from the 7th consciousness. As explained in previous posts, there are a total of 8 consciousnesses described by the Yogacara school of Mind-Only.

There are three qualities of this function that arises from the 7th consciousness which results in this self-view:

e Constantly present - When one is dreaming, this "self" is still there, it does not break in the middle for a single moment. Until one is liberated, this "self" does not break at all, that is why it is said to be constant.

e Investigation

e Consideration

These qualities are the 8th consciousness’ perceiving function, also known as Darsana-bhaga. So what happens is that the 7th consciousness takes the Darsana-bhaga and treats it as a "self"!

Originally, there is no "self". It is only the 7th consciousness which has grasped onto a function in the 8th consciousness, creating an imaginary self-view.

The personality-view, Sakkaya-ditthi, is classified under what is called "non-existent object". There is no actual object in the world there, but it is given a name - this is what is called a "non-existent object". Similarly, this “self" is also a "non-existent object”. It is imagined to exist out of emptiness.

How is it imagined out of this emptiness? It is because of the 7th consciousness' function, grasping onto the 8th consciousness' perception-function as a "self".

But why is it called perception-function (Darsana-bhaga)? This is actually the opposite of appearance-function (Nimitta-bhaga).

As an example, we see a vase of flowers. Our mind is the ability to see, whereas the vase of flowers are what appears as a realm. Hence, the perception-function is the mind that can see, whereas the vase of flowers are the appearance-function. In other words, one is the Subject while the other is the Object, they exist as dualities.
