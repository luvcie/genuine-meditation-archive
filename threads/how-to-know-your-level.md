# How to know your level?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 9 points · 6 months ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/6dmrui/how_to_know_your_level/>

In cultivation, we speak of the triad of realm, conduct and fruit.

For example, when one is in the realm of humans, one conducts oneself in humanly ways, and reaps the human fruit. There is also the deva vehicle for deva realm, the Sravaka vehicle for Sravakas, Pratekya vehicle for Pratekya, the Bodhisattva vehicle for Bodhisattvas - and the Buddha vehicle is inconceivable as expounded in the Lotus Sutra.

Therefore in practice, one can certainly measure one's progress. However, one should note that it is impossible for another person to show his or her realm to someone who has not reached that level.

Therefore, when asked if the dog had buddha-nature, a Zen master said 'no' to one student, and 'yes' to another. Have you achieved ro chig, also known as One Taste? The practice of lan chig kye chor is one of the practices that leads towards it, but if one has not reached a certain realm, how can you even conceive of it?

In this post I would simply do a very short description that is very basic, perhaps too basic for some of the more experienced cultivators, but very needed for those who have not reached this realm as of yet. This includes those of which have yet to reach the first jhana, which is the essential checkpoint into the Rupa-Heavens.

A beginner's mind is full of frustrations that arise continuously. One's body is numb, filled with blockages, and can be often sick. At this point, we do not even talk about internal objects, even an external object is problematic. For example, if a beginner places his attention on a flame, or a sound like the ocean, or a kasina - his attention drifts off multiple times in the span of a minute.

At this point, one has to examine - why is it that my attention has drifted off so many times? How do I cultivate theclear aspect of mind, such that this incessant monkey-mind does not take the scene and bring me into unconsciousness? The solution is firstly to clear the 6th consciousness and cultivate clear-seeing or clear-hearing.

This is done firstly by an external object. One listens, for example, to the sound of the waves, or a fan, or something continuous. Simply being awake, being conscious, being alert - one tends to the ear-sense. When these four things of body, sensations, thoughts and mental qualities arise - one relaxes away from them and 'tightens the grip' around the function of hearing, aiming it onto the meditation object.

Firstly, one's realm would be necessarily filled with many distractions. As one's practice extends over multiple sessions, the power of the mind increases, one's lucidity and awakeness is revitalised, and it becomes much easier to stay on the object without being distracted.

When one's attention is able to hold onto the object without distractions for at least 10 minutes, this is a good achievement, very decent. Then one can start to take on the task of internal objects.

Now what are internal objects? They are things that are within the sensory borders of our body. What is outside the skin (or aura, depending on one's extension) is considered external - what is inside is considered internal.

In the Yogic traditions, one would perform tantras such as inner seeing and focus onto one's chakras, such as the heart-point. However I would not recommend it as of yet if one's mind power is still insufficient. Instead, simply tend onto the breath without controlling it.

The breath is the wind-element, which composites the body itself. Gradually, one starts to feel the wind-element within the body itself, and this can become a very strong anchor for the mind. This is hence what one would call cessation-practice, or the development of calming of body and mind.

On the other hand, one should also practice its opposite - that is, as Patanjali taught, meditation without a seed-object. This is synonymous with the Zen method of no-method, the Dzogchen method of clear-gazing or Mahamudra. This is a recognition that all things come from the emptiness and return to the emptiness, the emptiness not being a spatial thing, but interdependence and the lack of self-nature in all conditioned things.

Such practices can involve the visualisation of the dissolution of the body, such as shared in this post. Otherwise, one can simply tend to the function of seeing without focusing on any object. Another method is a Taoist meditation which involves 'merging the sensation of the body with the universe' as one. The main purpose here is to feel an infinite emptiness without attaching to any particular object.

In the same way, one should hold this 'anchor' as if it was a jelly. If it is too tight, one should relax. If it is too relaxed, one should tighten. Slowly, one will also realize that when you are at an optimum level of tightness and relaxation, distractions will cease coming up as much.

However, this too, is a problem. In the first type of meditation, one binds onto form. The second type of meditation, one binds onto a false emptiness.

The true meditation is that of seeing the ocean as the ocean, and seeing the waves as too, part of the ocean. At no one point were they separate. When a thought, a wave, arises from the ocean, it is still mind. Form is emptiness, emptiness is form.

However, it would not be recommended for beginners to immediately jump to such a meditation because their minds are usually filled with too much sankhara forces, produced by the breaking of precepts of body, speech and mind. On top of that, one's samadhi power is not strong enough, and hindrances cannot be smelt away.

The idea is to let go, yes. However, letting go at the very start is akin to untying a knot on a rope - slight effort is required to pull out the knot, but it then becomes easy to unravel from then on. Reaching the physical cultivation phase is relatively easy - but when one enters the mental cultivation phase, it is non-linear and progress completely depends on one's merit and karmic hindrances.

      When your knee is bleeding with blood coming out in volumes, would you look at someone else and feel envy for them? Of course not, you would be intensely focused on stopping the blood, making sure that it was not infected. In the same way, you are unable to see your suffering, that is why you are distracted by these other thoughts.

      You must clearly understand what it is that keeps you in this endless cycle of suffering. Not the other person, but yourself. What exactly /s suffering? What exactly is the goal of all of this? What exactly is the problem you are trying to fix? If your view is unclear, you will be hitting blindly and miss the mark.

      When you are engaged in your everyday mind stream, it is like a river that gushes and gushes. It tells you what to like, what to not like, what to do, what to not do - it is an automatically firing messenger that takes over the pilot seat in your mind. When you relax and fall into unconsciousness, you allow this monkey-mind to take over the pilot seat.

      For some people, there are just tiny moments where this monkey-mind takes over. For others, the monkey-mind takes over completely in sleep. For yet others, the monkey-mind is incessant without stopping.

      The problem is that one is not awake, one is heavily identified with the ego, the incessant thought-stream, mental objects, sensations, body, mind qualities. Being so, one is not reunited with the ineffable, indescribable higher-mind that is one with the Universe.

      As you have found out, it is the act of realising that these things arise, stay and pass away. This simple act of observation creates a momentary gap between the observing-function and the incessant monkey-mind.

      What we need to do is to figure out how to calm down the monkey and make him obey you, instead of going on a rampage on his own.

      To do that, we have to learn how to reside in a somewhat-empty monkey-mind. This mind has 'cooled’ its fire and does not move around in a startled manner on its own. The deeper your practice, the more lucid your awareness, the more this monkey will listen to you and not give you problems.

      Sweep the false-thoughts away, they are empty to begin with anyway. Relax the bodily tension generated and let them go. Tighten your mind around the object. Simply keep doing this and you will realise that your lucidity brightens increasingly.

      The reason why you cannot pinpoint what it is that has gone wrong is because your body had become sick. The moment you try to 'grasp’ onto a moment, it has already fleeted away into the past. That vague sensation, that tip-of-the-tongue feeling is usually that of the body. When your lucidity deepens you will be able to see it more clearly. Do not feel troubled by it, simply relax, brighten the awareness on the object, give a light smile to soften the facial muscles, and continue. If you are feeling grumpy, just smile.

      If you understand this process, you can understand any method that is used to bring your mind to this stillness. As you go deeper, your mind will be able to reside in this stillness for longer periods of time. Please ask questions if you are still unsure.

      Whatever you can watch is conditioned and temporary. The awareness that is behind all of this, the function of observing all of this - this is more important.

      Instead of taking whatever you can see to be you, understand that it is not the self. By what you can see, this means - body, sensations, thoughts, and mind qualities.

      Whenever there is a reaction on one's part - perhaps one does not like something, or one likes something, or one expresses apathy towards it - these are all reactions that stem from a duality. This duality means - treating this as 'self' and that as 'not-self'. The actual fact is that everything is 'not-self’. Not even the incessant thoughts that continue inside your head continuously. The moment you grasp onto it, saying "this is my ideology, this is my perspective, this is my identity, this is me", you have taken something that is not-you to be you.

      By understanding that everything is not-self, one must remove every single notion of being selfish, of removing the ego. One example is that if one feels aversion towards something, it is indicative that one has already taken an identity. To be selfless is literally to have 'no sense of self'. To act in the welfare of all beings - not because you want to - but because it is the most natural thing to do when there is no sense of self. When there is no sense of ego, no fetters, there is only buddha-nature expressing itself in myriads of ways.

      This does not mean that the ego 'disappears’. It means that there is no ego in the first place. Everything that is seen as an ego is a mere illusion.
