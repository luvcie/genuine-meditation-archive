# External object meditation and blinking

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **pmd33** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 1 point · 1 year ago

Trataka

Zhine

I have been experimenting with developing concentration by placing my awareness on an external object, such as a tennis ball, or the tibetan letter "a" from the zhine instructions.

I found my concentration to be much easier to maintain than something like a mantra or my breath. It really looks like something I want to add to my practice, but I am a bit confused about some of the instructions I've read so far.

Apparently, blinking should be minimized as much as possible. The zhine instructions say this, but do not say why. I assume there must be a crucial benefit, so I researched further and other practices claim that blinking is correlated with inattentiveness or thoughts.

I always thought that states such as jhana arise when we lose all our clinging and merge with our meditation object. So I don't see why blinking would prevent that.

Before I try doing several 20 minute meditations with tears flowing down my eyes from the lack of blinking, I want to hear if anyone has any thoughts on this. Is the logic for the benefits of no blinking just a result of misunderstood tradition, or is it legit?

- **karmazopa** · 1 point · 1 year ago

  Strengthen your Samatha with the breath first. Certainly, you can do Samatha with an outer object, but the breath seems to be most helpful for majority of practitioners. You can try both methods and see which helps you have a more stable state of concentration. Try each method for a couple of weeks.

  I wouldn't focus on zhine techniques at this time. They're more advanced practices. Yes, you can do Samatha with your eyes open, as I and most other Tibetan Buddhist practitioners do, but the idea isn't to strive towards not blinking. Focus on the breath, not whether you're blinking or not. The not blinking happens naturally; nothing to concern yourself with at this time. If you practice with your eyes open, your gaze should be about 3-4 feet in front of you, looking at the ground. The focus of your eyes should be blurry or unfocused, so that you're not seeing the details of the floor/carpet/ground.

  There are certain practices where you look straight ahead, but without a clear focus. You're not starting at any object in the line of your sight. This, again, is more of an advanced technique.

  Strengthen your basics, before jumping into advanced levels. It's difficult to figure out Calculus problems if you're still trying to learn basic arithmetic.

- **BlissfulSavant** · 1 point · 1 year ago

  I can speak of Trataka on a flame. Maintaining contact without blinking is hard, initially the eyes would want to shut but don't let them. Why feed the body while you starve? Let it cry all it wants to, the body will adapt soon. I apply a cold water towel on my eyes after 20 minutes or so to rid myself of the excess heat however. This duration can be extended.

  If the eyes blink, you perceive darkness for a split second. More importantly you would be acutely aware that a transition happened. This is a concentration exercise after all.
