# Tips for eating vegetarian/sattvic diet?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sylamatek** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 1 year ago

Jump to the last two page breaks if you want to skip some background information.

Obviously I've known for years and years about how inhumane the commercial meat industry can be, even just at a fundamental level. I always brushed it off because I was a dumb kid that chose to ignore it so I could continue eating as much meat as humanly possible.

In the last year or two I've started to really process the whole concept in my head and examine it from a more compassionate standpoint and I'd like to try phasing it out.

That being said, I've always loved the taste of meat, and cheaper meat can be very filling, a great source of protein, and affordable for a college student. Because of those three things, switching to vegetarianism won't be a painless process unfortunately, but I feel like it's worth pursuing. I've also been interested in exploring a mostly sattvic diet, which is where this subreddit becomes a bit more relevant.

So I'd like to ask, what are some of the best ways to offset not eating meat financially and nutritionally?

In terms of protein, I guess the easy answer is nuts, but most nuts are quite expensive, and that's something I can't really sustain at 22. Eating handfuls of peanuts and almonds makes for a repetitive meal staple as well. I'm trying to eat low-carb on top of that (both for weight loss and because processed flour/sugar is also non-sattvic) so I would like to avoid loading up on whole grain rice, beans, and oats just to not necessitate eating a 1/2 bag of lettuce and 3$ of nuts per meal.
