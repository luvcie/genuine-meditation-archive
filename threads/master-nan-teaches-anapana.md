    - **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 1 point · 2 years ago

      It is not deliberately lengthening it, but noticing it.

      The only issue I have with this way of learning anapanasati when you first start out, is that you should not be placing a concept of a "gap" in between, because that will be enforcing a thought/idea about the gap. In this gap, it should be complete silence. However, even in the idea of "silence"”, this is not silence. All teachings in Buddhism use the "negation” principle to bring someone closer to the goal, rather than telling them the goal immediately, because it can mess it up.

      What you want to notice is the witnessing.

      There are the impermanent thoughts that keep rising, and this is the thinking mind. There is also the breath, which is impermanent. Like the thoughts, they rise and fall. So the idea here, is to synchronize the thinking mind and the breath.

      When you synchronize the breath and the thinking mind, the witnessing state becomes more prominent when the thoughts are lesser. This means that the breath becomes "lesser”, in the sense that it becomes more shallow, and the gaps widen. Then without conceptualizing this gap, you need to understand that this gap is simply the lack of birth/death of the breath or the lack of birth/death of the coarse thoughts.

      What is there remaining? What is there, even observing the rise and fall of the breath? What is there illuminating the coarse thoughts and seeing them rise and fall? By doing this, you naturally reside in the gap between breaths.
