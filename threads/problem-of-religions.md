# Problem of Religions

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 2 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/37hdsa/problem_of_religions/>

Today I wish to talk about the problem of religions and organized groups that operate with context to religion (or Buddhism).

One of the most dangerous things that can happen is when an unenlightened individual misunderstands the original teachings of the Buddha and reinterprets it in an erroneous manner. In fact, there are huge translation errors (even the subtle ones can mislead) in the Pali Canon translations. The reason for this is probably due to the inexperience of the translator. These translators are often academics or monks who have never entered jhanic states past the fourth jhana.

This presents a problem, because the real cultivators are often not involved in the translations - Instead, they retire to forests, mountains and their own circle of students and their teachings become less-known to all. There is another problem here. As the original teachings seem to "sub-divide" and gain more branches, the teachings become less accurate and more diluted. The teachings deviate from the original teachings and cause more confusion among the seekers of the Dharma.

The classic case presents itself when the students of teachers within these branches start to believe that their group lords over the other. This is fine if one has indeed attained enlightenment. However, this is not often the case. It is often propagated by a teacher who is not enlightened.

In fact, this is seen in other religions as well - this is not exclusive to Buddhism. Catholicism is often against Christianity - and even within Christianity, there are various subdivisions - All different interpretations of the teachings.

How do we solve this problem?

Firstly, we need the experience of an actual enlightened being. For example, I sought out the advice of many teachers who are indeed enlightened (to various degrees) - Non-returners, Arahants and Bodhisattvas. With their experience, they are able to give authentic, practical advice.

Secondly, we need accurate scriptures. The Chinese have always focused on the accuracy of their scriptures so we can certainly see a higher grade of accuracy. However, the problem lies in many English-translated scriptures. Most of the subtle language differences are missed. For example, 'jhana' is translated as 'concentration' instead of 'altered state of consciousness giving rise to bliss, etc'. Another example is how 'piti' is translated as 'rapture', which is inaccurate.

Thirdly, we must abandon all roots of religion and embrace a universal truth. In the world, there must surely be a single cosmic truth - No matter how many religions are out there, all of them are talking about the same thing. The only thing that differs is the degree of wisdom or perspective. For example, many concepts and experiential information in Buddhism can be found in the texts of the Catholic saints, Sufists, Kabbalists, Taoists, Yogi, etc. This truth is not simply exclusive to Buddhism.

"Buddha" means "One who has awakened". The one who understands the true nature of things is someone who is fully enlightened. There is only one true nature of things.

If one were to traverse the immaterial realms, you can immediately see that there is no distinction between the domains of Krishna or other enlightened masters. People use different words but they all point to the same things.

If you were to hear or read the teachings of another - Ask yourself: Does this person embody the seven factors of enlightenment? Has this person cut the fetters - the lower and higher fetters? Does this person have an addiction or compulsion? Is this person able to describe the immaterial realms and what each jhana and samadhi means? Does this person live in accordance to the Noble Eightfold Path?

Often, you might see an unenlightened and non-skilful teacher who misguides students. One might tell the student to disregard the phenomena experienced. One might even go as far as to declare a student "deranged", "mad" or "possessed by demons". There have been many people who have been told to "ignore the illusion". However, if one is experienced, one would realize that the experiences were components of the deep meditative experience. Instead of ignoring or shirking away from it, a good teacher will guide the student along.

Remember: You should only learn from your own experience, after testing it out for yourself. Texts, words, sayings - all of these are subject to all kinds of interpretation. There is no saying how accurate these words are. One can only verify it with one's own experiences.

What do you think? Have you had an experience like that?
