  Why has a sangha member down-voted this when this is a person who is suffering? This is not the Dharma the Buddha taught, which is a Dharma that is for the well-being for one and all - one must have right conduct and act kindly with wisdom.

  > “This may be more of a psychic spat, but whats odd is that it has taken toll on parts of my body rather then the ‘whole, to which case I am at odds with myself rather then being at odds with them.

  You are never at odds with anybody else. When one feels anger, it is one that has altered one's own physiology to match anger. When one feels loved, it is the deed of oneself. When one feels anything, it is because one has accepted that 'gift' and now has clung onto it.

  What you feel is what you have focused on. When you place your attention on these thoughts, they raise a physiological change in your body - these sensations are what you have claimed to be a "bad effect". Now notice that this, too, is a labeling which brings in even more thoughts to reinforce the idea, as if it has a mind of its own. It isn't you. You are not this ever-shifting construct. There is no "you" in this thought. If it was not there a moment ago, and now it is - It must be separate from you.

  Now place your attention on the breath and nothing else. When you place your attention on just the breath, there is no room for any other thought. When your attention moves back, you simply tend to the breath again and again.

  > Meditation cannot seem to over come it, and neither does compassion.

  There is right meditation, and there is ineffective meditation. When one meditates rightly, the conditions are fulfilled and a result is born from right causes.

  Compassion can only come when one has a mind that is unscattered. In your state, energy cannot arise, so where does compassion arise from? Therefore, tend to the mind - for your mind has mistakenly taken your feelings as "you".

  In between every thought is a space. It is a space that has no thought. In this space, there is infinite possibility for any thought to occur. Therefore, see that what you feel or hear are impermanent. They rise, and they shall fade away. Pay attention to your breath. See how it joins with every 'thought' of the breath. Then see the space in between the breath.

  Seeing this, one delights in the one-pointedness of mind. Bliss and joy arises from withdrawal of attention from sensory objects and fills one's body. One enters the first jhana and remains in it, delights in it, and the brahmavihara of compassion arises naturally in him.

  > Its gone as far as me and say my father having the exact same dream. And the same happened with my last boss, while I can see how that this may be some kind of delusion, the effect is none the less real.

  When one is in emptiness, there is no evil that can reach him. When one is boundless, where can anyone find him? Let go, and from the emptiness comes the bliss of the jhanas that burn away afflictions that trap you.
