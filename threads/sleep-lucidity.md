# Sleep Lucidity

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 9 points · 2015-11-02

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3r82zi/sleep_lucidity/>

This will be the first part on sleep lucidity. More will follow, as this can be a very deep topic.

You must realise that sleep is unconscious meditation. This is when your subconscious mind gains full control of the mindscape, and the conscious mind, for most people, becomes inactive.

Why develop lucidity? For a start, one should already be consistently practising the four references of mindfulness, no matter one is on the mat or off the mat - Eating, drinking, talking, etc. By doing this, one has a 'third-person' view of one's own thoughts, actions and words. This generates a detachment of awareness from the body.

By developing lucidity, you bring the same practice into sleep. And this practice can be manifolds powerful.

Meditate before bed, to bring the depth of awareness up.

Lie on the bed and relax completely, saturating in blissfulness, peace and luxuriant joy. Ensure that you can relax the whole body, to the point that you cannot feel the body anymore.

Relax the mind, and let go completely, going sequentially deeper and deeper, letting the mind sink. It can help to imagine your body melting away or sinking.

Still the mind so that no thoughts rise. Do this until hypnogogic patterns can be seen. Random images may start to flicker. You might see spectral patterns or kaleidoscopes or blurred colors and patterns.

You might feel intense vibrations, or any kind of unfamiliar phenomena, if you are not consistently meditating to that level yet.

Now if you can recall a previous dream, do it with the intent of finding something strange with it. Then, in the dream, realize that you are dreaming, and you will then be lucid.

Once lucid, examine your four references of mindfulness again. This will be on a very subconscious level, which is much deeper than in waking life, now that you have no physical sensations to obscure it.

Practice using your mind to explore your dream, such as moving around, teleporting, moving through walls, etc. These are similar to astral travel, but have a slight difference.

I will write more soon, but developing this very ability takes time.

One will need to do an anchoring habit, such as pushing your thumb through your other hand's palm, to remember keeping mindfulness on your four references. This allows you to awaken during the dream state when you find it bizarre.

One will also need to develop recall of dreams to increase sensitivity to the bizarre nature of dreams. You can do this by actively writing down your dream in the morning. When you first start, this might be just a few lines, but if you consistently practice, this will increase in detail and length.

The practice should start throughout the day - Such that one programs one's mind to continuously strive for mindfulness. Having skilful action naturally, one then does the same in the dream state.

While dreaming, do we actually go through the astral realm; or is it rather that we create our own dream realm, through which the astral can manifest or give intuitive communications? (or perhaps that is through the akasha?) I'd appreciate some elaboration or clarification of this. I believe there are some people that believe the whenever we sleep we astrally project, but I don't recall if it was you or another person who said that that is in fact not the case.

It is not a yes-or-no thing, but a spectrum, where the dream space intertwines with the astral space. Simply put, dream-space is usually when the brain still has activity, while a true out-of-body is when the body seems to cease activities completely - brainwaves disappear and heartbeat can stop. Most people do not achieve a full projection and can reside in between the two, within the spectrum.

It depends on how much wisdom one has attained, and how intensely one practices - to the point that the waking reality is seen as much as a dream as the sleeping state is.

When I get random images, I try to focus on touching sensation. If I touch something in the dream, I become lucid. I cannot mantain lucidity during the night probably because I will feel too tired, but I can if it's morning. Other tricks I use are watching my hands, shouting "increase lucidity", do a spin pirouette when it's fading, and remaining perfectly still if I wake up, waiting for connecting to other lucid dreams.

If you are able to maintain lucidity throughout the night, then perhaps you can practice going deeper into fourth jhana, and have an out-of-body experience. In this way, one typically feels vigorous, with the energy that rises during these deep states of meditation.
