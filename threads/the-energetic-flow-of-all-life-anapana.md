# The Energetic Flow of All Life (Anapana)

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 5 points · 2015-09-25

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/3maksx/the_energetic_flow_of_all_life_anapana/>

I realized that I did not discuss this in the subreddit's posts (although I relayed these instructions through messages), so please forgive me for having not noticed. There is a very crucial point that needs to be made for practitioners of breath meditation.

This is a core principle that many people erroneously do not learn of, because it involves a profound understanding of what this technique does. Anapanasati is the mindfulness of energetic movement, not physical respiratory mechanisms.

Let us dissect what Anapanasati means.

ana = Inhalation, contraction. When air is sucked into a balloon, the rubber material stretches. This is about the intake of energy.

pana = Exhalation, expansion. When air is released from a balloon, the rubber material loosens. This is about releasing of energy.

sati = Mindfulness (the four references)

When your body inhales, it is known in biology as an 'active' process. This means that your muscles are straining and taking in energy. However, many people have taken this in the wrong way, and even monks are teaching people to inhale by pushing your abdomen outwards.

For this, we must look at our energy system. There is a vortex that stores our primordial life force which lies inside the navel (1.5 inches deep) and slightly lower. The Taoists call it the Lower Dantian (or immortality field). The energetic system criss-crosses with the physical system so they often work in tandem.

If you notice, now, inhaling is all about taking in air. The Indians say that this is the intake of prana. But many people have got it wrong. It is not the air that drives energy, but the whole body. The whole energy body is breathing. If you are aware and sensitive enough, whenever you breathe in, it might seem as if you are taking in energy from every single pore of your entire body.

Hence, when we inhale, it is more natural for us to retract the lower abdomen backwards towards this energy field. It is not that expanding the abdomen is bad, it is simply more efficient energetically to breathe in and retract the lower abdomen. When breathing in, imagine breathing through your entire body.

This is how a fetus breathes in the womb of the mother, when it has to rely on the umbilical cord to receive maternal 'breath'. In order to take in energy from its navel, it needs to contract the lower abdomen.

Exhaling, on the other hand, is about releasing of energy. By exhaling, your whole body 'breathes' outwards. Hence, release your lower abdomen and allow it to move outwards.

It is important to understand that the 'breath' is the gateway to understanding this. The breath acts like a stimulant to the energy system. In esoteric alchemy, the breath causes circulation of various energy points in the body (but that is too complicated and I won't talk about it here because it serves no use).

So how is it relevant to the flow of life?

Each inhalation is like a birth of a life, each exhalation is like the death of a life. What is life anyway, but a form of energy?

Look at the world around you. All things expand and contract, all the time, ebbing and flowing. It is the principle of activeness and passiveness that makes things cyclical.

If you take a microscope and look into that microscopic level, you will see all of these cells pulsate with rhythmic inwards and outwards flow.

Homeostasis is a word used to describe how a system stays within stability, balanced between its inwards and outwards flows. Nature is constantly flowing and ebbing. The entire system is doing that, even right now.

You are part of the flow.

Be aware of the energetic flow of your own body. This is sati with regards to the breath.

Now once you are aware of the ana and pana... What lies in between?

Between inhalation and exhalation, you have a period of silence, where there is no flow. Be aware of this pause. It is a momentary cessation of coarse thought.

The more aware you are of it, then the longer it will become. Eventually, you will reach samadhi.
