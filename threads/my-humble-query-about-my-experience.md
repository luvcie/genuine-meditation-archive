# My humble query about my experience

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **reddamnit2** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 1 year ago

Hi All.

Thank you for accepting my request. I come from an Advaita background. I have been studying Advaita Vedanta as taught by Shankara/Gaudapada for 9 years now. Other than the principle Vedantic texts, I have also studied parts of the epics Mahabharatha and Ramayana. I have also tried to study about Tantra and other philosophies including various forms of Buddhism. This I have done out of academic interest. I am aware of the basic tenets of almost all

these philosophies but not in detail. There are few things that I noticed such as : 1) there seems to be an innate connection between Tantra and Buddhism 2) Tantra/Buddhists emphasise more on meditation and practise than the Vedantins. Although, I have been studying Vedanta for 9 years, although my understanding was better than before, I could not control my senses. In practise, I was still a hedonist. I had access to everything which could destroy my mind and have it enslaved to senses. Finally, I moved out to secluded location, renounced all superfluous things slowly. It was extremely difficult because even after I started living alone with bare minimum necessities my mind hovered over those things such as delicious food etc. But thanks to my daily study, as if like a final push, I read that sankalpas(resolution of mind, I shall meditate, I shall be happy etc) are the root cause of entangling of mind. So basically I stopped thinking. I imagined myself to be in an ashram in front of a guru sitting silently(without thoughts, but with that one single thought). This was a tremendous thing because I started progressing at a rapid pace after this. The anxiety, the confusion, the doubts all started subsiding. This particular process is called Upasana, in sanskrit. After few weeks or perhaps a month, I was able to regulate my desires. I only took food as if it were medicine.

It was during that time, I bumped into the thread - truth about deep meditation. That gave me an incentive to go deeper and made me believe that, I might actually attain something if I practice diligently. I would like to share this meditation experience which I only had last week. Please note that, I have taken a holiday from work and I have dedicated my entire time towards pratising Yoga, meditation and self-study. I still have couple months of time. I am 28 years old btw. Surprisingly it spanned more than 15 mins because until recently, I have never meditated more than a min or two. I sat in half-lotus position, held the jnana mudra and Shambhavi mudra (partially close your eyes, gaze at the middle of the eyebrow visualizing a deity or a guru or any other preferred form). The interesting thing here is that, I had never been able to hold onto Shambhavi mudra before but it just felt right. I loosened the grip over my body, visualized my guru (not a personal guru) and sat calmly conscious of the fact that I was trying not to think. I repeated OM few times. I said to myself, I am without support, I am not embodied, I am not etc until I involuntarily stopped thinking. Now, I could 'see' my guru as if he were real. This felt so good that I remained in this state for about 15-20 mins. Then I realized that I was not able to breathe properly and that attention brought me back.

Now here is the part which I thought was new. When I opened my eyes, at first I felt disoriented slightly. Then I had to survey the place around slowly and realized I was in my home. For a few mins, I just sat as if dumbfounded, or as if I just woke up from my sleep. For a few more mins, I was observing everything around as if I were a child. After a few mins I got up and resumed mundane work. But while I was at it, I could still 'feel' the silence in 'contrast’' to what I was doing. This lasted only for few more mins which felt like moments. Since then, I have been getting random flashbacks of my life. It is not just mere recollection. It is as if I could survey the places that I am remembering. I was amazed at how detailed my recollections were. It's been a week since I sat in this meditation and even now I get vivid glimpses of memory. The last thing is, lack of sleep. It is as if I have binged on red bull. What does your good-self make of it? What level of Jhana do you think it is? I would also like to mention that, I can easily get into meditative state, mainly due to Shambhavi mudra but have not got the opportunity to get back to that state. I am back at my parent's place for 3-4 days.

  > EDIT : Does one have to be a buddhist (which type) to enter the jhana states? Any comment by /u/refugeinbuddha

  No, one does not have to be a "Buddhist” to enter jhana states, in a religious sense. As long as one has right morals, meditates correctly and follows the noble eightfold path, regardless of any religion, one is already a genuine Buddhist. "Bodhi”, from which the word "Buddha" is derived from, means truth. As long as one is following the path of truth, one is a Buddhist.

  In that sense, it is entirely possible that one enters jhana. However, as you noted, you did not experience joy or bliss, which is characteristic of the first jhana. Therefore, it might be likely that you stilled your mind, but it did not develop into the full joy that erupts from the heart.
