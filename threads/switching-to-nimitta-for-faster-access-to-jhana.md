# Switching to Nimitta for faster access to Jhana

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Technique` · 5 points · 2 years ago

This post was made to address this major question as highlighted by /u/elnoxvie in the question here.

Note: There are a lot of teachers who talk about nimitta, but their understanding of it is merely surface-level. I must ask you to start reading this post with a fresh perspective.

Firstly, what is nimitta?

In English, this is normally translated as "sign of absorption". However, from personal experience, this should be known as "characteristics" instead of "signs".

The Buddha often emphasized the importance of recognizing this sign, and instructed his students to grasp this sign as the meditation object. This is seen in the Sangiti Sutta (DN 33.1.25) and Satipatthana Samyutta Sutta (SN 8).

Sangiti Sutta

...when one has properly grasped a sign (samadhi-nimittam), has well considered it, applied one's mind to it (supadharitam) and has penetrated it with wisdom (suppatividdham pannaya). At this joy (sukha) arises in one, and from this joy (sukha), [bliss (piti) arises); and by this bliss one's senses are calmed, one feels happiness as a result, and with this happiness one's mind is established in absorption.

Satipatthana Samyutta Sutta

So too, monks, here some wise, competent, skillful monk dwells contemplating the physical body, ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world. While one dwells contemplating the physical body, one's mind becomes absorbed (jhana), one's corruptions are abandoned, one picks up the sign (jhana-nimitta). One dwells contemplating the (5 Skhandas) body (rupa)… sensations (vedana)… perception (sañña)… mental states (sañkhara)… cognition (viññana)… ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world. While he dwells contemplating phenomena, his (her) mind becomes absorbed (jhana), one's corruptions (nivarana) are abandoned one picks up the sign (nimitta) of absorption (jhana).

Also please note that the right term is jhana-nimitta or samadhi-nimitta, not nimitta itself. Nimitta is used here for short-hand's sake.

There are many interpretations of nimitta, which are merely limited understanding. Some people believe a spherical orb of light to be nimitta.

The real understanding of nimitta is that it is a characteristic of jhana. This nimitta can appear in ANY of the senses: Sight, sound, taste, hearing and touch.

The most common nimittas that one experiences can include all or just one of the below:

Visual nimitta: Bright lights, auras, streaks of light, strobing light, shimmering light, etc.

Auditory nimitta: High-pitched whistling, noise-like pitch, water-gushing sounds, whirring. (It appears to come from the middle of the head and all directions)

Kinesthetic nimitta: Tingling pleasure of the body.

Now what is the said technique of using any of these nimitta for one's meditation?

The problem with using the breath is this: Although it is potent, at a certain point of concentration, the breath starts to become shallow and hard to perceive. But it is by this time where the auditory and kinesthetic nimitta have surfaced.

Hence, in order to sustain the absorption state to keep it from scattering, one shifts the anchor from the breath (or whatever meditative object you use) to one of the nimitta. Being characteristics of jhana, they do not disappear like the breath.

So what is the purpose of switching to the nimitta? It is not the nimitta itself that creates jhana. Nimitta is already a characteristic that surfaces from jhana. What allows one to deepen into Jhana is not focusing on the nimitta, but sustaining the state of absorption.

Things to note:

These nimitta can appear small at first, but can increase exponentially or gradually.

For example, the kinesthetic nimitta can result in an out-of-body experience, as described in the Jhana thread. The visual nimitta can result in hallucinatory-like vividness or patterns of light/glows.

      > Are there mental nimitta opposed to sense-level nimitta?

      It is a gradual experience but they are not separate.

      Physical tingling -> Mental tingling -> Out-of-body experience

      The nimitta does not ever go away, it expands as absorption increases.

      > Can the state of absorption itself be used as an anchor; are the factors of each jhana also nimitta?

      At every stage, you discern the factors and let go of one trait. For example, from second jhana, to move to equanimity, you must let go of the physical tingling so that it proceeds into purely mental bliss. You can say that the nimitta do intersect with the jhana factors. It is much easier to follow the traits of each jhana as described by the sutta though. You must realize that the reason why the Buddha selected these particular traits to describe is because they remain as part of the seven factors of enlightenment and are consistent.
