# Fear

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **TamSanh** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 4 points · 2 months ago

Hello all,

Perhaps you may help me with my particular predicament?

Sometimes, during my sits, when I start to get deeper into meditation, I will begin to hear creaking noises, or clicking noises. In my mind, this conjures images of ghosts phasing through walls. The reason these images appear is because, during a meditation retreat several months ago, I had a few encounters with non-humans that has left me associating surrounding noises with them, in a non-pleasant way.

Thus, I feel I am in a bit of a quandary. In one hand, I know my concentration is not strong enough to overcome my fears and attachment to form, but in the other hand I feel like I am arrested by those same fears, and prevented from attaining that higher level of concentration necessary to relinquish those fears.

Perhaps the correct answer is to forget the ordeal, completely, and just continue to watch those fears, but my lay life has left my mind in a space where simple watching is not simple. I feel like the only solution is to go on retreat, but I am reluctant to do so at this time, due to other obligations.

Any advice in the matter, I would humbly appreciate.

Peace

EDIT The sublime states of the brahmaviharas have helped to give me a base from which to work with in observing and confronting fear. While there are still inklings of fear that arise, I think I see now how to steady the mind so that it does not follow. Thank you all very much for your kind answers. I will continue to practice.

  Paramananda will obviously have a better answer to this based on actual experience, but I recall I had fears about this in the past and I asked him about it.

  He mentioned a story about monks at the time of the Buddha were harassed by tree spirits in the forest while meditating, inducing in them horrible sights, smells and noises. They ran to the Buddha and asked what to do. Buddha, in return, told them to practice metta and infuse themselves with the brahmaviharas. Doing so, the spirits couldn’t touch them. It would be like ice touching boiling water. Filling oneself with Yang Qi from cultivating such mental states of joy, compassion, loving-kindness and equanimity, one is rendered untouchable by these beings.

  Another option is to use a lower demon dispelling mantra such as Om Ha Li Di Ga and various other mantras such as Omerlah as a way of calming the mind and entering a protective mental state. Paramananda made a post about mantras in the past here. Yet another is to use the Vajra-fist mudra when fearful.

  Another thing that could be helpful is to imagine your spiritual teacher or guide whether that be Buddha, Jesus, or a personal Guru, and merging with Him and feeling his protective coat all over you - imagining golden covered light emanating around you like a egg-shell made of light - impenetrable by harmful forces - filled with divine loving light. I personally find this brings me joy and can take me out of stressed/disturbed mental states if done with vigour and faith.

  These are just some ideas off the top of my mind. I hope they can be of aid to you.

- **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 5 points · 2 months ago

  As /r/Sherlockian_Holmes wonderfully described, those are good ways to calm down the mind.

  If it helps you, before you sit for meditation, you can do either of the following or all of them:

  1. Prostrate to the Triple Gem and dedicate your entire meditative practice to the benefit of all beings. Having this steadfast in your mind, it does not matter what happens to you - because compassion is foremost.

  2. Before sitting, invoke the plea: "I offer the fragrance of my conduct, samadhi and wisdom to the Buddhas in all Ten Directions, and may I receive help and guidance in this meditation."” Visualize in your mind's eye great Dakinis, Dharma Protectors and gods standing around you in brilliant effulgence, guarding the doorways, the windows, the walls, all around you.

  3. Chant a mantra 108 times - this helps to still the mind and make it equanimous. Furthermore, it establishes contact with an enlightened being.

  After sitting, dedicate all your meditative efforts to the merits of other people - perfect your conduct consistently and carry your meditation to your daily life.

  Lastly, a disciple of the Buddha does not fear death - even in the face of death, one should have loving-compassion for the suffering of oneself and others. Radiate metta, cultivate the four brahmaviharas, and take care of Dharma - then you will protected by Dharma.
