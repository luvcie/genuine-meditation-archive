# Talk About Jhana Factors

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Discussion` · 7 points · 2 years ago

Here is the roadmap again:

1. Wholesome mind guarded by excellent sati practice

2. One withdraws from sensory pleasure, gains first jhana factors.

3. One no longer needs to sustain attention, gains second jhana factors as a result of tranquility.

4. One gains deep equanimity, where one becomes profoundly untouchable, gains third jhana factors.

5. One becomes void of pain and pleasure, gains fourth jhana factors.

6. Having culminated in four jhana factors, one then sheds the physical body and has a mind-created body (yin-body emanation) with full faculties.

7. Exploring the siddhis, as well as the four immaterial attainments, one then fully understands the Four Noble Truths.

8. One "merges" with the Divine/sat/God (yoga) and there is enlightenment. After enlightenment, one then applies this transcendent knowledge and works it into the material world, teaching others.

I am encouraged by the progress that many have made, so I thought that a thread like this would be good for some who have tread stages of the path to share their experience with others who have not.

Of course, a vast majority of the members in the online sangha have attained a least first jhana. However, there are some who are newer or have not yet had the faith to take up a rigorous practice. I am hoping that this thread can spur inspiration among the members.

So perhaps some things you can talk about, if you have it:

e What allowed you to feel joy/bliss in your meditations?

* What makes you consistent in experiencing the jhanas?

e Did you lose track? How did you recover?

e Any other things you want to share

  Here is whats working for me at the moment. I do pranayama twice daily. Before each session I have found it very important to do a relaxing exercise where I scan the body making sure most of the body is comfortable (there may be pains but I let them be there). When I feel quite comfortable I will set the intention to apply and sustain attention to either the breath+gap between or piti if it is present. Recently I have found that the longer attention is sustained, the better results I see and it is also easier to keep sustaining the attention. For a long time I have only been sustaining the attention shortly and then losing mindfulness to thoughts repeatedly. Really intending to have 100% sustained attention with the breath, just like giving attention away, and then doing it has helped tremendous. It seems to make the mind state quite impenetrable to thoughts. But a single glimpse in attention can make thoughts appear again. Hence it is also important to keep applying attention whenever a glimpse happens. I think I am closing in on mastering first jhana.

  Some notes

  Metta towards yourself seems to have calming effect on the mind. Do it at the start every meditation and can also be used if restless/ too many negative emotions.

  If there are too many thoughts, then watch them in a detached manner. Eventually the frequency of thoughts arising will slow down and make it possible to onepointing on the meditation object. Similarly with any feeling tone, excitedness, boredom etc. Labelling in and outbreath with meditation word like bud-dho is effective in learning sustaining attention.

  Sustaining attention is important, don't let mindfulness wander in thoughts. Decide on a clear object and set the intention to apply attention and sustain it for 10 mins ie.

  Piti has to stabilize before switching to it else the focus is unstable. I found recently that it works better to meditate on breath for a little longer while piti is stabilizing instead of switching focus immaturely.

  Find a body scanning that makes you relaxed and do at beginning of sessions to learn to do it effectively. Relaxation is key.

  If piti is not rising, try inducing the attitudes 'joyfull’ and 'relaxed’. Sustaining attention and being comfortable and relaxed is key.

  If there is restlessness, give the mind something to do, and gradually do it slower. Like scanning points in the body.

  If there is boredom, induce the attitude 'concentrating more makes this interesting '
