# Road block when breath ceases

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **He_of_the_Hairy_Arms** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 4 points · 1 year ago

Hello,

It's been awhile since I posted here, but today I think I had a small success and also a new difficulty and wanted to see if anyone had advice. I was keeping my grip gentle but consistent on the sensation of my breath, and after some difficulty, found my breath dwindling down to almost nothing, and quickly. At the same time my head filled with light and the beginnings of piti. However, here is where I got stuck. With no breath, or very little breath, to attend to, I didn't know what to do. I found it very difficult to simply stop worrying about the breath and switch over to the joy as my meditation object, yet at the same time when I tried to set the joy aside and focus again on breathing, my breaths felt too effortful, as if I didn't need to be breathing so much.

Perhaps this is just the next step for me to get the hang of, but does anyone have advice on how to proceed from here?

Thank you as always.

  exhaust your lungs completely and drop all will and connection to them and let yourself sag like a corpse or sack, not breathing and continue to just observe and drop connection to your body - note: not holding your breath just completely losing all desire to influence it or want it and leaving yourself to just sag like an empty sack, and just observe your meditation and after an uncomfortable wait when you wonder if this is really what you want to do, you'll see your body kick back in and take over the breathing but extremely deep and slow (will feel like drawing a single thread of breath very deep and slow) - allow it to do its thing but stay detached from it and just observe from your new detached perspective and let your body do whatever it wants. At first you will feel dead and you'll be scared, but stay steadfast and resolute in maintaining your perspective and attention on your meditation. The elation will come, but even that isn't really the key - the key is to maintain your detached perspective.

  You'll develop what I can only describe as a very deep and slow thread of breath - it's very obvious and you'll learn to recognise that feeling in future and be able to use it as a yardstick for other practises to know you've hit your mark
