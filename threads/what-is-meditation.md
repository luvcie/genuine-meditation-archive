# What is Meditation?

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2u2mrq/what_is_meditation/>

Note: Do not skip this post as a basic thread.

One of the biggest problems of the current state of suttas is the inaccuracies of translation of abstract concepts by unenlightened/inexperienced translators. For example, you might have thought that Thanissaro Bhikkhu was a monk and hence his translations can be trusted. The fact is that there are a lot of nuances in the abstract concepts that are wrong.

He himself admitted that meditation gave him a headache. Most of the non-abstract concepts can be quite accurate, however a big problem exists when some of these suttas are badly translated. The original Sanskrit versions are what contained the real knowledge.

A big example of this is that many translators tend to equate "phala" with "maga", meaning that practice is the same as attainment. This is not so. The Buddha has explicitly announced that it was "maga" that led to "phala", meaning that when one practices, it will inevitably lead to the fruit of the Path.

Now knowing that there are inaccuracies in the meditative translations, especially that of the English versions, you must exercise caution even when following suttas. Compare it with your own experience.

Here, we come to the topic of "What is Meditation".

What is meditation?

This question is extremely important. To understand what meditation really is, you need to answer this question: What is the goal of meditation? The goal is obvious: To end suffering and to be enlightened.

Now let us look at what many meditation techniques consist of. They mainly consist of a person focusing all their attention on one object, which is usually a form of sensory-object (This was explained in this thread[1] ).

What exactly is this? Why does focusing lead to any deeper states? To understand this, we need to look at first Jhana. The factors of the first Jhana include: Vitakka and Vicara (sustained and applied attention), Bliss (piti) and Sukha (joy) birthed from withdrawal.

The theme of this first jhana is Withdrawal. But withdrawal from what? It means a withdrawal from all the other senses, leaving the meditator applying focus on a single sense-object. This is exactly what Vitakka and Vicara means.

That is what many people call meditation "technique". All of those techniques allow one to enter first Jhana, to reach the point of one-pointed attention (Vitakka and Vicara). Having one-pointedness, one then enters bliss and joy.

But is meditation merely defined by its technique of entry into first Jhana? Not so. Meditation encompasses the whole length of the eight samadhis and beyond, into enlightenment.

If I could phrase meditation into a definition, I would say this:

It is a skillful means of learning how to let go.

We beings have it upside-down. Naturally, nothing was ever grasped.

      If you look at this thread, it tells you the characteristics of the first Jhana. Take these factors to be the ingredients of the cuisine of first Jhana.

      So let's look at what you require first:

      > """ o Applied and sustained attention. e Joy. This joy is often felt throughout the whole body as a tingling sensation. Mental bliss arises as well. ...... B,

      How do you get the first ingredient? Well, to get there, you need a meditation object. One-pointedness on a meditation object such as the breath gets you to this "applied and sustained attention”. Once you have it, what else is needed? Joy and bliss. This is what you described as the tingling. It is a simple physical pleasure. You don't need it to be full-body as of yet, although it is quite possible to be.

      Now, to move into the second Jhana, you need this:

      > """ e Applied and sustained attention => Unified Awareness. e Joy and Bliss from Tranquility. This bliss feels more that of tranquility, as compared to the withdrawing in the first jhana.

      Now how do you get the ingredient of unified awareness? You detach from the meditation object that brought you to applied and sustained attention and let it automatically roam onto the physical pleasure you feel. You don't need to draw it upwards. As if one was bathing in powder of blissfulness, the body fills up with bliss and becomes saturated in it. Feeling the bliss permeate the whole body, if you remain in this, the whole body may start to feel a full body tingle.

      Once you have done so, move to the third Jhana:

      > e Unified Awareness. e Faded Joy. The physical tingling has usually reduced by a lot by now, there might be remnants of mental tingling pleasure. e Pure Bliss. Bliss now exists alone and there is a very strong equanimity happening.

      The only ingredient you need to remove is physical joy. Now it switches to a different kind of pleasure, which is quite mental in nature.
