# Sylamatek's Meditation Journal

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sylamatek** · *(OCR)* · `Journal` · 3 points · 2 years ago

Some background, I'm a 21 year old that has been practicing meditation for a small amount of time. I began last fall, but stopped for almost 8 months. I've picked up again and have already passed where I last stopped in terms of time per day.

Currently I meditate for 25 minutes twice daily, once either when I wake up or when I get home from school, and again shortly before bedtime.

I cushion myself on a folded up blanket and sit half-lotus style, burn some incense, and use an app called Taoist Meditations. It has a timer that will play singing bowls every 30 seconds to help you re-focus when your thoughts wander. It also has a built in journal and Tac Te Ching to reference, along with some basic definitions of the different kinds of meditations one can do.

While practicing mindful meditation, the main thing I've noticed when I enter access concentration is that my sense of hearing seems to be incredibly attenuated. I'm assuming that since my sense of taste is relatively unstimulated, my eyes are closed (most times), and I consistently smell the same incense, this allows me to focus solely on hearing.

I have not experience any nimitta since I resumed meditating but I know that with time they will probably come again.

I usually alternate which foot is over the top in half lotus between sessions, and on my right foot especially I often get nasty amounts of pins and needles post session from my foot falling asleep.

  "Access concentration” is a commentary term that is found nowhere in the suttas, hence I do not support that term. In my own experience, 'access concentration' is a term that does not help. It simply gives meditators another name to think about.

  Instead, it should simply be withdrawing from sensory pleasure, seeking a wholesome mental state (balanced between torpor and anxiety), then vitarka (applying attention) and vicara (sustaining the attention for as long as possible without distraction), reaching ekagatta (one-pointedness), giving rise to bliss and joy.

  In simpler words, it is firstly stopping any sensory attachment. Next, give rise to a wholesome mental state, such as lovingness. Use this lovingness towards the meditation object. Keep the attention on it without straining but also not getting distracted. Continue. Eventually joy rises from the heart, along with tingling that can spread to full-body.

  Jhana-nimittas rise from absorption. Some might hear high-pitched ringing or a bright light. Because the conditions that cause absorption is a one-pointed mind, etc - you should keep your mind on the original meditation object while being aware of these things rising.

  When the breath, for example, becomes too subtle to feel, then switching to something like the tingling bliss can help to maintain the mental state and deepen the absorption.
