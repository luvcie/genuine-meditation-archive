# Be careful when listening to advice on meditation

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 6 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2t5xtt/be_careful_when_listening_to_advice_on_meditation/>

The very first question you must ask yourself before you listen to any advice is this:

Is this person enlightened or at least a stream-enterer? Are these the words of an enlightened master? Are these words, if they are, accurately translated and placed in a correct context? Were these principles compatible that of the Buddha's original instructions?

The Buddha is the Fully Awakened One. Even before his awakening, he learnt yogi techniques that brought him into the seventh & eighth immaterial-samadhi. It was the Buddha who rejected these teachings and transcended beyond that of teachings. What made him even more noble was that he dedicated his life to sharing his teachings to people. As such, while there were many enlightened beings, Gautama Siddharta is indeed a "fully" awakened one, where it was completely due to his merit that he awakened to.

As such, there is no advice that can compare to that of the Buddha's. Now it is also difficult to say if some of the suttas were originally the words of the Buddha. One must hence be vigilant and investigate the phenomena on one's own.

There are all kinds of frauds in this world, pertaining to meditation. The worst thing is when people claim enlightenment falsely when all they have had is a very small and deluded attainment. Hence exercise your eye of wisdom - While skepticism should not be placed where it should not be found, where it becomes speculation instead, one should investigate the truth, then be open to whether this truth is false or real. Open-minded investigation is also one of the keys to successful spiritual cultivation.

The best way to determine is to look for the fruits of attainment as described by the Buddha.

Strive well and let peace take you to dharma.
