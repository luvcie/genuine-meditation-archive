# Having Trouble Concentrating On the Breath?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *(OCR)* · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · `Advice` · 9 points · 2 years ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/2uxsry/having_trouble_concentrating_on_the_breath/>

When the beginner breathes in, the mind behaves like a wild monkey, jumping through many thoughts. When an advanced practitioner breathes in, the mind behaves like a tamed monkey, hanging onto just one thought.

How do you get to this point? Firstly, you must understand this principle.

When the breath is roused in any way, there is always a thought that is associated with it. The mind and breath are linked in this way.

For example, inhalation is associated with inwards movement of breath. This is associated with the thought of the breath itself. This is akin to the "stress" of birth.

Exhalation is associated with outwards movement of the breath. This is akin to the "stress" of death or passing away.

When you look at everything around you, what does not take birth or pass away? There is none. There are things that last for very long periods of time, but in the end, it disappears like a sandcastle.

Now look at this breath of yours. When this breath takes form within the body, it is similar to taking birth. When this breath loses form within the body, it is similar to passing away. Each movement of the breath is associated with the stirring of the mind.

If you do have trouble trying to perceive that stirring of the mind whenever the breath moves, then you can try the following exercise:

1. When you breathe in, mentally say the word "In" for the full duration of the inhalation.

2. When you breathe out, mentally say the word "Out" for the full duration of the exhalation.

3. When there is the slight pause in the middle, do not utter any word. There should be no stirring of the mind.

4. Doing this, you will be able to identify exactly when it is that your mind stops stirring. You continue doing this "In" and "Out" for as long as you like, until you are able to sustain just one thought of "In" when inhalation, and "Out" in exhalation.

5. When your mind is still and you are able to do it, then remove the words and simply focus on the thought of the breath itself.

There are actually variations of this. I do not recommend counting breaths as they can be very distracting and counter-productive.

You can use the words "Bud-dha" or "Dham-ma" or "Sang-ha". Ajahn Lee Dhammadharo used the "Bud-dha" mantra a lot when teaching his students. There is really no difference, because this is all part of Anapanasati. You need to be able to recognize the stress of inhalation (like birth) and stress of exhalation (like passing away).

And of course, doing so, you will arrive at its middle. But do not conceptualize it! There is a reason why the Buddha did not talk about this gap. It should not be cognized, otherwise you would be stirring your mind in this gap, thinking and clinging onto this concept. Simply place your mind into the length of the inhalation and exhalation.

I hope this helps people concentrate on the breath better.
