# What is Original Mind?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **refugeinbuddha** · *परमानन्द Paramananda* · 4 months ago

<https://www.reddit.com/r/genuinemeditation/comments/39ppgk/what_is_original_mind/>

I think that this is essential to be pointed out, before anybody starts to fall into false practices. I tried to simplify it to remove as much jargon as possible.

• Note: This is in reference to the Surangama Sutra[1] , and anybody who is serious about wanting to understand how to gain liberation needs to study this sutra.

The Buddha said that there are two minds.

The first is the thinking mind. This is the mind that we are constantly identified with. Due to its unceasing nature of having thought after thought, we have always thought that we are this very mind.

The second is the spirit/bodhi mind. This is the true nature. Some might call this spirit, the divine or the higher self. This is spirit, or the "essence". When this essence moves, our thoughts move, our awareness moves and then it functions outside as objects.

What is an object? An object is anything that you sense with your six senses of sight, hearing, taste, touch, smell and thought. What you see, what you hear, what you taste, what you touch, what you smell and what you think - All of these are objects!

And where do these objects come from? When the essence moves, these objects function.

The problem is this: We identify ourselves with the thinking mind, instead of the bodhi mind. This has caused huge problems. This can lead people down paths of completely wrong views.

Thinking about enlightenment, that is an object. Thinking about this or that, those are objects. Hearing a word, that is an object. Seizing onto an idea of emptiness, that is an object. Seizing onto repression or suppression, that is an object. Clinging onto an ego, that is an object. Having the idea of an ego, that is an object. Having the idea of duality, that is an object. Do you see where I am getting at?

The bodhi mind is the one that is left after all objects are gotten rid of. It is what remains when all impermanence is stripped away. It is a fundamental illumination that gives birth to everything else.

When you sit in meditation, you notice all kinds of things. You notice your breath, your posture, your method and all of this. You think of enlightenment. You think of having a state. You think of reaching jhana. You think of all the things you have learnt.

Stop! Why do that? It is like emptying a tank from the bottom, and adding more water on the top. The tank will never empty.

For example, when you notice a thought arising, some people try to chase away the thought. However, you want to imagine this.

In spring, the trees are covered with green leaves. The branches can hardly be seen, but they are what gives birth to the leaves. Now when autumn comes, the leaves all fall, leaving the branches exposed.

Just like that, you should let all objects fall away spontaneously so that your bodhi mind remains. How is this done? When conditions are met. Not dwelling in these thoughts, that is the condition.

When there is a person going around telling you lies about the world, what do you do? You do not go up to him and do anything to him. You simply ignore him. You simply treat a fool as a fool, and go around with your own business.

If you understand that the body is a false shell, then you should understand that your thinking mind is also a false shell. Treat it the same way as you do to your body.

By taking identity on these objects, that is where we are confusing ourselves even more. Thoughts upon thoughts - It creates a sticky web of endless objects.

Not dwelling. Even in "emptiness", this is clinging to the idea of emptiness and not enlightenment.

I hope this helped.

If the mind is already emptied, but then you arouse a second thought, wishing to empty its objects, this means that this mind is not yet empty, and is again carried away by objects.

I agree completely. I think that his point can be expressed more clearly in the following point: The mind is not emptied, because the original illumination has always been empty.

From the perspective of a busy thinking-mind, because one identifies themselves with this, it seems as though the clouds are drifting apart to let the moon be seen. This is why the enlightened sages always use "negation" to say what it is. It is only by rejecting what is the "not-self", can this bodhi-mind be approached - This is known as Jnana Yoga in yoga circles.

It is possible for one to gain realization with a snap. To suddenly see that fundamental illumination when one has gone into a deep absorption state. But even if one has seen, there is still the stage of cultivation of the three kayas/bodies. Also, there are always abstract fetters binding one back (hence the various degrees of enlightenment) - It is easy to see, but freeing oneself of the fetters takes diligence.

  "Sudden enlightenment" indicates realization. But it is important to understand what this means.

  In the case of the Buddha, he had already gone through his physical cultivation through his extreme austerity practices. It was only when he restored the health of his body with the supplement of external chi that he finally broke through the barrier.

  For Hui-Neng, he was said to be the reincarnation of an enlightened sage. For such individuals, it is a case of burning their remnant karma. There is no way to know the actual case behind him, but my teacher often said that even Hui-Neng retreated to cultivate for a few years, after receiving his direct transmission from the Fifth Patriarch.

  Seongcheol felt as if "a bright light had suddenly been lit in complete darkness," and that he had finally found the way to the ultimate truth

  From what I gather from here, it seems that he has gained realization. It is like a glimpse out of Plato's Cave. Once you have a paradigm shift, you cannot go back to the same way of viewing anything. However, you still need to work on cultivation. It is only that the goal has become far clearer. To move towards a "full awakening", one has to be diligent to cultivate the three kayas.
