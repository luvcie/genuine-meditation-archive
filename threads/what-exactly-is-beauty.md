# What exactly is beauty?

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **Sattv** · *(OCR)* · `Question` · 2 points · 1 year ago

I want to address the next questions to Paramananda since I feel that a very penetrating insight is needed to answer them satisfactorily.

What is beauty? What is it's primordial nature and source? How is it that a voice, a face or a work of art can be beautiful? When and why does it get impregnated with beauty?

In only a week I've suddenly gotten very sensitive to physical/artistic manifestations of beauty, I couldn't feel it so clearly before, and it strangely became obsessive really fast (I particularly liked the face of a girl, and a group of them that sing and dance), now it has become a urging drive to understand the actual nature of the beauty I feel, beyond it's temporal expressions and intuitively believing that I must learn something important out of this. I've tried to understand it but these are extremely muddy waters to explore with the light of my small insight, this is how far I can get:

I see the girl dancing and singing, she radiates beauty capable of being felt by most people with little to no attuning needed, a part of it comes from qualities in her face and body, and another part from qualities in the way she sings and dances, it's also because from my perspective these qualities appear positive and beautiful. She is perceived as the source of the beautiful actions (and in part her body too, keeping it fit, healthy, applying makeup...) and in turn it gives the impression that she's beautiful, but she is not her body, personality or mind, her true self (purusha) of pure consciousness appears to be the primary cause of the consecutive beautiful qualities (prakriti) in the same way that the void or the monad is the source of maya and duality, thus it seems to me that her true self transcends or is beauty itself, and the beauty in maya is the expression of qualities and structures that we deem virtuous, in the sense that they point to God and/or we feel they get us closer to our divine natures. Not all of us perceive the same qualities in the same thing or are able to perfectly distingish what is virtuous and what is not, that would explain a part of how beauty is said to be in the eye of the beholder. There are also sciences and proportions in nature like math, divine geometry, astrology... that are perceived as beautiful and it could be said it's because they point to God because of their intrincate order, sophistication and abundance. Another good example would be how virtuous qualities in a person's personality seem to augment his beauty, perfectly fusing together in a single entity with his virtuous physical qualities even if they're few. It also makes sense how beauty is described as "angelic" sometimes.

Is this correct? What do I lack? I suppose the next step is to not only approach it mentally but to always feel the complete process that is beauty from it's emanation to it's expressions, that would be nice to avoid getting caught in only temporal expressions of beauty.

EDIT: Wow, writing my insights about this has cleared my ideas and significantly relaxed the obsession, I feel very relieved now that I've given myself a decent answer but I'm still very much looking forward to your response and perspective on beauty, /r/refugeinbuddha.

  Beauty is a perception that is internally experienced. What is beautiful to one might not be beautiful to another. However, the experience of beauty can be found in many beings - Why is that so?

  It is involved in the feeling that arises when one lets go. When one is mesmerized by a certain sensory form, the thinking consciousness temporarily ceases. In this state of non-thought, joy and bliss arises. However, one must differentiate this from entertaining thoughts of thrill as pleasure.

  But alas, all things that arise will fade away. If one were to seek sensory pleasure, the Buddha said, it leads to indulgence. If one were to avoid pleasure to extremes, the Buddha said, it leads to extreme asceticism. Residing in the middle path, one attends to the jhanas - from this jhana one experiences beauty - one experiences joy and bliss beyond comparison - the source that comes without conditioning of sense objects.

  Do not shun the mental pleasure that arises which does not arise from the 5 sensory gates. However, do not be attached to it either - for what rises can surely disappear.

  When one enters the jhanas, one's mindfulness faculty becomes heightened. With this intensity, one can penetrate into the truths of many things. Insights flower in things. Residing in the four jhanas as one goes about one's day, one feels beauty regardless of any pain or pleasure perception.

  There is no external object as one might think - all is only ever experienced within the mind and its consciousnesses. Therefore, there is no "out there"”, but only "within". When all faculties are purified, one sees far with precision and outside of maya.
