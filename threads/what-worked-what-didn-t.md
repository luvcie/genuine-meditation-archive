# What worked, what didn't

**r/genuinemeditation** · by **blissfulencounters** · *(OCR)* · `Discussion` · 4 points · 1 year ago

Hey sangha friends,

I thought I'd post some of my own personal investigations in my meditation sessions here... So here goes. I don't claim it as the best so if you find something better just add on to the thread :)

What worked...

o I realize that everytime I drop down to relax, the last muscles that relax completely would be my Trapezius (or back muscles) that reach up to the neck... So my first instinct now is to immediately focus on dropping them to relax them completely.

While doing that, I'd also pull the shoulders slightly back a little... They tend to open up my chest and relieve the pressure of the body pressing against the chest...

Focusing on the 'knowing' or the mental state works for me... As refuge says, thought links to breath eventually, and I found that it worked for me. My breath and thought would synchronise... then when there was this little gap where my thought momentarily stops, the breath would go away together...

Opening up to joy is really really important... so try to soften and have that feeling of surrendering to whatever is happening... so in a way it's as if you're just baring yourself, becoming naked to whatever is happening... (crude description I know...)

If you have your eyes open, then one way I found was to soften your gaze slowly... Then going into peripheral vision. This was what I learnt in qigong lessons or Tai Chi, so what it does is that it drops you into this state of huge relaxation and cuts away the mental chatter...

Vegan diet! I replaced cow's milk with rice milk for calcium... I get sufficient sunlight to increase vitamin D for calcium absorption... I take spinach/lentils for iron and vitamin C for absorption... I take wheatgrass... Huge spread of vegetables to increase amino acid diversity... Yeah. Fruits too, to feel fuller. Nuts for needed fat, if not you're not going to get healthy levels of hormone. Add some Omega-3 supplements too.

An aerobic cardiovascular workout helped me a lot too. I'd do 20 minute jogs or runs, or just simple walking.

I carried out waking-hour mindfulness by imagining myself as a third-person perspective... then seeing thoughts arise, feelings arise, etc... You'll be surprised when this happens... It's as if you notice all the patterns.. the stereotypes.. the habits that pop out that you never knew governed your actions... and others.

To learn how to relax last time, I used to do these self-hypnosis tapes that helped me with progressive relaxation... my first experience came from the Silva Method. But yeah it can be really helpful for relaxation...

What didn't...

e I realize that when trying to do full-lotus, if I elevate my butt too much it causes a huge ton of strain on my ankles... so I now try to get my butt as close to the ground as possible...

If you're in huge huge pain, don't try full-lotus until your legs soften up... I injured my ankles and knees before like that...

Meditating in dark places will surge your melatonin, especially if you're slightly fatigued... Poof, there you go... deep in sleep! I find myself drooling over my shirt when I woke up in the past, so now I make sure that the room I'm in is decently lit, so that the body's bioclock doesn't just plunge down to sleep-mode...

Skipping meditation for more than a day just does huge impairments in my mood... It's a lesson in impermanence I guess, another reason to strive on...

Tracking my own attainments... I realized that the more caught-up I was in whether I opened this or that, or whether I did this or that, or what stage I'm in... I felt as if the more I was stuck in that stage. When I just dropped it all, and simply met the conditions to get to that, then it became consistent over and over again.

Just sharing what I've learnt!!! Looking forward to you people posting too ;)

Strive on mates.
